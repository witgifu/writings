There was a knock on Cliopher's office door, just as the midnight bell rang. He looked up, expecting to see a page. Instead, it was Conju.

Cliopher immediately began turning over folders to find his diary. “I'm terribly sorry, Conju, did I forget? Oh stars, today has been so busy.”

Conju rolled his eyes. “I don't think it's possible for you to forget an appointment, Cliopher. No, I finished with himself and then came to see if I could find you, so you could deny the *most* delicious rumour.”

Blinking, Cliopher tidied his desk absently. “Oh?” he said, wondering what it could be.

Conju raised an eyebrow and asked, “Did a pirate queen fall in love with you, and that's the reason you're always turning everyone down? And did you have twins, a boy and a girl, and she took the girl to teach her to be a pirate, and you secretly keep the boy in your room teaching him to be a spy?”

“No,” said Cliopher, and then sighed, “Although it's not as wrong as the usual rumours. I wonder who…” He tried to remember who had been around when he had come back and told Kiri. Probably a lot of people, he hadn't been thinking clearly.

Conju slid into the office and closed the door behind him. “Do tell,” he said, “The pirate queen? Surely not.”

Struggling to make eye contact, Cliopher said, “I found out today I have a son. His mother is in the navy, so rather the opposite of a pirate.” By way of explanation for his current circumstances, he added, “I spent all afternoon trying to prepare, and now I'm catching up on work so that I'm not behind tomorrow.”

“That's not the kind of thing to joke about, Cliopher,” said Conju.

Cliopher put his head in his hands. “I'm not joking,” he said, slightly muffled, “He's four. I had no idea, none. I could have walked past him a hundred times in Solaara and not known.” He probably did, if Dimiter had gone to one of the festivals that the Emperor opened. 

Conju interrupted the start of Cliopher's latest guilt spiral. “Well… but he's not legitimate, is he? You weren't married? I'm sure a suitable foster could be found,” said Conju. He looked slightly confused, but not worried.

Cliopher didn't know when he'd stood up, but he found himself on his feet, leaning over his desk. Awkwardly, he forced himself to relax a little. “Legitimacy isn't a thing among Islanders. We add to family, not cut people off.” Unable to stop himself, he added, “Of course adultery is looked down upon, if you swore fidelity and broke it. But the _child_ would never be blamed.”

“Oh,” said Conju, clearly surprised at Cliopher's strong reaction. Cliopher was also, to be honest with himself, a tad surprised. Given Conju had no interest in women, it was possible he hadn't thought about what it might mean to have a child of his own since he was a teenager. His attitude would no doubt be shared by many at court, who would no doubt thrill to have yet another reason to look down on Cliopher, the barbarian upstart.

Conju's usual businesslike demeanour returned. “Well, in that case, yes of course, that's going to be a big change. How can I help?”

Now it was Cliopher's turn for shock. He didn't want to burden his already very busy friend. “You don't need—”

With a dismissive wave, Conju interrupted. “Don't be ridiculous, Cliopher. You can't possibly expect to do everything alone, in whatever few off hours you give yourself.”

Cliopher slumped back down into his chair, which rocked slightly with the force. Conju was right. With no family here, and a demanding job, how could he responsibly care for a child? Could he actually offer anything better than the care home, or the foster home Conju proposed? Perhaps he would have to quit, or find a different role in the Service.

But how would he ever tell his Radiancy? His lord depended on him, as he did Conju. Needed them both. Oh, he was sure his lord would cope if he died or was incapacitated, the protocols were ready and waiting. But to simply quit? Or request reassignment?

“Please,” he said softly, hating how weak he sounded.

“The first thing you are going to do is apply for two weeks of paternity leave. I listened enough to you explaining how it works, don't tell me you're not eligible,” said Conju, firmly, “And then you're going to move out of that cupboard.”

“I sent a note to the Palace facilities office,” said Cliopher, “They sent me back some lists, as a single parent with one child I'd be entitled to a three-room apartment…” He pulled the relevant papers from a folder. “I haven't decided which yet, or contacted them about moving.”

“Cliopher,” said Conju firmly, “Did you actually tell them you're the Secretary in Chief of the Private Office? Or even the Private Secretary?”

“No?” said Cliopher, “There was a form, and I filled it in.”

“Of course you did,” said Conju, “Let's go down now and get your keys sorted then.”

“But…” said Cliopher, picking up his writing kit, “I haven't really looked at the list. And surely the office will be closed.” Conju wasn't going to insist on waking someone up, was he?

“There is always someone on duty. Some people don't trust magic locks, and they get drunk, and lock themselves out,” said Conju, as they walked through the dark and empty main room of the Offices of State.

“But the first part then, which apartment to pick? I guess I should just take whichever is closest, they're probably all very similar.” Cliopher opened his writing kit, and tried to find the relevant page, a skill he was well-used to doing while walking.

Conju snorted, “Put the list away, Cliopher. Both the Private Secretary and the Secretary in Chief have grace-and-favour apartments. Possibly the Hands of the Emperor does too, although most of them lived in the Imperial Apartments.”

“Oh,” said Cliopher, as they hurried down a fourth flight of stairs, “Do you know where those residences are?”

“Ystharian Wing and Collian Wing, four floors down and two corridors out,” said Conju, turning left into a hallway.

“Wait, isn't that second one very close to *your* apartment?” said Cliopher, “And when I worked for Indrogan, we had offices near the other.” He couldn't remember seeing any of his predecessors as Private Secretary living there. But as one of the secretaries hadn't even made it through the bows, perhaps nobody had bothered moving into them. Or of course, they could have been damaged in the Fall.

“Yes,” said Conju, “They were both occupied during the Littleridge negotiations, so they must have been repaired as residential space.”

“I wouldn't want to force anyone else to move out,” said Cliopher, as Conju knocked primly on a door.

“Don't be silly, Cliopher,” said Conju, and the door opened in front of them.

“Lord Conju! Which of your friends is upstairs trying to convince a vase they were school champion boxer?” said an old man, with a cackle at his own joke.

“None tonight,” replied Conju smoothly, following the old man inside the small office, “I was hoping you might be able to let us in to the apartments for the Private Secretary and the Secretary in Chief, so I can help Sayo Mdang choose which he will take up.” He gently indicated towards Cliopher, who stepped into the room with them.

“Finally convinced sir to stop living at the edge of the continent, eh?” said the old man, and turned around to survey what looked like a giant wooden honeycomb. He flitted his fingers across it, picking up one thin wooden tag, and then tapping irritatedly at an empty slot.

“Oh yes,” he said, and sighed, “I'm afraid, sir, I can only give you access to the Secretary in Chief at the moment. Lady Denovan and Ser Ponon had rather a set too, and we've had to put them in separate suites until we can fix the plumbing. But I'm sure you'll take this one anyway, it's far nicer.”

He handed Cliopher the thin strip of wood, which Cliopher accepted. “Once you're happy, sir, come down tomorrow, and we'll get you and your staff, and your friends,” he gave Conju an eyebrow waggle, “Access permanently. But that'll let you in until then. Do try not to lock it inside, or we'll have to bother the wizards.”

Cliopher nodded, “Thank you very much.”

“No trouble, no trouble,” said the old man. Cliopher was pretty sure he heard the man snicker as he closed the door.

“Does he….?” asked Cliopher, as they headed back up.

“Think were going to go at it like rabbits on the dust sheets? Absolutely,” said Conju, “But you wouldn't want some prude manning that desk, would you?”

“I suppose not,” said Cliopher with a smile.

They trekked back upstairs in silence. Cliopher disliked that his positions came with multiple homes that were just meant to be left empty if he wasn't using them. How many aristocrats had both a family apartment and a grace-and-favour one? Did—

“Yes, I have a small suite, inside the Imperial Apartments. I don't use it, staff sometimes put their children there. Or it's used as a meeting room. Once there was a litter of kittens and their mother living there.” Conju sounded mildly offended by the last part, but Cliopher suspected he had fallen in love with the tiny cats just as much as anyone else.

The door to Cliopher's new apartment was unremarkable. Unlike the carved door to the an Vilius apartment, which was just out of view up the hall and around a corner. Given it has previously been a half hour walk for Conju or Cliopher to visit before, it seemed decadent to be so close. Or to be considering being so close; Cliopher still wasn't sure about accepting this apartment. Surely, like Conju's extra suite, it could be put to better use.

Cliopher pushed the door open, and stepped inside. With a shake of his head, Conju clapped the lights on. The vestibule looked more like a waiting room than a home, with benches built in on both sides. At the end was an abstract stained-glass window that Cliopher though might have meant to represent the city the Palace used to sit beside. Astandalas the Golden.

“I suppose some of your predecessors must have worked from here,” said Conju, moving forward without hesitation. Indeed, the door on the right led to a room with a large desk. “It's serviceable,” was all Conju said about it. It was hard to be sure at night, but it looked as if the view out the narrow windows might be of the neighbour's wall.

The next door down led to a hallway, which Conju gave a more approving nod. Two further doors revealed a formal dining room in the old Astandalan style, and a large parlour. Another door led to yet another hallway.

“It should be primary suite, and then two more if I'm not mistaken. Sometimes it's three,” said Conju, opening a door to a room with a massive Alinorel bed, and large windows out onto the gardens. “Bit bare,” he added, “But I suppose you can put your decrepit couch in here.”

Cliopher was about to protest the slight to his favourite piece of furniture, but Conju snapped the door shut and moved down to the next one. “That's a choice,” he said, and Cliopher moved to catch up and look inside.

The rest of the apartment had been unremarkable, in a late-Astandalan style. Lots of marble, dark woods, gilt edges, and red dado walls. This room had clearly been decorated for someone in particular, because everything was white. The bed was painted white, the floor, the walls. It reminded Cliopher of the snow that sometimes fell in Astandalas.

“I suppose I can always go heavy on the carpets and tapestries,” said Cliopher.

“Or redecorate,” said Conju, dismissively, “Are the rest…”

The next room was decorated much like the first, although smaller. The last one was entirely empty. “Barely large enough for family, perhaps a companion used it,” was Conju's only comment on that one.

The final door in the hallway Conju dismissed, “The service rooms, I'm sure they're fine.”

“Wait,” said Cliopher, “Is there a kitchen?”

“Cliopher,” said Conju dryly, “I have never known you to even warm up a bowl of soup in your room.”

“I make coffee!” Cliopher protested.

Conju didn't stop him opening the door, but he did roll his eyes. The kitchen was perfectly adequate, as was the small staff parlour and the two bedrooms. Although, Cliopher couldn't possibly imagine why *he* would need six bedrooms. Even with his son (his son!) they surely only needed two.

“Sufficient,” declared Conju as they circled back to the entrance. “Now, I will organise for your things to be moved tomorrow morning, so you can spend the day doing whatever else you have on your list. Don't worry about any extra furniture for the boy, I'll handle that too.”

“Aren't *you* working tomorrow?” checked Cliopher, feeling overwhelmed again.

“Yes,” said Conju, waving dismissively, “I have staff, I can delegate. Speaking of staff, you will need at least one person. I don't suppose I can convince you of two? No, don't frown like that. You'll insist on sending him to the childcare and the public school as well I suppose.”

Cliopher hadn't thought as far as school. But he wasn't going to have his son raised by a nanny like Conju no doubt would have. He didn't even really see the argument for any staff. Cliopher was no aristocrat, puffing up his importance by having a train of people following him around.

“Hm, I see you're about to argue,” said Conju. “Will you bring your son along to the midnight meeting when there's an emergency? Or leave him to wake alone and toddle around the Palace to find you?”

Horrified, Cliopher sank down onto the bench. The image of a little boy looking for him through the bewildering Palace halls gripped him. “I can't… I can't leave Dimiter in the children's home, Conju. He's my son! And I can't send him home, what if his mother comes looking for him?”

“Of course,” said Conju, clearly surprised by how hard his words had hit, “I only meant that if you had someone else here…”

“It should be family, but,” said Cliopher with a sigh, “We must work with what is possible.”

“I'll see if we can't find you someone. And if there was a real emergency, Cliopher, you know I or Rhodin would help,” said Conju.

“Thank you,” said Cliopher, “And thank you for helping me now. I should probably go back to the office and—”

“I will send someone to fetch the paternity leave form, and I will explain matters to his Radiancy in the morning. Saya Kalikiri already knows?” said Conju, and Cliopher nodded mutely, “Then she can fill in for tomorrow morning. I'm sure she already offered.”

“She did,” said Cliopher, “She said it would be only fair, given she had a year of maternity leave.”

“See? It is only you that expects you to save the world *and* raise a child alone,” said Conju, gently resting a hand on Cliopher's shoulder.

Cliopher looked up at his friend, immensely grateful. Not just for the many things he was offering to do, but that he wasn't trying to force Cliopher into the way *he* would have done things. He smiled.

“Now,” said Conju, taking back his hand, and putting on his usual court face, “You are coming back to my place and going to bed.” Cliopher decided to stop arguing. He was tired.
