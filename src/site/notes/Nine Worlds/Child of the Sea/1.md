If there was something keeping Cliopher fit in the Palace of Stars, it was probably how far apart the meetings seemed to be held. Not in time, no that would be too convenient. Only in space. Now that he was head of the Service, he could in theory tell all the ministers to come to the Offices of the Lords of State. That seemed like a good way to annoy a lot of nobles over nothing much, so he let them pick the time and location, and he wrote the agendas. It was efficient.

It did mean that pages would sometimes have trouble finding him, or perhaps more precisely, catching up to him. His daily appointments were listed on his office door, so they knew the general area he should be found in. However, if they didn't want to interrupt him in a meeting (and they mostly didn't) then they had to meet him coming or going.

So it wasn't a surprise when a slightly winded page called out “Sir!” from the bottom of a staircase. Cliopher did his best not to make the pages jobs any harder than they already were, so he politely waited for the young woman to make it to the top of the stairs, before continuing with her beside him.

“You have,” she said, trying to catch her breath, “A personal message. Marked urgent.” She handed it over.

Cliopher handed to her his notes from the meeting with the Education Minister. “Can you take these back to the office? That'll give me time to read whatever this is before the Local Infrastructure Review.”

“Of course, sir,” she said, and trotted back off the way they had come with the bundle of paper.

The outside of the envelope was unremarkable. It was cheap, and could just as easily be from his bank, or some resident of Solaara sending out mass marketing pamphlets. He'd done his best to crack down on those, waste of paper that they were, but if the sender was willing to pay for postage there wasn't much he could do. The stamp indicated it had come from the city, and not further afield. He slipped his finger under the edge, and pulled it open.

It wasn't from his bank. It was from the free hospital. Cliopher had badgered the city council and the Health Minister for such a long time to get a free hospital. He wasn't even sure that in the end they'd agreed based on his arguments, but rather just to get him to shut up and go bother someone else for a while. Well, it was all the same to him.

The letter started by letting him know about the unfortunate death of his mother-in-law of pneumonia. Cliopher was reasonably sure he'd never accidentally got married, and that he didn't have a mother-in-law. He had to read the address a number of times to be sure it actually had his name on it. Then it said that due to these sad circumstances his son (at this point he checked the address again) had been put into temporary care. They had tried to reach out to his wife, but been informed she was currently at sea. Could he please contact them as soon as possible with proof of identity, and they would share the location of his son so he could make further arrangements for his care.

Cliopher rather wished he *hadn't* read the letter before the Local Infrastructure Review. It must be some kind of mistake, but it would no doubt require him to go into the city to clear it up. The more time he took in doing so, the longer this poor child would be in care, and away from his actual father. Whoever, or wherever, he was.

Frowning, Cliopher headed instead for the Offices of the Lords of State. He arrived at the same time as the page. She looked embarrassed, but he smiled and continued without a comment to her.

“Kiri?” he called out, rushing through the front room.

“Yes, sir?” she said, opening a door to a meeting room.

“Is there anyone else we could send to the Local Infrastructure Review?” he asked, and before he could even clarify why, several hands shot up from the surrounding desks.

“Easy done, sir,” said Kiri, “Everyone likes that one, sir, they're all good-looking guild masters.”

It had never occurred to Cliopher that they were attractive. He'd just liked how everyone was used to handling budgets and could be trusted to do percentages on their own. “Thank you,” he said, “That's the last one for the day.”

“Even better,” said one of the young secretaries, “Maybe we'll get invited for drinks!” The others around them giggled or rolled their eyes.

“I'll trust you to pick someone who will remember to take noes,” said Cliopher to Kiri, before turning around and heading out again.

Out the front gates, he jumped on the main loop omnibus, throwing a coin into the fare box. Then he wriggled inside, trying to find a place to stand where he wasn't elbowing anyone else. The ‘bus was full of Palace staff and visitors. His robes were unremarkable (he spotted five other members of the Service) except for their unusual colours.

The free hospital wasn't far, and he wasn't the only person who got off there. Three of them made their way to the front desk, Cliopher letting the others queue ahead of him. The waits might be longer, and the facilities more basic, but he knew based on the letters the Office of State received, that the hospital was very much appreciated. He had plans to extend it, enable more preventative healthcare rather than just helping when things had gotten so bad you couldn't look after yourself.

While he waited, he did a mental review of the work so far today, and what he needed to do before tomorrow. There were two reports he should read, and another that could do with a critical editing pass before it was presented to the Council of Princes in a few weeks time. He almost pulled it out of his writing case, but then he made it to the front of the queue.

“I received a letter about a recent death, but I think it should probably have gone to someone else? There's a child involved, so I thought I'd come in person to try to clear it up as quickly as possible,” said Cliopher.

The man at the desk took a look at the letterhead, and then called back to someone else behind him. They in turned looked at it, and nodded.

“It's taken a bit to track you down, Sayo Mdang,” said the second person, “But come down to the offices, it's always possible we've got it wrong.” They walked around the desk, and then let Cliopher through a locked door.

“We're not trapping you in, sir,” they said, “Only sometimes it's safer to lock these doors. Desperate people, you know.”

“Stealing things?” asked Cliopher. He hadn't heard the hospital had problems with that.

“No,” they said, “If only. But we only have so much budget. Sometimes people come badgering the staff. Begging for untested treatments they've seen in newspapers. They don't want to hear that it doesn't usually work, or even if it does for some people, it probably won't for their loved one.”

“I understand,” said Cliopher, and made a mental note to see if he could get the recent laws passed to stop people deliberately advertising incorrect pricing extended to unverified medical claims. And perhaps extend the counselling services of the hospital to family members.

They eventually knocked on a door marked, “Community Services.”

“Yes!” cried an exasperated voice from inside. “Though if you're here to tell me Sayo Mdang is _also_ dead I might need to scream.”

Cliopher's escort opened the door, and ushered him inside. “No,” they said calmly, “He's here.”

“Sayo Mdang!” said a woman with two pens sticking out of her hair, and a chubby face, “And you really are in the Service! I know I'm not supposed to talk about the time fluctuations, but they do make my job so much harder, and I'm so very happy to find I'm not talking to your confused great-grandchild.”

“Uh,” said Cliopher, “Sorry, but I'm still not entirely sure that you have the right person? I'm not married, and I don't have a son.”

The escort raised their eyebrows. “I'll leave him with you, then, Saya Innara,” they said, and left.

“I wondered,” said Saya Innara, moving a stack of folders from a chair to the floor, “Have a seat.”

Nervously, Cliopher sat down. He tried to think how he could possibly have had these things happen to him and not noticed. It seemed, despite all the many problems Zunidh had experienced since the fall, unlikely. Not, alas, entirely impossible.

“I should check you are who I'm looking for first. Cliopher sayo Mdang, Hands of the Emperor, Secretary in Chief of the Offices of the Lords of State?” she asked.

“Yes,” said Cliopher, putting his writing kit down next to his feet.

“You have some identification? Your service paybook would do,” said Saya Innara, with a smile.

Cliopher popped the catch on his writing kit, and drew out the paybook. He could have shown her the seal he had as Secretary in Chief, but that seemed like boasting. Wincing at how beaten up it looked, he handed it over.

“Before the Fall! Oh, I'm glad to see that,” she said, and flipped to the name page. There, in the blue-black ink of the Treasury, was his name and a list of his most recent appointments. He really should replace it, it was very nearly full.

“All in good order, thank you,” she said, and handed it back. Cliopher slipped it away in his writing case again. “And you are from the Vangaveye-ve?”

“Yes,” said Cliopher, uncertain why that was relevant.

“You came back from there with the first ship to make it through after the Fall?” she asked, moving papers around on her desk, clearly looking for something. She hadn't taken a seat, because the one behind the desk was stacked with boxes.

“Yes,” said Cliopher. He'd tried not to think about that journey, since he'd got back. It hadn't been bad, obviously nothing like the trials of the outward journey. But he'd been upset, and uncertain, and it just seemed to fit with a time in his life he'd moved on from. He was the Private Secretary to the Lord Emperor, he was _busy_.

Now, however, he did think back. “Oh,” he said softly, “Oh no.” Leaning back in the chair, he raised up both hands to his face. “I don't, we weren't married,” he said softly, “I don't even remember her name. She was the ship's carpenter?”

“Yes. Lieutenant Momo Yana. She decided to put you down as the father on her son's birth registration. Cliopher sayo Mdang, employed by the Service in the Palace of Stars,” said Saya Innara, pointing to the paper she had been looking for.

“That's me,” said Cliopher, swallowing. His mind raced, trying to remember. They'd used protection, hadn't they? The memories were hazy with how drunk he had been and the years in-between. 

“As I said in the letter, her son has been living with her mother in Solaara since then, while Lieutenant Yana was working,” continued Saya Innara, “Two weeks ago Saya Yana fell ill unexpectedly, and her neighbours brought her and the boy here. She passed away last week. We have tried to contact the Lieutenant, but we believe she hasn't visited her mother and son in quite a while.”

“I—,” said Cliopher, and stopped, not sure where he was going. He had a son? Why had Momo never told him, but put his name on the birth registration? It wasn't like he'd been far away; if he'd known, he would have done something to help. At the very least sent money for the boy's care, even if she wanted nothing else to do with him.

His mother was going to flay him alive. She would brook no excuses that he hadn't known, to her, it would be inconceivable that he had been so irresponsible. To be honest, it was. If he'd met anyone now, he would hope to stay friends if they didn't remain lovers.

The guilt churned inside him. Now it was too late, now the boy had gone years, the family presumably thinking he didn't care. The child---

“What's the boy, my son's, name?” he eventually asked.

“Dimiter Yana,” she said, “He's about four.”

Had Cliopher told Momo about his cousin? Or had she heard about the explorer, and named him without knowing? Perhaps, he could find her and ask. Of course, when she found out her mother had died, she might want to take the boy herself. Cliopher could hardly blame her for that. Until then.

He would do everything he could, of course. Dimiter needed a home, and someone to love and care for him. Perhaps Cliopher wasn't the best choice, with nobody in Solaara for support. But he was who Dimiter had now, and Cliopher would simply have to do his best.

There was so *much* he would need to do. His little rooms as the end of the Alinoral wing wouldn't be suitable. He'd need somewhere closer to his own work, and to the Palace childcare. Somewhere with more than a main room and a bathroom. A proper home for a child to live in. His child. Dimiter.

“What do I need to do?” asked Cliopher, nervously.

“Yes!” said Saya Innara, loudly. Then at a more reasonable volume, added, “Sorry, sir, uh. You have to understand. My job is to reunite people, but that's often much harder than it sounds. It is so wonderful every time I can pull a child _out_ of care and into their family.”

Cliopher nodded, mutely. He'd seen the reports, the statistics. The care homes struggled to find enough space. The city did what they could with 'outdoor' care to keep children with parents who had little or no money to look after them. But even so, parents were sick, abusive, disappeared. It was objectively good news that he could be the reason there was an extra tick in the 'reunited' column.

“You'll get short-term temporary custody,” said Saya Innara, handing him an official form. “Then you'll need to present yourself, Dimiter, and his birth certificate at the city court. If they can confirm paternity, and usually the wizard there can, you can get permanent joint custody.”

“I wouldn't want to take him from his mother,” said Cliopher quickly.

Saya Innara shrugged. “It only has to go back to court if the two of you can't agree on what 'joint custody' means. If she turns up and demands Dimiter and you hand him over, the courts don't really care until you sue to get him back.”

Cliopher raised an eyebrow. He could see how her job had led her to an _unusual_ view of the law. However, she had a point. It would only really matter if they disagreed.

“You said his mother hasn't visited recently?” he asked, cautiously.

“It's hard to *know* if it's just the time fluctuations,” she said, and tapped on the desk, presumably to ward off time disturbances, “But the ship she's listed as crew on has docked in Solaara at least six times since he was born. None of the neighbours can recall seeing her. There's not even any records of her sending some of her pay to help her mother.”

“You have access to that?” asked Cliopher, shocked. Bank transactions and pay records were protected by quite strict laws, from Imperial days.

“I have to get a search warrant at the court,” she said, not all offended, “But I can get access to them when I need to. When it's a matter of trying to track down an orphan's living relatives.”

Cliopher nodded. “Okay, so I just take this form to,” Cliopher looked down at it, “The Parkside Children's Home. And they will hand me my son?”

“Yep,” she said, “Oh, they will check your documents again, I hope. He should have some things with him. When you get permanent custody, you'll be able to claim his inheritance. The council have probably locked it up in a storage unit somewhere, I imagine it's just the furniture and so on from the flat.”

Putting the form into his writing kit, Cliopher thought it would be good for Dimiter to have as many familiar things from his old home as possible. Being taken from his grandmother, and then shoved into a children's home full of strangers, and then into the Palace, was going to be scary.

If Lieutenant Yana had died, or formally given Dimiter up, he might have considered trying to work out how to send the boy home to his family in the Vangaveye-ve. Where Dimiter could grow up surrounded by the extended Mdang family. That path seemed awfully presumptive when the boy's mother was still alive, just hadn't been able to be reached yet.

“Right,” he said, “Okay.” Standing up, he took a deep breath. “Thank you for all your hard work, Saya Innara. For my son, but also all the people of Solarra.”

She smiled. “Maybe in the next budget cycle, you could get me another filing cabinet?” she asked, looking around the room with a sigh of resignation.

“I'll see what I can do,” he said with a smile, wondering if perhaps the hospital could really do with a librarian, or an archivist, and a few more social workers. And who he would have to wheedle to get it.

Waiting at the bus stop, Cliopher considered what he needed to do. There was no way he could go to the children’s home today, he didn't even have a bed for Dimiter to sleep in. The first thing he needed to do was go back to the Palace and try to organise his life so it had space for his son.
