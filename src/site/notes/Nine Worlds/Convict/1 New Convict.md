The regular slap of the guards' shoes approached, and Cliopher scrambled to stand against the back wall of his cell. Inspections were usually two in the afternoon, not so early in the morning. They must be bringing in someone new. Across the hall he could see the three cells, where the prisoners in each were standing just as he was.

To his surprise, the man between the guards was as dark as a high noble. They didn't get nobles in here. Nobles could be fined, or in extenuating circumstances, executed. They didn't get sent to hard labour. Presumably then, despite the colouring, he wasn't a noble. Or at least, not one born on the right side of the sheets.

He had clearly been beaten. That could be the work of the guards, or the professionals, or from before his arrest. The guards were having to hold him up. They stopped at the door to Cliopher's cell. 

"Back up, convict," said the guard on the left. 

Cliopher was already against the wall, but he knew how this went. "Yes ma'am," he said, firmly but not too loud.

The right guard unlocked the door, while the other two held the new prisoner. Less so that he couldn't escape, and more so that he didn't fall down.

"Ma'am, will the medic be arriving soon? All convicts are entitled to medical assistance," said Cliopher, "Regulation 27.2."

The guard with the keys said casually, "Shut up, convict. Hands up."

Cliopher raised his hands up beside his ears, elbows out. "Yes, ma'am. But he needs medical attention, ma'am. He should be able to stand without assistance."

The two guards carrying the new man dropped him onto the bottom bunk, and then all three turned to Cliopher. "We don't care," said the one who had first spoken, "And we don't like being told how to do our jobs by convicts."

"But ma'am," said Cliopher. The guard backhanded him across the face, before he could say any more.

"If you're so keen on him, convict," said the guard with the keys, "You patch him up. And while you're about it, get his name out of him so we can finish up the paperwork."

All three left, without another word to Cliopher, or any of the other prisoners.

"I don't know why Madon bothers," said one of the convicts down the hall, "It's never worked before. Not when he complained about the food, or the clothes, or the bedding."

"Or the cleanliness, or the work hours," added someone else.

"Or the beatings!" added a third person, and there was a general chuckle.

It was true, none of Cliopher's protestations had any effect. But just because he hadn't found a sympathetic guard yet, didn't mean there wasn't one. If he could just get them to _read_ the regulations for themselves, he was sure they would see it was possible to have a much more efficient and civilised way of running a convict prison.

With the ease of long practice, Cliopher ignored the comments of the other convicts. He understood that they needed something to laugh about, and he didn't really mind being the butt of their jokes. Instead, he turned to the new man who had been dumped in his cell.

He was thin, and tall, wiry muscle rather than thin from hunger. There were bruises all down his body, but not the welts of a whip or cane that Cliopher expected to see. 

"Sayo?" said Cliopher, moving slowly in case the new man jumped him, "How are you feeling?"

There was no movement, apart from the shallow rise and fall of the man's chest, under his tunic. Breathing was good. Cliopher reached out a hand for the man's wrist, hoping to check his pulse.

As he half-expected, the man slipped his wrist out of Cliopher's gentle grip, and grabbed Cliopher's arm. The man's eyes flew open, and then he squeezed them shut again.

"Where am I?" he whispered.

"Room 215, block B, of the Zhangora IV Rehabilitation Centre," said Cliopher, wishing the other man would loosen his grip a little.

"Are you...?" the man asked, but didn't seem to have the focus to finish his question.

"Cliopher Mdang, province of the Wide Seas. Here for accessory to piracy," said Cliopher. It was a well-worn introduction. Piracy got you hung, drawn, and quartered. Knowing about piracy and doing nothing about it got you a ten-year sentence. Most of the people in here were low-level drug dealers, or middle-men for stolen goods. None of them were _dangerous_, most of them were really here for the crime of being poor.

In his darker moments, Cliopher knew that part of why the guards were so cruel was to make this place worse than the grinding poverty that was all most of them knew outside. Part of it, of course, was just that they were bored, small-minded, and cruel.

"Ah," said the man, not opening his eyes again, but finally releasing Cliopher's wrist, "In that case I should probably continue to decline to identify myself."

"So you're here for... contempt of court?" asked Cliopher, still a little worried about the man's injuries.

"Probably," he said, "There was a lot of shouting. Someone might have hit me on the head."

"Shouting?" said Cliopher, confused. He'd been to two courts on Zunidh, and one on Ysthar, before his conviction. They'd all been quiet an imposing, and devoid of anyone who didn't have to be there. "What court was that?"

"_The_ court," said the man, with a wave of his hand, "Palace of Stars."

"You were brought before the Emperor—no, wait, sorry. Are you bleeding anywhere? Anything broken?" asked Cliopher. He _definitely_ wanted to hear the man's story, but first aid came first.

"Don't think so?" he said, and rolled onto his side with a groan, "Lots of bruises. Headache."

Cliopher grabbed the leather jack from the sink, filled it up, and brought it over. "Sorry it's not very clean," he said, awkwardly, "They won't give me any sand to scour it properly."

"Water?!" said the man, sounding amazed. Cliopher handed it over, and the man guzzled it down.

About to grab it back, Cliopher said quickly, "Careful! Don't make yourself sick."

"S'true," said the man, sadly, and began sipping more slowly, "Thanks."

Squatting down next to the bunk, Cliopher asked, "Do you have a nickname, or something else we can call you?"

"What's the new man, Madon?" called out someone else, "Or are you still trying to give him his Cirthian rites?"

"That's _Aurelian_ rights, idiot," said his roommate, with a laugh, "Cirthian rites is when they dunk you in that lake." A general debate arose around which particular rites belonged to the Cirthian's and which didn't, and if they were in fact also the same cult that worshipped Aurelius Magnus as a god. 

Cliopher ignored them, looking at his new cellmate. "You could play Aurelius Magnus," said Cliopher, "You have the nose. And the accent."

The man groaned, "I know. I'd rather be the Seafarer King though, not much of a fan of Aurelius honestly."

"You don't have the eyebrows," said Cliopher, dryly, "Elonoa'a was Kindraa, and they all have spectacular eyebrows."

"How do you know what the Seafarer King looked like? Or his name?" said the man, sitting up, and handing the empty jack back to Cliopher.

"How do you know the Astandalan Emperor's name, or what he looked like? And Elonoa'a was Paramount Chief, never a king," said Cliopher, rinsing out the jack and putting it back.

The man's brow furrowed in concentration, "Are there decedents of... Elonoa'a... still around? Or pictures?"

"Relatives, yes. My childhood friend Bertie is one. And he's described in detail in our songs," said Cliopher, quietly. Then added more cheerfully, "If you've not got a nickname, we'll just have to give you one then. You said you didn't want 'Aurelius'. So how about 'Magnus'."

"Magnus?" called out one of the others, "Ha! You take a good look when he washes, Madon, and tell us if he earns it!"

There was a general snickering, which rose to the level of laughter. "Sorry," whispered Cliopher, "I didn't think of that, uh, you might be stuck with that nickname now."

The man now known as Magnus flopped down dramatically. "Do you think the guards will be able to write 'Magnus Dick' with a straight face?"

Cliopher snorted, "_Please_ tell them that's your name for the intake form. They'll probably punch you for it, but I'd say it'll be worth it anyway."

Magnus sighed, "I was hoping to be rescued before we got to the stage of _paperwork_. If they hadn't _drugged_ me, I would have rescued myself."

"You can pick locks?" asked Cliopher, "I can do simple ones, but the ones here are beyond everyone except Icle the Pick, who's a blackleg anyway."

"Black... leg?" said Magnus, "Some kind of injury?"

"Turncoat, plant, spy, scab. Dobbs people in to the guards," said Cliopher, with a wave of his hand, "Works when we declare a strike."

"You have a _union_? In a _convict prison_?" said Magnus, "I thought the others were all mocking you for trying to enforce the rules?"

Cliopher shrugged, and climbed up to the top bunk. "Yeah, but they listen when I tell them the waste we're sorting has mercury in it, and we should demand gloves."

There was a silence from the bottom bunk. "Fair," said Magnus.

Rolling to face the wall, Cliopher smiled. Perhaps he'd found his first real ally.
