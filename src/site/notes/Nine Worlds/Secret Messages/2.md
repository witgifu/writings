When the attendant informed you that the page had returned with the reports, you had still been hopeful that they might contain useful information. By the time you entered your library, and saw that there were only three, you had become significantly more doubtful.

The village between Amboloyo and Old Damara was, you knew, tiny. But to see that a sum total of three documents had been written about it in the hundreds of years since it had been conquered by Astandalas was a little disappointing. It was possible that simply meant the area was peaceable, the land safe and fertile. Given it was the current target of the Prince of Amboloyo's expansionism, it seemed unlikely.

Opening the cover of the first report, you were momentarily cheered to see the name of your indomitable secretary as one of the authors. Hopefully that would mean this was at least legible and not too dense with excuses. The title, on flood prevention, did make you slightly concerned that this village had simply been ignored before the Fall. You put it to the back, to read last. As a treat.

Alas, the first two reports involved only academic discussions of the local deities. With a suppressed sigh, you turned to the final report.

Two-thirds of the way through the flood prevention report, part way through a section, indeed in the middle of a sentence, there was a break. If you had only been skimming the pages you might not have noticed, but amongst a detailed description of the materials used for riverbank reinforcement, a letter began. Addressed, somewhat curiously, to a 'Tenebra'.

A name I had not heard, or said, in a long, long time.

You paused, momentarily, wondering if this was something you should read. Perhaps it would be best to turn the page, find where the report resumed, and ignore this insertion. After all, it must have been here for some years, the report was dated to the twelfth year of your reign.

I couldn't resist reading it.

There were only three pages, just the one letter. It was clearly part of an ongoing correspondence, between Tenebra, and when I looked ahead to the sender, it was indeed Aurora. I knew other people had written stories, poems, even songs about the Red Company. Some of them were even based in fact. This was, as far as I could recall, the first time I had encountered anyone writing about my allegorical fiction.

Tenebra was clearly travelling, although from the letter it wasn't clear why Aurora wasn't with her. It didn't sound like Aurora was in the tower, asleep, because Tenebra was expecting a reply. The letter was filled with small details of a market she had visited, the books she had browsed through, and the bookseller she had chatted with. It reminded me painfully of Jullanar's dreams of opening a bookshop. I wondered if she ever had, somewhere on Daun perhaps, or near one of the universities outside the Empire on Alinor.

For some reason I itched to read more of the letters. If there were any. Possibly whoever had written this had never written more. The author of this letter had to have been one of the report authors, as the handwriting was clearly an exact match.

You blinked. It wasn't just a match for the rest of the document. You were intimately familiar with it. So familiar you almost hadn't noticed, because it was the _default_ writing you read. It was even the writing almost all of your own words were in.

Cliopher sayo Mdang.

Did you dare ask him about it? Probably not. It would be cruel to spring it on him, and what else could you do? If he admitted to writing it, he would be admitting to treason. While you adored all his petty treasons, if the guards heard him say he had indeed written such a thing, it would be hard to protect him.

More than anything, you had to protect Cliopher Mdang. He clearly had no sense of self-preservation. Not just now, but even when he first came to the Palace of Stars. Hiding his treason in a report in the Archives! It was wonderful.

You looked at the reports again. Moving exactly as usual, you picked up the pages of the letter, removing them from the folder. You put the reports to the side, and then took those three pages with you. Without any comment, you went through the various rooms and antechambers, back through your official study, and into my private study.

Gleefully, I tucked them into the bookshelf there. Perhaps someday, if I was lucky, I would be able to add to my collection.

You returned to the official study. At the appearance of your Groom of the Chamber, you reflected that it probably was well past time you were in bed. Even if only so he might retire for the night as well.

“We are finished with the reports,” you said, calmly.

The cavalier bowed, and with a slight gesture, directed one of the other attendants to see to it. Knowing that Conju was friends with Cliopher, and it was possible word might get back, you continued.

“Amboloyo will be disappointed.”

Then you let your Groom lead you away to be undressed.
