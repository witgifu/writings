Kip looked frantically around his cupboard of a room. All the junior secretaries had been ordered to tidy their rooms for inspection, which was absolutely not a standard practise. Which meant it was actually Saya Pinya giving them all a tip off that their rooms were going to be searched.

It was obvious to Kip that she wasn't doing it out of kindness. The offices were short-staffed after a few nobles went home to cry to their mothers about having to work meant getting up before noon. So she was desperate to keep everyone she could, and didn't want anyone taken off for having contraband.

Kip did have contraband. Or, well, things that would probably be declared contraband if someone found them. He wasn't daft enough to have copies of Fitzroy Angursell's works lying around, even in fake covers. But he was, apparently, daft enough to have the most recent chapter of the epistolary story he'd been writing with Basil. Between Aurora and Tenebra. Which he hadn't yet had time to encode.

Not that it was much of a code, swapping the pronouns and names to be about various members of their family. But it was enough that it could slip past the very lazy censors in the postal office. Only Kip couldn't manage to draft it in that code. And so his drafts needed to be encoded, and then burnt.

If he'd had a few more hours… He could just burn it now, but it was a good chapter. Possibly his best yet. If he tried to write it again later, it would be worse.

He needed to put it somewhere nobody would bother looking. Which was a challenge, because his room, even after two years, was very barren.

Sweeping his eyes across the room, he saw the report on flood protection he'd been working on. Well, they were unlikely to search it. Even the person he was supposed to be writing it for was probably only going to read the summary.

With a grimace, he arranged the pages of the chapter so they were about two-thirds of the way through. Same handwriting, same cheap paper, even the same ink. It was a good solution.

What Kip hadn't expected was that Saya Pinya would come with the two guards, and a low-ranked wizard. Somehow having his supervisor inspect his room felt almost as bad as her seeing him naked. Or worse, really. Orgies were less popular than they had been, but if you spotted your boss at a party you could usually avoid them.

Given how small Kip's room was, he and one of the guards had to wait outside because there wasn't room for them to stand inside. This meant his boss, a wizard, and a guard were looking through all his things without him even able to know what they'd touched. He didn't like it.

Thankfully, they spent less than five minutes. 

The wizard said, as she stepped out, “Nice shell collection.”

Kip mumbled his thanks, knowing better than to correct her that his *efela* were more than curios from vacations by the sea.

Saya Pinya came out carrying his report. “I see you finished it early,” she said, “I'll take it now, and you can start on the monthly report tomorrow.”

A cold trickle of horror ran down Kip's spine, which he tried to cover with a bow.

Without another word, they moved down the hall to the next room.

Kip went into his room, shut the door, and slumped down on the bed. If they read it, he was dead. If they didn't, it would be sent down to the Imperial Archives. He wouldn't be able to steal it back, the Archives had spells to alarm if you took out the originals.

The safest course of action would be to leave it. Pretend nothing was wrong, that it was perfectly fine and in fact saving him having to hand in the report himself.

At least he'd be able to go down to the Archives and encode a copy to send to Basil.
