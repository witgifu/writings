Kip's head was hurting from crying so much. Aya made him drink some water, but then let him lie down and nap. Sleeping was good, when he was asleep, he didn't have to think about things. Like wishing he hadn't remembered.

Some time later, possibly late afternoon by the light, Jiano came to see him again.

“Your lord wrote you a note,” said Jiano, and he was indeed holding a folded piece of paper. He squatted down, across from Kip, who sat up, rubbing his eyes.

“Mhhh?” said Kip, still a bit groggy, “Whatsit say?”

Jiano handed it over with a smile, “I don't know, I didn't read it.”

Cliopher felt hollow. He handed the note back to Jiano, and said, “You can read it, if you like.”

Jiano glanced at it, and said, “You're going back then?”

“Yes,” said Cliopher, “Please apologise to Mian for me, the village may use the wood for whatever they would like. Thank you, for everything, Jiano. And please pass on my thanks to Aya as well.” Then without waiting for a reply from Jiano, he picked himself up, and left their home.

Cliopher didn't think too much about what he was doing, just worked his way around the outside of the village. Then left it, along the path that led to the beach and the little palace. When the path spat him out onto the white sand, he walked along the edge near the trees. It was less-sure footing, with some sharp grasses, but Cliopher appreciated having to focus on where he put his feet.

This approach took him to the back of the house, although he supposed it would be possible to see him from the verandah. He slipped into the bathhouse, and stripped to wash. The pants he left, they were almost certainly ruined. It felt good to scrub himself down, and then to soak. It wasn't as if he hadn't washed while he was away, of course he had. But it felt right, to remove everything. He paid particular attention to his fingernails, trying to make them look as close as possible to they had on the sky ship over.

Once he was clean, he took one of the bath robes provided to wrap himself in for the return to the house. Slipping into his bedroom, he very carefully didn't look at the bed. From his luggage, he pulled out a clean uniform, and put it on. He sat at the little desk, pulled out a few reports from the back of his writing case, and began to work.

The light was fading, when Conju knocked on the door.

“Yes? Oh, it's you, Conju, come in,” said Cliopher, looking up and putting his pen down.

“Will you be coming to dinner?” asked Conju, not giving away anything on his face.

“Have I been invited?” asked Cliopher, his own best court face in place, “I would not want to intrude or assume. I can eat in the kitchen.”

“What? Of course you are invited, Cliopher, don't be absurd,” snapped Conju, “But don't come in your uniform. Surely you have _something_ else clean?”

“Yes, I do, thank you,” said Cliopher, “It's just harder to notice if I get ink stains on this.”

When Conju left, Cliopher packed away his work, and changed as instructed. Then he went to the main room, where they usually waited to be told when dinner was ready. He hesitated slightly at the door, but opened it and walked in quietly. Zerafin and Ato smiled at him.

His Radiancy was seated by the fire, a book in his hands. At the prescribed distance, Cliopher dropped gently into the obeisance, and waited. 

After some while, one of the other chairs creaked. Cliopher guessed that perhaps Ser Rhodin had adjusted himself. At the sound, his Radiancy's hands slid down the book cover, and closed it. Then Cliopher saw him abruptly make the gesture to rise, which Cliopher followed.

“Have a seat, Cliopher, I'm sure dinner will be ready shortly,” said his Radiancy.

“Thank you, my lord,” said Cliopher, selecting one of the chairs but keeping his eyes on his Radiancy's hands.

“Are you well, Cliopher?” asked his Radiancy.

“Yes, thank you, my lord,” said Cliopher, not looking up.

The silence after that was awkward, but Cliopher didn't know what else he could say. It had become painfully clear to him that he was here on sufferance. That whatever relationship, whatever closeness, he had previously had with his lord, he had broken irreparably. All he could do was to try to be the person he had been before they had come here. He hid his golden palm in his robes.

Thankfully it wasn't long before they were called to the dinning room. Cliopher left the two places closest to his Radiancy to the others, and sat on his Radiancy's left, at the bottom of the table. If this had been a formal court table, that would have been the lowest-ranked place. Although if this had been a court table, his Radiancy would be dinning separated from all of them.

After the blessing, his Radiancy said, “We should return to Solaara.”

Cliopher couldn't help twitch slightly, at those words. Thankfully he wasn't holding anything at the time, so made no sound or spill. Was he included? He still wasn't entirely sure. Suddenly he remembered it was _his_ responsibility to return them to Gorjo city, at the least.

“Yes, my lord,” he said quietly, not looking up from his plate, “I will make enquiries as to how we might send word to the yacht captain.”

“Oh, no need,” said his Radiancy, looking out the open window, “One of the birds can take word for us.”

This astonishing proclamation was followed by the arrival of a parrot. Then the rather more mundane task of writing a note to the captain, and trying to work out how to fasten it to the bird to cause it the least problem.

Cliopher slipped out of the dinning room, and went back to his bedroom to pack. He had no idea how long a bird message would take, but he didn't want to be caught out.

Once again, it was Conju who came to find him. “Are you hiding?” he asked, standing in the doorway.

“Yes,” admitted Cliopher, who was cleaning out his writing kit. “You saw how awkward it was before dinner, I thought if I tried to just be… professional… I would be less likely to make a horrible mistake again.” He caught a flash of the gold of his palm, and clenched his hand into a fist to hide it. Vou'a was a trickster god, perhaps this was his idea of fun, to embarrass and torment Cliopher.

“Will you talk to him?” asked Conju, carefully.

“About what?” asked Cliopher, uncertainly, “I can't risk being sent away again, Conju.”

“About why you didn't want to leave,” said Conju.

“I told him,” said Cliopher, “I love him. If he doesn't love me, that's fine, and I'm sorry I hurt him, and it won't happen again. What else can I say?”

“You know it's five days until the opening of the next Court session,” said Conju, coming in and closing the door, “He stayed to find you. I think he would have missed the opening, if we'd had to. He was distraught.”

“What!” said Cliopher, whirling around and scattering nibs across the floor, “I wasn't gone longer than a week, surely?”

“Two weeks, exactly,” said Conju, sitting on the edge of the other chair. 

Cliopher knelt down to pick up the pen nibs. “I'm so sorry, Conju, I had no idea. That must have been very distressing. I suppose I should go and apologise to his Radiancy, shouldn't I?”

“He called it a 'psychotic break', brought on by incredible stress,” said Conju, seeming to ignore Cliopher's question.

The term was vaguely familiar to Cliopher, although he was surprised his Radiancy knew it. Perhaps it had come up in a report that Cliopher hadn't paid as much attention to as he should have.

“You moved and talked so differently, Jiano Speaker wasn't entirely sure you were the same person, that's why Ludvic and I went to see you,” said Conju, “I thought you were playing some terrible prank, until it became clear that you didn't recognise us. That you couldn't even understand us.”

Embarrassed, Cliopher tipped the pen nibs into the little box they lived in, and returned it to the writing case. 

“I'm sorry,” said Cliopher again, somewhat lamely. What else could he say? Those days seemed almost like they had happened to someone else.

“He was worried that if you remembered, you might lash out, hurt someone, possibly yourself. That you had been hurt beyond endurance, and your mind had walled it off,” said Conju.

“I _said_, I'm _sorry_,” said Cliopher, a little more forcefully, carelessly throwing his penholders into their section, only to have the first one go in wrong, jam, and block the others. “I don't know why it happened, I just couldn't cope with being sent away _again_, I guess.”

“Again?” said Conju, gently, “I'm sure I would have known if our lord had dismissed you—”

“No, not him,” said Cliopher, finally realising that he wasn't going to be able to pack sensibly in this mood, and collapsing onto the chair. “I told you about Ghilly, right?”

“Your ex-fiancé, married to your university friend who everyone calls 'Toucan'?” asked Conju, leaning forward, “A bit.”

“Did you ever get me drunk enough that I told you how we broke up? And what I did after?” said Cliopher with a sigh.

“You only said she broke it off because you were too ambitious,” said Conju, with a thoughtful look.

Not looking at Conju, Cliopher sketched the embroidery on the hem of his tunic with his finger. “After she explained that I didn't belong with her in Gorjo City, I walked right from that to the harbour, to the first trade ship to make it through the storms after the Fall. I asked to join up for their return journey, and went back to Solaara.”

Conju sat there, looking at him for a while. Eventually he said, “Cliopher, I don't think he wants you to apologise. He doesn't blame you for what happened.”

“What *does* he want, then?” asked Cliopher, “I would give him _anything_, Conju, you know I would.”
