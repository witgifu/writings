Kip woke at dawn the next day, and for want of anything else to do, he helped weed the village gardens. Being useful was important, whatever village you found yourself in. 

Around mid-morning, Aya came and found him. “The shaman says they need to go into a trance to check you, and it will be easiest if you're lying down.”

“Oh, I didn't see anyone arrive?” he asked, curiously. He'd wondered what the shaman might be like. But of course, if they were already set up it would be rude to interfere.

“They're in the spirit house now,” said Aya, with a smile, “Come back to my place.”

Kip did as he was asked. As he lay down on the sleeping mat, he wondered if he would be able to notice anything. Probably not. He stared at the ceiling, and for want of anything else to do, planned more of his vaha building project.

After a while, Aya returned. “The shaman says there's no magical problems,” she said softly, “They think it's either divine or medical. We're trying to convince them to talk to you, in case it's divine, but...” She sighed, and sat down next to Kip, who sat up to join her.

“They know something of what happened to you, hani,” she said, putting a hand gently on his shoulder. “They don't want to explain, they say it's something private. But they seem scared of talking to you.”

“I don't understand,” said Kip, annoyed at the shaman, but mostly annoyed at himself for whatever had happened. “Are they worried about angering the gods by messing with… whatever it is?”

Aya rubbed his shoulder. “No, I think they're more worried about you. I suppose it's possible that if it's divine, and they touch it, it could get worse. Or if it's medical, it might distress you.”

Kip snorted. If it was medical, well. “Could I ask the urumà? Or do you have any other Poyë lorekeepers here? I'm not a fan of Imperial doctors.”

“We have a midwife,” said Aya, thoughtfully, “And she does a bit of whatever else we need, but anyone who needs more than a quick bandage goes over to Gorjo City. It's not so far.”

If Kip did that, his mother would know, and there would be a big fuss made of it. If there was one thing he didn't want, it was a big fuss. He was fine, really. Yes, he might be missing some memories, but he could still make new ones. It wasn't like he'd forgotten something _really_ important, like the Lays or his own name.

“Maybe I should just work on the vaha and go to Loaloa,” he said, thinking it though, “It's not like it's getting any worse… is it? You haven't noticed me forgetting anything more?”

“No,” she said softly, “It doesn't seem to be getting worse. I'll go and see if the shaman has changed their mind.”

Kip sat there, waiting in the house for some time. Eventually he decided that he needed something to do with his hands, so he left Aya's house, intending to go back to weeding her garden. Just as he slipped out the front door, he noticed the velioi were in the centre of the village. The two from yesterday, plus another one in a kilt, and one in a hooded robe. Why could they possibly be here? 

Unless… the shaman was veloi? Surely not. The Empire had practitioners of Schooled Magic, like Saya Dorn had been. Although if they _were_ a Schooled Magic wizard, that might explain why they weren't keen to touch anything to do with the Vangaveye-ve gods. The gods of the Empire, the Sun, and the Moon, demanded service and worship. Not like Ani and Vou'a, who had earned respect.

Well, if the shaman was only afraid of talking to Kip because he was nervous about encountering the gods of the Vangaveye-ve, that fear was completely unfounded. Filled with new resolve, Kip walked over to meet them.

The two spearmen noticed him first, the one from yesterday made some gesture that the second one acknowledged. Neither of them made any move to either point Kip out, or to shoo him away. Then the short man saw him, and took a step back, obviously startled. That prompted the wizard, or shaman, or whoever they were to turn around. The hood was deep, but it had slipped back slightly, and inside it Kip could see golden eyes…

He stumbled, and dropped to his knees on the path. His mind was telling him it was impossible. The Sun-on-Earth couldn't be here, couldn't be looking at him. It must be an imposter, or some other member of the Imperial Family. His body remembered what to do, and dropped into the deepest form of the obeisance. All he could hear was his heart thudding in his chest, and his too-fast breathing.

Those eyes had been the Lion Eyes. Nobody else had those, glowing with magic. And nobody could look the Emperor in the eyes. The taboos weren't just etiquette, they were real, he would be blinded, and then executed. He had done it, again, and this time he wouldn't just be fired, he would be killed. 

Surely the guards, for that's what the spearmen were, he remembered now, Imperial Guards, surely they would take him away. Where were the rough hands grabbing him and dragging him off? Were they giving him a chance to kill himself first? Avoid the execution of a commoner for treason? Could he do that? Aya could tell his family what had happened for him.

He reached around to pull out his pen knife, and finally hands did grab him. Pulled the knife out of his loose grip, and tossed it away. He had been too slow, too indecisive. Couldn't even kill himself properly.

Arms wrapped around him, and pulled him up. But it wasn't one of the guards, it was Aya. “Shh,” she said, “Shhh, come inside Kip, come inside. They've gone, they've all gone, it's just us now.”

Kip realised he was crying, great sobs wrenched from his chest, tears falling down his face unchecked. He let himself be led by Aya back into her home, back to the sleeping mat. She kept up a steady patter of calming sounds, pushed him down onto the mat, and then sat close next to him and hugged him tightly.

Eventually his tears subsided, and he just lent into Aya, his eyes closed. He was alive, by some miracle. Why was the Emperor here? Why had *he* been the only magic-user available? Kip didn't understand, but he could feel the answer was there, somewhere, if he looked, he would know what had happened. Did he want to look?

*Is this where you stop?*

“Did he, did they, tell you what happened?” asked Kip, his throat sore and croaky.

“No,” said Aya softly, “But it seemed like maybe your lord had hurt you somehow?”

“I'm trying to remember,” said Kip, “I think maybe I hurt him? We had an argument?” He scrunched up his eyes, trying to recall anything.

“Jiano said something about the moon? The lunar eclipse?” said Kip.

“The Moon Lady came down,” said Aya, stroking Kip's shoulder, “She, um, tried to seduce your lord.”

At those last three words, everything came crashing back.

“Oh no, oh no, oh no,” said Kip, crying again. “*I* did, but my lord blames himself, and sent me away. He sent me away. That's why I was going to Loaloa, it was the only place left.”

Then he just sobbed into Aya's arms, like a child. That had been Conju, who had come to find him, and Ludvic. Conju had though he'd been joking, but Kip had forgotten how to speak Shaian. And his lord had checked on him, to see if somehow his magic had hurt Kip.

Only two people had hurt him. He'd hurt himself, and his lord had hurt him by sending him away. If he'd been sent away for what he had done, that would have been horrible, but understandable. But to be sent away for his so-called protection, to be given titles, and wealth, as if he wanted any of that.

Jiano came in, and sat on Kip's other side, holding him as well. “Do you remember now, Kip?” he asked. 

Kip just cried, but Aya said, “He remembers some of it.”

“Ludvic told me what happened,” said Jiano, “Or what he saw happen, anyway. It sounds like you are both blaming yourselves for hurting the other.”

Kip nodded, and Aya handed him a rag to wipe his nose with.

“He wouldn't listen,” said Kip, and even to him it sounded like a weak excuse, “He sent me away. What was I going to do?”

Kip could feel over the top of him, Jiano and Aya were sharing a significant look.

“Would you be willing to talk with Conju?” asked Jiano.

“Ye-es,” said Kip, “Would you stay though? In case something happens, or I forget again, or I can't remember Shaian.”

“You've been speaking Shaian since just before Jiano came in,” said Aya, “I think it came back with the rest of your memories.”

“Oh,” said Kip, surprised and worried that he hadn't even noticed.

“I'll go and let Conju know,” said Jiano, “But don't worry, Kip. We'll both stay with you.”

Jiano was gone such a short time, that Kip realised Conju must have been waiting in the village. He felt guilty that he was causing so much trouble. He should just finish the vaha and sail away and not bother them; it was clear that his Radiancy did not want to see Kip again, and that was fine. His friends, well, they were all still in his Radiancy's service. It wouldn't be fair to ask them to choose, and anyway, he doubted they would enjoy life on Loaloa much.

Shortly after Jiano sat beside Kip again, Conju came in.

“Hello… Kip?” said Conju, looking unusually uncertain. He was always so calm and collected, no matter the situation. That worried Kip in turn.

“You can call me Cliopher, if you prefer, I don't mind,” said Kip, not quite making eye contact. Suddenly he realised what a mess he must look. Sitting in his underwear, that was probably dirty from all the gardening he'd been doing. Like a begger he had seen in Cisven, before the Indrogan Estate opened.

“Are you, were you, hurt?” asked Conju, looking a little more calm.

“I wasn't _injured_,” said Kip, defensively, “He sent me away, so I left.” Aya hugged him.

“I'm sure he didn't mean _immediately_, Cliopher,” said Conju, one eyebrow raised, in a look Kip was very familiar with.

Kip looked up, looked at Conju directly. “He said you would all go back to Solaara, and I was to stay here. I'm not sure what else that could have meant, especially when he then went to go and find you.”

“He thought you had been hurt,” said Conju, quietly, “He went to get me because he thought you might be able to talk to me more freely.”

“He should have listened to what I said!” snapped Kip, and then retreated back into Aya's arms, looking down at his own knees. “I said I didn't want to go, I _begged_ him not to send me away. The only time he hurt me was when he tried to _bribe_ me to leave him.”

“He what?” said Conju, startled. It wasn't as if nobody ever tried to bribe Kip, lots of people had. He'd even told Conju about many of the more amusing variations.

“Wanted to make me a duke, and give me a sky ship,” said Kip, quietly.

“That's not a _bribe_ Cliopher, that's a reward, a gift for all your hard work over the centuries,” said Conju, exasperated. Kip could tell Conju was surely thinking about the time he had tried to return the raise the Emperor had given him.

It was a typical noble trait to draw a very careful, very thin, line between 'bribe' and 'gift'. Kip liked a much cleaner, more solid one. He'd spent a long time finalising it for the Offices of State. The sky ship would definitely count, it would be vastly over the allowed gift budget. The policy, he realised, had neglected to include noble titles. He'd have to write to Kiri and warn her to amend it.

“I didn't do it for _that_ kind of reward,” said Kip, bitterly, “I didn't even get to finish half the things… never mind. It doesn't matter. I'll go to Loaloa, and he can forget I existed, say whatever he likes to Kiri and the papers. I won't bother him.”

There was a long, awkward pause, and Kip just let it run. If his lord didn't want him back, then he wasn't going to let some Imperial tell him how to live his life. If his lord didn't like that he was planning on living in what Kip was sure he thought of as 'abject poverty' then too bad.

“Will you visit your mother?” asked Conju, gently.

Well, thought Kip, that was rather a low blow. “Eventually,” said Kip, evasively.

“What will you tell her?” probed Conju.

“That I was fired for having sex with my boss,” said Kip, taking a perverse joy from the way Conju flinched at the statement. Especially given a number of Kip's bosses back in Astandalas _had_ propositioned him.

“Is that… what you think happened?” asked Conju, clearly still struggling.

“Yes,” said Kip, quietly, “Unless you think it was for some other mistake I made? Perhaps suggesting this entire holiday in the first place? Or perhaps he thinks I am part of the _box_.”

“Cliopher, no, you know that's not true,” said Conju.

“Then ask him to write me a Letter of Termination, and see what he puts as the reason on it. It's form IB-AT-7, there's one in my writing kit,” said Kip.

“Kip,” said Jiano, softly, “I think Conju is trying to say that he didn't fire you, that he was assuming you'd want to resign.”

“Does it matter?” said Kip, suddenly exhausted from all this emotional turmoil, “He sent me away. I'll go finish the vaha and sail to Loaloa.”

“What if he changed his mind?” said Jiano. Conju gasped, because of course, officially, that wasn't possible. His Radiancy's will was perfect.

Not wanting to let in hope, only to have it dashed again, Kip fell back on formality. “If my lord was so kind to consider the supplications of his servant, and so moved to admit his servant again into his presence, who would refuse?”

Conju sighed. “Try to get some rest, Cliopher. I will see what I can do.” He left, but Kip could overhear him asking someone outside, “Did you know he brought a full set of the Service Forms with him _on vacation_?”
