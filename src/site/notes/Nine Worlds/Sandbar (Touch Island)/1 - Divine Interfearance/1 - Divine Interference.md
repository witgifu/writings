They had been sitting together on the sandbar, enjoying the sun and the view of the sea. They had been talking of hard things, of family, of love, of duty.

“I was the first of all the Marwns of the Empire ever to become Emperor,” said his Radiancy.

Cliopher should have said, “I am sorry, my lord.” Or inquired if his Radiancy knew anything of the other Marwns. They had spent the past fifteen minutes discussing how the taboos and requirements of Empire had meant his Radiancy had so little.

Instead, as if Cliopher was with Vinyë or Basil, he had reached out and covered his Radiancy's hand with his own. A simple gesture of sympathy. An anchor, to say you are heard and understood, and your friends stand with you. His Radiancy could not possibly be Cliopher's friend.

They both jolted back from the touch. 

Cliopher fell into the full formal prostration, his nose in the sand. 

His Radiancy scrambled to stand up, still wearing flippers. He looked around frantically for the guards, who were standing on the shore. Even at this distance, Ato and Pikabe must have sensed something of the distress of the two men on the sandbar, as the two guards gestured at each other. Ato ran into the water, Pikabe back to the house.

Cliopher saw neither of them. Nor did he see his lord above him, shaking like a leaf in the wind.

“Cliopher?” said his Radiancy, his voice high and piercing.

“I am so very, very sorry, my lord, I do not know what came over me. I should never have done such a thing—” Cliopher realised he was about to start babbling, and shut his mouth. One of the guards would arrive soon, and take him away. He did not need to add 'blathering' to the pile of his own shame.

“Cliopher?” said his Radiancy again, his voice still concerned, but closer to his usual register.

“Yes, my lord?” said Cliopher, his face still hovering just above the sand.

“Did I injure you, Cliopher? Can you get up?”

This surprised Cliopher enough that he rose to kneeling. “I am fine, my lord, only ashamed that I could do such a thing.”

There were tears running down his Radiancy's face. Cliopher had never seen him cry, not once. Of course, Astandalan manners frowned on crying in public, and his Radiancy was in public everywhere Cliopher had ever seen him. Except perhaps now, on this sandbar.

Ato arrived, running up out of the water. His uniform soaked.

“Cliopher has had a turn, Ato, and may have injured himself,” said his Radiancy, in his most formal and serene voice. It was missing the note of vibrancy that had arrived in the past few days.

Ato saluted, and turned to Cliopher. “How are you feeling, sir? Are you dizzy or nauseous?”

“I'm fine, Ato, I'm sure I can swim back by myself,” said Cliopher, slightly awed by his Radiancy's dissembling.

“My lord,” said Ato, squatting down beside Cliopher, but twisting to look at his Radiancy, “Did you see where Sayo Mdang was injured?”

“His hand,” said his Radiancy, with a very slight hesitation in his voice.

Cliopher lifted both his arms, and turned his hands over, palms to the sky. His right palm was the usual olive-pink. His left was gold.

“Oh,” he said without thinking, “it's beautiful.” He stroked the gold with the fingertips of his right hand. It tingled slightly, but not unpleasantly.

“Sir,” said Ato, in a soft voice, clearly worried, “does it hurt? Does anywhere else hurt?”

“No, it doesn't hurt. I—” Cliopher didn't know what to say. He felt that he should explain what happened, but he also did not want to contradict his lord. “I don't know what came over me.”

Cliopher looked up from his gold palm to his lord. He stood perfectly straight, his hands behind him, any evidence of tears lost to the hot sun.

“Were you hurt, my lord?” asked Cliopher, timidly, suddenly aware that perhaps if he hadn't been injured, perhaps his lord had instead.

“No,” said his Radiancy quickly, before Ato had a chance to scramble up. “Cliopher,” he paused, and closed his eyes, “there is some magical component, or possibly divine. We will need to examine you more closely back at the house.”

“Of course, my lord,” said Cliopher, and went back to admiring his golden palm.

The rest of the guards had arrived at the beach by this point. Ato began waving at them in large versions of the normally discreet guard signals. Pikabe and Ludvic stripped down and entered the water. Ludvic was carrying something.

Cliopher looked back at his Radiancy. His eyes were still closed, possibly in a light trance trying to understand what had happened to Cliopher.

Was it possible that the impulse had been sent by a wizard, a mage, or a god? 

A wizard, as they had noted, stood out even in Gorjo City. It just wasn't that common to be trained in Schooled Magic in the Vangaveye-ve. Saya Dorn was the only one Cliopher had ever known of any power. 

Most mages went to train as shamans. They didn't generally deal in mind influence. Anyway, whoever it was would have had to have been even further away than the guards. And why would a shaman place such an impulse?

But a god? Well, the gods of the islands could be tricky and fickle. Cliopher supposed it was possible that Vou'a had decided to play with him. Although he'd never come to the attention of the gods before.

The most likely cause, he thought, was his own foolishness. But if his lord said there was something more happening, there was no greater authority on Zunidh. No one knew more about magic.

Ludvic arrived with a flotation bladder, and a rope. Ludvic and Pikabe saluted. “Pikabe and I can go with you, my lord,” said Ludvic, “Ato will bring Cliopher.” Ludvic gave Cliopher an unusually stern look, which he assumed meant he was to follow instructions.

His Radiancy nodded to Ludvic, and walked back into the water.

Ato handed the flotation bladder to Cliopher, “Please hold this with your good arm, sir.”

Cliopher had a brief moment of confusion where he thought Ato meant his gold hand. Embarrassed, he grabbed the bladder with his right arm. Then Ato tied the rope around Cliopher's waist, and insisted that he was not to try to swim, just to float.

The sky was the perfect colour, and the water a relief from the hot sun. Cliopher did rebel just a little by kicking slowly. He also admired his hand some more.

It felt like the rest of his skin, just a bit more sensitive, like his palms usually were. He didn't generally feel attracted by gold or jewels, but something about the way this was living gold mesmerised him. Or perhaps it was the lingering effects of whatever made him reach out and break the taboos, risking death by immolation.

They arrived at the beach, and Conju rushed over with robes and towels. Cliopher could see his Radiancy standing near the high-tide mark, looking back. He had already been dressed.

Cliopher meekly submitted to Conju's insistence of helping him dry and dress. But he drew the line at Ato and Pikabe carrying him.

“It's just my hand,” he said, with a sigh, “I can walk.”

“Yes just your hand *and maybe your mind*,” said Conju, folding the towels into a basket. Cliopher did not help the situation by staring at his hand.

In the end they compromised, and Cliopher let Pikabe hold his arm as they walked back to the house. Pikabe also insisted in a much slower pace than Cliopher felt was necessary. Conju rushed ahead of them to consult with their lord on what was needed next.

By the time Cliopher arrived at the verandah, everyone else had organised themselves in the common room. His Radiancy had taken his customary seat, and Conju hovered next to him. Ludvic and Ato stood as guards by the door.

“Sit here, Cliopher,” said Conju, gesturing to a couch near his Radiancy. 

Cliopher sat on the edge, “I feel fine, my lord, it was probably just too much sun.”

His Radiancy raised one eyebrow at Cliopher. “We need to see if we can find what caused it, and what the lingering effects are.”

Cliopher ducked his head. “Of course, my lord, my apologies. What do you need from me?”

“Sit comfortably, and try to think of something relaxing. A nice memory, or a familiar song.”

Cliopher's first thought was the warm feeling of his Radiancy's hand under his own. His second was admiring the gold on his hand as he floated across the lagoon. Then he spotted the disguised copy of Aurora on the table. He sat back in the chair, and began reciting it in his head.

His Radiancy lay down, and closed his eyes. Cliopher tried to focus on Aurora, and ignore the tingling of his hand, in reaction to whatever his lord was doing. 

It seemed like almost no time later that his Radiancy sat up. “There is a twist in the magic of the taboos, and a faint haze of the divine about you, Cliopher.”

Cliopher looked nonplussed, “Is it dangerous, my lord?”

His Radiancy considered the question. “It is probably always dangerous to come to the notice of the gods,” he said, his eyes still bright with magic, “but I cannot see any curse or compulsion. In fact, it would seem that whatever it was, has made you immune to the taboos.”

Holding out his golden palm, Cliopher asked, “And this, my lord?”

“Is likely permanent,” said his Radiancy, with a small sigh, “think of it as a magical scar. From the taboos.”

Cliopher rubbed his hands together gently, “It seems to me like a tattoo, my lord, it is too beautiful to be called a scar.”

His Radiancy smiled, a small thin smile. But Cliopher was warmed to see he was no longer so anxious.

“Have you or your family ever come to the notice of the gods before, Cliopher?” asked his lord.

“My great-uncle jokes that he's married to Vou'a,” said Cliopher, “but that's just an old-fashioned way of saying he likes men.”

“The same Vou'a who this house is the meeting place for?”

“Yes… I have met my great-uncle's boyfriend, my lord. I lived with them for a time as a boy. I don't _think_… well, he is the god of mysteries, so I suppose it is *possible*.”

His Radiancy looked into Cliopher's eyes, and held his gaze. This time it did not weigh him down, it felt more like an embrace. “You might want to consult a local mage, they may be more familiar with the signature of your gods. I have not encountered whoever has blessed you, Cliopher.”

Cliopher swallowed hard. He was *not* comfortable with the idea of being blessed by the gods. Any blessings handed out by the Son of Laughter were very likely to be double-edged. Like being immune to the taboos.

“My lord, if I am immune to the taboos, am I also now subject to them?” he asked, quietly. He would miss fresh fruit, much as his lord did. But not being able to touch his friends and family would be much harder.

“Thankfully, no,” answered his Radiancy, “I would not consider that a blessing. This is similar to protections a mage might spin against magical attack. But this is tied to, twisted inside, the taboo against touching the person of the Emperor.”

A blush was creeping up Cliopher's face. “I would *never* presume, my lord. But you may have seen in Ikialo village that the people of the Vangaveye-ve touch much more than is common in Solaara. There would be no objection on my part.”

Conju looked daggers at Cliopher, but Ludvic and Ato made no reaction to this statement. Even by Cliopher's standards, this was walking over 'possibly treason' and right into 'treason with a precedent for conviction'.

“Thank you,” said his Radiancy, and picked up his book.

That afternoon, Aya and Jiano invited them to the market in Ikaiva village. 

The next day at the market, their group had mostly dispersed. The guards to the local handicrafts (and the admiration looks of the younger villagers), Conju to discuss recipes, and his Radiancy to discuss politics with the various village speakers. Cliopher found himself at an _efela_ stall, admiring a strand of cowries.

He chatted a bit to the stall-holder, until for the first time in his life Cliopher was asked a question out of the _lays_. “What is your name, what is your island, and what is your dance?”

Shocked, it took a moment for him to gather the answers. “I am Cliopher Mdang of Tahivoa. My island is Loaloa. My dance is *Aōteketētana*.”

The vendor gave him a piercing look.

“What interest do you have in Cliopher, honoured one?” asked his Radiancy, from just behind Cliopher.

Cliopher turned swiftly to face his lord, still holding the beautiful gold-rimmed cowrie strand. He had not noticed his arrival.

“I thought I should see where he is going, and where he has been,” said the vendor, somewhat cryptically. He gave no honorific, but his Radiancy took no offence.

Turning back to Cliopher, the vendor said, “I have heard of three brothers who left. One went as far as he could to see what there was to see. One went searching after his heart. And one went to sit at the feet of the Sun.”

Cliopher felt horribly awkward, with his lord standing here listening. And yet, turned back to the vendor, and put the cowries down on the stall. Very quietly, and wishing he dared say it in Language, so his lord might not understand, he said, “I sit at the feet of the Sun.”

“What will you bring home?”

To Cliopher's immense astonishment, he felt a hand on his shoulder. He turned to see who else had arrived. But it was not Conju, or even Ludvic. His Radiancy had reached out. Cliopher offered him a timid smile, wondering what he saw in this exchange. His lord squeezed gently.

“I will bring home the hearth-fire of a new life for the world,” said Cliopher, half to the vendor, and half to his lord. He had never admitted to his lord what all his changes meant, what he had wanted to bring to Zunidh. Perhaps his lord had seen anyway, for he smiled back.

The vendor gave a bubbling laugh, like a kookaburra. “You say that as if you mean it.”

“He does,” answered his Radiancy, but his eyes on Cliopher.

There was a long silence. Cliopher, trapped by his lord's admiration, and the warm comfort of his hand.

The vendor broke it with another sudden laugh. “I suppose I will have to believe you two.” 

His Radiancy smiled at the vendor, his usual polite smile. Then he stepped away from Cliopher.

Cliopher walked away with the string of cowries in their woven wrapper, and wondered at the negotiation.

---

All those at the market were enamoured by his Radiancy. He had a way with people, with common people, that Cliopher had never noticed. Or perhaps, he corrected himself, had never been allowed to notice. When did the Son-on-Earth make illusions, or discuss fishing, or debate poetry? When was he _allowed_?

It was good, Cliopher thought, that he had this chance now. Perhaps they would make able to make more occasions in future.

In the days following the market, Cliopher could feel something brewing, like an approaching storm. His lord swam, and invited Cliopher snorkelling; his lord read, and played the harp, and invited Cliopher to play chess.

To everyone surprise, not the least Ludvic's, his Radiancy gifted his Commander of the Guard a book of Ludvic's own poetry. He also arranged a special supper, with Ludvic's favoured spicy foods.

They could not have known that Ludvic's response would spark the fire in their lord. 

The next day, Ludvic asked the question. “I have been wondering, my lord, whether it had occurred to you to retire.” The discussion that followed was tense. In the end, his Radiancy stormed out, leaving Conju prostrate on the floor. The rain came down like a wall.

Conju did not understand. Cliopher was not entirely sure if he understood, except that his lord was so clearly in pain.

Aya and Jiano arrived to invite them all to the lunar eclipse party. It was the only break in the tension of the afternoon. The tension came to a crescendo, more than an end, when his Radiancy returned just before dusk.

As their lord stepped into the common room, Cliopher felt formality closing in on them. Perhaps it was a protection, a safe retreat for all of them, from the charged emotions of earlier. Cliopher fell, with Conju, into the full prostration, his heart full of uncertainty that he had no words for.

With their lord's permission, Cliopher rose. 

Conju did not. With shaking voice, and keeping his head firmly down, Conju begged to be excused from his Radiancy's service. He would not move, until to Cliopher and Conju's astonishment, their lord knelt before Conju. His Radiancy asked for Conju's forgiveness, asked him to stay.

It was a tableau out of a story, a myth. Cliopher thought perhaps he had not taken a breath for quite some while.

Then Conju said, “Oh, my lord, please get up. I cannot bear to see you kneeling.”

“And it will ruin my robes, won’t it?” said his Radiancy, with a flash of that other man, one which turned thoughtful as he looked around and saw a complete lack of any means whereby to lift himself up.

Both Pikabe and Cliopher rushed over. Pikabe offering his ceremonial spear, Cliopher his golden hand. His Radiancy looked between them, and said “Thank you, both of you.”

To Cliopher's utter delight, his Radiancy took his hand, and hauled himself up.
