The journey back was as calm as the initial one, but the Glorious One was much less sick. Conju hovered nearby, but their lord seemed to be contemplating the horizon. Cliopher spent the journey trying to plan what to do when they arrived in Gorjo City.

If the Glorious One did not wish to make for the sky ship directly, the best hotel in the city was the Dolphin. He quietly informed Rhodin of the directions, and also of how to get to the provincial palace, if the Glorious One would prefer to drop the disguise. Cliopher still wasn't sure if he'd been dismissed from his lord's service, so he made no plans for himself.

They disembarked at the city marina, and Rhodin presented all three options to their lord. The Glorious One flicked his eyes to Cliopher, but there was nothing on his face that Cliopher could read. "The hotel," said the Glorious One, "I will have a lie down."

Cliopher bowed to his lord, and then set off at a brisk pace to ask for the hotel's best rooms. He was embarrassed to have to do this negotiation with one of his cousins, especially when he was unable to give the name of the main guest, but by the time the others arrived Cliopher was ready. He had not cleared the lobby, as he was sure Conju would have managed, and his cousin was still hovering around.

Despite this, his lord seemed to welcome the cool quiet of the ducal suite enough that a little of his visible tension lifted. Cliopher retired to his own room to give his lord space to rest. There, he fretted.

His lord had neither told him he must not return to Solaraa, or that he could go with them. Desperately, he tried to think of any other arguments, anything else he could offer. He thought, perhaps, that in those last moments before the boat had arrived, his lord had, he hoped, accepted that Cliopher was unharmed. Cliopher was still uncertain if he had hurt his lord, he thought he might have, even if only in his reaction in the morning. Had Cliopher put his lord back in the box, when he had prostrated himself in fear? Had he made the same mistake as Conju?

It was frustrating that all the forms of apology and reconciliation were co-opted and constrained by etiquette. He was no poet or musician, who might make a grand gesture. His art-form, if one could call it that, was the government report.

Well, perhaps it might help him calm his tempestuous thoughts.

Rhodin knocked on the doorframe, "Conju is staying with his Radiancy, come show me your culinary delights, Cliopher."

Cliopher looked up from his analysis, which had grown to cover the small desk and a good portion of the bed. "The pub downstairs is quite good," he said absently, "As is the Jilkanese noodle place around the corner. The kebab place is only marginal, there's better closer to the markets. Any of the fish places are fine except for the one with the green banner, their oil is old."

"You are coming with me," said Rhodin, "So I won't have to remember your list."

"Oh, but..." said Cliopher, looking around, "I'm almost done."

"Then you can tell me all about it while we eat. You do remember eating, yes? Only I think all you've had today is a cup and a half of coffee."

Cliopher blushed. That might well be true. He couldn't remember breakfast or lunch, just a long string of worry.

He sighed, cleaned and put away his pen and ink, and joined Rhodin in the hallway. It was dark by the time they stepped outside, to Cliopher's surprise. His mind still mostly on the report, he took Rhodin to a chargrill place he hadn't even mentioned.

"Do you have a conclusion yet? And should we get the baby octopus or the prawns?" asked Rhodin.

"Nearly," said Cliopher, "And both. Also the thin sliced sweet potato."

"What are you missing?" asked Rhodin, as the teenage waiter pointed them to a table in the corner. "Beer?"

"Yes please," said Cliopher, giving Rhodin the seat with the better exit routes and clearer view of the room. "It would be useful to know which god it was," said Cliopher, checking over the specials on the wall, "And I wish I knew what *he* wanted."

Rhodin paused while spreading olive paste over his slice of bread. He gave Cliopher a single raised eyebrow, in clear imitation of Conju.

"I was hoping one of the lore keepers might be able to hope with the first question," said Cliopher, "Although I am not sure how much time I have."

"You always seem to find time, Cliopher," said Rhodin, with a shake of his head.

At that moment Cora walked in, and dragged her friend over to meet "Kip and his handsome friend". By the time Cora left them, Cliopher thought that perhaps Rhodin would have forgotten his interrogation.

"His Radiancy adores you, Cliopher," said Rhodin, managing to make Cliopher blush and wince. "You are fantastic together."

Cliopher didn't really know what to say to this pronouncement. He decided that while perhaps Rhodin was very good at intrigue, he wouldn't be taking relationship advice from the man who offered to garrote every man who broke up with Conju.

When they were enjoying the cool evening breeze outside the restaurant, Cliopher said, "I do feel better having eaten, thank you."

"My pleasure, you were quite right about the octopus. Now, where do we find your lore keepers?" said Rhodin, admiring and being admired by a group leaving a wine bar.

"Oh, well, over that way," said Cliopher, waving in the general direction of Uncle Lazo's shop.

Rhodin, very obviously ignoring the hint, smiled and said, "Lead the way!"

As they wound their way through the city, Cliopher was stopped by at least a dozen people who knew him. Most of whom had a comment about being surprised to see him, although a good few were clearly angling for an introduction to Rhodin. The walk, which was not very far, took about three times as long as Cliopher felt it should.

The elders, to Cliopher's relief, were gathered around a couple of games of backgammon. Uncle Lazo spotted him first.

"You look like a man on a mission," said Uncle Lazo, "What brings you to Gorjo City again so soon, Kip?"

"I am here with my lord," said Cliopher, trying to contain his nerves with his court face. It was hard to do in a place, with people, that felt so very much like *home*. "But I had a question for the zamà."

A tiny old lady turned around to face him, from where she was perched on a stool. "Eh? What's Kip got up to, Lazo?" she said. Then she took one look at him, and started laughing.

She looked around, her snorts gaining in intensity. "Of course Tovo's not here, he never is when there's mischief about."

Cliopher bowed over his hands to her. "My apologies, Buru, but I am not sure I understand?"

The other elders drifted off, giving the four of them some sense of privacy.

"Someone told you you've been marked by a god, eh?" said the zamà, "And you come to me to know who and why."

"Yes," said Cliopher, impressed that she could tell with a glance what had required a trance from the Lord Magus.

She stood up, and started walking around him. Cliopher rather felt like he was being stalked for chips by a seagull.

"You're Vou'a's, and that's no surprise," said the zamà, and Lazo smiled. "But the gift you've been given isn't for you. Someone did Ani a good turn, and she's returned the favour."

Cliopher blinked, repeatedly. If it wasn't for him, there was only one other person it could be for. His mouth opened in a small 'o' of surprise, and his heart rate increased.

"You alright?" said Rhodin, gripping his shoulder gently.

Cliopher nodded, but wasn't able to find more words. Would his lord be willing to accept the gift? He could imagine his horror at the thought of a god giving a person as a gift. Someone immune to the taboos. Before the Fall people had tried (and failed, miserably) to curry favour with the Emperor by presenting him concubine-candidates.

But... perhaps that wasn't the gift. Gifts of the gods were rarely so simple. Neither was it wise to ignore them.

"There's too much velio magic bound up in it for me to tell you more," said the zamà, "But then you've got a mage who knows plenty about that, eh?"

Did she know who his lord was? That was surprising, nobody else at home had worked that out, or even seemed that interested in knowing.

"You're dripping in so much protective magic," she continued, with a chortle, "You look like a baby wrapped in every blanket the parents were given."

Rhodin nodded, and Cliopher wondered who had placed it. Was it something Rhodin had done? He knew the second-in-command of the Guard had some magic, but he had thought not enough for those kinds of spells. Which would leave... his lord? His lord knew Cliopher had no magic, and presumably had been worried by one or more of the reports Rhodin had brought him. Cliopher's heart warmed at the thought.

Cliopher pulled his mind back to the question that had brought him here. "Why would Ani...?" he said, running through the tales in the _lays_ that spoke of the gifts of Ani. Then suddenly he remembered what great work his lord had just completed. "Oh," he said, softly, "oh."

"See!" said the zamà, "They don't even need us anymore, Lazo." But she was smiling at Cliopher and Rhodin.

Uncle Lazo shook his head. "If you're around for a few days, Kip, your sister has some big do on tomorrow with the orchestra. You know your mother would want you to go."

Cliopher sighed, "I will see what my duties to my lord allow."

Lazo looked curious, but the zamà elbowed him in the hip. "Be nice to your boy, I hardly get to see him."

"My apologies for not coming to see you last time I was here," said Cliopher, bowing over his hands again.

The zamà winked at Cliopher. "So polite! Shame you've got no magic, boy, or I'd have snapped you up. None of _mine_ are so well-mannered, Lazo. Where do you find them?"

Cliopher waved goodbye to his uncle, as he turned to the zamà to complain that not every lorekeeper could pick from all the Vangaveye-ve.

"Was that the answer to your question?" asked Rhodin, as they walked back to the Dolphin.

"I suppose," said Cliopher, "Although I think it's given me half a dozen more."

Sometime between midnight and dawn, well after the bar downstairs had closed, Cliopher finished the report. It wasn't quite as well-researched as he would have liked, but he was hardly going to visit the Astandalan temple to the Sun in Gorjo City and grill the old priest there about the Ouranatha. So he tried to accept it, dropping it off at the little table outside his Radiancy's bedroom. The guards would notice it at the dawn shift change, or Conju would when he went in next.

By the time Cliopher got into bed, it felt like the report had grown in his mind. He must have drifted off to sleep, because he had strange dreams about it. Conju brought it to him, confused by why he had written out the full cycle of the _lays_ in Islander to give to their lord. Rhodin admitted to breading and deep-frying it, claiming it was delicious. Ludvic gently explaining the Glorious One had burnt the report and the bed he had been in to ashes, after reading the first page.

He woke, just before dawn, feeling more tired than when he had gone to sleep. Frustrated, he got up and washed. Then he sat in the little parlour, a cup of coffee in one hand, and a book he couldn't focus on at all in the other.
