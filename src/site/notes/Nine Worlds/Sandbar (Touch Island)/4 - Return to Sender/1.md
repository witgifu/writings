Cliopher woke up, and as always before coffee, it took him a little while to orient himself. He was in the vacation house, on Lesuia. Last night it had been the lunar eclipse party. And then.

He rolled over, his mind completely unwilling to finish that thought. There, beside him, was the Lord of Rising Stars. His breath, his chest, falling and rising gently. Kip looked further, to the door. Two guards, Ato and Ludvic were standing there now, keeping watch.

The light suggested it was early still, just past first light. Had his Radiancy being doing the dawn rituals on his own here? Cliopher didn't dare wake him, he looked so beautiful asleep. Serene in truth, rather than careful composure learnt through years of fear.

A bird greeted the dawn, not far from the window. His Radiancy jerked awake. He was rigid, looking at the ceiling, breathing rapidly.

“Did you have a bad dream?” said Cliopher, softly.

“I—” said his Radiancy, and Cliopher watched as his lord put on the serenity, and disappeared. “Was it a dream, Cliopher? What happened last night?”

“The Moon Lady came in answer to your prayer,” said Cliopher, now starting to feel nervous, “She… awoke something in you? And we came back here, and kissed and...”

His Radiancy closed his eyes, lying perfectly still. Cliopher had a flash of memory, of visiting the bier of the sleeping Emperor. Cliopher reached out his golden hand, to reassure himself that his lord is still there, but stopped just short of touching.

“My l-lord?” he said, his voice shaking, “I—I regret nothing, except that now I am afraid I have hurt you, somehow.”

“Cliopher,” said his Radiancy, “It is I who hurt you, I who took advantage, who abused my position for my own selfish—”

“No!” said Cliopher, loudly, unable to let his lord go on berating himself, “I promised I would tell you if I felt uncomfortable, what I liked, and disliked. And I did. I love you, Tor.”

“We should leave,” said his Radiancy, still looking at the ceiling, “Return to Solaara; you need not come with us. You can retire, here, to your home. We will make you Duke of Ilkano, and we will give you the next sky ship. I am sorry, I know it cannot possibly make right what I have done—”

Cliopher rolled over onto his front, and slid feet-first off the edge and onto the floor. His mind was a freezing wasteland. It was all his worst fears come true. His lord was sending him away. He couldn't think what to say, he just lay, shaking, in the formal prostration. Tears ran unchecked down his face. His Radiancy wasn't listening, didn't understand, and Cliopher didn't know how to make him.

“Oh, Cliopher,” said his Radiancy, sitting up, “Please, get up. I—” he swallowed, and then slipped off the other side of the bed. “I'll send Conju,” he whispered, and fled the room. The guards followed.

Huddling into himself now, Cliopher continued to cry. It was shameful, he knew, but he couldn't stop. He had been so _close_, so close to belonging, to making so much good in the world, to standing beside his lord. Why had he gone and ruined it? Why had he made such a fool of himself, and hurt his beautiful lord?

There was a knock at the door. Cliopher was in no fit state to answer, and didn't try, just huddled further into himself and hoped whoever it was would go away. They were all going to go away now, weren't they? Leaving him here. The most beautiful place he had ever seen, now the place of his greatest grief. He let out another sob, and then made a very loud sniff.

The door opened, and Conju slipped in. Vaguely, Cliopher realised he must be making sound deliberately because he knew his friend, his... once-friend, could walk silently when he wanted. Cliopher shook, and sniffed, ashamed that a great man like the Cavalier an Vilius was witnessing his disgrace.

"Oh, Cliopher, Cliopher," said Conju, his voice cracking slightly. Kneeling down next to Cliopher, Conju said, "Let's get you..." and then looked up at the door, "Varro, I need you to carry Cliopher to your room. Pikabe, ask the staff to strip this room and clean it."

"I can..." said Cliopher, and tried to push himself up. But then he realised what must be happening. The guards were arresting him. He had hurt the Emperor, and he would have to pay the price. Was that worse than being left here, alone? The shame of both was like a knife inside him, making it hard to breathe. Varro picked him up easily, and Cliopher simply shook in his arms.

They took him down the hall, and into one of the guard's bedrooms. He was placed down gently onto one of the beds. Conju, never one for showing physical affection, rested a hand gently on his shoulder.

"Are you in pain, Cliopher?" he asked gently, "I brought some medicines, just in case."

"No," said Cliopher, his voice sounding hollow and detached. "I... I might have hurt the Glorious One, Lord Conju... you should..." he struggled to finish the thought, much less the words. He curled more tightly into a ball, hiding his face under his arm, and continued crying.

He knew that Lord Conju would be carefully suppressing expressing any awareness of what Cliopher's words meant. That Cliopher had accepted that he could no longer call the Emperor _his lord_, was not a part of the inner household allowed to use the endearment _his Radiancy_. That Cliopher knew that Lord Conju should distance himself from the taint of association with a man shortly to be either sent home in disgrace or executed. 

"His Radiancy is fine, he has gone for a walk," said Lord Conju, still gently. Then in his more usual tone, "Well, not _fine_, he is anxious that he has hurt you."

"He... I... he..." said Cliopher, in between sobs, "He is sending me away. If... if he has not changed his mind... I always knew I would overstep some day..."

The only thing Cliopher could hear was his own laboured breaths and sniffs. Eventually a handkerchief was shoved under his arm. He rubbed his face on it, but fresh tears kept running down his face.

A whispered conversation started up above him. He couldn't hear much, didn't want to hear much, until Lord Conju snapped.

"Rhodin, if you want to protect his Radiancy in spirit as well as body, you will go and talk to Ludvic and find out what happened," said Lord Conju, in his most commanding aristocratic voice.

Then in much more pleasant tones added, "Varro, could you get some coffee for Cliopher, he always thinks more clearly after coffee."

Not long after there was the delicious smell of Vangaveyen brew. Cliopher uncurled a bit, and realised the handkerchief he was holding was soaked. Lord Conju took the chance to swap the cloth, and shove the mug of coffee next to Cliopher's nose.

It was enticing enough that he uncurled a little further, and pushed himself up on his elbow.

"It works best if you drink it," said Lord Conju, wielding dry humour as armour.

Cliopher didn't manage a smile, but he did manage to take the mug and drink some coffee. Trying to think about nothing else but the aroma, the taste, the warm mug in his hands. Good coffee was *always* good.

When he finished it, he looked a little mournfully at the bottom few grounds. As if they might turn into more coffee.

Lord Conju took the mug from his hands, and passed him a full one. 

"Thank you, Lord Conju," said Cliopher, and his voice only shook a little bit.

"Ludvic says his Radiancy... offered for you to retire immediately?" asked Lord Conju, gently.

Cliopher nodded, took a too-large sip of hot coffee, and sniffed while trying to swallow as it burnt his mouth.

"Because he thought he had hurt you?" clarified Lord Conju.

"But he didn't!" insisted Cliopher, "I told him, but he wouldn't listen, so I must have hurt him, but he doesn't want to say, because he knows what the penalty would be, and he's trying to protect me, even though that's highly illegal, not that the Emperor can act illegally, but anyway, if I did hurt him I could never live with myself, and if he sends me away, I can't… I can't…" And then, despite the mug of hot coffee, Cliopher burst into tears again.

Lord Conju grabbed the mug back before Cliopher spilt it.

When Cliopher looked up again, his sobs having slowed, Commander Omo was standing in the doorway. "His Radiancy has sent a parrot for the boat captain." Then turning to Lord Conju, added, "He has asked you to pack."

"I'll see to your things too, Cliopher," said Lord Conju, and slipped out of the room.

"Commander Omo, there is," gasped Cliopher, between sniffles, "A blank writ, of attainder. In my writing kit."

Commander Omo tilted his head in query, but didn't say anything.

"If, he is leaving... without me. It would be the easiest..." Cliopher couldn't finish explaining. Would he have to fill it out? No, surely Commander Omo or Ser Rhodin could. Nobody in the Vangaveye-ve would care, they would probably think it was a joke.

"You will be coming back to Solaara," said Commander Omo, gently, "And not under arrest."

"How, how can you be so sure?" asked Cliopher, wishing he had another clean handkerchief.

"He is pacing," said Commander Omo. Cliopher could imagine it, the rhythm he knew so well, the triangle path, marked out on the sand. The Glorious One had the entire beach, but he had carefully put himself back in the box.

"If, if I don't go back with you," said Cliopher, "He's not going to be able to retire, is he?"

"No," said Commander Omo.

Cliopher nodded, and pushed himself off the bed. He would not let the Glorious One re-enter the cage. Perhaps he would never want to see Cliopher again, that was fine. Kiri could act as secretary, and Cliopher could work on the reform program. He could stay away from court, and focus on the audits and policy. It had never been his aim to have his name on the work, he did it for its own sake.

With a deep breath, Cliopher stood up and walked past Commander Omo, and back to his bedroom. The staff had already cleaned it, but Lord Conju wasn't there. Cliopher had a quick wash, and then looked at his uniform. It didn't seem right to put on the red-and-black, and he hadn't thought to bring the standard Service uniform. He picked his plainest outfit, and packed everything else away, setting his bags outside the door.

Taking his writing kit, and his heart in his throat, Cliopher went to the dining room. It was, to his relief, unoccupied. He pulled out a notebook from his writing kit. The full notes on the reforms were back in his room in Solaara. But he could start planning now, how the Glorious One could start devolving power away from himself and the Princes.

It only took him a couple of hours to finish a first draft of the immediate restructure. It was while he was correcting his own work that Lord Conju found him again.

"Are you working, Cliopher?" he asked, confused.

"Yes, Lord Conju," said Cliopher, putting down his red pencil, "I want to ensure the Glorious One can retire."

"Cliopher. We are not going to shun you. Stop it," said Conju, a sting in his voice.

"Sorry," said Cliopher, looking down. He'd always been rubbish at etiquette, but he hadn't wanted to presume. "I don't know what to do, Conju," he whispered.

"You don't have one of your many protocols covering this exact situation?" asked Conju, a small smile on his face. Obviously trying to needle Cliopher.

"There's one for if I die unexpectedly, and one for if I'm temporarily incapacitated," said Cliopher, "But they all pre-date the Glorious One's decision to retire. I don't think I can reform the mundial government from the Vangaveye-ve." He took a deep breath, "But I will try, of course."

"Talk to him, Cliopher. Don't resign just because you feel you should," said Conju, gently.

Cliopher looked at the other man, a wry smile on his face. That last statement perhaps cut a little too close for both of them, given what had happened between Conju and the Glorious One earlier in the vacation.

"Very well," said Cliopher, "Please let me know when would be a good time to beg an audience."

"Cliopher?" came the Glorious One's voice from behind Conju.

With the practice born of his long years of service, Cliopher stood without knocking anything over, and turned. Conju slid open the door to the common room. At the first glimpse of the Glorious One's golden sandals, Cliopher folded down into the full formal prostration. 

He didn't know what title he should use, but the Glorious One had called him by his first name, so he said, "My lord," into the floor.

"You still want to help me to retire?" asked the Glorious One, softly, giving the gesture to rise.

"Of course, my lord," said Cliopher, ignoring the gesture, "I love you and I want you to be free. I understand that you may no longer wish to work with me, I only beg that you let me return to Solaraa to help the Service plan the transition."

"Solaraa will not be safe for you," said the Glorious One, "The Ouranatha will see the disturbance in the taboos, and will not understand. They have one solution to such problems."

"Then surely they will notice, even if I do not return, my lord?" said Cliopher. He didn't think the Council of Elders likely to succeed in hunting him down in the Vangaveye-ve, but he could well imagine the collateral damage of their attempt.

Irritated that the Glorious One thought he was protecting Cliopher, he continued, "I have been aware that I risked their wrath, my lord, from the first day I looked into your eyes. If you forbid me from the sky ship, I will take the sea train, and if you ban me from that I shall take a ship, and if you declare me a Terror of Astandalas and forbid anyone from transporting me, I will build myself a boat with my own hands and sail to Solaara."

"Cliopher," said the Glorious One, "Will you not look at me?"

Tears once again running down his face, Cliopher looked up and into the golden eyes. There were also tears welling there. "Oh, my lord, I am sorry," said Cliopher, "I did not mean to distress you. But I could not enjoy retirement, knowing you could not do the same."

"My retirement is not worth throwing your life away for, Cliopher," said the Glorious One, "If they did not kill you, they would declare you a god as well. I have become accustomed to being guarded, being separate, being treated as divine; I could not do that to anyone else."

"And you would not, my lord," said Cliopher, "Any more than you asked them for the Imperial Cult. At any time they could declare Conju divine, or the cat Kiri keeps in the Offices of State, or themselves." He paused, thinking for a moment. "And of course, if they _do_ make such a claim about me, whichever god it was might take objection."

The reminder that there was an actual god involved clearly gave the Glorious One pause. The gods might be constrained in their spheres of influence, but within them, they were more powerful than even the Lord Magus. Would the priest-wizards have the gall to call the Sun to their defence? Cliopher struggled to imagine the famously jealous Astandalan god taking kindly to the way they had replaced him with the Emperor in most worship and rituals. It would be amusing to imagine the Sun turning up in the Throne Room and simply redirecting their plea to the Sun-on-Earth.

"No doubt," said the Glorious One, "They will recognise that it would be ill-advised to invoke the true gods. But they are not without magic of their own, nor popular support."

Rhodin coughed politely. The Glorious One gestured for him to speak, and he said, "If it was a popularity contest, Cliopher would win handily."

Cliopher blinked in surprise. It hadn't occurred to him that anyone really knew who he was, outside the Palace. And the Service. They certainly didn't in the Vangaveye-ve.

"Even if we gave you a full roster of guards," said the Glorious One, sounding ever so slightly tense, "There is still the matter of magic."

It was true, Cliopher's rooms at the end of the Alinoral wing were a long way from anywhere. Suddenly it struck him what he was arguing for. That the Glorious One should take him back against his wishes. He had hoped, last night, that perhaps something more than the influence of the Moon Lady had brought him and the Glorious One together. By the light of day, it could not be more clear that the Glorious One wanted to be as far away from Cliopher as possible. He sank back down in shame. If the Emperor would rather be caged than see Cliopher again, that was his choice to make.

"My lord, my most sincere apologies for saying I would stalk you back to Solaara and implying that I might in any way force my company or attentions on you. It was wrong of me, and it makes me sick that I would even think of such a thing, when you have so clearly told me that you wish to never see me again."

"No," said the Glorious One, but it came out slightly strangled, "You didn't... I didn't..."

Nervously, Cliopher lifted his head again to look at the man he dared to hope might be his lord. "Please," he whispered, "I love you."

The frozen silence was broken by a whistle from outside. The boat had arrived.

The Glorious One made the gesture to dismiss Cliopher, and he scrambled up and back. Conju slid the door to the common room closed again. Shaking slightly, Cliopher packed up his writing kit, fetched his bags, and headed down to the water.
