Cliopher did agree to tell his mother. So Tor, Cliopher, Conju, Ludvic, and Oginu went to visit Cliopher's family house. Conju had been there before, and if Cliopher had felt nervous introducing his noble friend to his family, he felt positively ill at the prospect of introducing Tor.

That wasn't helped by meeting so many people on the way. Tor was dressed in his 'disguise' in silver wizard robes. But even in their casual vacation dress, Cliopher and his companions were wearing in far finer fabrics than anyone else they walked by. Every cousin they met asked Cliopher what event he was going to, and were disappointed or surprised to find out he was only going to visit his mother.

After the fifth delay, Cliopher switched to taking a back route. Suddenly realising how this might be perceived, he said, "I'm not trying to hide you, Tor, I would just like to get there before noon."

Tor smiled, "When you said eighty-nine cousins—"

"Fifty-nine," corrected Cliopher, without thinking.

"Fifty-nine," agreed Tor, with a sparkle in his eye, "I hadn't quite understood how many people that would be."

"I'm related to about a third of the city, by blood or marriage," said Cliopher, checking before they crossed a canal-bridge, "Thankfully we aren't obliged to tell all of them."

When Cliopher rang the doorbell, it was his sister who answered.

"Kip! Again! What a surprise!" then taking in his companions added, "Oh, and Conju, hello, and more friends, from Solaara surely. Do come in."

Vinyë led them upstairs, without waiting for introductions. "I'm afraid I'll have to head out for rehearsal soon Kip. There's a big concert on tonight, for the anniversary you know, oh I would love it if you could all come."

In the parlour was Cliopher's mother, Aunt Oura, and Vinyë's two grown children. They all looked up politely, Eidora recognising Conju with a smile.

Cliopher had come to the problem of introducing Tor and his mother. Feeling he had more unknowns than equations, he took a deep breath and guessed a solution. 

"Mama, this is my *fanoa*, Tor Damara. Tor, my mother, Eidora Mdang; Aunt Oura; my sister Vinyë; her children Gaudy and Leona."

Vinyë mouthed *fanoa* with a clear question on her face. Gaudy looked up from his book, back down, and began frantically flipping pages.

Eidora smiled, and waved them over. "Well, well, you've been hiding this one close Kip. So how did you two meet? Do you work together?"

"Yes, we do," said Kip, "That's how we met, when I was appointed Tor's secretary."

"Oh! So you were his *lord*, Tor? Kip, you should have said. That makes so much more sense! Well, Kip *is* always writing about you Tor."

Kip blushed, uncertain what to say. Everyone seemed to be taking this remarkably well.

"Did you come home just to tell Eidora, Kip?" asked Aunt Oura, teasing.

"No," said Tor, to Kip's surprise, "We went to Navikiani on Lesuia for a vacation," (Vinyë gave Cliopher a meaningful look that he couldn't parse) "And Cliopher proposed to me. We thought we should tell you, Eidora, before returning to Solaara."

"Oh," said Eidora, "Are you going back today?"

Tor looked at Kip, who thought *equals*, and swallowed hard. "We haven't talked to the sky ship captain yet, but we aren't expected back immediately."

"I'm sure Eidora is itching to have a celebration," said Aunt Oura, "Maybe down at the beach, we probably don't have enough space here."

"Yes, I think so," said Eidora, thinking, "Not tonight, of course Vinyë, perhaps tomorrow? It's a shame Tovo isn't here. Maybe just a small party now, and we can have something bigger next time you're here. You could invite all your university friends as well Kip."

"We will have some kind of event in Solaara, and you will all be invited, of course," said Tor warmly, "I imagine that will have to be in at least a few months, Conju?"

Pausing before replying, Conju answered, “That would be very much appreciated, my lord, there will be a lot to prepare.”

Gaudy gasped, looking between his book and Tor, and blushed furiously. Tor looked at him with a neutral smile, and a slight tilt to his head. Cliopher tried to see if he could read the title of the book.

“You are in my book, ah, sir," said Gaudy, holding it up.

Cliopher finally read the title, ‘Zunidh: A Modern History’.

"I would imagine so," replied Tor, enigmatically, "I hope Kip is there too."

Gaudy looked confused, and Tor clarified, "The Littleridge Treaty? Decimalisation, the Post Office, the Ministry of the Common Weal? Surely they all get a mention?"

"Of course, sir," said Gaudy, "But... The Littleridge Treaty, for example, was agreed between the Princes and the Last Emperor, sir. I don't believe Uncle Kip is a prince...?"

"Not yet, at least," said Tor brightly. Kip blushed furiously, and wished he felt confident enough to kick him.

"Who wrote that history, Gaudy?" asked Tor. Cliopher stiffened, suddenly realising why he recognised the title.

Gaudy flipped to the front, his brow furrowed. "You're right sir, it doesn't list an author. It's just 'The Private Offices, for the Imperial Beauraucratic Service Examinations'," he said, reading from the title page.

"Kip," said Tor, smiling wryly, "I think I might need to be involved in the next revision. You can't write yourself out like that."

Eidora, who had been looking more and more confused, finally asked, "Are you saying that our Kip was involved in the Littleridge Treaty in some way? That's the thing Quintus said made his business possible."

Cliopher wasn't *surprised* that was all his mother knew about the treaty, but he was a little disappointed. Clearly the isolation of the Vangaveye-ve, especially in those years, had meant they hadn't known of its impacts elsewhere.

"It was his idea," said Tor, and Cliopher made a small noise of protest. Tor raised an eyebrow at him, and continued, "He also wrote the first draft, and organised and led the negotiations for the final version."

"You led the negotiations, my---Tor," said Cliopher, flustered. 

"Only officially," said Tor, with a wave of his hand.

"Oh," said Gaudy, "So when the book says 'an initiative of the mundial government'..."

"Yes, you can read that as Kip's," said Tor, clearly enjoying himself embarrassing Cliopher, "And for the next edition we'll make sure he doesn't remove himself."

"Is that a book for one of your subjects?" asked Cliopher, hoping to change the topic.

Gaudy shook his head, "I was... I was thinking of sitting the Service Exam. If you don't mind, Uncle Kip?"

Once again surprised by his nephew, Cliopher said, "Not at all."

"We would both be thrilled," said Tor, "And after the Examinations Scandal everything is blind marked so you won't have to worry about favouritism."

Cliopher glanced at his mother, who looked tense, and his sister, who looked pleased. He knew (oh he knew) that his mother disapproved of him living and working away. He suddenly realised he'd been assuming Vinyë felt the same.

"It's double-blind marked, twice," said Cliopher, "And any discrepancies are taken to an independent panel of experts."

"We've been doing blind auditions for the orchestra for a while now," said Vinyë, "It's been wonderful, I really think we're getting a much better calibre of musician."

Talk turned to the orchestra, and the concert that night. Cliopher wasn't sure if Tor would want to go, and if he did, if he'd want to be incognito. Seemingly the very thin disguise had worked on his family, except perhaps for Gaudy, who looked slightly terrified the entire visit.

Eventually, they managed to leave, with only Cliopher committing firmly to attending that evening.
