Cliopher was part-way through his second cup of coffee, and still on the first page of the book, when he heard the tinkle of a fine china teacup being placed down on its saucer too quickly. Automatically, he stood, dropping the book on the chair, and abandoning his coffee on the side table. Drawn by an invisible force, he walked to the door to his Radiancy's room. There he stopped, unwilling to knock, unable to step away, scared to be caught and thought listening at keyholes.

"Come in," came his lord's voice.

Ato opened the door, and nodded to Cliopher. Trying not to shake, Cliopher stepped into the bedroom. He tried to focus on the room, to stop himself from saying or doing anything stupid. It was beautifully appointed, although not a performance space like the Imperial Bedchamber, which rather deserved the capitals. Where his lord's bedroom in the Palace of Stars was white on white, this room was panelled in carved woods, picked out here and there with a dash of colour.

Remembering just in time, Cliopher stopped beside the bed, and performed his obeisance. His lord was propped up in bed, holding Cliopher's report. The hands which Cliopher had admired so often, perhaps very slightly trembling. Was it anger? His fury on Leuisia had been wind and rain and large gestures. Was it fear? But no, Cliopher was familiar with the tight control of his lord's anxiety.

"Cliopher," said his Radiancy, "Are you feeling well?"

"Yes, my lord," said Cliopher, slightly surprised by the question.

"And this was your idea? Not Conju, or Rhodin's?"

"I did not even show it to them, my lord," Cliopher said, now perhaps slightly wishing he had sought their advice. Every report and proposal benefited from another hand to help edit it. "It is only a first draft; please tell me your feedback, my lord, and I will refine it."

"But you're sure, about the conclusion?"

"Oh, yes, my lord. I do not think it likely you will change my mind there, unless there is something very large that I have overlooked." This felt almost like their normal discussions, as if he had presented a recommendation to increase university places, or a review of drug and alcohol treatment facilities.

Conju, arriving with a fresh tea service, gave Cliopher an assessing look. Discretely, he poured his lord a fresh cup of tea, and set it within easy reach.

"Were you aware of what Cliopher was writing about, Conju?" asked his Radiancy.

Bowing, Conju replied, "I surmised he was working on plans for the changes to the government to enable you to retire, my lord."

Cliopher *had* been working on those plans, and he had incorporated some of their details into this document. But that was not really a precise description of what he had given his lord.

"I was given a similar report in the second year of my reign," said his Radiancy, not specifically to Cliopher or Conju, "Although of course not as well researched or reasoned."

Completely loosing his grip on what little etiquette skills he had, Cliopher gasped, and took a step backwards. "My lord," he said, panic rising, "I did not realise, I am so sorry, I hadn't considered—"

His Radiancy held up a hand, and Cliopher fell to silence. "As I said, Cliopher. Not nearly as well researched or reasoned. For example, the previous one didn't take into account the preferences of anyone involved, and the author seemed to think the Sun was some kind of fertility god."

Cliopher blinked. Even he knew the Astandalan Sun was about fire and justice and order. Perhaps, virility. Not fertility. It would presumably be in the archives somewhere, perhaps he could dig it up for a laugh. If, that was, he wasn't about to meet the same dismissal as whoever made that previous proposition.

Conju shot Cliopher a look that screamed "what have you done now" and also "I will tell you off later". Cliopher did his best to ignore Conju, and focus only on his lord.

"As you note, I am sure some of the ceremonial court details will need to be negotiated," and there was the small, wry smile that Cliopher knew from decades of seeing his lord accept his recommendations.

Unable to stop himself, Cliopher lifted his hand to his mouth, awe and joy flooding through him.

"I will, for example, probably still need to ennoble you," said his Radiancy, calmly.

Cliopher could not avoid letting out a small groan.

His Radiancy smiled, genuinely smiled, and said, "Oh, my dear Cliopher, don't ever change."

Blushing furiously, Cliopher nevertheless straightened back to his usual negotiating posture. "Would you like more time to consider the proposal in private, my lord?"

His Radiancy set the pages down in his lap. "No," he said softly, "You have persuaded me on the major points, and we should discuss the rest together. I'm sure Conju would advise we should do so over breakfast."

Conju bowed, and slipped into the dressing room.

Cliopher stepped forwards, slowly, to his Radiancy's bed. Until he was close enough that his lord could, if he wished, reach out and touch him. Pulling rather tightly on the hem of his sleeve, he said softly, "You accept my proposal, my... Tor? I can go back to Solaara with you?"

"Yes... but my dear Kip," said his Radiancy, throwing back the covers, "Are you aware how much this will constrain your life? Truely?"

Cliopher nodded, "I'll have to have guards, and until you have, ah, audited, the Ouranatha, I probably can't leave the Tower without you. Hopefully that won't take too long."

"And you will have to go with me any time I do leave. Every court session, every public audience, every hearing. Even afterwards, if we are to appear as equals. Everything you do and say picked over in minute details. Always controlled, on show. The slightest misplaced glance or gesture..."

"Tor," said Cliopher, taking another step closer, and interrupting what was clearly a painful and well-known list. "It's not forever. You have already remade the court so it is nearly unrecognisable to the few who really remember Astandalas. By the time we are done it will seem like a myth."

Not letting Tor get in the protest Cliopher could see he was rallying, he continued, "And until then, yes, I will let Conju choose what I wear. I will keep you company through the interminable banquets, and we will share the work together. I *want* to."

Tor sighed, but he was smiling. "Who would you like to tell first? And is there anything we need to do? Your report mentioned *efanoa*."

Cliopher's mouth started hurting, he was smiling so much. "*You're* sure, Tor? Really?"

"I have loved you for a long time, Kip. Perhaps from the first day I saw you," said Tor, pushing himself out of the bed. There was only *just* enough space for him to stand without touching Cliopher. Slowly, he reached up a hand. "It seemed impossible... I... I am sorry I panicked, yesterday."

Cliopher grabbed for it. "And I am sorry I fell to pieces," he said, ruefully, "I was so scared of being sent away." He couldn't quite look Tor in the eye as he said the last part.

"Was?" asked Tor, softly.

"Am," corrected Cliopher, his breath hitching. It had only been minutes since he had been given permission to stay.

"Oh, Kip," said Tor, gripping Cliopher's hand in both of his, "Never. I am terrified of *trapping* you."

"Never," said Cliopher back, just a fiercely.

They stared at each other, breathing deeply, until Conju coughed quietly from the dressing room door. They both turned to look at him.

"Yes, Conju, I'm coming," said Tor, with a smile. He let go of Cliopher's hands reluctantly.

"I'll see you at breakfast," said Cliopher, picking up the report from where it had been pushed aside on the bed.

He walked around the guards as they followed Tor into the dressing room. Tor had guards everywhere, even in the toilet. Surely that wouldn't be necessary for him, would it? Perhaps it just hadn't been reconsidered since the Fall. Something to ask Ludvic about.

Cliopher got another cup of coffee, and took it to the breakfast table. There he sat down to review his own report again. He noticed now that he's left off needing a full security review, and went back to his room to grab he writing kit so he could make corrections. Pen and ink obtained, he began annotating.

Looking up at the sound of footsteps, he saw Tor and Conju arriving. Cliopher didn't stand up, but he did look up and smile. From behind Tor's back, Conju gave Cliopher a look of horror. Tor beamed back, and took the chair next to Cliopher.

"Would you mind making some space for food?" asked Tor, looking over the pages spread out over three place settings and threatening the butter. "I see you've already got to work on draft two."

Blushing, Cliopher gathered the notes together. "Just fixing up some minor things, and those you've already mentioned."

"Kip," said Tor, gently, "Should we tell your family first?"

Cliopher blinked, and took a sip of coffee to his his nerves. "I thought maybe we should tell Conju, Ludvic, and Rhodin?"

Conju looked up sharply from where he was carefully cutting his toast into even batons. At the exact same moment, Rhodin appeared at the doorway and saluted.

"Should I get the Commander, sir?" asked Rhodin, "I think he will be here soon."

Tor waved him off, "When he arrives is fine."

Looking from Tor to Cliopher and back, Conju's face was very intently giving nothing away. This seemed to be a hard test of his resolve, however, as Cliopher spotted him very nearly startle when Rhodin reached across for the salt.

Luckily for Conju's nerves, Ludvic arrived promptly. He saluted, and then looked around the silent table before taking the last chair.

Tor looked at Cliopher, who realised that this was his chance to start practising *being* equal. "I wrote a proposal, and Tor accepted it," said Cliopher, nervously.

There was an awkward pause, before Conju said, "Yes, I saw that. What was it *about* Cliopher? You can't tell me it didn't have title."

"Oh," said Cliopher, not realising that Conju hadn't even read that far. Had Rhodin not looked it over as a matter of course? 

"*A risk analysis of potential responses to the gifts of the gods*," he said, and then, seeing blank looks on Rhodin and Conju's faces added, "The zamà said I was marked with the gift of Ani, the goddess of the ocean. I evaluated a number of possible approaches, and on the balance of the evidence recommended... recommended..." 

Could he say it? It seemed still impossible that Tor had accepted it. Conju was going to be horrified, and probably Rhodin as well. But Tor smiled, encouragingly, and so Cliopher rushed out the end of his thought.

"That we announce we are *fanoa*, a partnership of different equals, the greatest of friends, like Ani and Vou'a, or Elonoa'a and Aurelius Magnus, that this is the gift of the gods, and we do a thorough audit of the Ouranatha afterwards to weed out any malcontents, and until that is done I stick to Tor."

He took a breath, finally, and then buried his nose in his coffee. Cliopher had said far harder things to more hostile audiences before. He had negotiated in Nijan, and presented to the Council of Princes. Yet, somehow, telling his friends was harder.

"Is _fanoa_ related to fanaya?" asked Conju, cautiously. When Cliopher finally braved looking his friend in the face, he didn't look nearly as offended ad Cliopher expected.

"It would seem likely," said Tor, "A very few words entered Shaian in the days of Aurelius Magnus, mostly where the expanding Empire encountered a new material for which they had no name. Ahalo cloth, tomatoes, and so on. I would conjecture that when Aurelius described his relationship as _fanoa_ the definition in Shaian was drawn from what was seen of him and Elonoa'a."

Cliopher took the chance of Tor's explanation to try to recover his composure.

"_Fanoa_... is like a marriage?" asked Rhodin.

"It is a legally recognised relationship in the Vangaveye-ve," said Cliopher, "But many marry without equality, sometimes without friendship; conversely _fanoa_ wouldn't have assumed paternity, or even have to sleep together."

"But..." said Conju, and then bit his lip, clearly thinking better of whatever he was about to say, as his eyes darted from Cliopher to Tor. Finally, he said, "My... lords... what do you intend your living arrangements to be?"

Cliopher's heart started racing at the promotion in rank Conju had just given him. He didn't want—but that was going to be part of it, wasn't it? At least until Tor retired, being equals would mean Cliopher was elevated above his friends. His heart twisted, and he said, "Conju, Rhodin, Ludvic, in private, _please_ keep calling me Cliopher. I haven't changed, really."

Conju nodded, slowly. Feeling daring, Cliopher looked at Tor, trying to ask silently what he wanted. 

"I—" said Tor, quietly, "I do not wish to ask anyone to do anything that would make them uncomfortable. Nobody ever *has* called me 'Artorin', you know."

Without thinking too hard, Cliopher reached out a hand, and gently grasped Tor's. He wished he knew Tor's heart-name, the name he had given himself. Perhaps, someday, he would feel safe enough to share it. Cliopher would try to help him feel safe.

"I'll move in with you," said Cliopher to Tor, "It's not like I have much to move anyway."

Conju gave Cliopher an assessing look, "I'll arrange staff for you as soon as we arrive back, Cliopher."

Holding back a protest, Cliopher said instead, "Thank you, Conju." This was part and parcel of being equal with Tor. This was what Tor had fretted about, would be anxious that Cliopher would dislike. Well, Cliopher didn't _like_ it, but if he was going to have to wear ridiculous outfits, it would make sense to have someone who knew how to put them on him and take care of them.

"I'll have to reassign some of my work to others," said Cliopher, "And I won't be able to work from the Offices of State anymore."

Tor inclined his head, "I suppose I shall have to find another secretary. Good ones were in such short supply until you came."

Cliopher blushed, "Well, the appointment probably isn't seen as a way to quickly get rid of problem people anymore."

"It certainly didn't work with you," said Tor, very nearly laughing.

"No," said Cliopher, grinning back, "You're stuck with me now."

Rhodin took a sip from his coffee perhaps slightly louder than he would have normally.

"Right, sorry," said Cliopher, turning back to his three friends, "I should hand this over to you, Rhodin, for the security assessment. I accept I will need guards outside the Imperial Apartments, but I'm hopeful we can come to a compromise and I will be allowed to use the toilet and bathe unsupervised."

"I will let you know," said Rhodin gravely. Cliopher rolled his eyes, and handed over the draft proposal.

"Do you have any other questions?" asked Tor, "I am aware this is very unusual."

"No, my lord," said Ludvic, and Cliopher thought perhaps he caught a smile in the usual stoic expression.

"Only," said Conju, turning to Cliopher, "_Will_ you tell your mother, Cliopher? Or are you going to make me do it?"
