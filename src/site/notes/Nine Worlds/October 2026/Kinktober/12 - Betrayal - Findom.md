> Conju needs you — Cliopher

Rhodin looked at the note, his face carefully blank. “Thank you,” he said, “There is no return message.”

The page bowed, and trotted off. Rhodin was _supposed_ to be going on duty with his Radiancy in two bells time. He didn't like to make last-minute shift changes, but if Conju was upset in any way then his Radiancy would be upset. That simply wasn't allowed.

As he walked briskly down the hallway to the Commander's office, he considered what might be giving Conju trouble, and why he had gone to _Cliopher_ first. Malicious gossip was the game Conju knew best, indeed was probably better at than Rhodin. If he'd broken up with his latest boyfriend, the most common cause of distress, he usually flounced down to ask Rhodin to take him out for dinner. The only thing Rhodin could think of was perhaps they had been together, and somehow Conju had been injured.

“Can I swap out tonight?” said Rhodin, as he popped his head around the corner of the door.

Commander Omo looked up from the letter he was reading, giving Rhodin the full force of his attention. Rhodin slipped the note across the desk. Ludvic read it, and then looked at Rhodin again, nodded once, and went back to his letter.

The Cavalier was in his favourite parlour, curled up in his favourite chair. When the majordomo let him in, Rhodin could see at once that Conju was miserable. The chair across from him was occupied by Cliopher, who had a ledger and a stack of documents on his lap. 

They both looked up at Rhodin, Cliopher relieved, and Conju wretched. Rhodin immediately sat on the corner of the settee nearest to Conju, and reached out a hand.

“What happened?” he asked, softly.

Conju closed his eyes, and looked at the floor. Cliopher looked from Conju to Rhodin and back.

“Kirin ran off, taking rather a lot of money, and leaving debts in Conju's name,” said Cliopher, “I'm working out exactly how much.”

The embarrassment was practically rolling off Conju in waves. “I _gave_ him money. If he'd just _asked_,” he said.

“Gambling?” asked Rhodin, gently.

“Of course,” snapped Conju, “Just like my father and brother. But at least they only frittered away their *own* money.”

Cliopher looked like he was about to correct Conju, and then decided better of it. Rhodin was sure he was going to point out all the ways nobles ruining their estates also harmed the people who depended on them as tenants or employees. He'd apparently decided now was not the time.

Rhodin tried to work out how to phrase his next question. He didn't want to insult Conju, but he did need to know. If one of the criminal gangs was involved, this could get messy.

“Do you know who to?” he asked, looking to Cliopher this time.

Biting his lip, Cliopher flipped a few pages, and then replied. “Seventeen nobles of the court, two payday lenders, one bank, eight businesses in Solaara.”

That didn't sound like Kirin had been in the gambling dens, or to the more unsavoury lenders. A relief. Rhodin would love to have an excuse to take out even one of the criminal gangs, but he also needed evidence and resources.

A small noise escaped Conju. He stood up, and fussed with his robes. Then he went to the little shrine on the side of the room, and lit a stick of incense. Rhodin waited for Conju to burst out into an angry rant, but he just stood and watched the smoke curling up from the incense. Cliopher continued scribbling, shuffling papers, not looking at Conju or Rhodin.

It was Cliopher who spoke again. “Kirin stole around twenty-seven thousand valiants of jewels and art, and one hundred and eighty-four thousand from your bank accounts. The debts total another two hundred and seventy-six thousand, seven hundred and three valiants.”

Abruptly, Conju sat down on the floor, covering his face with his hands.

When he didn't get up, Rhodin awkwardly slid off the settee and joined him on the floor. Very lightly, he placed a hand on Conju's shoulder.

“It can be repaired,” he said.

“Can it?” whispered Conju, dropping his hands to his lap. He didn't look at Rhodin, but Rhodin could see the tears pooling in his eyes.

Cliopher shuffled the papers again. “There is no way the bank should have issued this loan without your witnessed signature on the paperwork,” he said, “Some of these withdrawals are suspicious as well, I suspect he may have forged your signature. And the terms of the payday lenders are, frankly, illegal.”

At that, Rhodin grinned at Cliopher. “Well then,” he said, “It sounds as if I should be seeking some arrest warrants. Certainly we should put out a 'person of interest' notice.”

But Conju shook his head, “How can I show my face at court?”

Rhodin asked him, faux-casual, “Can I have a look at the list of nobles? I am sure many of them can be persuaded to keep quiet. One way or another.”

Conju gave Rhodin one of his classic arched eyebrows. “Do I want to know?” he asked.

“I'm going to the Offices of State, to get a financial audit team sent out to these payday lenders. We should crack down on them quickly,” said Cliopher, looking up from the papers. “I'll ask Commander Omo to coordinate with the city police.” He stood up, and left. If he was leaving so he didn't have to hear what Rhodin had planned, well, Rhodin wasn't going to call him out for it.

Now they were alone, Rhodin pulled Conju into his arms. Conju went, limp and damp.

“Make me forget about Kirin, please,” he whispered.

“Any time,” said Rhodin. He picked Conju up, showering his face in kisses. Then Rhodin carried him to his bedroom. Finding and blackmailing those nobles could wait, what Conju needed first was to be shown he was deserving of love.
