By the time Conju arrived, Rhodin wondered if he might change his mind about tonight. The bells had stopped ringing a while ago, and it was clear that Conju's mind was back up in the Tower with his Radiancy. There had been nothing unusual at Court, but his Radiancy had trouble sleeping the past few nights. Rhodin had walked with him through the Imperial Apartments in the small hours, and wondered what kept that glorious mind awake.

Conju, still in all his cosmetics, although not in full court robes, looked at Rhodin. A less refined man might have sighed, might have complained about his day. Conju only lifted Rhodin's hand, and kissed it.

“What can I do for you, my friend?” Rhodin asked, looking from his hand to Conju's lips. The pink stain never transferred.

Looking down the hallway, Conju said, “I should probably get what sleep I can.”

“But you are tense,” said Rhodin, standing and moving behind Conju. He rubbed his thumbs down Conju's neck, and under the collar of his robe. Pushing the knots down and out. “Sleep will not come easy when you are like this.”

Conju didn't answer, but stretched, and leaned into the movement.

Shuffling him forward gently, Rhodin continued to work on his neck and shoulders. Given how close Conju worked to his Radiancy's skin, it was unsurprising that his muscles held him tight. When the slightest error could send you up in flames, there was a constant level of anxiety. Slowly, Rhodin moved him into his bedroom.

Once Conju was in front of his toilette table, Rhodin was out of his depth. There were at least three different liquids involved in removing Conju's cosmetics, and they all had matching cloths or wipes. Not even Conju's valet was allowed to help.

“I will give you a proper massage,” said Rhodin, as Conju slumped onto the chair and stared at his own reflection, “But I know you will insist on this first.”

“Yes,” said Conju, dropping his hand onto a stack of cotton disks, about the size of Rhodin's palm. Slowly, he pulled one off, and applied something from a cut crystal vial. That was used to clean off his eyes.

Rhodin continued rubbing Conju's shoulders, entranced by the complex process. Of course, Rhodin had watched Conju do this before, and had watched his friend remove cosmetics from his Radiancy as well. It seemed incredibly intimate, this slow shedding of the public face. He treasured that Conju allowed Rhodin to see him like this. Not every boyfriend was allowed to see this side of Conju, not for a while anyway.

It wasn't quite like Rhodin's uniform. A uniform projected a position, a belonging, it allowed people to look past you. Nobody looked _past_ the Cavalier Conju an Vilius, not unless it was because he was serving his Radiancy. He knew how to dress to send a message, command a room, ensure everyone knew who he was.

Rhodin removed the over-robe that Conju felt was needed even to undress his lord. He hung it carefully on the stand placed in the room for that purpose. Then he took away Conju's earrings, and his pendant. Playing valet to Conju was fun, in a way.

When he returned, Conju looked lighter. He wiped his face with a careful ordered pattern, and the coloured pigments disappeared into the cloth.

“Is that the old ideogram for ‘clean’?” asked Rhodin, surprised. He didn't know many of them, having had a rather sketchy education. But he thought knew that one, as it was often used to mark medical kits.

Conju blinked, and replicated his hand movements, watching himself in the mirror. “No,” he said, “It looks a little like ‘correct’, if that's what you were thinking of?”

“That must be it then,” said Rhodin, “You are always correct.”

The pun was rewarded with an eye roll, and a twitch and the corner of Conju's mouth. He grabbed another cloth, and suddenly twisted around in his seat, to face Rhodin's chest. Then, with a few swift strokes, he wrote something across Rhodin's skin. At least, that's what it felt like.

“I'm afraid I only know the one,” he admitted. No matter how much he had tried to make up for it, his lack of the usual education for a man of his class made him feel ashamed.

“Clean,” said Conju, with a smile. “It is made of ‘water’,” he added, repeating that part, “and… hmm… ‘argue’.”

Rhodin smirked, “I would never argue with you, because you are always correct.”

“Falsehood,” said Conju, writing on Rhodin again.

Smiling, Rhodin bent over to kiss the top of Conju's head. “I had no idea you had such a literary bent.”

With a snort, Conju threw the cloth into the basket with the others. “Hardly,” he said, “I have always been more interested in the decorative arts. But they are popular for embroidery patterns.”

“What should I draw on you with the massage oil?” asked Rhodin. He picked it up from the spot on the credenza.

Conju smiled, and shed his last layers of clothes. “Whatever you like,” he said, as he carefully folded them.

Rhodin took him by the elbow, and drew him over to the bed. “Perhaps your snake?” he offered, “Twisting around the tree of your spine.” He emphasised his point by drawing a finger over Conju's bare back. The other man writhed in anticipation.

They climbed onto the bed, and Rhodin began his work. “I know you could get better in Solaara,” he said.

“Don't fish for complements,” snapped Conju, “It's unbecoming. You know I like your company for more than just this.”

“Mmm,” agreed Rhodin, “There are people in Solaara who do massage with a happy ending.”

Conju wrinkled his nose. “I'm sure you would know. I prefer what we have.”

Rhodin pushed his thumbs into a particularly gnarly knot, and Conju hissed, and then sighed with relief. It was true, he did know, yet…

“Same,” he breathed into Conju's ear.
