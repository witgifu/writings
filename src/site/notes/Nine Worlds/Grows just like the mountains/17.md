Kip looked down on the guard with satisfaction. He, not being an idiot, was going to leave his prisoner tied up. It wasn't quite as satisfying to capture only the guard, he'd been hoping to get at least one of the aristocrats. However, it might have been harder to overpower two of them. A trap made of shards of ceramic and strips of fabric wouldn't be hard to get out of.

The fact that he'd been able to surprise and capture an Imperial Guard didn't speak well to their training. Or perhaps the man had only been impersonating a guard. Kip hoped so, for the safety of the Glorious One.

Hearing footsteps coming down the hall, Kip gently pushed the door shut. He'd already broken the latch so it couldn't lock on him, with a chip of rock shoved into the mechanism.

The guard looked up at him, and the door, moving his head as if he might call out. Kip had bound his mouth, but the man might get out a groan. Kip raised one eyebrow, and held up his knife. A carefully shaped sliver of ceramic fixed into the brush handle where the hair had been. The man averted his eyes and put his head down.

“Sayo Mdang?” came a voice down the hallway. One, to his surprise, he recognised.

“Ser Rhodin?” he said, confused. Why was the ex-duellist, now guard-recruit helping the Lord Treasurer? “Not happy in the guard? Thought someone else might pay better?”

“No,” said Ser Rhodin, and Kip frowned at the smile in his voice. “The Commander set the recruits searching for you. He has his reserve squad busy combing the Lord Treasurer's apartments and offices for evidence.”

“Oh?” said Kip, trying to decide if it was more likely he was being double-bluffed, or if Ser Rhodin could possibly be telling the truth. He didn't commit to either, but stashed his knife in his pocket and stood where he would most block the view of his captive.

There was a jingle of keys, and Rhodin added, “Yes, the Lord Emperor had him arrested on a writ of treason for interfering with his staff while carrying out the Illustrious One's direct orders.”

Not many people in the Palace of Stars would dare to lie about something like that. It was, technically, treason to misrepresent the actions of the Emperor. Still.

“I'm the undersecretary,” said Kip, “I report to the Glorious One's Private Secretary, the Cavalier an Vilius.”

Ser Rhodin pushed open the door, and grinned at Kip. It was a lazy grin, but Kip noticed the professional fighter still had his hand perfectly balanced on his spear.

“Except yesterday, when you attended him personally,” he said, “An important detail the Lord Treasurer overlooked. One of many it would seem. Where were you going?”

Kip shrugged. Part of him had wanted to go back to work, to see the look on the Lord Treasurer's face. It would be so sweet to see him wondering what had happened. The sensible part of him said to leave Conju a note and get out of the Palace and off Ysthar as quickly as possible. The Lord Treasurer would see one solution to the problem, and that would be Kip's death and possibly the death of anyone else who helped him.

“I am glad to see I wasn't really needed,” said Ser Rhodin, “Would you mind if I retied that man with official restraints?”

Kip moved aside to let Ser Rhodin have access, carefully placing himself between Rhodin and the open door. “You don't have a partner?” he asked, knowing that guards on duty always went in pairs.

“Guard Trainee Tiko is in the hallway, in case anyone else comes this way,” said Rhodin, and handed Kip his spear.

Kip took it with an arched eyebrow, as a voice outside, presumably Tiko, said, “Good to know you are well, Sayo Mdang.”

With practiced efficiency, Ser Rhodin removed Kip's ties and replaced them with his own rawhide strips. When he unbound the man's mouth, he immediately began talking.

“Trainee, you can't trust a word this secretary says, he's been trouble for the guards since he arrived here, and all his previous bosses have got rid of him as soon as they could,” he began, before Rhodin interrupted.

“It's a shame the rules prohibit me from muzzling you, because someone is going to ask me to repeat all this nonsense as evidence against you,” said Ser Rhodin, pulling the guard up.

“Evidence? On what charge?” said the guard, blustering. Kip couldn't believe his ears, had the man not heard the conversation?

“Treason, unless you turn on your employer and negotiate a lesser charge,” said Rhodin casually, like he was reading the specials board at a café.

The guard closed his mouth and looked silently from Kip to Rhodin and back.

Rhodin held out his hand, and Kip held out the spear without comment. “Actually, would you mind giving me your shiv? It'll set off all kinds of alarms if you take it into the Presence.”

Kip laughed, surprised once again. He fetched the knife from his pocket, and gave over his little tool. Rhodin secreted it somewhere, before taking the spear back.

“I have a penknife with me all the time,” said Kip, “I've never set off any alarms.”

Ser Rhodin nodded, and pushed the prisoner towards the door. “Which is a tool, created to be a tool, and you carry it intending to use it as one. If any of that changed, you would be in for a surprise.”
 
They joined Tiko in the hallway, who took the prisoner's other side. Kip wondered where exactly in the Palace that were, and how long a walk it would be to get back to the an Vilius apartment.

“Let's get you up to the Glorious One and show him his favourite has suffered nothing worse than a bit of dirt and an unpleasant night,” said Ser Rhodin, turning left.

“I'm *not* the Glorious One's—,” said Kip, but was interrupted by both trainee guards laughing.

“The Lord Treasurer was the Glorious One's longest serving minister and the most senior member of his government,” said Rhodin, as their footsteps echoed down the hall. At the end, a spiral staircase led up. “He forced the Glorious One to choose between the two of you, possibly inadvertently. The Lord of Five Thousand Lands chose you.”

Kip took a deep breath, and let it out again. Justice was not the same a favouritism. That nobody, except he hoped, the Emperor, seemed to agree with him, was not *his* problem. His concern was the quantity of work that was going to have built up in the hours he was captured. How quickly would they let him get back to it? If Lord Conju was still sick, which was likely, then Kip was going to be _doubly_ behind.

Then they reached the top of the stairs. Kip considered whether he should run as soon as they made it to a part of the Palace he recognised. It might be prudent, an arrest was no guarantee of a guilty verdict. Even if the Lord Treasurer himself wasn't able to take revenge, his family would quite possibly see it as their duty. All a commoner could do was appeal to their lord. Lord Conju had many wonderful qualities, and one of them was that he was not the kind of aristocrat who kept a small private army. Kip could hardly appeal to the Emperor directly, or even ask Conju to do so.

The strong sense of duty was warring with his desire to protect himself, Lord Conju, and his family, from any recriminations. If it had only been himself at risk, well, that was a risk he was familiar with. 

When they moved into a wider, more well-lit corridor, Ser Rhodin looked back. Something of the tension Kip was feeling must have showed on his face, because Rhodin said, “The Glorious One has asked us to bring you directly to him, as soon as you are found.”

Kip couldn't disobey a direct order from the Sun-on-Earth. He bit his lip, and said, “Of course, then we must make all haste.”

In the back halls and service corridors, nobody paid much attention to two guards hauling a bound man, trailed by someone else who looked a little grubby. As they moved into the areas where aristocrats and members of the Service worked, they got some curious looks. In the public halls, walking towards the very centre of the Palace, they got frank stares. Kip's only relief was that Ser Rhodin at least decided to take the secondary staircases, rather than the central spiralling ramp.

Finally, they appeared before the doors to the Imperial Apartments. At this point, Kip felt rather like _he_ was the prisoner being dragged in. There were four extra guards on duty at the doors. Including the deputy-commander of the guards. Both trainees saluted, and then stepped to the side and stood at attention, holding their prisoner. Leaving Kip exposed.

“Oh, thank all the starry hosts,” said the deputy-commander, “You're alive.”

Kip found it seriously disconcerting that this senior guard knew him on sight. Still, he should be on his best behaviour. “Deputy-Commander,” he said, and bowed, reasonably sure he guessed the correct degree for the man who was a minor Voonran prince.

The prince seemed to take no offence, and hurried Kip into the first antechamber. The pages on duty there stared at Kip wide-eyed, but said nothing. There were no other petitioners, and as they proceeded through the rest of the rooms, none in those either. It must have been because of the arrest, he reasoned, many people would be scrambling to work out what the new hierarchies of power would be in the court.

They were announced, and went into the official study of the Emperor. Kip, aware that he was not at all in a fit state to be in such a place, kept his eyes firmly on the ground as he walked in. When he saw the hem of the Glorious One's robe, he dropped into his well-practiced obeisance, and then deeper into the formal prostration.

Now that he was there, all Kip could think was that the Glorious One's words must have been misunderstood by the guards. Surely, he should not be there. What had he done, to come to the notice of the Lord of Ten Thousand Titles? The Emperor they were already calling 'Artorin the Good'. Kip was beneath his notice, and it was madness he had ever thought anything else. He had childish dreams of coming to Astandalas the Golden to change the Empire; that was the duty and work of the Glorious One. Concern for Kip shouldn't have even interrupted his day.

Kip's thoughts were spiralling so badly, he had forgotten to look for the Emperor's gesture. “Rise, Sayo Mdang,” said the Glorious One, eventually. Given the direct order, Kip did so, but kept his head bowed and his eyes downcast.

“We are glad to see you, Sayo Mdang,” said the Glorious One, and paused. 

“Glorious One, I am so sorry to have caused you any distress or inconvenience,” said Kip, trying to compose the correct words on the fly, having not expected to need to say anything. “Most merciful and serene lord, your concern for your lowly servant is such an honour as I could never have hoped to obtain.”

“Lord Conju, deputy commander, please escort Sayo Mdang home. We are sure he is in need of rest. The guards may interview him tomorrow afternoon.” There was a slight hint of warmth in the serene voice, and Kip was glad that the Glorious One clearly felt well towards Conju and the guards.

It was also, Kip was relieved to hear, a dismissal. He folded down gratefully into the obeisance, and backed out of the room. In the final antechamber, when the door shut, he turned to Conju. Kip didn't know what his own face showed, but Conju looked to be suppressing some deep emotion with a layer of officiousness.

“Well, Cliopher, we have our orders,” said Conju, gently grasping Kip's elbow, “Let us be on our way.”

They walked in silence from the Imperial Apartments, escorted by two guards in addition to the deputy commander. “Smile, Cliopher,” whispered Conju, “You have been granted an enormous honour, you're not being sent to your execution.”

Kip tried his best, and hoped he looked pleasant rather than constipated. It was a good thing the walk was short, as his face was hurting by the time they arrived at the doors. The two regular guards took up positions either side of the door, as if the an Vilius apartment was that of the Lord of Ysthar. Conju ignored them, Kip tried to copy him, as they walked inside.

When the door shut behind them, finally, Conju turned to Kip looking conflicted. “I was so worried,” said the aristocrat, “I didn't know what to do, I can't believe you're okay, Cliopher, are you okay?”

“I could use a bath,” said Kip, honestly, “That dungeon wasn't particularly clean. The bucket in the corner didn't even have a sponge on a stick.” Then realising Conju seemed a lot better than he had the day before, he asked, “What about you? Was the cold not all that bad?”

“Oh no,” said Conju, “In another hour or so the stimulant and painkiller concoction will wear off, and I'll be worse than I was yesterday. Also, my skin feels like it's crawling with ants and the edges of the world are blurry.”

Liliane stepped out from the service door. “My lord, Sayo Mdang. Perhaps a soothing soak and then an early supper for both of you?”

“Yes,” said Conju, rubbing his head, “An excellent idea.”

“I've set everything up in the baths,” she said, and turned, walking down the main hallway towards the private rooms. 

Conju nodded, and pulled Kip to follow along behind her. They went through a door which Kip had assumed was another bedroom. It had not occurred to him that there might be a bathhouse in the apartment.
