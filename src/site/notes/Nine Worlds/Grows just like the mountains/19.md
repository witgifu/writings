It wasn't any easier to approach the Imperial Apartments without Conju for the second time. That Kip was being escorted there by the guard didn't help, and they didn't explain why they felt it was necessary. He was mildly concerned that they thought it was because he was likely to run. Not making eye contact, he tried to pretend that the way people were staring at him was just because of the guards, and nothing to do with whatever rumours were circulating. 

Lilliane hadn't volunteered to tell him what the stories were, and he'd not been willing to ask. Conju would have asked, he told himself, as the first set of guards challenged him. Then Conju would have developed a plan to take advantage of whatever the rumour were; and a plan of what to do at court tonight. Should Kip go alone? He really didn't want to, he wanted to hide in their apartment and wait until Conju was well and could do it.

The final set of guards challenged him, and he tried to stand straighter. He could do this bit, up until noon. Then the guards would take him off somewhere to be interrogated. Everything else was after that. One step at a time.

His Radiancy was waiting by his desk, one hand resting on the polished surface. Kip stepped forward to the correct place, and performed his obeisance. This time he did remember to watch for the gesture to rise, and stood. 

“Good morning, Sayo Mdang,” said his Radiancy, serenely.

“Good morning, my lord,” replied Kip, and stepped towards his desk. Swallowing, realising he had already made a mistake by not using one of his Radiancy's titles, not offering flattering complements for being addressed at all. He sat down. The Emperor said nothing more, and the guards and attendants didn't flinch. 

Perhaps it was okay, then. Maybe he _should_ have asked about the rumours, not to learn about what people were saying of him and Conju, but to learn what they were saying about his Radiancy. When he was ready, Kip looked up, not to his lord's face (that would be a fatal mistake), but to the large sparkling collar he wore today.

It wasn't very far into the dictation before Kip realised he'd never visited the Offices of State last night, and so hadn't been able to get a copy of the days reports. This meant that there were many more places than usual where he had to leave himself a little star as a reminder to check the correct name or department. In addition, he'd had to assemble a new writing kit, and so everything he was using was either new or borrowed.

“We acknowledge the agency's fifty percent overspend you indicated, and accept that the flooding in Voonra will have increased grain prices there,” said his Radiancy, and turned.

Perhaps it was the late night, the unfamiliar writing materials, the stress of the day before. Kip said, “My lord, grain prices in Voonra went down after the recent floods.” He had been researching that when Conju had first invited him to a party. Hiding in the stacks, trying to correlate every source he could find to estimate the death toll. Then Kip realised that, here and now, he had interrupted and blatantly corrected the Lord of Rising Stars. The Sun-on-Earth who was, by definition, correct. Except when he was being misled by his ministers.

“My lord,” said Kip, into the silence that felt like it would swallow him whole, “The floods occurred after the second grain harvest had already finished; the community silos are built on stilts to protect them from rodents and floods. The price dropped by twenty percent, in line with the drop in the demand caused by the high death toll, which was concentrated in the fishing towns of the Honey River.”

“Thank you,” said his Radiancy, “Cross out the proceeding sentence. Instead: ‘In light of the overspend being substantial, an audit should be undertaken to ascertain for certain the causes. The provided explanation is unsatisfactory. Further to your requests for additional headcount—’”

Kip tried to calm his breathing and keep up with the dictation. Everything was fine, he had done the correct thing. His lord had _thanked_ him for the correction. Deciding that thinking about what had just happened now would only make his anxiety worse, and potentially cause errors, he put those thoughts away for later.

The rest of the session went smoothly, and it wasn't until Kip was cleaning his brushes that he realised what would be happening when he left. Whatever Conju might have said the night before, Kip didn't really believe he'd be treated like a high noble. He was honestly going to count himself lucky if he didn't get marched to Internal Security.

While Kip performed his bows, his Radiancy turned towards the inner doors. As he stepped forward, he called back, as one of Kip's cousins might have while leaving the house, and said, “We look forward to seeing you at court tonight, Sayo Mdang.”

Kip very nearly fell over at being addressed. He managed to get out a, “Thank you, Glorious One,” which he thought was doing quite well under the circumstances. Then his Radiancy moved through the doors; the guards, and attendants, going with him. 

Taking a deep breath and finding there was still air in the room, Kip gathered himself, turned, and went out through the antechambers. He stopped by the page's station to pass off a variety of materials. Usually he would have delivered most of those to the Offices of State himself, but he wasn't even certain he would be able to get back there today. Just in case he _was_ arrested, he even sent off his shorthand notes to the an Vilius apartment, to make sure Conju would have them.

Outside, he was met by two guards, Ser Rhodin in his trainee uniform, and the Deputy Commander. Kip bowed again to the Deputy, and waited politely to be directed or tied up. After a pause, the Deputy Commander said, “I suppose you haven't had lunch yet, Sayo Mdang?”

“I have not, your highness,” said Kip, trying to keep any apprehension from his voice. Were they going to drug him? Is that why he asked about lunch? Did the Imperial Guard have access to functional truth spells? He'd heard rumours, but it always seemed to Kip that if they had, they wouldn't have bothered with Internal Security. Not that torture actually ever got you _true_ answers, so maybe they did have them, and just didn't bother.

“Let's get something then, and Ser Rhodin and I can fill you in on the investigation while we eat.” The Deputy Commander turned, and led them towards the central cafeterias. They passed the large room with rows of tables and benches that was available to the Service and Palace staff. Also past the nicer dining room reserved for the upper secretariat. Finally stopping at a carved oak pair of doors, marked with a brass plaque engraved with 'Ministerial Dining Room'.

Kip balked. Ser Rhodin placed a hand gently on his shoulder, and offered an encouraging smile. The two other guards took up positions either side of the doors, and Kip followed the Deputy Commander inside, with Ser Rhodin behind him.

Inside, the light was dim and warm. The Deputy Commander was greeted with a bow and a murmur, and they were led around the edge of the room. Small dining tables were scattered around, with two or three people at each. Around the edge of the room were screens spelled for privacy and silence. Kip thought at first they were for the general protection of the room, until one of them slid open. The Deputy Secretary for Work slipped out, and with him came a burst of laughter which stopped as soon as he slid the screen closed again.

It was to one of these screened alcoves that they were led. Inside were, to Kip's irritation, dinning couches curved around tables. Not his favourite way of eating, but he could cope. The Deputy Commander took the highest position, in the right corner. Ser Rhodin, slightly to Kip's confusion, took the position to the left, the lowest. Kip, nervously, took the second-ranked position, on the Deputy Commander's right.

When the screen was closed, the prince smiled at Kip. “Where should we begin, eh?” he said, picking at the bowl of olives in front of him. “I suppose I should start by letting you know that we've arrested five people, although there could be more when we're done going through the ex-Lord-Treasurer's records. We've got the man impersonating an Imperial Guard that Ser Rhodin here arrested,” Rhodin gave Kip the _tiniest_ wink, and Kip said nothing, “The man at the top, of course. His nephew, who was doing the legwork and spell work, and two of the staff.”

Kip wondered if the nephew was the one who had asked him to write the proof-of-life note. Quite possibly, although if that was the case then the Lord Treasurer had been rather miserly to his family. Kip only smiled politely, and said nothing.

“I don't suppose you got a good look at anybody?” said the Deputy Commander. Ser Rhodin pulled out a notebook and pencil, obviously making sure Kip saw both.

“I only saw the face of the man in the guard uniform, your highness,” replied Kip carefully.

“He was the one who grabbed you?” said the prince, with a directness Kip found unusual.

“He met me as I was leaving the Imperial Apartments, going to the archives to complete some work for the Glorious One.” Conju had been clear that this was very important, so Kip made sure to repeat it. Deciding he would like to direct the notes Ser Rhodin was taking, he continued. “The man addressed me by name, and asked me to step aside to answer a question. I heard a snap behind me, which I assume was someone else triggering a cantrip. I woke up in the room where Ser Rhodin found me.”

At this moment, staff entered with plates of food. Bread, dips, mud crabs, oysters, quails, eggs, parcels of something wrapped in vine leaves, pears drenched in honey, cottage cheese balls studded with raisins. Far too much for the three of them, even if the guards were particularly hungry. Kip hoped the leftovers would be given to the poor of the city, but suspected they were sold by whoever ran the kitchen.

The Deputy Commander grabbed a quail; Kip selected a piece of soft bread, which he was more confident of eating decorously. Rhodin was still holding the pencil and notepad, although he did eye the crabs.

“I don't suppose the Lord Treasurer made a visit?” said the prince, picking off bits of meat with his fingers.

“Not that I was aware, your highness,” said Kip carefully. He considered mentioning the other visitor, but decided to not to until asked.

“Ah, well. Can hardly expect the man to sully his hands with actual work, eh? Don't suppose you know if Lord Conju kept the ransom note he received?” said the prince, nearly done with the quail.

“I did not ask him, your highness,” answered Kip. This was true, technically. Even if it occluded that Kip had seen not only the original but also the copy Conju had made for his Radiancy. Both of which were in Conju's papers in the library.

“Let me know if you do find it, but we can proceed without. The Lord Treasurer doesn't seem to have inspired much loyalty; his secretary and his nephew are both singing. Perhaps the Glorious One will be inclined to mercy.” He grabbed a crab next, pulling off its legs with a smile and sucking out the meat.

“Of course, your highness,” said Kip, with absolutely no intention to follow through.

Again with exaggerated movement, Ser Rhodin put away his notebook and pencil. Then he reached for an egg and began shelling it. Inside, much to Kip's surprise, was bright yellow. Possibly magic, or perhaps tedious labour. Curious, Kip took one, and found his was green and flavoured with onion.

“Will you be at court tonight, Sayo Mdang?” asked the Deputy Commander. Kip wondered what he was fishing for now.

“Yes, your highness,” said Kip, and then surreptitiously watching the other two men, added, “The Glorious One specifically requested I attend.”

There was the tiniest flick of alarm across the prince's face. Ser Rhodin only smiled politely.

“And Lord Conju?”

“Your highness, I would think it unlikely he will be recovered enough by then,” replied Kip. Did the man not _know_ Conju was sick? Why else would Kip have spent the morning with his Radiancy? Kip wasn't sure if the prince was playing an advanced game that Kip wasn't following, or if he was just stupid.

The Deputy Commander nodded. Then he picked up an oyster in each hand, and tipped them into his mouth. “They really know how to feed a man here,” he said, and then sighed. “Unfortunately, I have to run,” he said, picking up two eggs and putting them into his pocket. He shoved one of the vine-leaf wrapped parcels into his mouth, and now unable to speak, simply waved at Ser Rhodin and Kip as he let himself out.

Kip looked at Ser Rhodin, one eyebrow raised, as he nibbled at his egg.

“He thinks you're about to be appointed Lord Treasurer,” said Rhodin, selecting a crab.

Startled, Kip dropped his egg, and then watched it roll away under the table. “Ser Rhodin,” said Kip, feeling slightly nauseous, “Please don't joke about things like that. I've had a rather stressful couple of days.”

“I'm not saying you will,” said Ser Rhodin, “You'd know for sure, you write up all the proclamations and letters of appointment. But that's what he thinks.”

“I'm not even a noble,” said Kip, eyeing the oysters with suspicion, he doubted they could be really fresh, so far from the sea, “I'm not even in the upper secretariat. Lord Conju...” Kip sighed, and then looked at Ser Rhodin carefully. “Why are you here, exactly?”

Trying to swallow quickly, Rhodin said, “Butterface thought you'd talk more if your 'knight in shinning armour' was there. His words, not mine.”

“Butterface?” whispered Kip.

“That's what all the guards call him. Not when he can hear, of course, but I assume he knows about the nickname. Don't worry, the spells in here are very good.”

“_Butterface_?” said Kip, louder this time, and struggling not to giggle.

“It's a long story,” said Rhodin, “Do you have time?”

Kip grimaced. “If you're not going to drag me off to Internal Security, there's quite a bit of work that's been piling up while I was unavailable.”

“Another time,” said Rhodin, with a wink. Then he grimaced, “The story, not Internal Security.”

Smiling, Kip said, “Good, I'll hold you to that. Both of them.” He slid off the couch, and taking another piece of bread for the journey, went to find what chaos was going on in the Offices of the Lords of State.
