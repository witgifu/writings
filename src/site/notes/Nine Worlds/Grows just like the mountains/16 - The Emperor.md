When Sayo Mdang was announced instead of Lord Conju, you had to tamp down hard on my interest. He was not the only person in the Tower called into an unusual position by an unusually bad cycle of winter illness. Despite your lack of magic, you seemed to be immune. However, somewhere Conju was probably sick, and now a nervous Sayo Mdang had been sent in his place.

He did much better than the first time he had been in your official study, which was both a disappointment and a relief. We did not want anyone to *assume* we were unhappy, and remove him. It was painfully ironic that the fear of you was, entirely, created and enforced without you, and a matter in which you had little say. All you could do was control your actions and reactions, a never-ending performance of serenity.

So you made no comment, as Sayo Mdang prepared himself, and simply began dictating as if this was any other day. Twice you spotted him almost open his mouth to ask a question, before closing it again and bending his head over his work. They were precious, these glimpses of someone being human. A reminder you needed, that beyond your tower were millions more, who depended on you.

The memory of those two twitches could have powered you through the rest of your day. But then, as we were nearing the end of the allotted time, he did something no-one had dared do since your first awful master of etiquette had retired. Sayo Mdang (carefully, gently) corrected you.

I was so surprised, so thankful, I stopped and had to stare out the window to regain your composure. I could be *wrong*, like any other man, and people could tell me. What grace he had given me.

You turned, and accepted the correction. Knowing that even a slightly longer pause than usual could be read as disapproval, aware that you were always watched (and never seen). I relished 'Vangaveye-ve' on my lips, the right word, the true name. Given to me by one who knew it well and cared for it. For only someone who cared deeply, for whom it was not just identity, but soul-bond, would be so daring.

You continued, Sayo Mdang relaxed. The attendant and your guards relaxed. We had, you thought, weathered this storm. I had been given a jewel I would surely treasure for weeks, returning to this memory in the quiet of my bed, while swimming, bored at court, or perhaps even in my private study.

You should not have trusted that joy.

The reports the next day were missing Sayo Mdang's summary and organisation. You acted if nothing was out of the ordinary, and could only assume he had been dealing with more urgent matters. There had been years without it, hopefully it would return soon.

When Lord Conju appeared in the official study, you initially assumed his slightly slow movement and less-than-perfect cosmetics were simply because he had returned to work while still sick. There was nothing you could say that would help him; if you suggested he go to bed and send someone else, it would have been taken as a permanent dismissal.

All you could think of was trying to end the session early. You did your best, managing to forward a number of enquiries off to other people. We were wrapping up, when, as the Cavalier was turning the pages of a budget, a rather dirty piece of paper appeared.

He turned it, and unfolded it, with shaking fingers. You heard a sharp intake of breath. Carefully, you did nothing to show that you had noticed, but continued pacing as if you were thinking of what should be said next.

Reflected in the glass of a painting, I could see him tracing the letters. Lord Conju had always been so focused, the perfect image of the Private Secretary. He could have stepped out of the pages of the Atlas of Imperial Peoples. You contemplated the painting, and he gathered his resolve.

He could have turned the page in the report, and pretended nothing had happened. Maybe he could have slipped it off the desk into his pocket. Instead, he took a fresh sheet of paper, and obviously copied the note out. Then he put the copy under the one other document requiring your immediate seal, and set both in the tray on your desk for that purpose.

Lord Conju's bow as he returned to his desk was perfect, but for the slightest tremor in his hands. You made your way to your desk, and turned each page carefully, as you usually would. After all, it behooved you to check whatever you put your seal to.

The writing on the final page was not as fine as Lord Conju's best. But, given the content, it was understandable. The note demanded a public apology, and future support, from the Cavalier. In exchange for the safe return of Sayo Mdang. My mind raced, as I tried to think what you could do. Your hands slid over the pages, stalling for time. The gall of the Lord Treasurer putting his own name on such a thing was incredible.

It was the kind of thing you knew went on behind your back. Since you hadn't managed to wrench many of the noble privileges out of their hands, if they got caught kidnapping a commoner, the worst they could expect was a fine.

Below the demand was a note that must have come from Sayo Mdang himself. Telling Lord Conju not to give into whatever their demands were, and that he would try to break out. His courage cheered you, even as you knew there was little you could do to help. The guards, supposedly your guards, might agree to help Lord Conju to your face, but then would almost certainly quietly stand aside and let matters run their course.

Except, perhaps not. You paused, and looked towards (but not at, never at) Lord Conju. The games of fealty and patronage might be stupid, but they did have rules. As a high noble, born and educated, the Cavalier an Vilius lived and breathed those rules.

He had dared to copy this note to you for a reason.

“Do you know when Sayo Mdang was last seen yesterday?” you asked, mildly. As if any question from your lips could be anything other than deeply consequential.

Lord Conju was struggling to maintain his calm. “Glorious One, your guards informed me he left the Imperial Apartments at noon yesterday,” he said, a little of the incredible strain he must be feeling creeping into his voice. It seemed he held some genuine affection for Sayo Mdang. “I have not been able to find anyone who has seen him since, Lord of Rising Stars.”

It was a technicality, perhaps. But that was how these games of life and death and cruelty were played. And won. Cliopher sayo Mdang had spent one morning working for you directly, but that was a thread you could weave from.

“Guard Diego, we will need to speak to the Commander. It appears someone is interfering with our staff in the course of their service to us,” you said, voice calm, face serene. Now, the question was if the Imperial Guard were loyal to you, or to the Lord Treasurer.

Who possibly had no idea he had committed treason. Lord Conju looked rather like he had just been declared innocent of the same.
