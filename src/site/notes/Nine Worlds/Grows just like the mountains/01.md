“Ooh!” said a voice behind him, that Kip recognised with a twist in his stomach, “It's Madon, isn't it? Are _you_ the cute pet Ser Mikael brought along?”

Kip turned, it would be rude not to. “Lady Ullimire,” he said, with a bow that was perfectly correct. He offered nothing else.

“Oh you _are_, aren't you?” she said, looking him up and down without restraint. “Come to the library, Madon, I'm sure Lady Celia would also be keen to know your inner wild passion.” She licked her lips, in a way that Kip supposed was meant to be alluring.

Kip bowed again, “My apologies, Lady Ullimire, I am required elsewhere,” he said, without further clarification. This was, in fact, entirely a lie. Unless you counted the three other propositions he'd had walking down this hallway, which he had declined in the same way.

“Well,” she said, slightly huffy, “Come and find us when you've worn them out, hmm?” She flounced back into the library.

If Ser Mikael had _said_ the party was going to be a Fifth Imperial Decadent Style Dinner Party that ended with an orgy, Kip would probably have found an excuse not to come. Which, it had turned out, was exactly why Ser Mikael hadn't told him. He wanted Kip to be a surprise.

Not to _be_ surprised, he didn't care at all what Kip felt. It was a hard blow in a long line of them. What he had hoped might be friendships, that turned into someone just wanting to exploit him, or bribe him, or play some elaborate practical joke. Kip wasn't quite sure what this one was yet, he was hoping the latter.

Kip turned the corner and found himself back at the dining room. Where three frothy piles of skirts were giggling on one of the eating couches. By the door sat a young aristocrat who looked terribly bored.

“I was looking for the front door?” said Kip, hoping for directions.

The man gave him a questioning look, “Of someone in particular? It's oral only in here.” He added, with the air of a man much put-upon, “The ladies are betrothed.”

“Oh,” said Kip, unsure what exactly any of that meant. “Sorry, I meant the front door to the apartments. I'm feeling a bit tired.”

“You're probably better off trying to lock yourself in a cupboard,” said the man, as a squeal and a gasp escaped the pile of skirts. “Lord Igel gets mad if he finds out anyone has left one of his parties early. Bad look, you know.”

Kip hadn't known, but he could see what the man meant. He hadn't even known whose party it was until he had arrived. Finding out he'd been invited to a party hosted by the son of a prince, even as a 'plus-one', had been quite a shock. Then of course, Ser Mikael had proudly explained that he had got the invitation _because_ of Kip.

There had been a very popular production of The Seafarer King recently at the Astandalas Opera. If you couldn't get the actual cast at your party, well, the next best thing was surely a real Wide Seas Islander. Not that the Opera had, they'd painted the Shaian actor to the 'correct' colouring, and dressed him up in a costume that the papers claimed looked “exactly” like the Atlas of Imperial Peoples. Kip had his doubts about that.

When Ser Mikael had been interested in Kip's heritage, he hadn't been suspicious. Nor when Ser Mikael had asked what Kip knew of the story. Well, he would be next time. There would not be a repeat of being told to prepare himself for the mighty Aurelius Magnus, better known as Lord Igel in a copy of the operatic costume.

“You're—” began the young aristocrat, but Kip interrupted. 

“Cliopher Mdang,” said Kip, curtly, “fourth-degree secretary.” He bowed, because it had been rude to interrupt, and because the young man looked like he was probably one of the Upper Ten Thousand. The sort of person who could make his life hell if he wanted to.

The man didn't take offence, but nodded. “The Cavalier Conju enazo Argellian an Vilius,” he said politely, “Companion to Lady Lasara of Eldizorka, over there.” He waved his hand vaguely at the pile of skirts.

A tower of curls emerged from the skirts, and under it the well-bred face of a young woman with Shaian colouring and light brown eyes. “Which is why he's being such a stick,” said Lady Lasara, “Papa said he's not allowed any when he's escorting me!”

Any further comments from Lady Lasara were muffled when her friend threw the skirts back over her head. “You're not finished yet, Lasara,” came the voice of another young woman, “You're such a tease!” There was a fit of giggles from at least two voices.

The Cavalier raised his eyebrows at Kip, as if inviting him to share in his disdain for the young women. Kip didn't really mind what they did, as long as they were keeping their hands off of him.

“I don't suppose you know of a cupboard, Cavalier an Vilius?” Kip asked, trying to sound nonchalant rather than desperate.

“Not one that'll be free by now,” he said, stretching. “If you want to stay out of the way, here is probably the best place.”

Kip nodded, and took the couch next to the Cavalier.

“Ooooh, if you're staying, will you give us a show?” said one of the young ladies.

“Ah, no, sorry ladies,” said Kip, suddenly tense again. He spotted a book that had been dropped on the floor. He picked it up, flipped it open, and began reading.

“He's probably like Conju,” said Lady Lasara.

_A Modern Court Etiquette_ said the frontispiece. Kip wanted to throw the book, but he soldiered on. It was his shield, and you never know, he might learn something.

“What, a boring old stick?” said one of the other women, rather cruelly. The others giggled.

There was no reaction from the Cavalier to this statement, and Kip wondered if it was an insult he had heard many times before.

“No, silly,” said Lady Lasara, “Papa set him on me because he only likes men.”

Kip carefully did not look at the Cavalier, and pretended to be very absorbed in the first chapter of the book. It was, unsurprisingly, a list of all the Imperial titles ordered by precedence. It took a full five pages.

Part way through chapter two (Introductions), Kip stopped actually reading and started thinking about work instead. He needed to know what the death toll had been from the landslides in Voonra last year, but most of the bodies had been buried by the disaster. He was trying to piece together the information from tax and education records. But he had the feeling that if he could just cross-reference one more data source, he could get the uncertainty down below two percent.

“Sayo Mdang?” came the voice of the Cavalier. 

Kip was inordinately pleased to hear his name pronounced _almost_ correctly. He looked up from the book with a polite smile. “Yes, Cavalier an Vilius?”

“It's probably acceptable for you to leave now,” said the Cavalier, mildly, “The ladies and I will be making our farewells.”

“Conju...” whined Lady Lasara, “Why?”

“Because you have a dress fitting at eight, Lady Lasara,” said Cavalier primly, “You can bring your friends back with you if you like. But it should be lights out in an hour.”

Lady Lasara groaned, and stood up. Her two friends also emerged from their pile. “I might go back to Rudy's, sorry Lasa, she doesn't have a bedtime,” said one of them, leaning on the other. The one presumably known as Rudy, kissed the first one on the cheek.

“Conju!” snapped Lady Lasara, “You spoil everything!”

“My apologies, Lady Lasara,” said the Cavalier, with a bow that Kip was certain he did not mean at all. He then successfully herded the three young ladies in front of him and out of the room.

Kip jumped up to follow, keen not to miss this chance to be directed out of the apartments. They took a turn that Kip was sure hadn't been there before, and found the door with ease. Kip wondered to himself if Lord Igel had put some kind of illusion over the door earlier.

They exited with some decorum. Kip bowed to the others, and turned to head into the centre of the Palace. The Cavalier was trying to chide Lady Lasara towards a flight of stairs, presumably her family also had rooms in the Ystharian wing. Kip had a long walk back to the bottom of the Voonran wing.

---

Kip looked up from the rainfall records for Voonra, to find the Cavalier looking at him. Deep in the driest (or in this case, wettest) records was one of the few places in the Palace Kip could work without interruptions. His actual desk was in the Collian wing, and was placed right along a main walkway, so he tended to be interrupted by everyone and everything.

However, Kip had learnt the hard way that being grumpy when a noble interrupted you was a short step away from at best a formal reprimand. So instead of saying what he wanted, which was to tell the Cavalier to go away, Kip said, “My apologies, Cavalier an Vilius, I hope you have not been waiting long?”

“Not at all, Sayo Mdang, I only just found you,” said the Cavalier, leaning on the bookcase.

“What can I do for you, Cavalier?” asked Kip, politely.

The Cavalier gave him an assessing look, which Kip found odd. If the Cavalier had gone to all the effort of finding him in the stacks of the Archives, why did he not already know what he wanted? He had said he was a companion to a young lady, so what need did he have of a fourth-degree secretary anyway?

“There is a party I think will be more to your tastes,” said the Cavalier, and slid a piece of parchment out of his pocket, and placed it carefully atop Kip's notebook. “Tomorrow night, the Earl of Aumale claims to have a new composition by the Anonymous Master of Astandalas. Supper and music, that's all.”

“Will you be there, Cavalier an Vilius? And Lady Lasara?” asked Kip, caught quite off-guard. Before the Cavalier had a chance to answer, or take offence, Kip clarified, “That is to say, thank you very much for your consideration, Cavalier an Vilius. I would of course be most pleased to attend.”

“Not at all,” said the Cavalier, with a small smile, “I will be there, but Lady Lasara will be dinning with her family.” He bowed, and left.

Kip tried not to hope this was an overture of friendship. At best, it could be the Cavalier looking for someone he thought wouldn't chatter through a musical performance. At worst, well, he was probably looking for someone ignorant to look smart in comparison. He sighed, and put aside the invitation. 

There was something fishy about these rainfall numbers. If he could just figure out what.

---

Kip arrived at the door to the Earl of Aumale's apartments a full quarter of an hour before the indicated time. Knowing that to be early was considered a faux pas, he lurked among the statuary in the hallway. It was an unfortunate collection of 'mythical' beasts of the type that were half-and-half. Unfortunate, because rather than being frightening or alluring, they looked slightly amusing.

It was while Kip was staring at the half-dolphin, half-pigeon, that the Cavalier arrived. “I don't think the artist has ever seen a dolphin,” muttered Kip, who hadn't yet noticed anyone else was there, “Unless it is meant to be a seal?”

The Cavalier smiled, “I don't think they've seen a pigeon either, pigeon beaks curve downwards.”

Kip jumped, saw who it was, and smiled. “Good evening, Cavalier an Vilius,” he said politely, with a bow, “I see we are both early.”

The Cavalier sniffed, “The fashion for _lateness_ is one I will never understand, Sayo Mdang. I do hope the Earl is not too enamoured of it.”

Nodding, Kip said, “It is most inconvenient.”

“Shall we, then?” said the Cavalier, gesturing to the door. Kip smiled, and walked over with him.

The music was probably not by the Anonymous Master, in Kip's opinion. But the supper was good, the wine was excellent, and the Cavalier's quiet observations about the Earl's taste in music were amusing.

“Where do you think he found two violinists who both have terrible taste in cravats?” whispered the Cavalier, as they stood together by the vegetable pastries and watched the crowd.

“Possibly the same place he found a flautist who spits between movements,” replied Kip, “Not the same place he found the chef, I think.”

“Well no,” said the Cavalier, “I would imagine that was his husband, the Baronet, who found them. The Baronet doesn't care at all about music, but he _does_ have an excellent cellar.”

“He was perhaps a little too generous with the cellar then,” said Kip, eyeing his own glass, “I should not have drunk so much when I need to work tomorrow.”

The Cavalier looked at his own glass, “Hmm,” he said, “You may have a point there. Where do you live?”

“About as far down the Voonran wing as it is possible to be, in a room that is only very technically not a cellar. It's something like an hour walk,” said Kip with a sigh, and put his mostly-empty glass down. “I should probably start heading back.”

The Cavalier also put his glass down. “I'm about a twenty-minute walk further down the Ystharian wing. If you'd rather crash there?”

Kip hesitated. He didn't want to sleep in his uniform and then wear it again tomorrow. Nor did he have his writing kit with him, or any of the documents or reports he'd need. “Maybe another time, Cavalier an Vilius? I have documents I'll need in my room, and if I have to walk there anyway I may as well do so now.”

“Another time then, Sayo Mdang,” said the Cavalier, with a wry smile.

They parted by the pigeon-dolphin, with a plan to meet again in three days time.
