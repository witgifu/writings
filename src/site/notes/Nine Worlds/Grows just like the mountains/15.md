Conju sneezed violently into his breakfast pastry.

“Are you planning on attending the Emperor in this condition?” said Kip, watching the younger man consider the spray with horror.

“Of course—not—a-chOO!” said Conju, rocking in his chair with the force of the sneeze. “You'll have to do it.”

Kip stared at Conju, spoon half-way to his mouth. “You aren't going to ask the Master of Offices to send a substitute?” he asked, his voice squeaking a little.

Conju's eyes only watered a little as he blinked at Kip in surprise. “What do you think _undersecretary_ means? Aren't you the one that said it was a holding position for the next secretary candidate?”

If his hand was shaking a little as he put the spoon down, Kip thought it was justified. “I didn't think...” said Kip, eventually.

“That I would get sick?” said Conju, and sneezed again, this time thankfully into a handkerchief. “Well, I'm afraid I'm human, and sometimes I get a winter cold. What happened before when you were sick, or your boss was sick?”

Kip shrugged, “I went to work, drank a lot of coffee, and tried not to sneeze on any important documents. Everyone did. I assume the Upper Secretariat paid for cures, or could afford to take days off, I don't remember ever seeing them sick.”

“There's no cure for a cold, at least not yet. When you have it next week, Cliopher,” said Conju, and paused to blow his nose, “You will not be leaving this apartment. As I won't be today.”

“But,” said Kip, “We'll fall behind. I need to chase up the answers to last week's memos with the Department of Infrastructure, and I can't do that if I'm taking dictation.”

“They'll have to wait for next week, there are only so many hours in the day Cliopher,” said Conju.

With a sigh, Kip said, “I'd better go and put on my best uniform then.” He could have argued longer, but Conju looked like he needed a nap, and hadn't even finished breakfast.

“Thank you,” said Conju, and sniffed miserably.

Usually, Kip approached the Imperial Apartments behind Conju. Today, he was alone. As always, the guards challenged him. “Cliopher Mdang, undersecretary to his Radiancy,” he said in response, and then added, “Lord Conju is sick, so it's just me today.”

The guards passed word on, and Kip was admitted at every door. With no little fear, he kept his eyes firmly on the floor in front of him. The rooms often had petitioners hoping to catch a moment of the Emperor's time, or pass on this or that message. Both Conju and Kip had to develop a skill of politely ignoring them.

After the first messy obeisance, Conju had made him practice. So that part at least he was confident he would be able to execute adequately. When the Emperor gestured him up, he rose and made his way to Conju's desk. As quickly as he could, he set out his things, and readied his pen.

The room was beautiful, the stone lit by the sunlight, and sparkling in the light of the Emperor. With some difficulty, Kip kept his eyes on his desk, and off the many artworks placed around. Trying to channel Conju, he focused entirely on the work he needed to do right now.

Twice the Emperor mentioned someone, and Kip had to bite back a clarifying question. He maintained his determination until the last fifteen minutes of their time together.

“And the same to the Prince in the Vonyabe,” said the Lord Emperor, turning in his pacing.

“My sincere apologies, Glorious One,” said Kip, aware he could well be writing his own execution order next, “Did you mean the Vangaveye-ve, in the Wide Seas province of Zunidh?”

There was silence in the room. Kip was sure the attendant who waited at the side and both guards were suppressing horror at what he had done. But there was only one voice that mattered in the room, only one opinion. He dared to hope that after many days of seeing the work that man did, the turn of his mind, the questions he sought answers to, that maybe, he would rather be corrected.

For a moment, Kip wavered, and considered throwing himself on the floor and begging for forgiveness. But then, the Emperor turned again.

“Yes,” he said, “The Vangaveye-ve,” carefully copying Kip's pronunciation. Then he continued with the next paragraph. Slowly, Kip let out the breath he had been holding, and tried to focus on his work.

As he left, he was partly still thinking how relieved he was that the Lord Emperor had taken no offence. The rest of his mind was occupied with trying to work out how to finish all the work he needed to do before the next morning. It would certainly be a late night, so he decided to begin with the longest walk first, and took a less popular rout than the main staircase to hurry down to the archives.

Near the front door to those endless rooms, one of the Imperial guards stopped him. That wasn't entirely unusual, he had just come from a service corridor and wasn't wearing the uniform of either the Palace staff or the general Service. It didn't *have* to be about his behaviour in the Presence.

"Sayo Mdang?” said the guard, and his voice was professionally bland, not stern. Kip's heart only skipped one beat.

“Yes, that is me,” said Kip, “How can I be of assistance?” He had learnt early on in Astandalas that excessive formality with the guards usually did better for him than the joviality or sarcasm that were his instincts.

“I'm looking into a letter from the Glorious One that seems to have been misplaced, and I was hoping you might give an account of it,” said the guard.

Kip supposed this must be about something that previous secretary to the Emperor had misplaced. With as small bow, Kip said, “I will of course assist as I can with your investigation.” This was going to take even _more_ time out of his day. Furiously trying to think how he was going to see any of the people he was actually _supposed_ to, he didn't notice another person come up behind him, just heard the snap of a cantrip.

“Thank you,” said the guard, and that was the last thing Kip remembered hearing for some time.

---

Kip woke in what was unmistakably a dungeon. Possibly an oubliette, given that the only light seemed to be coming from a grate in the ceiling. Although it was so dark, there might be a black door in one of the walls. He was slightly surprised he warranted such treatment. Commoners, in his experience, were dragged off to Internal Security directly, and then disposed of, one way or another. Cells were for people who might get trials, or at least a hearing.

Interestingly, he wasn't bound. Still in his uniform, even with his sandals. That seemed generous, when the straps could easily be turned into a garrotte. They _had_ taken his writing kit, so at least they had realised that a prisoner shouldn't be left with a knife. Surely the Imperial Guard were more professional than this? Yet, he clearly remembered talking to a guard, and if it had been, say, a disgruntled duellist, surely they wouldn't have the means to shut him in a dungeon of the Palace of Stars.

As he lay on the stone platform, it became clear to him that there was _one_ person who did, in fact, have both the motive and the resources. Someone who might consider that Kip had been the cause of his public shame, and might think to take that out on a commoner. If the Lord Treasurer had the means and the cause though, surely he would have just had Kip beaten? Or murdered in some grisly way? Why hold him? He simply didn't have enough information.

With Conju sick, and Kip missing, the Master of Offices was going to have to find someone else to substitute tomorrow. Kip hoped they wouldn't be _too_ incompetent, and Conju wouldn't have quite so much work to fix when he was able to attend upon the Lord Emperor again. If his captors had taken his writing kit, maybe they would have the presence of mind to send it to Conju, so he could perhaps do something with the notes Kip had made this morning.

Some time later, it was hard to tell with no natural light and almost no sounds apart from the gurgle of a pipe somewhere above him, footsteps approached. They approached his own level, rather than the grate above, to Kip's relief.

“I'm going to give you a paper and pencil,” said an aristocratic voice, “And you are going to write what I tell you.”

“No,” said Kip, sitting up.

“No?!” said the voice, clearly not expecting to be disagreed with, “I don't think you're in *any* position to bargain, Sayo Mdang. You will do as you are told, or we will kill you.”

“You would have killed me already, if you didn't need me alive,” said Kip, “But anyway, there's not enough light in here to write by, and I'm obviously not just going to write whatever you tell me to. Some confession to crimes I don't even know about, or an attempt to forge my work, no doubt. And I'm definitely not going to write in _pencil_ so you can just change whatever you don't like.”

There was a silence. Eventually, the footsteps faded away again. Kip tried to remember if he could recall who that voice was. It didn't sound like the guard, or the Lord Treasurer. So perhaps there were at least three people involved in whatever this mad plot was. Three people who weren't used to threatening someone. They could have broken his legs, or his left arm, or many other things Internal Security knew how to do that didn't actually kill you.

Did that mean they didn't even want to *hurt* him? How bizarre. 

Some further time later, Kip began exploring the cell with his hands. The light only fell in the middle, onto the floor that looked to be the bedrock the Palace of Stars was built on. The walls were also stone, his fingertips found, hewn rather than built, on three sides. Where the voice had come from, it was built up, and indeed there was an iron door there. He thought there might be a smaller sliding door built into the bottom of it, presumably for more easily passing food to prisoners. 

The only other features were a wooden bucket, the stone platform he had woken on, and an iron ring on one wall. Kip hoped they wouldn't decide to chain him to that, with his arms above his head. A slow death from suffocation and exhaustion usually followed.

The footsteps eventually returned. “I am going to give you a brush, ink, and paper. Write whatever you like to _Lord_ Conju, to tell him you are alive. If you do so, we will feed you.” A small bell rang, and the light in the cell brightened suddenly.

Kip blinked, trying to clear his vision. He heard, rather than saw, the little door slide open. Something was pushed through it, and then it was abruptly shut.

“Good to see you're learning,” said Kip, smiling, “I assume you don't kidnap people very often.”

There was no response to this statement, but Kip hadn't really expected one. He was mostly just trying to annoy whoever it was for his own amusement. They needed him alive, and probably in reasonable condition, so he could get away with it. It felt good, not having to scrape around an aristos ego, and just say what he felt like.

Picking up the decrepit ink brush, he tried to shape it into a decent tip by pulling out a few hairs that were bent beyond rescue. Then he licked it into shape before looking at the little bowl of ink. It was a miserly amount for a decent letter, but he supposed it was enough for a little proof-of-life note.

“What's your name?” said Kip, conversationally, “I wouldn't want to leave off the usual greetings complementing my hosts.”

No response. Kip shrugged, and wrote in shorthand. Then he blew on and waved the paper to dry the ink. “Does it pay well, being a henchman?” said Kip, “Or is it something you do for the way it attracts the ladies. Or the gentlemen.”

“Are you finished?” said the voice from outside, “Or does your love-letter need another post-script?”

They were going to have to do better than implying Conju and Kip were _lovers_ to rile him up. He'd heard that complaint from many people in the Service, since he'd moved into Conju's apartment. It didn't really bother him, it wasn't like he was going to be dating any of those people, so their opinion on his living arrangements really didn't matter.

“Oh, do you have any tips?” asked Kip, “I'm sure you have a lot of experience keeping a patron *on side*.”

There was a sharply indrawn breath, and a slow exhalation. Something had landed. Kip smiled, and said, “I'm done. Open the slot and I'll push it out.”

The door grated again, and as he had promised, Kip pushed the note out. But he also took the chance to see what he could of the person and the space beyond. As he expected, he saw the fine leather slippers and silk hems of a high aristocrat. Turned hems, he noticed, having had Conju explain carefully how to spot such details, and what they meant. As soon as Kip's note was outside, the slot was slammed closed, very nearly catching Kip's hand. But he _did_ spot a glimpse of the other person's hand—dark and tattooed. A few rings, but they looked old and a little worn. So, if this person _was_ the one responsible, he might just want money.

Somewhere there must be a lackey who had brought him here, Kip couldn't imagine those beautiful slippers dragging his limp form through the Palace. The space outside had looked like a hallway, and the only other thing Kip noticed was that the floor was slightly higher, and so the door to Kip's cell would have to open inward.

“What!” said the voice, “What kind of scribble is this? It's illegible! I thought you were a secretary, aren't you supposed to have _good_ handwriting? Or are you really *just* there to bed the Cavalier?”

Kip barked out a laugh. “It's scribal shorthand,” he said, “Lord Conju will be able to read it perfectly fine.”

The voice huffed, and grumbled, but eventually left. Kip didn't bother to point out that he'd been left with the light in the room still bright, the brush, and the ink bowl. Presumably his captors thought they were harmless. A little cheap pottery dish, smaller than his palm. A wooden stick, with an old lump of squirrel hair stuck to it. Enough light to work by. Kip smiled, as he tried to decide how best to make use of them. He wouldn't even need to chip his efela ko.
