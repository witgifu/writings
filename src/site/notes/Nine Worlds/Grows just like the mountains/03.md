The letter to Ghilly was the most painful thing Kip had ever written. It took five drafts to get something even moderately coherent. At one point he seriously considered just writing “sorry” over and over again until he filled up the page.

After seven drafts he had to take a break. The headache from crying was making it hard to see. He collected his writing kit, and his drafts, and headed for one of the refectories in the centre of the Palace.

The long walk helped, even if he felt like the afternoon sun had some kind of personal enmity with him. It did feel a bit better to know he was doing the right thing by Ghilly. It would be better to tell her in person than via letter, of course, but since the round trip home would probably take two years, that was out of the question.

In the refectory, was Conju. He had a single steamed bun in front of him, which he was staring at it like a junior wizard trying a new cantrip. Kip collected a cup of coffee and sat next to him.

“Was it bad?” asked Kip, sipping his coffee.

Conju looked up, startled. “Oh, good afternoon, Cliopher,” he said, seemingly automatically. “Yes, I mean, no, it was fine.” He resumed staring at the bun.

Kip went back to the serving hatches, and collected a cup of tea and another two buns. He put the tea and one bun in front of Conju.

“You've probably stared that bun to death,” said Kip, “Try eating this one.”

He nibbled at his own, while he watched his friend (they were surely friends now) shred the new bun, and continue staring at the old one. When Conju showed no sign of touching the tea either, Kip decided further intervention was required. Conju had clearly been shaken by whatever had happened, which was impressive for a man so clearly comfortable with court.

Kip pushed a blank sheet of paper and a red pencil over to Conju. “Write down the positions you were told you might be able to get. When you're done, I'll write a précis for each, and you can edit my letter to Ghilly.”

Conju looked at Kip, and put down the bun. Silently, he picked up the pencil, and wrote three lines before shoving it back to Kip. Equally silently, Kip handed Conju the last draft of his letter, before looking at the list of positions.

Normally, Conju's hand was elegant and formal. These red words were scribbled in the kind of handwriting that Kip imagined noble tutors tended to beat out of their students. He bit his lip.

> Secretary, Master of Offices
> Undersecretary for Cultural Works, Treasury
> Private Secretary, Emperor's Household

Kip had a suspicion for which had rattled Conju so. But he had offered three reports, and so he started at the top. 

---
##### Secretary, Master of Offices

* Known to be lazy
* Unwilling to adapt anything new until forced
* Often takes bribes of all kinds
* If he likes you, opens up nearly any other appointment in the Service
* Usually has 3–4 secretaries he pits against each other (see previous point) and they do everything for him (see first point)
* Manages the administration of the Service exam, the Pages, and general staff appointments
	* If put in charge of the Pages, could be managing a lot of people
* Unless you mean his _personal_ secretary, who handles his diary and dictation
	* They get yelled at by everyone angry with the Master of Offices (see first and second points)
	* Would not recommend

##### Undersecretary for Cultural Works, Treasury

* Reports to the Secretary for Tithes, reports to the Lord Treasurer
* Lots of liaising with Palace staff
	* Loaning of objects for exhibitions
	* Maintenance and storage of objects
	* Schedule and planning of presentations, e.g. music, dance
* One or two direct reports, most other work handled by Palace (see above) or Imperial Guard
	* This has created some conflict in the past due to confusing management lines
* Secretary for Tithes
	* Known for _not_ taking bribes
	* Rumours of embezzlement
	* Otherwise seems competent
* Lord Treasurer
	* One of the last remaining appointments by Eritanyr

##### Private Secretary to the Emperor

* Appointed by the Master of Offices, fired by the Emperor
* Handles correspondence, dictation, diary of private audiences
* Sometimes has one report
	* Presumably also appointed by Master of Offices
	* Seems to be a holding position for the next private secretary
* Usually last about six months
	* Unsure if this is just because Master of Offices is an idiot
	* Most of them have left the Service
* Position usually needs filling _urgently_ when open
* Emperor not known for taking bribes or embezzlement
	* How would the Emperor even embezzle? You can't steal from yourself.
	* _Artorin the Good_ generally agreed on — but information is third-hand

---

By the time Kip had finished, Conju had covered the first page of his letter with red pencil, and was well onto the second. He was also pleased to note that some of the second bun seemed to have actually been eaten, and the teacup was empty.

Kip got fresh cups of tea and coffee, and watched Conju work. He would sit perfectly still, the pencil pointed at the first word of the current sentence. Then, like a cat pouncing, he would strike out one or two words. Another pause, and he would insert a word, in his usual elegant hand. Finally, he would add a margin note, or arrows indicating to swap something.

Conju finished the letter, and put the pencil down precisely parallel with the page. Then he looked up at Kip, and nodded. “Thank you for the tea,” he said, politely, and took a sip.

“Are you ready to swap back?” asked Kip, gently, “Was there anything you wanted to tell me about your day first?”

“Not yet,” said Conju, and took Kip's notes while returning the letter.

Kip looked around the mostly empty refectory. Three o'clock was too late for lunch, and far too early for dinner. It was mostly being used as an impromptu meeting room, except for a few guards who seemed to be holding a book club.

Still wondering what had spooked Conju so badly, Kip examined the letter. The entire right margin just had “Passive voice, consider revising”, and a series of arrows to various sentences. Kip grimaced. In an attempt to actually _say_ something, he'd reverted to writing about his own actions as if he was writing a work report.

He was about to pull out a fresh sheet and start a new draft, but he stopped himself and looked up at Conju. The other man had put down the page of notes Kip had made, and started shredding his original bun.

Kip reached out a hand, and put it gently on top of Conju's, pushing the bun back down to the plate. Conju didn't flinch, but Kip heard his breath catch.

“If you're going to have a panic attack, we should probably head to the infirmary, or your room,” said Kip gently.

Conju gave him a completely blank look.

“Okay then,” said Kip, “Let's go back to your room.” He picked up all the papers, putting them in his writing kit. Then he tugged at Conju's elbow, and directed him out of the refectory.

“I'm not sure what the best route is from here,” said Kip, “How about you lead, and I'll tell you about the time Basil and I tried to sneak into a hotel and steal a giant shell.”

Conju nodded slightly, and began walking towards the Ystharian wing. As they walked, and Kip talked, Conju lost some of the 'stunned fish' look. By the time they reached Conju's room, he was smiling very slightly at Kip's description of the hotelier's rant.

Kip gently led Conju over to the sofa, and they both sat there. Trying desperately to remember everything Uncle Lazo or Buru Tovo had ever said or shown him about dealing with people coping with trauma, Kip took a deep breath.

“You don't have to take any of these positions, Conju,” he said, trying to project calm, “None of the people you met today know you, and whatever they said or did, you are worthy of love and respect.”

Conju took a series of too-short and too-shallow breaths. Kip grabbed his hand, rubbing his palm. “You're safe in your room, Conju, with me, Cliopher,” he said gently. “Look around, what are five things you see?”

“Chair… window… table… rug… Cliopher,” said Conju, faintly.

“What are four things you can feel?”

“Sofa… hand… robes… cushion.” Conju sounded a bit more confident.

“Three things you can hear?”

“You, that damn tap dripping, the wind.”

“Two things you can smell?”

“Bergamot and lavender,” said Conju with confidence.

Kip smiled at him, “What can you taste?”

“Ugh, over-brewed refectory tea,” said Conju, and licked his lips, “Why are they so cruel to good tea?”

“I don't know,” said Kip, “They do the same thing to the coffee, if it's any comfort.”

Conju sighed, and then took a deep breath. “I will not be taking a position in the Treasury,” he said, in his most clipped, and formal voice.

Nodding, Kip pulled out the sheet of notes he had made for Conju, and crossed out the Treasury section vigorously. “Noted,” he said, “It will not be raised again.”

“You would recommend against the position with the Master of Offices, Sayo Mdang?” said Conju, still using formality like armour.

Playing along, Kip replied, “It requires a particular temperament, Cavalier an Vilius. If you were aiming for high office, it could be a step along the way, my lord. However, there are other paths that might be more suitable to your talents. There will be other opportunities in future, I am sure.”

Conju raised one elegant eyebrow at the honorifics, but didn't correct Kip. “Which leaves us with… the poisoned chalice?”

“Say rather, the option where there is the least information. Where I have been forced to draw most from rumour and hearsay, Cavalier.”

“So you would take it, if it were offered to you, Cliopher?” said Conju, dropping the formality suddenly.

Kip laughed, “As if it would ever be offered to me! Only if the Master of Offices ran through every other noble in the Palace who could read and write.”

“Which is daft. I haven't written shorthand since I sat the exam. You'd have to teach me how to take dictation, I've only ever done it for my sister as practice. The relevant skills I have are organising a schedule, and declining invitations politely,” said Conju, his tone light but his words bitter.

“You don't have to take it, Conju,” said Kip, carefully hiding his jealousy, “Although if you wanted to go out in a blaze of glory, it would be the place to do it.”

“Yes, that is definitely the way to have my family welcome me with open arms,” said Conju, dryly.

“They wouldn't approve of you joining the Service?” asked Kip, surprised. Most noble families thought it was an excellent place to shove otherwise superfluous children. “I thought your mother encouraged you to take the exam?”

“They'd have no problem with me joining the Service, it would probably be a relief. No, they would be worried about me being so close to the Emperor.” 

Kip didn't ask why, but he left a gentle silence that seemed to hold the question anyway.

“My father was friends with Eritanyr, back when he was a Prince,” clarified Conju.

“Ah,” said Kip, a few things sliding into place, “My great-uncle met Prince Eritanyr as well. That was one of the reasons the rest of my family didn't want me to come here. Interestingly, my great-uncle was fine with me leaving.”

Kip lay a gentle hand on Conju's knee. “Emperor Artorin is not Eritanyr. Nobody would ever have called Eritanyr _the Good_. I'm sure your parents know.”

Conju nodded, and looked up and around the room. He took a slow, careful breath, and then said, “Liliane? Are you here? Have you heard the whole conversation?”

A door slid open, and Conju's valet stepped out. “Yes, my lord. And most of last night,” she didn't look at all abashed about this.

“Do you think I should take it?” said Conju, seriously.

“It's not my position to say, my lord,” said Liliane, “But I think your lady mother would approve.”

Conju rolled his eyes. “Do you think *mother* would mind if we moved into the family suite?”

“I think your lady mother would be happy to see you maintaining the family suite for your sister's occasional visits, my lord,” said Liliane, “I am sure your salary would very easily cover the additional costs, so she should have no concerns there.”

“Thank you, Liliane,” said Conju, “I'll think about it and let you know.”

Liliane bowed, and slid the door closed again.

Frowning slightly, Kip tried to understand the exchange. He wasn't sure if Conju had been asking about his mother's opinions, or Liliane's. Also, he was mildly disturbed that she had been listening to his own confessions about his relationship and work troubles.

Conju drummed his fingers on the arm of the sofa. Then he crossed and uncrossed his ankles. Finally, he stood up and walked around the room. From behind a sliding door (which turned out to hide a cupboard) he fetched a workbasket. He sat back down, opposite Kip this time, on one of the floor chairs.

From the workbasket, he pulled out an embroidery hoop. His needle and the green silk thread flashed in the light as he started stitching.

“Cliopher,” said Conju, not looking up from his work, “Cliopher...” His words trailed off, but his hands kept moving.

“Yes, Conju?” said Kip, with a smile. It was nice to see this artistic side of his friend.

“Will you send that letter?” said Conju, tensely.

“Of course,” said Kip, “I promised I would.”

Conju nodded, and smoothed out his stitches with a beautiful silver tool. “I want to… suggest an arrangement. But I don't want you to think that I had any kind of ulterior motive in telling you to break it off with Ghilly. And I don't want you to feel trapped, or forced into agreeing, or that I would try to undermine you if you said no. If you say no, or if you want out at any time, that's fine. And it's not all or nothing, we could just be friends, but I want you to know how I feel.”

Conju took a moment for a breath, and Kip got the chance to interrupt without feeling rude. “Conju, please, I have no idea what you're hinting at. Please just say it directly.”

Startled, Conju knocked the thimble off his finger, and had to chase it under the table. By the time he emerged again, Kip could see he was blushing.

“The only way I could take this position and not get thrown out of the Imperial Study on the first day, is if you help me,” began Conju, holding the thimble tightly, “And since I would not want to presume on a friendship of a few months like that, and anyway it would be entirely wrong to do so, I would ensure you received the undersecretary position.”

Kip's eyes went wide, but he didn't dare interrupt.

“The common assumption then would be that I was your noble patron, as you explained,” said Conju.

But now Kip couldn't help interrupt, “Well, you _would_ be,” he said, fiercely.

Conju winced slightly, “Which then means I have to make sure you have,” and here Conju paused to wave vaguely around the room, “Accoutrement appropriate to your station and mine. And since space in the Palace, you might have noticed, is both often empty and rather overcrowded, I would imagine that even a room like this one would be beyond my means to give you.”

“I don't need—my current room is _fine_, Conju!” protested Kip.

With a firm shake of his head, Conju said, “No, it wouldn't do. It would reflect poorly on me, which would reflect poorly on my family and the Emperor.”

Kip looked ready to argue further, but Conju held up a hand. “This is one of those court culture things that you're just going to have to accept, Cliopher. I don't make the rules, but I do have to live by them.”

Rolling his eyes, Kip said, “Alright then.”

“So,” said Conju, and picked up his embroidery again, “I would have to move into my family suite, and give you a room there. Which everyone will assume means we are lovers.”

Kip chewed his lip. Conju was correct, that would be the assumption. That was, in some sense, the only kind of noble patronage Kip had been offered before. Although, in this case Kip thought it was unlikely, he hoped it was unlikely, that he would be turning up to work with strategically placed makeup and a conspicuously full schedule.

“I'd rather that than the current assumption that since I'm a 'barbarian' I must be up for anything with anyone,” said Kip, not hiding how much that hurt.

“Perverts,” said Conju, scathingly. Then he said, “But, be that as it may, all the prior comes as, they might say in the market, a bundle deal.” He put down his embroidery, and spread his perfectly manicured fingers across the table. “_If_ you want to try being lovers — and it is entirely optional Cliopher, I cannot stress that enough — then I would be open to that. Or not. Or just seeing if living in close quarters drives us both insane.”

“Alright,” said Kip, feeling a little like his world was turning upside down, “Well, maybe we can deal with that later? First let's see if you can get the appointment. There's no point doing anything if you don't survive the first day.”

“Oh gods,” said Conju, leaning back, “I'm going to need a uniform, which I'm not going to have any time to tailor, because I'm going to have to spend all night practicing.”

“First you have to go back to the Master of Offices and accept the position, and find out when the start date actually is. You could be lucky, maybe the Emperor is on holiday,” said Kip, with a smile.

“The Emperor,” said Conju firmly, “Does not take _holidays_.”
