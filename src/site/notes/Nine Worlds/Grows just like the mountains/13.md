Conju fell artfully into an armchair, his eyes glinting. “I would call that a success, Sayo Mdang,” he announced, as he accepted a glass of lemon water from Liliane.

“Well, _Lord Conju_, we were certainly _seen_ by a good many people. I think I shall treasure the look on the Education Secretary's face for a long time,” said Kip, sipping from his own glass. He was tired, but still too energised by the sound and light of court to head to bed.

“When he looked at you with the worst fake smile I have ever seen? He looked like he'd been poked in the buttocks with a pike,” said Conju, smiling much more convincingly. “By the way, I have a potential target and co-conspirator for your plan to end duelling.”

“Who? When?” asked Kip, sitting up straight, “I didn't see you talking to anyone without me.”

“I didn't talk to them, I saw them,” said Conju, waving a hand, “I need to find the name of the suave swordsman—”

“You don't even know his name!” interrupted Kip, with a laugh, “And it's not a _conspiracy_, it's a schedule for legislative reform. And, and! What do you mean, by 'target'?”

“If you will let me get a word in, Cliopher, I will explain,” said Conju, looking suddenly serious.

Kip slipped back down into the chair, waving for Conju to continue.

“We need to give the Emperor an excuse to want to ban duelling. The _easiest_ way to do that, would be to have a senior noble kill another in a duel, accidentally or deliberately.”

Kip wanted to ask so many questions. Instead, he took a sip of his drink.

“That is probably not a good idea, all things considered,” said Conju, in what Kip thought was in the running to be one of his strongest examples of understatement. “But we might be able to get someone angry enough to _threaten_ to have one. So we need a hothead who thinks they are a much better fighter than they actually are, and an obviously much better fighter who would be willing to appeal the matter before the Emperor.”

When it became clear to Kip that Conju wasn't going to say anything more, he asked, “Why would a pet duellist agree to that? If our plan goes ahead they would be out of a job.”

“Mm, no, not one that *likes* the job,” said Conju, “Do you think we could get them a position in the Imperial Guard?”

“The Imperial Guard do actual work, you know,” said Kip, with a chortle, “Why would anyone leave a cushy job occasionally smacking stuck-up lordlings for one with regular hours?”

“For a start, it has regular hours,” said Conju, counting on his fingers, “It has standardised pay, which nobody tries to short you on by paying you in wine instead. It's much better respected. If you do fight, it would be with actual criminals.”

“In theory,” muttered Kip.

“Oh,” said Conju, quietly.

“They hand people over to Internal Security. I used to work next door to one of the 'workrooms'. I could hear the people begging as they were dragged along the hallway,” said Kip, blandly. It hadn't stopped when they had been handed over. He had gotten used to it, the sounds eventually blending into the background noise, like when he worked next to the carpenters. Or so he had thought, until he moved offices again, and started being able to sleep properly.

“Noted. I will add Internal Security to the List,” said Conju, firmly.

“The List?” asked Kip, curious.

“Of irredeemable institutions. Along with the Offices of State, and whoever sets the Service uniforms,” said Conju, as if this should be obvious, “I have yet to decide about the Ouranatha. I like working toilets, but I dislike the way they mix magic, power, and religion.”

They sat in silence for a while, Kip enjoying Conju's casual treason. “At least the guard actually do merit-based recruitment,” he said, eventually, “Although by the looks of those in the Imperial Apartments, the Inner Guard is limited by rank. Would a pet duellist actually be able to get in? They use spears not swords.”

“Of course they would be able to,” said Conju, “The martial arts follow a standard course of study. One of my sisters went all the way. She is in the Army now.”

“Are you saying you know how to fence, Conju?” asked Kip, stunned.

“Heavens no,” said Conju, “I can shoot just enough to satisfy my mother, and not enough that my sisters will drag me out hunting.”

It was some days before Conju managed to collect the name of the man he thought might be willing to play 'bait'. (Conju objected to this term, on the grounds they weren't trying to catch anyone, Kip persisted in using it.) Eventually, Conju secured a meeting with him. Kip stayed out of it, arguing that if the man disagreed with the idea, he could take it out on Kip.

So it was that Kip found himself waiting one evening, for Conju to return with news. Liliane kept dropping in far more regularly than was usual, Kip suspected she was slightly nervous that a date with a swordsman might not go so well for her Cavalier. He quietly accepted the excessive cups of herbal tea without comment.

They both jumped when they heard the sound of the front door opening, Liliane rushed out. Kip waited, nervous when he realised there were three voices in the hall. Conju, Liliane, and a man who was presumably the one Conju had gone to meet. Why had Conju brought him back? Well, there was one _obvious_ reason Conju might have brought him back, but when they came into the parlour instead of continuing to Conju's bedroom, he dismissed it.

Kip stood up, as they turned towards him. He thought he spotted a flash of anxiety cross Conju's face, but then the usual polite smile returned.

“Cliopher, this is Ser Rhodin; Ser Rhodin, Cliopher sayo Mdang, undersecretary to the Emperor,” said Conju.

Kip immediately detected what the anxiety had been about. Did a commoner in the Imperial Household outrank a minor aristocrat in the employ of another household? He was glad it was Conju who had made the call and not him. That was the kind of etiquette that gave him a headache and reminded him far too much of his failures at the Service Exams.

Ser Rhodin didn't seem to take offence. Rather than Conju's elegance, he had an unaffected flamboyance. He was tall, as Kip expected a court duellist to be, and had the sort of constant awareness of his own poise that Kip usually associated with performers.

“I understand you're the intelligence of this operation, Sayo Mdang?” said Ser Rhodin.

“I'm drafting a recommendation to change the Palace rules, if that's what you mean,” said Kip, and looked at Conju for an explanation.

“He said he was willing to flirt, and appeal to the Emperor,” said Conju, with a small shrug.

“And Lord Conju kindly suggested I should apply to the guard now, before they get swamped with applicants,” said Ser Rhodin, “Although many of my… current coworkers… are fortune hunters and so will probably try to convince their lords to keep them around as courtiers or personal guards.”

“What are you looking for, Ser Rhodin?” asked Kip, curious, since he seemed to be denying an interest in marrying for money.

“Something interesting to do,” Ser Rhodin replied, “Mystery, secrets, adventure.”

Kip looked at him slightly bemused. The Palace of Stars, the very heart of the Empire, hardly seemed the place for mystery and adventure. Secrets, for sure, but mostly because of the vast amount of embezzlement, grift, and blackmail going on. Neither, for that matter, did the Imperial Guard seem like the place to find those things. The army and navy travelled the nine worlds. The Imperial Guards hung around the Palace looking well-muscled.

“Is flirting with the Lord Treasurer's lover enough adventure for you?” asked Conju, casually.

Ser Rhodin winced, “I'd rather not have a whipping on my conscience, thanks. His lordship is very much of the 'it takes two' mindset.”

Conju gave Ser Rhodin a long look. “That *girl* — and yes I know she is seventeen, but she's there because the Treasurer is blackmailing her parents — needs someone who will stand up for her.”

“That's different then,” said Ser Rhodin, “Do you know what they're being blackmailed over? If we can sort that out and get her out, make it look like me, that sounds like even more fun than flirting.”

“Conju,” said Kip, suddenly worried, “You said nothing about a teenager who doesn't even know what we're doing. If the Emperor blames us, or Ser Rhodin, and protects his Lord Treasurer, this seventeen-year-old could be in a worse position than she is now.”

“Do you see any reason, in anything that has gone through your hands, to doubt his Radiancy?” asked Conju, eyes wide and voice challenging.

“I *choose* to stay,” said Kip quietly, “But I have a choice. I know the risks. I don't even know this young woman’s name, and I will not choose for her.”

“Lady Meryt,” said Conju carefully, “I met her, when she was younger. She wanted to go to university and study agriculture.”

Ser Rhodin held up a hand, “I'm not proposing we kidnap her, Sayo Mdang. We find the evidence her family are being blackmailed with, and work out how to destroy it. Then we *ask* her to dramatically run away in a way that lays the blame on me. When the Treasurer challenges me, I petition the Emperor to intervene, because I don't want to cause chaos by accidentally killing him. The intervention I propose is whatever you give me.”

He looked between Conju and Kip. They both nodded. “Worse comes to worst, I always could kill the Treasurer,” he said lightly, and relaxed into his chair.

Kip put his head in his hands. “*Please* don't,” he groaned.

Liliane placed a fresh pot of herbal tea down with a just-audible tap.

“Yes, Liliane?” said Conju, turning to her.

“Oh, nothing my lord,” she said quickly, and Conju raised his eyebrows disbelievingly.

“Well, my lord, I could likely get a verbal message to Lady Meryt,” she said carefully, standing perfectly straight, “Her maid is a passing acquaintance. I think it would be unwise to trust to a written one.”

Ser Rhodin looked at the teapot, carefully not at Liliane. “Do you happen to know what her family is being blackmailed over?”

“I couldn't say for sure, my lord,” she said, diffidently.

“But you could guess?” asked Conju, gently.

She bowed, before answering, “The gossip is that Lady Meryt's family makes unwise investments, it may be related to that.”

“It doesn't even have to be real,” said Ser Rhodin, “It just has to be believable, a good forged letter from someone claiming they were scammed could be just as bad as a genuine one.”

“And since the Lord Treasurer is in the position to _know_, people will assume it's true,” said Kip, “Alright. Let's work out the details.”

---

Lady Meryt, it turned out, was very receptive to the plan. Perhaps a slight tendency toward adventure novels had played a part, but she was intensely aware of the risks she was taking. She passed back to them where the records in question were held, and who was in charge of them.

So it was that Kip found himself in what Ser Rhodin insisted was a service corridor. With Lady Meryt, her maid, and a surprisingly heavy carpet bag.

“Ser Rhodin has arranged to meet us at the gate with transport out of the Palace,” said Kip, looking with concern at the quantity of dust in the hallway. “He will give you a letter of introduction for his friend, who will meet you at The Bee at the Border in Alinor. It's run by my cousin's parents-in-law, lovely people.”

Lady Meryt positively bounced along, her maid looking rather more worried. “And then I get to go to _Tara_,” the young lady said with a grin, “And study! Whatever I like! Sayo Mdang, someday I'll write a book about this adventure.”

Kip smiled at her enthusiasm. “If you could wait until I retire, my lady, I would appreciate it. I'm not sure that it will enhance my career.”

She laughed. “Oh of course,” she said, “My tell-all biography will have to wait until I retire from being a famous academic and speaker. Then I shall shock the whole nine worlds.”
