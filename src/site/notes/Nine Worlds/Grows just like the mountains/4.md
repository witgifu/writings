Conju hurried off to the Master of Offices, and Kip went back to his room to pack at least an overnight bag. If he was going to be helping Conju practice late into the night, they both agreed it would only be fair if Kip slept over again. So Kip looked at his towel and tried to work out how to pack enough that he didn't miss anything, but not so much that he looked like he was already moving in.

In the end, he decided it really only was one night. He could always come back tomorrow evening, in fact he probably would. Pulling open his cupboard, he told himself that Conju _already had mugs_, and he would have no time to play oboe tonight. He tried to pack sensibly. A clean uniform, folded so it wouldn't crease too badly. Clean underwear, a toothbrush, a clean nightshirt, a comb. All the things he needed for work. And finally, his copy of the Service Regulations. Just in case they needed to reference anything.

The walk back was long enough that Kip started to think that there was no way this madcap plan of Conju's could work. Firstly, the Master of Offices surely _knew_ that Conju had very few of the required skills, and expected him to fail. Thus making an easy excuse for the Master of Offices to clear out yet another troublesome useless noble.

Next, even if by some miracle Conju did survive the first day, there was no way he'd get a permanent appointment immediately. While waiting, it would become clear what trick they were trying to pull on everyone. They would honestly be lucky to walk away with their heads still attached. If, by some miracle, Conju did manage to get the job first try, well. It's not like he would be immediately allowed to appoint Kip. So Kip wouldn't be able to do much to help him. He would only have his off hours and spare time. He'd be trying to do _two_ jobs, and while he _was_ pretty skilled and quite efficient, even he needed to sleep sometimes.

Finally, even if everything went exactly to plan, eventually whatever mysterious problem that struck every Private Secretary would come for Conju. The Cavalier Conju an Vilius would flounce back to his family estate and tell grand tales about his adventures in the Palace. Kip would go home, probably. Or get sent back to the pool of unattached Lower Secretariat members filling in on projects.

It was in this decidedly sombre frame of mind that he arrived back at Conju's room.

Conju, having a much shorter task and walk, had arrived back first. He was sitting on a floor-chair, staring at the sliding door opposite. His embroidery sat untouched in the workbasket next to him, and a steaming cup of tea was also being ignored.

“Hello?” said Kip, tentatively, “Did it go well?”

“Tomorrow, nine o'clock,” said Conju, completely flat.

Liliane emerged, and traded Kip a cup of coffee for his bag. Kip sat down at the table, next to Conju. He took a long sniff of the coffee, which was much better than the usual Palace fare.

“What level are you?” said Kip, taking a sip. It tasted good too.

Conju pulled out a little booklet, and handed it to Kip. It was a standard issue Imperial Bureaucratic Service pay-book, black cardboard cover with gold lettering stamped on the front. Kip opened it to the first page, where it had Conju's name filled in.

“Upper Secretariat, Level 1, Pay Band 1,” read Kip. “Well, that excuses you from the uniform requirements. You only have the dress code. And you'll probably get an automatic bump to Pay Band 2 if you get the appointment permanently.”

Kip tried not to feel sad about how easily Conju had gotten such a senior position. A position and level which, no matter how hard Kip worked, he would never be given, simply because of his birth. Conju would never have asked if Kip hadn’t told him to. It wasn’t Conju’s system. It still hurt a bit.

Kip flipped through the book. It looked exactly like his had when it was new. Now, his was a little dog-eared. There was a story that went around the pages that if you filled it before it fell apart, you got some kind of special pen as a reward. Kip was reasonably sure that wasn't actually possible, they were very cheaply made.

“What do I do?” said Conju, still staring at the door, “What have I done? What will I _wear_?”

Looking around for his bag, so he could fetch the Service Regulations, Kip repeated what he had written before. “Whatever the Emperor asks you to do, but I imagine it's mostly taking dictation and then writing up clean copies. Taking notes in meetings. Oh, and you might get to handle some form correspondence, I'm not sure how much of that is handled by the Offices of State.”

Liliane had placed the book on a small side table. Kip picked it up and flipped through it. “Oh right,” he said, when he found a relevant page, “the Personal Secretary has a special uniform. But don't worry about that for now, first you have to be formally accepted.”

He scanned down the pages, licking his thumb to turn them. “Here we go,” he said, placing the book in front of Conju, “Dress Code for the Upper Secretariat, Level One.”

Finally tearing his eyes from the door, Conju looked at the heading Kip was pointing to. Kip watched him quickly scan through the few lines. “Oh,” he said softly, “That sounds...”

“I assume you already own something appropriate?” said Kip, with a smile. “In fact, you could probably pass off what you were wearing yesterday evening as meeting that.”

“Russet is _not_ one of my registered family colours,” said Conju, with a sniff, “And those robes were midnight blue, not black.” He twisted slightly in his seat. “Liliane?” he asked, raising his voice very slightly, "I'll need the black silk robes tomorrow, the plain not the figured or watered, and the mantle from Seldra. Oh, and plain sandals.”

Liliane slid open a door. “Of course, my lord. Any jewellery?”

Conju looked down at the book again. “I don't have a personal signet, so no rings. I could wear the brooch mama gave me?”

“Very good, my lord,” said Liliane.

Kip nodded. “Sounds like you've sorted out what you're wearing. Do you have a writing kit?”

“Not a _secretarial_ one, like yours,” said Conju. They both looked up, to see Liliane placing a beautiful little silver and leather box on the table. Kip was immediately jealous. “Thank you,” said Conju to Liliane, and opened it up. It was silver gilt inside, with clever little fixtures for holding ink bottles so they didn't spill, a retractable pen, and even a tiny drawer for stamps.

“No ink stone?” asked Kip, curious, “Or brushes?”

“Brushes were out of style for a while so it only has a pen and bottled ink,” said Conju, with a slightly far away voice. “Is that a problem? Do I need brushes?”

Kip shrugged, “Most of us prefer to do Chancery hand with brushes, but a nice flexible nib will work.”

“I have a letter case somewhere I can use for paper and documents. Is there anything else I need?” asked Conju, carefully and slowly closing the writing set.

“That should be fine, I think. But don't put your pens away yet,” said Kip with a small smile, “How long did you say it was since you took dictation in shorthand?”

Conju let out a low sigh.

“Let's start out easy so you can get back into the rhythm of shorthand first, and then I'll increase the difficulty,” Kip said, suppressing a grin. It was *fun* to be teaching, to be helping someone else practice what he was good at. “I'm told it's like riding a bicycle, you never really forget.”

“I don't know how to ride a bicycle,” said Conju, flatly, “I only ever learnt to ride a horse.”

They practiced together for a good few hours. Kip composing the kinds of things he _thought_ the Emperor _might_ dictate, based on what he had seen float down the many layers of the bureaucracy. Conju slowly gaining confidence that he did, in fact, still remember these skills. 

Liliane supplied them with tea, coffee, and water. But, Kip noted, no alcohol. Apparently she had decided that 'hair of the dog' was not going to help.

Kip took gleeful pleasure in playing the part of those irritating members of the Upper Secretariat who were the worst at giving dictation. He remembered one case where the Undersecretary for Rare Diseases had dictated a report where she never mentioned a single section break, but then complained when the returned document had the wrong section titles. In reference to that, he dictated an entire page that was a single run-on sentence. Conju's ability to maintain a calm face was most impressive.

At some point, without being asked, Liliane started putting out food and drink on the table. Kip and Conju both looked up, chagrined. 

“Try not to faint in front of the Emperor, my lord,” said Liliane, “It would not reflect well on your family.”

The ten o'clock bell rang, and they both looked guiltily at the food again. “Sorry,” they both mumbled, and each picked a pastry. They ate in silence for a while, Kip admiring Conju's perfect stack of completed notes.

“Do you want to practice your Chancery script?” asked Kip. 

Minutely adjusting the stack of paper, Conju said, “I hope it is adequate, my mother always insisted invitations and responses be written in Chancery, so I have actually used that.”

Still, Kip had Conju write out one of the letters in Chancery to check. He offered a few tiny corrections, but admitted it was actually just as good as what he saw others in the Service do.

In his opinion, most Service staff tended to cut corners with it, and not care if others did as well. He had decided a while ago that it was one of the things he was not going to mention. What did it really matter if there were flaws in the script of an order forcing hundreds of people to move, so a tithe house could be built? He would rather fight to move the tithe house.

They continued reviewing other potential tasks, Kip explaining the standard formats for various Service documents. The Regulations book proved quite useful. Kip also explained how some more mundane tasks like getting paid or requesting leave worked.

By carefully whisking things away and placing them out, and occasionally looming over them, Liliane managed to get them into bed not much after one o'clock in the morning. It was a little strange, being managed like this, but Conju was clearly used to it.

---

The next workday was possibly the most distracted Kip had ever been. He did remember to post the letter to Ghilly, but he struggled with everything else. In the end he put aside the synthesis he was supposed to be writing, and cleared out his backlog of meeting notes instead.

Theoretically, you could submit formal meeting notes in shorthand, because everyone used the same shorthand system. In practice, reading anyone else's shorthand was usually slow and painful. If meeting notes only had to be archived, people often didn't care and just shoved the shorthand into the archive folder. Kip did care.

Knowing nobody would ever read them didn't make him feel much better. They had a tendency to build up under piles of more urgent tasks. Now was a good time to clean them out.

There wasn't time, in his half-hour lunch break, to get to the centre and back. So Kip didn't get the chance to check on Conju as soon as he left the Presence. Instead, he ate a dry wrap in the office kitchenette, and listened for any gossip.

Either word had yet to make it this far down the Collian wing, or the Duchess's loud breakup last night was more interesting. Kip didn't even hear word that anyone new had been sent to the Emperor. Just that the previous secretary had been fired after loosing an important document.

By the official end of his workday, Kip was practically shaking. As soon as the bell rang he collected his things and walked briskly out of the office. 

“Not putting in your usual extra hour to make the rest of us look bad, Madon?” called someone behind him. There was an echoing round of snickering. 

Kip ignored both. He actually usually worked late because he got dumped with the work his superiors hadn't finished and needed done by the next day. If he hadn't stayed back, the same snickerers would have called him lazy and stuck up.

Arriving back at Conju's room, Kip hesitated. Would Conju have come here? Might he not be out celebrating or commiserating with other friends? 

Also, even if he was here, would he want Kip to visit? Maybe he would rather be alone. They had just spent two full days together. Kip knew he could be a bit much.

Well, he didn't have to go in. He could just ask Liliane. That would be fine. He would leave a message with her saying where he was having dinner, and Conju could come if he wanted. Or send Kip a note.

Kip knocked on the door. Liliane opened it, but Conju was right behind her.

“You have to help me, Cliopher,” he said. 

Kip smiled and stepped inside. “Of course, Conju. Tell me what happened.”
