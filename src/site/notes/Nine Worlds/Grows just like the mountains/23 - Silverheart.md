Silverheart was a holiday for families. Conju's family wasn't so far from the Palace that he couldn't have gone home if he had wanted. But he claimed that not much of his family would be there, and he would rather stay at the Palace. With Kip. If there were undertones to that, well, Kip decided to treat them with caution. They were friends, perhaps lovers. It was always hard to be certain where one stood with nobles.

They spent the day together, relaxing. Liliane was visiting friends in the Palace, and the other staff had gone into the city to visit family. Being there alone made Kip feel so much younger. Like he had been left alone in the Mdang house, with none of the elders to tell him off if he ran down the stairs or used too much hot water.

At noon they made a raid on the kitchen together. Conju couldn't cook, and Kip could only really make the basic islander foods his Buru Tovo had taught him. Together they muddled through, eating mostly bread and vegetables and fruit. Kip did fry some bacon, but he kept waiting for one of his aunts to come in and chide him and demand to take it over.

Then they exchanged presents. Kip had been so anxious about what to get Conju, who demanded perfection in everything. It wasn't like he could afford the kind of jewellery nobles typically exchanged, made of fine materials even if not actual gold and gems. Basil had come to the rescue.

“I'm sorry it's not very special,” said Kip, awkwardly, handing over the gift wrapped in decorative paper.

Conju smiled, and carefully removed the ribbon without cutting it. Just as delicately he folded back the paper. Two tapering candles, tied with gold ribbon. One small ceramic bottle, a cork held in place with wax.

“It's from the Woods Noirel,” said Kip, twisting his hands nervously, “My cousin lives there, I think I told you. They don't sell the wax, something about it being bad luck, but they use it for themselves. And the bottle is honey jack, made by my cousin.”

“They are beautiful,” said Conju, and Kip thought, he hoped, he sounded sincere. “I have always liked the scent of honey, but not so much how _sticky_ it is. Thank you, Cliopher.”

Kip smiled, knowing indeed that Conju disliked anything that stuck to his hands. Whenever possible he preferred to eat with utensils, and to wash his hands as quickly as possible. And he did love scents. He had shared his hobby of making perfumes with Kip, who had been impressed with all the artistry and science that went into it.

Then it was Kip's turn. Conju handed him a dark blue silk bag with an orange drawstring. Inside was a little glass vial, and a disk, like a coin made of wood, on a ribbon. The vial, Kip could guess, was some kind of perfume. The disk was carved with geometric fretwork.

“I know you don't like wearing scent on your skin,” said Conju, “If you put a few drops on the medallion, and hang it with your clothes, they will be lightly scented instead.”

“Thank you,” said Kip, smiling. It was very thoughtful. He uncapped the vial, to see what the scent was, thinking perhaps it might be lavender, or rose, or something else traditional he had seen in Conju's workbench.

Tears sprang immediately to Kip's eyes. It was a warm chocolate smell, with a background of greenery, and a little salt. Somehow, based only Kip's descriptions, Conju had managed to re-create the smell of _tui_ blossom. After so long with nothing more of home than the _Lays_ in his heart, it was astonishing.

“Thank you,” he said again, as the tears ran down his cheeks, “Oh, Conju, it's magnificent. Are you _sure_ you didn't use any magic?”

Conju smiled. “No, just practice, and a good nose.”

Carefully re-stoppering the vial, Kip put the gift carefully on the table, next to where Conju has placed his gift. Then he slid closer to Conju on the settee, pressing their hips and thighs together. He looked into the other man's eyes, and saw there reflected some of his own joy.

Gently, carefully, Kip reached out a hand and cupped Conju's cheek. It was so soft. He was meticulous with both hair removal and skincare. Slowly, Kip tilted his head and closed the distance between them. They had shared a few careful kisses yesterday and today, uncertain perhaps what the other wanted exactly. This time Kip managed to relax, and simply enjoy it.

When they broke apart, there was a sparkle in Conju's eye that hadn't been there before. He slid a hand along the outside of Kip's thigh, curling his fingers at the end as if in question.

Kip bit his lip. “I've only ever… I don't know…” he started, but then couldn't face looking at Conju any more, couldn't work out what to say next. He looked down at his own hand, twisting the corner of a decorative cushion.

“You haven't… been with a man, before?” asked Conju, gently.

“I have…” began Kip, paused trying to work out how to explain, and then felt the confession just bubble up out of him. “Only people at work. Ghilly at home. So not only women, but not… not good.”

Had that even made _grammatical_ sense, wondered Kip, still unable to look up. The shame burned at him. What he'd felt unable to refuse, what he'd been coerced into. His stomach curdled, as flashes of those times came unbidden to his mind.

“Oh, Cliopher,” said Conju, his normally controlled voice quivering, “I only want to do with you what you want, love. I want to make you _happy_.”

“I *am* happy,” said Kip, “And I want to make _you_ happy.” He took a deep breath, and finally looked up at Conju again. The younger man looked worried, and Kip felt guilty that he had killed the mood.

“Will you tell me what you like?” asked Kip, and then added more slyly, “Or I could try kissing every bit of your skin and take notes.”

Conju's eyes went wide, but so did his smile. The blush was adorable. “Only if I can do the same to you, my dear,” he said, his voice soft. “Pencils only though, I don't want to spill ink on the bed.”

Kip actually laughed at that, and his laugh was bright and happy and free. That was exactly the Conju he loved. “Of course,” he said, pulling Conju up from the settee, and sneaking another kiss, “We must establish a rubric, and a methodology.”

“Fuck,” said Conju, following him into the library. Their hands were clasped, but Conju's other hand roamed freely over Kip's shoulders and back. “Only you could make report writing sound so hot.”
