Court was one of the most necessary and tedious parts of your day. If you didn't hold court, (you had tried) then your nobles became fractious, and had a tendency towards literal backstabbing that you found unfortunate. Court seemed to give them a place to practice _metaphorical_ backstabbing, which was your preference (and, you hoped, the preference of your peoples).

So you sat and watched them. Factions swimming around each-other, edges dissolving into snipping. Or on one occasion, (I treasured the memory), a dance-off. The Lord Treasurer headed the old guard faction, that you were slowly, painfully, chipping away at. You had managed to cleave the military off, and force them into their own loose association. It had been a relief. 

Princess Indrogan was the centre, but not the head, of the faction that had once been your mother's. It was more amorphous, being defined mostly by its opposition to the Lord Treasurer. As such, it was rather prone to infighting, until the Lord Treasurer announced a plan or policy they could unify against.

Smaller groups would bubble up, collect enough people to worry the larger factions, and then be taken out. This was usually a peaceful process. A little blackmail, perhaps, or a bit of bribery. It would be nice, someday, if your court could do without those. For now, you simply tried to stop the violence, and limit the worst of the cruelty.

If sometimes you daydreamed about summoning a firestorm and burning them all away, well. It was only an idle fancy. None of them needed to know. It wasn't like you had access to magic anyway. Locked away, with so much of whom I had thought I had been.

Tonight, while Indrogan danced with her favourite, and the Lord Treasurer grumbled to a High Priest, a newcomer appeared.

Yes, it was dull enough up on the dais that you noted every person attending for the first time. Visiting scholars, wizards seeking ordination, rich businessmen, and poor nobles. Many only came for one night, which given the dress code required for admission was an achievement in itself. The great and the good, (there were few of the latter), came every time. You were here, so they were here.

And now, your secretaries were here. Well, your secretary and undersecretary. The Cavalier looking confident (he had been before), and Sayo Mdang looking distinctly uncertain.

Some nobles, Indrogan and the Treasurer among them, liked to bring an entourage to court. Ministers might bring their Secretaries (who were really heads of department, and had probably never taken notes). Ladies and Gentlemen of leisure brought paid companions. Pet duellists were a popular choice, as were wizards, although both were expensive. Broadly anyone who owed you enough to follow you around rather than leave in search of better conversation.

Had the Cavalier dragged Sayo Mdang along to make himself look more important? You watched them move around the room. With practiced ease the Cavalier went from group to group, talking for a little while and moving on. Introducing Sayo Mdang? It seemed like it.

Finally, the dancing began. Tonight started with 'Little Nothings', an old favourite. To your surprise, the Cavalier did not leave Sayo Mdang on the side, while he danced with more important people. Instead, he formed a group of three with Sayo Mdang, and to your further astonishment, the Secretary for Infrastructure. All three, it turned out, were credible dancers.

As the Cavalier and Sayo Mdang continued to join the next three dances, you wondered if perhaps they were more than work colleagues. Were they lovers? It was a very common sort of nepotism to appoint one's lover as a secretary. To take one's secretary as a lover was so common that there was a sub-genre of romance novels about it. Although, of course, he was technically _your_ undersecretary, not the Cavalier's secretary. You had a brief moment of amusement, imagining what title a novelist might give to such a tale of three men. Would 'Under More Than Secretary' be too gauche? Perhaps 'Two Hands Hold the Pen'?

The way the two young men held each other through the waltz, gently but with confidence, only reinforced your assumptions. They swirled through the room, eyes seemingly enraptured by their dance partner. A smile, a laugh. What would the book about them be called? 'A Report Between Friends'? 'Ink Between Sheets'?

Your attempts to make terrible puns were interrupted by a different thought. Sayo Mdang had looked frightened, when you had summoned him, and you had assumed it was _you_ he was frightened of. Perhaps that had been pride on your part. Had Sayo Mdang reason to fear the Cavalier? 

Their dance set drifted closer, and you tried to discern any expression passing between them. You did your best to see how Sayo Mdang looked when the Cavalier's attention was elsewhere. They both looked perfectly pleasant, but then under the gaze of so many others, in a court where showing your emotions was worse than passing wind, how could they do otherwise?

You had no discrete spies to send after them. Of course, you could have them both questioned by Internal Security. The result of which would be that they both confessed to treason, because they had been tortured. It was one of many systems around you that spectacularly failed to serve their stated purpose.

Protector of the People, they called you. Yet how could you protect (if you even could), when you couldn't even know. You weren't supposed to know, to take an interest in, the private lives of your staff. You should pretend they didn't have any, as you didn't.

Sayo Mdang performed a tricky dance manoeuvre, that required taking a small leap and scissoring the legs. The effect was rather lost in court robes, except that if you tried and failed, you tended to fall over. Many choose to simply do the leap, and wiggle a bit to imitate the effect. When Sayo Mdang landed, the Cavalier gave him a wry smile, and Sayo Mdang returned a broad grin. You would assume in hope, then, that this relationship across class boundaries, was one of mutual affection.

After such an enlivening number, there was a break in the dancing. You lost sight of the Cavalier and Sayo Mdang for a little while, until they reemerged with glasses of shaved ice covered in sweet syrup. Once again, the Cavalier began a tour of the more political members of your court.

You wished you had thought to observe him more closely before he was sent as your secretary. Had he always been political? You thought not, you tended to keep a closer eye on those people. If he had come from the ranks of the Upper Secretariat that wafted around your ministers like moths to lanterns, you would have at least recognised him by sight. But you had recalled his sister first, and so he had belonged to some other group.

The Cavalier could not be said to have the _usual_ physique of a pet duellist. Of course, true fighters could come in any shape, as someone I had once known had shown me. Those who attached themselves to noble families to 'settle matters of honour', usually tended towards thin, wiry, and the more impoverished ranks of the aristocracy. The Cavalier was dressed far to well to be working because he needed the money.

Perhaps, though, a sympathetic mother had passed on. And now a sister was less inclined to fund his lifestyle. Yet, against this theory was the competence of the man. If he had wanted a job that was mostly complaining that other people hadn't finished his work, he could have surely found a position in the Treasury managing, say, mineral specimens. He could have stayed there, happily soaking up the spare change that leaked out of the purse of the Empire, for twenty years.

A mystery worth paying attention to, this pair. What, exactly, was their goal? So far it seemed they had shown themselves to be (you had to admit) the best secretaries you had ever had. Efficient, they had shown initiative, and were ruthlessly productive. 

Perhaps they wanted your job. It was a shame you couldn't give it to them, honestly.
