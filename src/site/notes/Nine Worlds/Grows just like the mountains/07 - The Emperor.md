Finding a cover note on top of the morning reports was such a pleasant surprise, that you managed to eat an entire sweet bun before the usual nausea hit. You forced the second down by taking tiny bites each time you confirmed the summary was both accurate and useful. It pointed out two relationships you might not have seen, and very subtly implied that one of the reports was far less detailed than it ought.

The attendant at breakfast today looked so excited that they might levitate. You had no way to tell them it wasn't anything particular about the buns. It was nice though, that you could make them happy. You hoped they wouldn't be too disappointed if tomorrow you could only manage to force down half a bun.

When your new secretary arrived, they presented you with most of the previous day's documents complete. This was also a pleasant improvement in your day. You flipped through them, afraid that you would find them full of errors, but no. There was only one, and if you were honest, it might have been that you had said the wrong word.

It was noticeable that two hands had worked on the letters. This left you immediately curious who else was involved. You had long suspected that your secretaries dumped making fair copies on whoever in the Service they could convince or force. Sometimes you would see every document presented in a different hand. Who was the aid to the Cavalier? 

And which of the two of them had written the summary for your morning reports? For you were sure that the handwriting there matched one of the two in the stack of documents. Would you dare to ask directly? How were they dividing the work? It wasn't obvious to you. These thoughts raced through your mind as you signed, sealed, and approved. 

Once more, the morning with the Cavalier was productive. If this continued — but no, it wasn't worth thinking so far ahead. It was enough, for now, to enjoy the way his pen danced across the page. He had shown initiative, with the summary. He had shown competence, with your work together so far.

At the mid-morning break, when the attendants hovered awkwardly, you stopped dictating. You moved away from your desk slightly, to give them space. As you turned, you noticed the Cavalier slip from his desk (did he need the toilet?) to the guards at the door. A whisper and he passed the guards his notes so far today. They opened the door — who could be there? Another stack of paper was handed through from beyond the doors to the Cavalier.

While the Cavalier moved to place the document he had received in your tray, you had a flash of realisation. It must be the Cavalier's aid, beyond the doors. This was your chance. 

“Who is it?” you inquired, mildly.

“The undersecretary, Glorious One,” said the Cavalier, his voice perfectly even, but his body frozen at the question.

You signalled for the guards to open the door. In the room beyond, you saw someone in the uniform of the Lower Secretariat spin around. “Come forward,” you said.

The undersecretary stepped forward slowly, clutching at the notes the guards had just passed on. As soon as he could, he dropped into the obeisance. You were used to people approaching you in fear; I wished with all my heart that they never had any cause.

“Cliopher sayo Mdang, Glorious One,” said the Cavalier.

A commoner? You were thrilled at the thought. Even the kitchen-hands in the Imperial Apartments were minor nobility. I was very briefly tempted to dismiss the Cavalier on the spot so you could appoint Sayo Mdang. But that would be cruel to both of them; the Cavalier had done nothing wrong in his work, and Sayo Mdang looked terrified.

“Rise,” you said, since of course he would not know the hand signal. You sat down, and drew close your cup of tea.

When he made it up (it looked like he could use some practice, and I giggled inside) you said, “You are our undersecretary.” You took a sip of tea, and provided him with space to reply.

“Yes, Glorious One,” said Sayo Mdang, uncertainly.

“You prepared these documents for us,” you said, and gestured at those newly arrived.

His eyes darted up, before he looked back down at the floor. “Yes, your Radiancy,” he said, and his voice only trembled a little as he blatantly contradicted you, “Lord Conju and I prepared them.”

“You wrote the summary for our morning reports,” you guessed.

“Yes, Glorious One,” said Sayo Mdang.

“Very good,” you said, please both that you had guessed correctly, and with the work that he had had done.

Sayo Mdang took this as a chance to escape your presence. You did not blame him, I would escape if I could.

The Cavalier looked slightly shaken, but not as much as you expected. You had wondered how much work, apart from taking the dictation, the Cavalier was doing. He wouldn't have been the first person to take this job for the 'prestige' and then try to do as little work as possible. But no, that wasn't fair to the Cavalier. The lazy secretaries had been slow at dictation, and when you had asked them to make fair copies immediately the results had been regrettable.

You would watch, carefully. Perhaps you might spot the Cavalier at court, and see who he talked with, what faction he belonged to. Careful reading of Sayo Mdang's summary would likely be the only window you had on him.

Briefly, I entertained the fancy of inviting them to dinner. Talking over wine and cheese, asking after their families. Did Sayo Mdang have a brother in the army? The Cavalier a sister at Tara?

Such things were not for you.

Well, there was a new stack of document to look through. You picked up the proclamation on the top and admired the immaculate Chancery hand.
