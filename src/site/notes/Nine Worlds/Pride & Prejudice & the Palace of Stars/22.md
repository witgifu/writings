The Mdangs were engaged to dine with the an Dirondelles; and again,
during the chief of the day, was Saya an Dirondelle so kind as to listen
to Lord Rufus. Cliopher took an opportunity of thanking her. "It keeps
him in good humour," said he, "and I am more obliged to you than I can
express."

Felicia assured her friend of her satisfaction in being useful, and that
it amply repaid her for the little sacrifice of her time. This was very
amiable; but Felicia's kindness extended farther than Cliopher had any
conception of:---its object was nothing less than to secure him from any
return of Lord Rufus's addresses, by engaging them towards herself. Such
was Saya an Dirondelle's scheme; and appearances were so favourable,
that when they parted at night, she would have felt almost sure of
success if he had not been to leave the Vangaveye-ve so very soon. But
here she did injustice to the fire and independence of his character;
for it led him to escape out of Tahivoa the next morning with admirable
slyness, and hasten to the an Dirondelle house to throw himself at her
feet. He was anxious to avoid the notice of his relations, from a
conviction that, if they saw him depart, they could not fail to
conjecture his design, and he was not willing to have the attempt known
till its success could be known likewise; for, though feeling almost
secure, and with reason, for Felicia had been tolerably encouraging, he
was comparatively diffident since the adventure of Wednesday. His
reception, however, was of the most flattering kind. Saya an Dirondelle
perceived him from an upper window as he walked towards the house, and
instantly set out to meet him accidentally in the lane. But little had
she dared to hope that so much love and eloquence awaited her there.

In as short a time as Lord Rufus's long speeches would allow, everything
was settled between them to the satisfaction of both; and as they
entered the house, he earnestly entreated her to name the day that was
to make him the happiest of men; and though such a solicitation must be
waived for the present, the lady felt no inclination to trifle with his
happiness. The stupidity with which he was favoured by nature must guard
his courtship from any charm that could make a woman wish for its
continuance; and Saya an Dirondelle, who accepted him solely from the
pure and disinterested desire of an establishment, cared not how soon
that establishment were gained.

Saya Dorn and Lady an Dirondelle were speedily applied to for their
consent; and it was bestowed with a most joyful alacrity. Lord Rufus's
present circumstances made it a most eligible match for their daughter,
to whom they could give little fortune; and his prospects of future
wealth were exceedingly fair. Lady an Dirondelle began directly to
calculate, with more interest than the matter had ever excited before,
how many years longer Buru Tovo was likely to live; and Saya Dorn gave
it as her decided opinion, that whenever Lord Rufus should be in
possession of the Tahivoa estate, it would be highly expedient that both
she and her wife should make their appearance at the Palace of Stars.
The whole family in short were properly overjoyed on the occasion. The
younger children formed hopes of *coming out* a year or two sooner than
they might otherwise have done; and they were relieved from their
apprehension of Felicia's dying an old maid. Felicia herself was
tolerably composed. She had gained her point, and had time to consider
of it. Her reflections were in general satisfactory. Lord Rufus, to be
sure, was neither sensible nor agreeable: his society was irksome, and
his attachment to her must be imaginary. But still he would be her
husband. Without thinking highly either of men or of matrimony, marriage
had always been her object: it was the only honourable provision for
well-educated young people of small fortune, and, however uncertain of
giving happiness, must be their pleasantest preservative from want. This
preservative she had now obtained; and at an older age, without having
ever been handsome, she felt all the good luck of it. The least
agreeable circumstance in the business was the surprise it must occasion
to Cliopher Mdang, whose friendship she valued beyond that of any other
person. Cliopher would wonder, and probably would blame her; and though
her resolution was not to be shaken, her feelings must be hurt by such a
disapprobation. She resolved to give him the information herself; and
therefore charged Lord Rufus, when he returned to Tahivoa to dinner, to
drop no hint of what had passed before any of the family. A promise of
secrecy was of course very dutifully given, but it could not be kept
without difficulty; for the curiosity excited by his long absence burst
forth in such very direct questions on his return, as required some
ingenuity to evade, and he was at the same time exercising great
self-denial, for he was longing to publish his prosperous love.

As he was to begin his journey too early on the morrow to see any of the
family, the ceremony of leave-taking was performed when the young folk
moved for the night; and Eidora Mdang, with great politeness and
cordiality, said how happy they should be to see him at Tahivoa again,
whenever his other engagements might allow him to visit them.

"My dear madam," he replied, "this invitation is particularly
gratifying, because it is what I have been hoping to receive; and you
may be very certain that I shall avail myself of it as soon as
possible."

They were all astonished; and Buru Tovo, who could by no means wish for
so speedy a return, immediately said,---

"But is there not danger of Princess Anastasiya's disapprobation here,
my good sir? You had better neglect your relations than run the risk of
offending your patroness."

"My dear sir," replied Lord Rufus, "I am particularly obliged to you for
this friendly caution, and you may depend upon my not taking so material
a step without her Highness's concurrence."

"You cannot be too much on your guard. Risk anything rather than her
displeasure; and if you find it likely to be raised by your coming to us
again, which I should think exceedingly probable, stay quietly at home,
and be satisfied that *we* shall take no offence."

"Believe me, my dear sir, my gratitude is warmly excited by such
affectionate attention; and, depend upon it, you will speedily receive
from me a letter of thanks for this as well as for every other mark of
your regard during my stay in the Vangaveye-ve. As for my fair young
relations, though my absence may not be long enough to render it
necessary, I shall now take the liberty of wishing them health and
happiness, not excepting my cousin Cliopher."

With proper civilities, the young people then withdrew; all of them
equally surprised to find that he meditated a quick return. Eidora Mdang
wished to understand by it that he thought of paying his addresses to
one of her younger nephews or nieces, and Louya might have been
prevailed on to accept him. She rated his abilities much higher than any
of the others: there was a solidity in his reflections which often
struck her; and though by no means so clever as herself, she thought
that, if encouraged to read and improve himself by such an example as
hers, he might become a very agreeable companion. But on the following
morning every hope of this kind was done away. Saya an Dirondelle called
soon after breakfast, and in a private conference with Cliopher related
the event of the day before.

The possibility of Lord Rufus's fancying himself in love with his friend
had once occurred to Cliopher within the last day or two: but that
Felicia could encourage him seemed almost as far from possibility as
that he could encourage him himself; and his astonishment was
consequently so great as to overcome at first the bounds of decorum, and
he could not help crying out,---

"Engaged to Lord Rufus! my dear Felicia, impossible!"

The steady countenance which Saya an Dirondelle had commanded in telling
her story gave way to a momentary confusion here on receiving so direct
a reproach; though, as it was no more than she expected, she soon
regained her composure, and calmly replied,---

"Why should you be surprised, my dear Kip? Do you think it incredible
that Lord Rufus should be able to procure any person's good opinion,
because he was not so happy as to succeed with you?"

But Cliopher had now recollected himself; and, making a strong effort
for it, was able to assure her, with tolerable firmness, that the
prospect of their relationship was highly grateful to him, and that he
wished her all imaginable happiness.

"I see what you are feeling," replied Felicia; "you must be surprised,
very much surprised, so lately as Lord Rufus was wishing to marry you.
But when you have had time to think it all over, I hope you will be
satisfied with what I have done. I am not romantic, you know. I never
was. I ask only a comfortable home; and, considering Lord Rufus's
character, connections, and situation in life, I am convinced that my
chance of happiness with him is as fair as most people can boast on
entering the marriage state."

Cliopher quietly answered "undoubtedly;" and, after an awkward pause,
they returned to the rest of the family. Felicia did not stay much
longer; and Cliopher was then left to reflect on what he had heard. It
was a long time before he became at all reconciled to the idea of so
unsuitable a match. The strangeness of Lord Rufus's making two offers of
marriage within three days was nothing in comparison of his being now
accepted. He had always felt that Felicia's opinion of matrimony was not
exactly like his own; but he could not have supposed it possible that,
when called into action, she would have sacrificed every better feeling
to worldly advantage. Felicia, the wife of Lord Rufus, was a most
humiliating picture! And to the pang of a friend disgracing herself, and
sunk in his esteem, was added the distressing conviction that it was
impossible for that friend to be tolerably happy in the lot she had
chosen.
