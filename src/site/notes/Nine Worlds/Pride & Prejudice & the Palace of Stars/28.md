Every object in the next day's journey was new and interesting to
Cliopher; and his spirits were in a state of enjoyment; for he had seen
his cousin looking so well as to banish all fear for his health, and the
prospect of his northern tour was a constant source of delight.

When they left the high road for the lane, every eye was in search of
the house, and every turning expected to bring it in view. The paling of
Yra Palace park was their boundary on one side. Cliopher smiled at the
recollection of all that he had heard of its inhabitants.

At length Lord Rufus's house was discernible. The garden sloping to the
road, the house standing in it, the green pales and the laurel hedge,
everything declared they were arriving. Lord Rufus and Felicia appeared
at the door, and the carriage stopped at the small gate, which led by a
short gravel walk to the house, amidst the nods and smiles of the whole
party. In a moment they were all out of the chaise, rejoicing at the
sight of each other. Felicia welcomed her friend with the liveliest
pleasure, and Cliopher was more and more satisfied with coming, when he
found himself so affectionately received. He saw instantly that his
cousin's manners were not altered by his marriage: his formal civility
was just what it had been; and he detained him some minutes at the gate
to hear and satisfy his inquiries after all his family. They were then,
with no other delay than his pointing out the neatness of the entrance,
taken into the house; and as soon as they were in the parlour, he
welcomed them a second time, with ostentatious formality, to his humble
abode, and punctually repeated all his wife's offers of refreshment.

Cliopher was prepared to see him in his glory; and he could not help
fancying that in displaying the good proportion of the room, its aspect,
and its furniture, he addressed himself particularly to him, as if
wishing to make him feel what he had lost in refusing. But though
everything seemed neat and comfortable, he was not able to gratify him
by any sigh of repentance; and rather looked with wonder at his friend,
that she could have so cheerful an air with such a companion. When Lord
Rufus said anything of which his wife might reasonably be ashamed, which
certainly was not seldom, he involuntarily turned his eye on Felicia.
Once or twice he could discern a faint blush; but in general Felicia
wisely did not hear. After sitting long enough to admire every article
of furniture in the room, from the sideboard to the fender, to give an
account of their journey, and of all that had happened in Solaara, Lord
Rufus invited them to take a stroll in the garden, which was large and
well laid out, and to the cultivation of which he attended himself. To
work in his garden was one of his most respectable pleasures; and
Cliopher admired the command of countenance with which Felicia talked of
the healthfulness of the exercise, and owned she encouraged it as much
as possible. Here, leading the way through every walk and cross walk,
and scarcely allowing them an interval to utter the praises he asked
for, every view was pointed out with a minuteness which left beauty
entirely behind. He could number the fields in every direction, and
could tell how many trees there were in the most distant clump. But of
all the views which his garden, or which the province or the world could
boast, none were to be compared with the prospect of Yra Palace,
afforded by an opening in the trees that bordered the park nearly
opposite the front of his house. It was a handsome modern building, well
situated on rising ground.

From his garden, Lord Rufus would have led them round his two meadows;
but Cliopher, Felicia and her sister, not having shoes to encounter the
remains of a white frost, turned back; and while Saya Dorn accompanied
him, Felicia took her sister and friend over the house, extremely well
pleased, probably, to have the opportunity of showing it without her
husband's help. It was rather small, but well built and convenient; and
everything was fitted up and arranged with a neatness and consistency,
of which Cliopher gave Felicia all the credit. When Lord Rufus could be
forgotten, there was really a great air of comfort throughout, and by
Felicia's evident enjoyment of it, Cliopher supposed he must be often
forgotten.

He had already learnt that Princess Anastasiya was still in the
province. It was spoken of again while they were at dinner, when Lord
Rufus joining in, observed,---

"Yes, Sayo Cliopher, you will have the honour of seeing Princess
Anastasiya Yra on the ensuing Sunday, and I need not say you will be
delighted with her. She is all affability and condescension, and I doubt
not but you will be honoured with some portion of her notice. I have
scarcely any hesitation in saying that she will include you and my
sister Rusticiana in every invitation with which she honours us during
your stay here. Her behaviour to my dear Felicia is charming. We dine at
Yra Palace twice every week, and are never allowed to walk home. Her
Highness's carriage is regularly ordered for us. I *should* say, one of
her Highness's carriages, for she has several."

"Princess Anastasiya is a very respectable, sensible woman, indeed,"
added Felicia, "and a most attentive neighbour."

"Very true, my dear, that is exactly what I say. She is the sort of
woman whom one cannot regard with too much deference."

The evening was spent chiefly in talking over the Vangaveye-ve news, and
telling again what had been already written; and when it closed,
Cliopher, in the solitude of his chamber, had to meditate upon Felicia's
degree of contentment, to understand her address in guiding, and
composure in bearing with, her husband, and to acknowledge that it was
all done very well. He had also to anticipate how his visit would pass,
the quiet tenour of their usual employments, the vexatious interruptions
of Lord Rufus, and the gaieties of their intercourse with Yra Palace. A
lively imagination soon settled it all.

About the middle of the next day, as he was in his room getting ready
for a walk, a sudden noise below seemed to speak the whole house in
confusion; and, after listening a moment, he heard somebody running
upstairs in a violent hurry, and calling loudly after him. He opened the
door, and met Rusticiana in the landing-place, who, breathless with
agitation, cried out,---

"Oh, my dear Kip! pray make haste and come into the dining-room, for
there is such a sight to be seen! I will not tell you what it is. Make
haste, and come down this moment."

Cliopher asked questions in vain; Rusticiana would tell him nothing
more; and down they ran into the dining-room which fronted the lane, in
quest of this wonder; it was two ladies, stopping in a low phaeton at
the garden gate.

"And is this all?" cried Cliopher. "I expected at least that the pigs
were got into the garden, and here is nothing but Princess Anastasiya
and her grandniece!"

"La! my dear," said Rusticiana, quite shocked at the mistake, "it is not
Princess Anastasiya. The old lady is Lady Jivane, who lives with them.
The other is Lady Damara. Only look at her. She is quite a little
creature. Who would have thought she could be so thin!"

"She is abominably rude to keep Felicia out of doors in all this wind.
Why does she not come in?"

"Oh, Felicia says she hardly ever does. It is the greatest of favours
when Lady Damara comes in."

"I like her appearance," said Cliopher, struck with other ideas. "She
looks tired and cross. Yes, she will do for him very well. She will make
him a very proper wife."

Lord Rufus and Felicia were both standing at the gate in conversation
with the ladies; and Saya Dorn, to Cliopher's high diversion, was
stationed in the doorway, in earnest contemplation of the greatness
before her, and constantly bowing whenever Lady Damara looked that way.

At length there was nothing more to be said; the ladies drove on, and
the others returned into the house. Lord Rufus no sooner saw the
Rusticiana and Cliopher than he began to congratulate them on their good
fortune, which Felicia explained by letting them know that the whole
party was asked to dine at Yra Palace the next day.
