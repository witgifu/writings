Sayo Bennet was a page, and pages were meant to trot quickly through the Palace of Stars, delivering messages, fetching, and so on. Possibly doing other things Cliopher had explained at length, and Rhodin had half-listened to. They were stationed all over the Palace, and so they could be found in nearly any hall or stairway.

Pages were not, Rhodin was reasonably sure, meant to stop in an alcove and then seemly do the old soldier's trick of sleeping with their eyes open. One way or another, it was highly suspicious. So Rhodin watched the young man, from his own much better alcove, to see what happened next.

The page stood there for long enough that Rhodin was beginning to think he was only shirking. Something he might write a note about to Sayo Kalikiri, but not even worth bothering Cliopher with. Then, with a start, the page scuttled down the hall, and through a door. Rhodin followed.

He could not explain to Conju, or, even more embarrassingly, to Commander Omo, why he took leave of his senses, and entered the room immediately behind the page. When he reported to the Commander, he was only instructed to see to his injuries, and apologise to the page. Perhaps Ludvic considered the embarrassment to be punishment enough.

“He was behaving so oddly, I thought him under the influence of some drug or spell,” said Rhodin, as Conju dabbed salve along his arm.

Conju rolled his eyes dramatically, “Yes, behaving very oddly, I'm sure, for a man being followed by an Imperial Guard.”

“I'm not entirely useless at my job, people don't usually notice me when I don't want them to,” said Rhodin, defensively.

“If you'd asked for his personnel file, I'm sure it would have listed his magical gift,” said Conju, “Or you could have entered the room via a different route, or simply waited until he left, or come back another day to investigate the room.”

“Yes,” said Rhodin, “I almost wonder if… well, never mind. It's done now.”

Conju looked Rhodin directly in the eye. “If you think for one minute I'm not going to bring up how you were taken out by a page armed with only a censer of incense at every opportunity—”

“I know,” interrupted Rhodin, “I have accepted that also.” He hadn't been _taken out_, exactly, only very surprised, and rather singed along his right arm. When the page had realised who he'd attacked, he'd dropped to his knees to beg to be allowed to plead for clemency to the Lord Emperor.

“If he had been under some kind of spell, surely you would have felt that,” said Conju.

Rhodin couldn't deny the logic. His magical gift was exceedingly weak, but his passive sense was quite strong. It was how he'd made it to the village of Solaara, after the Fall. And, he thought with some fondness, how he'd got past numerous poorly-laid protective spells, before.

There was, perhaps, one minor consolation to his fumble. Having the undivided attention of his friend, whose gentle hands had carefully treated each mark on his skin like a gaping wound. No, he wouldn't complain too loudly, if he got to spend an hour wrapped in Conju's soft hands.

As Conju went to move them away, to pack away his salves and tools, Rhodin gently reached over and took it. He brushed his lips across the knuckles, appreciating as always how diligent Conju was with his skincare. Rhodin could have gone to a healer for treatment, but they would have mostly been concerned with infection. Conju knew what to do to make sure all the marks faded as quickly as possible.

“Thank you, most gentle lord, for you kind ministrations to such a foolish man,” he said, offering Conju his best smouldering look.

Snatching his hand back, Conju did put away all his things. Well, that was entirely expected. He was _fastidiously_ tidy, which Rhodin found rather charming.

“I am seeing someone for a second date _tomorrow_, Rhodin,” said Conju, snapping the clasp on the box for emphasis. “Why don't you take that smile down to the bars of Solaara, I'm sure you can get what you want there without trying.”

This was also true. Conju seemed to be offering Rhodin many uncomfortable truths this evening. When Rhodin felt like being admired by beautiful women, he did indeed head down to the bars. Mostly those where he paid for the service; it was altogether more simple to be sure there were no expectations on either side. That wasn't really what he wanted.

Rhodin tried again. “So, if this new man survives tomorrow, then I will go elsewhere,” he offered, “But I am not asking for myself. You look as stressed as when Cliopher takes his holiday.”

“You have put my schedule for the evening out of order,” said Conju, but not as sharply as he might have.

“It won't happen again,” said Rhodin, pouting and bowing his head.

“Don't lie to me Rhodin, I know it will,” said Conju. He stepped nearer to Rhodin though, rather than further away. “I am glad you came to me, at least this way I can be sure _himself_ won't get worried seeing your injuries.”

“You won't tell him?” asked Rhodin, batting his eyelashes.

Conju bit his lip, and put his face just in front of Rhodin's. Rhodin froze, awaiting judgement.

“That depends,” said Conju, “On how good you are tonight.”

Inside, Rhodin's heart sang, his spirit revelled in the rightness. Rhodin said only, “As my Cavalier commands, so shall I be.”

The Palace bells chimed, and Conju nodded. “You will wash, not getting any water on this bandage. I will arrive at my apartment in half a bell, and expect to find you naked in my bed.”

“Yes, Lord Conju,” said Rhodin, staring into his friend's eyes.

Conju broke the eye contact with a snort, and silently slipped from the room. Rhodin left in the other direction, the bounce restored to his steps.
