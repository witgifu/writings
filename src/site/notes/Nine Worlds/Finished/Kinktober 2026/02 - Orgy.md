Rhodin stretched himself out on the eating couch, carefully watching where their host, the Duke of the Dagger Islands, had placed everyone. There were nineteen guests, which was an intimate sort of gathering.

To Rhodin's left was a daughter of one of the interchangeable lesser Jilkano princes. She took her place with a sashaying confidence, that Rhodin had a professional appreciation for. He knew she was a honey trap, but she wasn't here for him. Her eyes were on the man opposite, a portly priest-wizard.

Their interaction kept him occupied so well that he didn't immediately pay attention when someone took the place on his other side. Theoretically Rhodin was making a purely social appearance here, but he did feel slightly embarrassed that the voice took him by surprise.

“Ser Rhodin,” said Conju. Rhodin could hear the smile in his voice.

Rhodin twisted his neck, to see his friend, of course given the higher status position. “Lord Conju,” he replied formally, “Always a treat to see you at any party.”

The prince was on the other side of Conju, which seemed like a mistake on the Duke's part. If one was going to play court to the Groom of the Chamber, one should at least put him next to someone interesting. Well, Rhodin was interesting, but Conju already knew _him_. The prince was as dull as dishwater, which was why his daughter made an excellent spy.

If nothing else, Rhodin could talk to Conju, and get the benefit of his best gossip. They'd missed their last catchup because of some issue Conju was having with the lace-makers. Rhodin had read the note and decided it was a Conju-and-Cliopher problem, and wouldn't need any input from him.

“What,” said Rhodin, leaning back, “Do you think the entertainments will be?”

Conju tutted, and sipped his sparkling wine. “I hope it's better than those trained mice.”

“They _did_ dance,” said Rhodin, “Although I don't think anyone had any of the cake afterwards.”

They continued through the courses, both of them keeping sharp eyes on the rest of the guests. Most of them were the Duke's usual crowd, although both the wizard and the scholar at the end were new. After the fourth remove, some kind of elaborate dish of pork covered in silver foil to make it look like fish, Rhodin found why the wizard had been invited. 

With much re-arranging of his robes, he bowed to their host, and then announced in a reedy voice, “In honour of our host, and his close relationship with his Serene and Radiant Holiness, the Lord Emperor.” He then proceeded to wave his hands, scattering powders that produced various sparkling effects. First in the shape of the Duke's family crest, and then above it the Sun in Glory.

It was all, Rhodin thought, very trite. Also, the Duke didn't _have_ a close relationship with his Radiancy. Certainly Conju had spent more time with the Lord Emperor today than the Duke had in the past year. Rhodin had spoken to his Radiancy more in the past week than the Duke had in his life.

“Foolish,” muttered Conju, quietly enough only Rhodin could hear.

The next dish arrived, a sculpture made of multicoloured jellies embedded with curls of vegetables. Rhodin hoped it tasted better than it looked.

The final course, a fruit soup, was served with a sweet wine. Rhodin sniffed it, and immediately muttered under his breath to Conju, “Stimulant.”

Conju returned his glass to the table without drinking any, as did Rhodin. Neither of them felt the need for that anymore. There was an age past which the hangover was worse than any fun, even if the expensive kind weren't particularly habit-forming.

Not a quarter bell later, the prince's daughter had spread the wizard out, and her and a friend were taking turns grinding on his lap and face. Her father had left them to it, and moved to the billiards room with the Duke and his husband. The prince's long-suffering wife was kissing one of the maids, and Rhodin decided to see if they required any help.

Half a bell later, Rhodin returned to the dinning room, having collected enough dirt on the prince to keep him in line for a good many years. Once he would have simply blackmailed the man for money, but these days he worked for a *cause*. It could really put a spring in his step.

Conju was still in the dining room. Rhodin nearly rubbed his eyes when he saw who was cuddling up next to him. The prince's daughter's friend was on one side, and the prince's wife on the other. That made no sense at all.

“There you are,” said Conju, when he caught sight of Rhodin.

Made concerned by Conju's acid tone, Rhodin only bowed in response.
