It turns out that the 'day' that had been claimed for the journey to the portal island was perhaps a little optimistic. Once Sayo Tovo is happy with both Oginu's and Sayo Navan's sailing abilities, he declares that they can take shifts. Sayo Navan sailing with the winds, currents, and stars to direct him. Oginu with the finding charm, plus the corrections of the others.

Thankfully, they have all packed food, and three of them also know how to fish. None of them is body-shy enough that using the ocean as a toilet is a problem. But Oginu, at least, is relieved when not long after sunset on the second day, Sayo Navan points to a spot on the horizon, sparkling in the moonlight. 

“There it is,” he says, “Although by the time we make landfall it will be too dark to head into the jungle.”

Oginu and Ser Rhodin have mage lights, but Oginu at least is happy to bow to Sayo Navan's local experience. If there are predators that come out after dark, he would rather not find out the hard way. 

“Death adders?” asks Sayo Tovo, curious.

“Yes,” says Sayo Navan, “They're all over the Epalos.” They both nod sagely, and Oginu decides he doesn't want to ask if the 'death' part of the name is dramatic or literal. He hopes they aren't likely to come onto the beach.

Oginu and Sayo Navan pull the vaha up above the high-tide mark, and then set about making camp before the moon sets. 

“Would you like me to set up the tent, Ser Rhodin? Or I can make a shelter from just the oilcloth, that will be faster,” offers Oginu. 

“It's a nice enough night to sleep under the stars,” says Ser Rhodin, admiring the sky, “Unless anyone has reason to think it will rain?”

“Rain?” says Sayo Tovo, “No.”

Based on that assessment, Oginu sweeps an area of sand flat with a dry palm-leaf. Then he lays out their rubber sleeping mats. Sayo Navan and Sayo Tovo also have sleeping mats, theirs woven from a local fibre Oginu doesn't recognise. 

Sayo Tovo deputises Sayo Navan to get the fuel for the fire, requiring specific plants that Oginu is not sure he would correctly identify. Especially not in the dark. One of them is the palm-leaf Oginu had used as a broom. Before long Sayo Tovo has a happy fire going. 

They cook some fish and yams in the coals on the edge. It is a pleasant, if slightly odd, group of people to be adventuring with. Certainly more fun than trying to show the twelfth guard recruit how to correctly hammer in a tent-peg, having still not had time to erect his own shelter.

After they've eaten, and finish the last of their waterskins (Sayo Navan assures them there is a spring they can find as soon as it is light), they all relax for a little while. Sayo Navan pulls out a sketchbook, which he has to hold close enough to the fire to make Oginu nervous. 

Ser Rhodin gets out what looks like two metal leaves, covering the top and bottom of a small spool of thread. The partially-worked project, however, gives away what it is: some way of making lace. Watching for a little while, Oginu realises that he actually recognises most of what Ser Rhodin is doing, the knots are all familiar, even if it is thread rather than rope. 

Oginu had brought a book, but he is unwilling (unlike Sayo Navan) to hold it close enough to the fire to read. So instead he stretches out on his sleeping mat, and admires the stars. Away from the lights of Solaara, or even Gorjo City, there are so many more to see. Constellations he knows well seem to bejewelled, with more points of light twinkling across them.

Listing to the insects, smelling the sea air, Oginu stars humming quietly to himself. Perhaps it is being in such a very different place, or being with Sayo Tovo. Or maybe it is the latent influence of standing guard so often while Sayo Mdang worked. He realises he is humming _Aurora_. With a cough, he stops, and switches to a work-song for doing laundry instead.

“That tune before was different,” says Sayo Tovo.

“Ah,” said Oginu, awkwardly aware that he was with the _deputy commander of the Imperial Guard_, and that the song was _highly_ illegal. “It was popular a while back, I think.” Across the fire he can see that Sayo Navan's face is twisting, as he desperately tries to contain his laughter. After a few more seconds he loses the battle, and lets out a snicker.

“I've often heard Cliopher hum it while he works,” says Ser Rhodin to Sayo Tovo, “Perhaps he did so at home too?”

“Oh, yes,” says Sayo Nevan, between snickers, “That would be it.”

Oginu says nothing more, just lies back on his mat and pretends he can disappear into the sand.

The next thing he notices is the dawn. Well, not the dawn exactly, but rather the bird in the nearest tree, who greets it with a call like a rolling belly laugh. Reminding him somewhat uncomfortably of his faux pas the night before.

“Kookaburra,” says Sayo Nevan, when he sees Oginu staring at the bird, blearily. “And yes, they always laugh like that.”

Sayo Tovo walks over to the tree and looks up at the bird. “We're awake, you can stop now.”

To Oginu's surprise, the bird doesn't fly away. And it does stop laughing. Perhaps Sayo Tovo has a magical skill with birds. Or perhaps he just knows kookaburra behaviour, and thought he would show off to the foreigners.

They pack up their minimal camp, burying the coals in damp sand to make sure no stray breeze relights them. Then Sayo Nevan looks at the other three. “Let's get going then,” he says. Ser Rhodin and Oginu hoist their packs. Sayo Tovo only has a few things in a string bag, but perhaps Sayo Nevan is carrying some things for the much older man.

Not far in, perhaps half an hour, as promised, Sayo Nevan points to a little stream of water, bubbling down over a little waterfall and into a pool. “Don't drink from the bottom,” he says, “But refill your waterskins from the top.” He and Sayo Tovo have gourd bottles, which look very light when empty.

While Oginu refills them all, Sayo Nevan pulls out a very long and large knife. He uses it on a plant that looks something like a palm, although it has much less trunk. Oginu concentrates on getting water into the bottles and skins, rather than over himself.

When Oginu is finishes, he is able to take a better look. Above, he can see what must be fifty bananas, arching over on a long stem that is swaying as Sayo Nevan hacks at it. With a creak and a crash, the plant stem folds over, and Sayo Nevan cuts it off entirely. Then he saws off about a dozen bananas from the top, where they are nice and yellow. He hands them to Sayo Tovo, and then drags the rest off to bury in some leaf litter.

“When we come back, the rest should be ripe,” he says, as he walks back. They divide up the bananas, for a surprisingly delicious breakfast. As Oginu has often found, fruit tastes best when it has spent less time travelling. Copying Sayo Tovo, he discards the skins by throwing them as far as possible into the jungle. Presumably something will want them, even if it is only beetles.

Only a few steps onward, Sayo Nevan shows them a gap between boulders that must be the entrance. “Now,” he says, “This first part you can see, but you mustn't linger. Keep up a brisk pace. We'll get to the cave proper, and then I'll need you to close your eyes.” They all nod.

The path between the boulders opens into something of a canyon, filled with luscious life and iridescent butterflies. Oginu can see why Sayo Nevan started with the warning not to linger, he can feel the temptation to stay in this beautiful place and simply marvel at it. Perhaps half a bell on, they reach the sandy entrance to a cave.

“You have a mage light? With a cover?” Sayo Nevan asks Oginu. He removes it from his pack, and hands it over. “I'll need you to close your eyes, even Buru Tovo,” says Sayo Nevan, “We wouldn't usually take anyone through here.”

“I know not to look at what I'm not meant to see, boy,” says Sayo Tovo, closing his eyes and holding out a hand.

Ser Rhodin copies Sayo Tovo, but holding one hand towards Sayo Nevan, and the other towards Oginu. Once he has his pack resettled, Oginu closes his eyes and takes Ser Rhodin's hand. “Ready, Sayo Nevan,” he says.

Perhaps a quarter-bell later, Sayo Nevan says, “You can open your eyes now.” When Oginu does, he can see Sayo Nevan holding up the mage light. He drops Ser Rhodin's hand, and looks around. They're in a natural hall, pillars formed of stone drips. It's beautiful, in an eerie sort of way. The mage light sparkles off every surface as Sayo Nevan moves it.

Ser Rhodin pulls the finding charm from his pocket. He holds it still for a moment, the other three watching him intently. Then he smiles, and points to the right, between two pillars, and up slope into the dark. “That way,” he says, “I can feel it too, it feels… safe.”

Oginu can only take Ser Rhodin at his word. There's nothing around that Oginu can sense as magic. Not that he's been to another world before, perhaps that magic always feels like cool cave air, and smells like water heavy with minerals.

Sayo Nevan hands the light to Ser Rhodin, who leads the way. Before long they're out of the hall, and into a passage. And then, at the end, rather improbably, is a door.

Oginu turns to Sayo Nevan, who looks surprised as Oginu feels. “I've never been past the place where I told you all you could look again,” he says, “But I admit I never expected the portal to be so… literal.”

Sayo Tovo is grinning, and Ser Rhodin has a broad smile as well. Reaching out his empty hand, Ser Rhodin presses the latch, and the door swings open.
