When admitted, Conju offers the Grand Duchess the very deep bow she is owed as a princess of the imperial family. After all, it is as the sister of the Emperor, and not the ruler of Damara, that he approaches her now. She was raised at the court, he knows she will immediately understand the implication.

Conju proffers the letter respectfully. “From his Radiancy, your grace. He asked that I answer any questions you might have.”

She takes it, and flips it open. After reading the first line, she makes a small gesture of dismissal. Her maid, companion, and majordomo all quietly exit the room. Suddenly, Conju is nervous about what his lord has written.

“Take a seat, Lord Conju,” she says, when she has reached the bottom. The Grand Duchess also sits, elegantly and formally on the edge of the couch.

Conju chooses a chair opposite, and follows her request.

“Have you read this?” she asks, tapping the letter.

“No, your grace,” he answers. Conju is not offended by the question, the letter wasn't sealed. He would not read a letter between his lord and the Grand Duchess. Many would have.

“He has never before called me his sister,” she says. This is not a question, but there is perhaps a question behind it.

“I know his Radiancy has, has always had, the greatest respect for you, your grace,” he says, trying to answer the question she has not asked, “He has probably just had his greatest shock since he woke from the Fall. I know he was very concerned that you understand, what he might have asked you to do, is not a duty or an obligation.”

She nods, as if that does indeed answer some of her questions. “He has asked me to hold court,” she says, and Conju wonders what feeling swirl beneath that polite face. “I have only ever attended the courts of his Radiancy and his uncle.”

Not 'my brother', and not 'our uncle', or even 'my uncle', Conju notes. Distancing herself from the crown, and the imperial family.

She was raised to be Consort, but that was a long time ago, in a different world, to a very different sort of Emperor. It has never occurred to Conju before, what the Grand Duchess felt about the complaints that the Emperor refused to marry her. 

“Technically, your grace, questions on the correct running of court functions are a matter for the Master of Etiquette,” says Conju, and they share a carefully polite look. The Master of Etiquette can be consulted once they have made all the decisions. “Many of the rituals of court are constrained by the taboos. I can see that it would be possible for a host who was not his Radiancy to choose the level of formality. They could sit on the lower dais, as the Hands of the Emperor might, for the entire session. Or they might choose only to do so to open and end the court session, and otherwise join the discussion and dancing.”

The Grand Duchess looks down at the letter again. Conju understands, if she accepts, the court might infer this means a change in her relationship with his Radiancy. They have both been very careful to manage expectations.

“It is unfortunate timing, to have his Radiancy and his Hands both unavailable,” she says, and again there is a question she feels unable to say.

“The absences are related, your grace,” says Conju, unsure how much he should say, how much his Radiancy has said. Yet, his Radiancy did say to answer *any* questions. Uncharacteristically, he decides she should probably know as much as he himself does. She has always shown herself to be the height of discretion (how could she not be, surviving so many years betrothed to Shallyr).

“Cliopher sayo Mdang fell from the roof of the Palace, probably a bell ago. Something alerted his Radiancy, and he was able to slow Sayo Mdang's descent, but not sufficiently quickly. His Radiancy then opened a portal under Sayo Mdang. He does not know where to,” says Conju. 

Now that he puts it so bluntly, it sounds absolutely mad. And yet, what could Conju do? Fetch Princess Anastasiya to yell at his Radiancy for wasting magic and time on his secretary? Hardly. Even if Conju had no personal interest in Cliopher's wellbeing (his heart twists, what strange environ has his friend been dumped into?), it is clear to the entire household that his Radiancy loves Cliopher dearly. If Cliopher had died, if he had even been significantly injured, his Radiancy would be devastated.

Additionally, the man is functionally running the world government. That is a hard thing to find a substitute for at short notice. Conju hopes Cliopher's protocol is good, and doesn't expect his Radiancy to do too much.

If the Grand Duchess is surprised or disturbed by this information, she doesn't show it. “When would the next court session need to be?” she asks, politely.

“The Council of Princes will no doubt run long tomorrow,” says Conju, and the Grand Duchess inclines her head in agreement, “So we can cancel evening court tomorrow on those grounds without surprising anyone. There is, thankfully, already no court scheduled for the day after. The day after that is supposed to have a court dinner, but we can move that to the afternoon or evening court instead.”

The Grand Duchess considers. If Conju thought it would help, he would happily beg on his knees before her. However, she has always responded better to reason than flattery.

“The longest this could possibly go on would be until the end of session in four weeks,” says Conju, painfully aware of the scope of the search, “After that we would need to put in place longer term solutions.”

“Do you know what those might be?” says the Grand Duchess, finally asking a direct question.

“No, your grace,” says Conju honestly, “Anything I suggested would only be speculation.”

“You perhaps, Lord Conju, know his Radiancy best of all. Do you think it possible he may be,” she pauses, and takes a deep breath, “Unable to return?”

It is not a question Conju has ever considered, the kind of forward-thinking he leaves to Cliopher. But Cliopher isn't here. He thinks back to how his lord was behaving, what he was saying. “His Radiancy takes his duty very seriously, he would not abandon his position. More than anyone, he is aware of how much he is needed.” Conju doesn't say how hollow his lord looks when he has been working late for days on end. He doesn't say that he can see how anxiety eats at his lord faster than Conju can feed him. He doesn't mention Woodlark.

There will, of course, come a day when his Radiancy passes away. Unless the Ouranatha manage to actualise their apotheosis. Quietly, Conju wishes to see neither.

“Very well. I will do this thing my brother has asked of me,” says the Grand Duchess, “We will do it as simply as possible. I will open the court session, and close it. Otherwise, the upper and lower dais will remain empty.”

Containing his shock at the change of address, Conju says, “My most sincere thanks, your grace. I will coordinate with the Master of Ceremonies and the Palace staff, and send you regular updates. If you have any need of me, do not hesitate to send word at any time of the day or night.”

“Thank you, Lord Conju,” she says, and Conju thinks her smile is more than just polite, “My brother needs you more than I do, please do not let me keep you.”

Conju bows, and leaves. He walks carefully back to the Imperial Apartments. Not too fast, not too slow. Yes, he has somewhere to be, but it is only important, not urgent.

His Radiancy has left the official study. There are no guards outside the private study, which Conju had half expected there to be. So his lord must be further in. Conju sweeps through, watching and listening. He suspects he knows where he will find his lord.

Conju is not wrong. The guards stand outside the small sitting room where his Lord's trancing couch is. A nervous attendant hovers by the door.

“Lord Conju, _he_ said not to disturb him, but the pages sent a note from Commander Omo,” the attendant says, clearly glad to hand over the note and the decision-making to someone else.

Taking the note, Conju instructs the attendant to prepare him the usual equipment to rouse his Radiancy from a trance. He may not need it, but it is better to request it now if he does. Conju opens the note from Ludvic, suspecting the Commander has not actually intended it for his Radiancy directly.

> Ysthar and Alinor ambassadors have agreed to help. Rhodin will reach out to contact on Voonra. Offices of State will try to find if any merchants have contacts on Colhélhé. Does his Radiancy think it likely he landed outside the borders of the Empire?

Yes, this note is for him, not for his Radiancy. Conju silently enters the room.

His Radiancy is lying on the couch, neatly arranged. Absent. Conju will never not find it slightly eerie, the way his Radiancy's body looks when his soul has left. He will also never let anyone know he feels that way.

If his lord has not roused by midnight, Conju will bring him back. Then attempt to convince him to go to bed, and hopefully get some sleep. It is likely to be a long night, he does not have anyone he trusts to watch his Radiancy. Except Ludvic, and Rhodin, who are needed elsewhere.

Conju has no magic of note, no skills as a spy or a diplomat, no idea even how to organise a search party. All he can do is try to make his lord comfortable, to do his best to keep every other worry at bay, so his lord can work. As he dims the mage lights to the level his Radiancy prefers when he comes back from a trance, Conju wishes he could do more.

His Radiancy returns on his own. Conju can see the moment the soul returns, and he sets out those things which ease it. Fragrant tea, ginger biscuits, a superbly soft lap blanket. He lights a beeswax candle, less refined and with a rich honey scent.

Long, thin fingers, flex gently against the blanket. The imperial nostrils flare, once, twice. Eyelids flutter, and Conju looks carefully down.

A deep breath. A small stretch. His Radiancy is present again.

“Any news?” he asks immediately.

“Commander Omo reports the Ystharian and Alinorial ambassadors are eager to help, and hopes to soon contact resources for Colhélhé and Voonra,” Conju pauses before choosing his wording carefully, “Your sister, the Grand Duchess, my lord, has agreed to hosting court when needed for the rest of the session.”

His Radiancy lets out a small puff of breath, and picks up a biscuit. “Thank you, Conju,” he says. He sucks on the biscuit for a few moments. 

“Commander Omo was wondering, my lord, if it was more likely that Cliopher is within the bounds of the Empire?” asks Conju, hesitantly. Often it takes longer than this for his Radiancy to be able to answer questions. Conju hopes that perhaps his lord has learnt something in his trance.

Then he sits up suddenly. “Yes,” says his Radiancy firmly, “In fact, it's probably Alinor. It *might* be Voonra or Colhélhé. The time difference on Ysthar has given it quite a different flavour. Yes… there was something of the echoes of schooled magic.”

Conju bows, and gives the guards a speaking look. They open the door, and pass the word on. The guards will find Ludvic quickly.

Gently, Conju coaxes his lord to eat three biscuits, and drink the tea. It is only a white tea, so late at night, with jasmine. When his Radiancy's complexion looks normal again, Conju relaxes a hair. If he has been doing a very great work of magic, that can be slow. This trance must not have been too strenuous.

His Radiancy seems disinclined to eat the fourth biscuit, so Conju dismisses offering more supper. “Would you like to retire early, my lord?” he asks instead.

“That would probably be wise,” says his Radiancy, “If I am going to drift off while reading, it would make sense to do so in bed.”

He leavers himself off the couch, and stands up. Conju does not mention his lord is still clutching the lap blanket. It has been a long and stressful day.

Since his lord is arrayed in the more simple clothes he wears in the apartments when no company is present, Conju is able to dismiss the other attendants. He feels his lord is more vulnerable tonight. 

Discreetly he places a stand next to his lord, for when he is ready to release the blanket. As quickly as he dares, Conju wields the dressing wands. Somehow, when his Radiancy steps off the stand, he is once again clutching the blanket. Of course, Conju says nothing, only walking behind his lord so he can close the bed curtains.

“Thank you, Conju,” says his Radiancy, again.

“My pleasure, my lord,” says Conju with a deep bow. He pulls the curtains closed, and dims the lights so they are only barely enough for him to not walk into anything.

Although, after so many years, would he walk into anything, even if it was completely dark? He beckons the night attendant to their position behind the screen. Then he makes his way to his workroom.

It is much later when Conju himself turns to the daybed and sighs. He has organised enough, he thinks. In his itchy eyes and heavy hands he can feel the creeping tendency to make mistakes. At morning shift handover, he can explain to everyone what is changing.

Conju carefully strips off his robes, and hangs them up. Then he climbs onto the bed, and covers himself entirely with the blanket. Finally, where there is nobody who can see or hear him, where he will not be disturbed for at least an hour, he lets himself cry.

What a fucking mess Cliopher has got himself into this time.
