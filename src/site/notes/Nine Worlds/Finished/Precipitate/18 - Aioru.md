The flagship was the last of the sky ships to dock at the palace in the Vangaveye-ve. Unlike the dock at the Palace of Stars, it could only fit one ship at a time, and naturally the priority had been securing the provincial records and the palace itself. Nobody wanted anyone loyal to the Princess trying to destroy documents or incite rebellion.

So Aioru disembarks some hours later, as part of his Radiancy's entourage. Across the bridge, down the tower, and into the main hall. Where there is, of course, a throne for the Emperor. Previously always empty, just a symbol of his power. In the hall await him a crowd.

As his Radiancy processes in, the people in the hall fall into the obeisance, and the guard cordon holding them back salute. His Radiancy says “You may rise,” as he walks to the throne, and sits. The hall is silent. Most of the people have got to their feet again, but a small group up near the dais have decided not to. Have they already been charged?

“We are here to investigate the violations of the Littleridge Treaty by Princess Oriana,” says his Radiancy, voice clear and carrying, “We will not hold anyone guilty by association, nor for failure to report what they did not know.” Aioru winces inside, he's read histories of the Empire of Astandalas. It wouldn't be outside the bounds of precedent for the Emperor to decimate the population and execute the entire palace staff. He is glad the Empire is gone.

Looking around, Aioru can guess that many of the nobles will want audiences to assure his Radiancy that _they_ knew nothing. He spots a card-table set up at the edge of the room, with four chairs but no cards. Catching the eye of one of the many guards, he starts carrying a chair up to the edge of the dais. The guard gets his meaning, and carries the table along behind him.

“We will hear petitions and confessions,” says his Radiancy, just as Aioru sits down.

“Please organise a queue,” says Aioru to the guard who brought the table. While the various people who consider themselves important bicker about what order they should be in, Airou has a moment to get out his pens, ink, and paper. There's actually not that many people, although a few more are creeping up from the general crowd to join the back of the line.

Most of Princess Oriana's staff from the Palace of Stars have already been dealt with by the guard on the flight over. However, her seneschal, who looks like yet another branch of the Jilkano rulers, is first to talk to the Emperor. Aioru diligently takes down them man's mostly empty prepared speech. His Radiancy lets the seneschal go on for some minutes professing his loyalty and ignorance, before holding up a hand for silence.

“Are you aware of the duties of a signatory to the Littleridge Treaty?” asks his Radiancy, quietly.

The seneschal looks around, biting his lip. It's an excellent question. If he says no, then he's incompetent; if he says yes, he's a traitor.

“Some of them, Glorious One,” he says, his voice less confident than when he was declaiming earlier, “I am responsible for the tithe collection for your illustrious self.”

“Who was responsible for publicising the proclamations from the Throne of Glory? You?” asks his Radiancy.

Aioru looks closely at the man, who is sweating heavily. “Glorious One,” the seneschal says, very quietly, “That would be the responsibility of the secretary.”

“Who is your brother,” says his Radiancy, serene as always, but such words can only be cutting.

“Half-brother, Glorious One,” says the seneschal, and falls to his knees, “Please have mercy, it was wrong of me not to make inquiries, but I swear, Glorious One, it was only because I assumed her highness could not be so foolish.”

His Radiancy waves his hand, and a guard pulls the seneschal back up to standing. “We hold no one for the crimes of their relatives,” says his Radiancy, and Aioru wonders how his lord feels about the Imperial Family, “If you will testify what you know, and nothing else comes to light in the audits, we see no reason to hold you. Do not leave the city.”

The seneschal performs his obeisance again, and backs out of the hall, looking relieved. The line progresses. One person in the queue confesses to an unrelated minor theft, apparently out of sheer terror at the Presence. His Radiancy asks the very young man to return the spoon, and tells him not to do it again. Aioru wonders if his Radiancy is hiding a smile, Aioru certainly is.

As the queue thins, Aioru wonders that there seem to be no bureaucrats at all in the palace. There are plenty of servants, kitchen staff, even a somewhat ancient priestess of the Imperial Cult. Did the seneschal count the tithes _himself_? Does the city council manage all local taxation? What data are the budgets Aioru reviewed based on? Surely _someone_ has been managing this province? He knows there are reports returned by the various industry bodies, who has been reading them?

He scribbles a note, asking Lord Conju very politely if _he_ has found anyone who might have information on how the provincial government has been run. Surely the exceedingly capable and rather intimidating Groom of the Chamber has already taken charge of the palace staff. He folds it up neatly, and asks a guard to pass it on.

Not much later, a very nervous looking young woman in a clerk's robes approaches him. He nods to acknowledge her, as he scribbles shorthand notes for the last of the petitioners. The head gardener is asking for permission to continue with the plantings. His Radiancy actually seems interested in the descriptions of the plants and arrangements, but perhaps in deference to the woman's age or her obvious fear, he lets her go quickly with his full support.

Commander Omo approaches his Radiancy next, and so Aioru has a moment to shake out his hand. “Yes?” he says to the woman, who is twisting her hands in her robes and watching the throne with wide eyes.

“Sir,” she says, nervously, “I was sent by Lord Conju, I believe you asked for me? I'm the accountant, Dala Kindraa.”

“I'm Aioru, from the Offices of State in the Imperial Bureaucratic Service,” he says, not unkindly, “Who did you report to?”

“Um,” she says, “Officially the Princess's secretary, of course. In practice, I helped the seneschal with the tithes, and sent the accounts through to the secretary. But I never once got a note back about them. I do answer some enquiries from the Secretary in Chief of the Offices of the Lords of State… do you know him?”

Aioru does his level best not to react dramatically to this question. “Sayo Mdang?” he says, as calmly as he can, “Yes, of course. He is the head of the Imperial Bureaucratic Service. How is the property tax collected, then? And the import duties?”

“Property taxes are assessed and collected by the city and island councils, and import duties are managed by the port authority,” says Saya Kindraa, sounding like she's more confident with this question, “As with the tithes, it is portioned between the local authorities, the princess's household, and the mundial government. Would you like to see the ledgers?”

These would presumably be the originals of the ones Aioru reviewed in the Archives in the Palace of Stars. “So education, policing, waste collection… is all managed by the city or island councils?”

“Yes sir,” says Saya Kindraa, with a smile, “Although the hospitals are overseen by the Department of Health, and the university is managed by the academic senate.”

“Thank you,” he says, his mind whirling, “I am sure the audit team will be looking for your cooperation.”

“They've kicked me out of my office and told me not to come back for a week,” she says with a shrug, “If you need any help…”

“Can you read my shorthand?” says Aioru, and spins around his last page of notes.

“Yes sir,” she says, looking it over slowly, “You'd like these written out longhand? And sent to the legal team?”

“Please,” says Aioru, looking up at his Radiancy, wondering what was going to happen next, “Oh, drat, I need you to swear to the Secrets Act first.”

She looks at him curiously, “What, sir?”

“You'd be a temporary contractor, dealing with sensitive records...” he looks from Saya Kindraa to his Radiancy, “I don't suppose you ever sat the Imperial Service exams?”

“The what, sir? I don't think so,” she says, nervous again, “I have my accountancy license at home, sir, if you need to see that? Or my university degree?”

“Sayo Aioru,” says his Radiancy, and Aioru startles and drops his pen.

“Yes, my lord?” says Aioru, carefully cleaning up the ink drops with a rag, while keeping his eyes on his lord.

“You would like the assistance of this clerk?” says his Radiancy, “Who appears to be the only member of the provincial bureaucracy?”

“Yes, my lord,” says Aioru, realising that his Radiancy has heard every word of their conversation. And so must know exactly who has effectively been running the province, since it clearly hasn't been the princess or any of her staff.

“Did you swear an oath of service to Princess Oriana, Saya?” asks his Radiancy.

“N-no, Glorious One,” says Saya Kindraa, clearly uncertain if she should drop into the obeisance, “I responded to an advertisement in the Ring-o-News.”

“How very modern,” says his Radiancy, “It seems then, that with the arrest of Princess Oriana and her secretary, you are the only remaining member, and thus head, of the Provincial Government of the Vangaveye-ve.”

At this, Saya Kindraa does actually drop to her knees.

“That should be sufficient, Sayo Aioru? Unless you have reason to suspect the entire government?” says his Radiancy.

“Of course, my lord, and thank you for your very wise advice,” says Aioru. His Radiancy is perfectly correct, the Littleridge Treaty established that the provinces are allowed to appoint their own governments following local traditions. If Saya Kindraa is still a member of the government, he can work with her on that basis.

His Radiancy turns back to Commander Omo. Saya Kindraa finishes the obeisance, and gets back up to join Aioru at the card table. One of the guards has kindly brought over an extra chair for her.

Shortly after, Lord Conju himself arrives in the hall. “Is everything well, Conju?” asks his Radiancy.

“Yes, my lord,” says Lord Conju, looking as calm and collected as always, “Would my lord care to take luncheon outside, perhaps? The view from the gardens is most attractive.”

His Radiancy pauses, with a small smile, “Very well, Conju,” he turns to Aioru, “We will adjourn for the day. Tomorrow morning we should see representatives from the local governing bodies, and in the afternoon the initial reports from the legal and audit teams.”

Aioru stands, as his Radiancy does. Saya Kindraa scrambles up slightly after him. They both perform the obeisance as his Radiancy leaves the hall, followed by his guards and Lord Conju.

“Is he always like that, sir?” says Saya Kindraa, pulling herself up.

“Is who like what?” asks Aioru, absently, wondering how he's going to contact suitable 'representatives'.

“The Emperor… is he always so…” she gestures in a big circle, but doesn't complete the statement.

“Yes,” says Aioru, and picks up his papers.
