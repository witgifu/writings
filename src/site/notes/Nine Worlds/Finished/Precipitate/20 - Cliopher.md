Cliopher hears the door snick shut behind his friends and colleagues. Conju promised he had relayed his Radiancy's exact words, and he has an excellent ear for such things. But a small part of him is still afraid, is missing the support of having Conju and Rhodin sanding behind him.

His Radiancy is staring at the door, and Cliopher realises he is not at all serene. He looks stunned. “The guards left,” he breathes, “I didn't… I haven't tried in… centuries I suppose…”

“My lord,” says Cliopher, still kneeling, “Should I go and call them back?”

“No! No. Let them be,” says his Radiancy, looking back down at Cliopher, “Oh, I am _so_ glad you are home safe, Cliopher.”

“I am glad to be back, my lord,” says Cliopher, unable to remove what must be a silly grin from his face.

His Radiancy _flops_ down into a chair, something Cliopher has never seen before. “The floor must be hard, Cliopher, and there are plenty of chairs,” he says.

Cliopher obeys the implied command, and stands, before selecting the chair next to his lord. He does it without thinking, as if somehow without the guards, and with this relaxed posture, his lord has become someone else. A colleague, a friend perhaps, an equal.

“I'm sorry,” says his Radiancy, and Cliopher pulls himself back from thoughts he has tried to suppress for years, “I very nearly ruined everything. I had to ask Melissa to hold court, I couldn't face all those people who didn't care. I barely held it together through the Council of Princes session; I think the Prince of Western Dair believes I'm furious with him. All I could do was sit there like a statue, staring into space. You were gone, what, three weeks? You've had longer holidays than that.”

This last statement is true, although not for a long time. After his lord granted him use of a sky ship for personal travel, he has been able to spend more time at home and far less time travelling. He focuses on that, because otherwise he has to think about how the Lord of Rising Stars is apologising to _him_. It is theologically impossible for the Last Emperor to be wrong. Even if you're not an adherent to the cult, it could be treason to say that he is.

“I'm sorry I didn't leave you with a better protocol, my lord,” says Cliopher, slightly stunned, “But it sounds like you were able to improvise.”

There is an awkward pause. Cliopher finally decides, in this weird liminal space inside the Port Authority building, to change the topic of conversation. “My lord, why did you come to the Vangaveye-ve?” he asks.

His Radiancy smiles, “You will be proud of me Cliopher, we are doing an audit.”

“An audit, my lord? Of what?” asks Cliopher, confused.

“The Provincial government,” says his Radiancy, with relish, “So far the Princess and her secretary have been arrested for violations of the Littleridge Treaty, and her seneschal for embezzlement.”

He can't help it, Cliopher's mouth falls open. “My lord?” he asks, his voice shaking.

“Yes,” his Radiancy continues, “It seems the only people governing this province that haven't been engaging in criminal activity are you, and one Dala saya Kindraa. Princess Oriana hadn't published a single one of our decrees.”

Cliopher has the strange feeling that the 'our' there is plural rather than imperial. He supposes he must look like a stunned fish. He _feels_ like a stunned fish. “I had no idea, my lord,” he protests, “Please believe me, if I had any inkling of such misconduct, I would have brought it to you at once.”

“Of course you would have,” says his Radiancy with confidence, “I remember when you tried to return your pay rise, worried it was bribery.”

Cliopher sighs, he is never going to live that down. Even if it was after rebuffing many _actual_ bribery attempts, some of which ended in arrests.

Suddenly, a few threads of their conversation weave together for him. “My lord,” he says, “When the audit is finished…”

His Radiancy's face stills. “Yes? I suppose we will have to urgently appoint a new provincial ruler.”

“Well, not really,” says Cliopher, awkwardly, “Saya Kindraa did all the required day-to-day work. The rest of the palace staff just handled the building and the Princess's endless rounds of parties.”

His Radiancy smiles, a mischievous twinkle in his eyes. “And you handled the budgets and paperwork in Solaara,” he says.

Cliopher bites his lip, “Yes, my lord. But my point was, that once the audit is finished, you would not need to return to Solaara immediately. Court has been dismissed. You could take a vacation.”

“A vacation?” echoes his Radiancy, but Cliopher cannot read any emotion on his face.

“If you would like, my lord,” says Cliopher, “There are many beautiful islands here, you could spend some time away from court.”

“Would you come, Cliopher?” asks his Radiancy, softly.

“Of course, my lord,” says Cliopher, blinking back what must be a reaction to dust in the building, “I would love to.”
