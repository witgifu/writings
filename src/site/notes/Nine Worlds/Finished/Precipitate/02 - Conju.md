Conju _had_ been serving his Radiancy a pleasant supper. Until his lord had turned a disturbing sort of purple colour (which could be hard to distinguish, on skin as dark as his, but Conju knew that skin well). Then his Radiancy had stood up abruptly, dropping his eating sticks to the _floor_, and left the room.

No, that wasn't the correct term. Run. He had run from the room. Had run to the study, and then outside. Surprising the guards as well.

Conju had missed seeing the first few moments, but had felt the positive wave of magic his Radiancy unleashed. Terrified of what could have caused the Lord Magus and Last Emperor such alarm, Conju had run too.

Now, they stood, staring out into the night. Conju carefully does not look at the guards, who are carefully not looking like they are concerned that their charge has lost his mind. Watching his lord more carefully, Conju thinks he is having trouble breathing.

Back in the study, Conju quickly gathers a tray with a carafe of water and a gold-rimmed glass. Then he darts back outside, hovering as close as he dares, trying to offer without interrupting. It is always possible that something truly terrible had happened; volcanic eruptions, landslides, or something of a more magical nature. Conju doesn't want to interrupt the Lord Magus in the middle of saving the world.

His Radiancy's eyes are closed tight, his breathing laboured. Conju is reasonably sure he isn't in a trance. And Conju is fairly sure his lord is fit enough not to be so injured by a short sprint through his apartments.

Finally, Conju resorts to a small cough. His Radiancy opens his eyes, and seeing Conju, glances down to the tray. He grasps the glass with both hands, shaking slightly. As soon as he has it, Conju takes a step back.

“Did you see—” says his Radiancy, drinking half the glass of water in a single gulp, “—anything?”

“My lord?” says Conju, unsure what he was supposed to have seen.

“Falling?” adds his Radiancy.

Conju refills the glass, while his lord holds it. The shaking has stopped. “No, my lord,” says Conju, and then adds honestly, “Although I was not looking.”

He looks now, and sees the same view as always at this time of night.

Pikabe speaks up, “I thought I saw something, my lord, maybe a bird launching from the roof?”

His Radiancy shakes his head. “Bigger. Send someone up, to check.”

Pikabe salutes, and drops back into the study to pass on word to the door guards. There are guards on the roof, Conju remembers. Although what they were guarding against, he was never clear about. Pirate sky ships dropping out of a cloudy sky?

Conju refills his lord's glass again. He spots one of his assistants has made it to the study, and signals to her for wine, water, and snacks. His Radiancy did not finish his supper, and now Conju will need to try to ply him with titbits when he is not expecting it.

Slowly, Conju manages to subtly offer his lord a chair, and then a glass of wine. His Radiancy slumps (there is no other word for it, and Conju finds this distressing) into the chair, and cradles the glass of wine without drinking. He will not take his eyes off the roofline, scanning it again and again.

It is hard for Conju to tell when his lord would like to be left alone with his worries (not that he is ever truly alone, but he is burdened by the weight of the world), or when he would rather have silent company, or distraction. They are waiting now, to hear back from the guards, and there is no sign that his lord is still working magic. So Conju decides to risk the final option.

Normally he would offer light gossip, or tales from the more outlandish news sources, but he feels now is not the moment. “My brother had a tame bird when we were young, that couldn't fly,” he says softly, “He found it under a tree, and took it to the gardeners. They told him to drown it of course, but he refused. He insisted on them teaching him how to feed the poor thing. It used to sit on his shoulder, wherever he went.”

“Like a pirate in a story,” says his Radiancy.

A knot in Conju's heart loosens. “I am sure no pirate of fame or story ever had a sparrow, much less one as ugly as that was.”

His Radiancy lets out a small sound, which might be a laugh or a cough. He takes a sip of wine, continuing to ignore the plate of small savouries Conju had placed next to him.

“The governess made him wear a shoulder pad, so it wouldn't mess all over his clothes. He would feed it whatever he liked least at any meal,” says Conju, with fondness.

“Do sparrows do well on spinach and mushrooms?” asks his Radiancy, softly.

“Well enough,” says Conju, “Our tutor also believed fermented beans were important for digestive health. But he could force none of us to eat them, not even the sparrow, no matter how often he tried.”

Conju glances into the study, and sees Ludvic (face white) and Rhodin (face pink) talking with a young guard he did not know. Something is terribly wrong.

Thankfully, they do not linger long in the study. Conju moves aside to make way for them before his Radiancy. And if that is perhaps a little closer to his Radiancy, well, there is not so much space here.

Ludvic genuflects before their lord. Conju's eyes go wide. He has never seen a guard kneel rather than salute. And here is the commander of the guard, on one knee before his Radiancy, his face still pale. Conju has not seen him look so disturbed, except perhaps at Woodlark.

Conju can also tell his lord has tensed. He knows his staff, his guards.

“My lord,” says Ludvic, “Sayo Mdang was on the roof earlier, admiring the view. The circumstances are not entirely clear, but it seems he slipped near the edge and fell. We have conducted an initial search, but have found no evidence below. Investigations are continuing.”

It is only with years of practice hiding his feelings that Conju manages to contain a whimper. Cliopher fell from the roof. It is too far, it is not possible to survive. That's part of the reason access to the roof is restricted. But Conju must not let his personal grief show. His Radiancy loves Cliopher, he will need Conju's support and care now more than ever.

His Radiancy takes a deep breath, and gives Ludvic the signal to rise. “You can call off the search for the body, Commander Omo,” he says, his voice controlled and serene.

Commander Omo stays where he is, but looks stunned.

“Or, at least, it won't be in the gardens. I hope,” his Radiancy pauses, and Conju notes the singular as his heart twists, “I believe, I opened a portal under him, as I slowed his fall. There wasn't, he couldn't have, I didn't—”

“*Thank you*, my lord,” interrupts (interrupts!) Ludvic. Gently, he asks, “Do you know where the portal might have sent him, my lord?”

His Radiancy takes a few moments to think. (He is not, Conju is certain, swallowing tears.) 

“Not anywhere on Zunidh. One of the other worlds. Somewhere habitable, not polar or the open sea. Likely within a short walk of a town or village, but probably not a city. On land, or,” and he pauses for a moment, before continuing, “Very near. Cliopher can swim, that should not be a problem. Somewhere that feels safe, so not an active war zone. Probably near someone trustworthy and helpful.”

This is an odd series of criteria. Conju is very thankful it will not be his job to try to turn it into maps and search plans. He will only have to try to coax his lord into eating and sleeping, which is not so different from any other time Cliopher has been away somewhere dangerous. Or when he went on the diplomatic mission to Ysthar.

“Thank you, my lord,” says Ludvic, and finally stands up. “If you gain any further insights on where the portal might have led, please let us know. I will co-ordinate with the Ambassadors here to begin with.”

“Cliopher had, *has*,” his Radiancy corrects himself, and Conju cringes inside, “A protocol for his own sudden disappearance or incapacity. We should action it.”

“Of course, my lord,” says Ludvic, once again in his usual calm tones, “I will send to Saya Kalikiri.”

His Radiancy nods, Ludvic salutes. The commander and deputy commander of the guard take their leave.

His Radiancy relaxes a hair, and looks at the glass of wine in his hand. Sipping it slowly, he looks out at the sky.

Mentally, Conju starts rearranging his Radiancy's schedule. The meeting with the Princes cannot be avoided, but Court sessions can be thinned to a minimum. No dinners, only afternoons or evenings. Unfortunately the easiest replacement for the Emperor is his Hands, but since both are unavailable, he may need to be strategic.

An idea occurs. While his Radiancy and the Grand Duchess try to keep her status as his heir quiet, perhaps she might be willing to help. If she could preside over some lesser functions, even just for a week or two, it would give Conju space to negotiate.

“Conju, we will probably need to do a great deal of magic over the next… while,” says his Radiancy. 

This is exactly what Conju expected. “Of course, my lord,” he says, “I will endeavour to cancel or postpone as much of your calendar as possible.” Pausing for a moment, gathering his courage, he makes an offer. “Since the Grand Duchess is visiting, I had wondered, my lord, if she might be amenable to hosting some court sessions.”

The Emperor gives Conju a most inscrutable glance. “We would not want to presume,” he says, calmly, “We know the Grand Duchess has always done any needful duty, but has desired to retire from court. But,” the Emperor pauses to look up, “Perhaps it would be possible to ask.”

He stands, and Conju slides out of the way. Collecting the tray, food, and drinks, Conju follows his lord back into the study. There, the Emperor sits at his desk, and pulls out pen and paper.

For a while, he simply sits there, staring at the paper. Conju places out the drinks and food again, hoping to tempt his lord, if he is thinking.

His Radiancy looks up at Conju with a small smile. His lord knows what he is doing. Conju takes a step back anyway, he must allow space for his lord to decline. His lord eats a cube of cheese, and a single roasted almond. Conju smiles back.

Returning to the paper, his Radiancy rapidly writes, nearly half a page without pause. Then he folds it carefully, and places it in the tray for outgoing documents. But he does not let go.

“Conju, could you please take this to the Grand Duchess?” asks his Radiancy, “And answer any questions she might have?”

There is only one possible answer, as much as Conju does not want to leave his lord’s side. “Of course, my lord,” he says with a bow. He takes the letter, and departs through the outer doors.

It is perhaps a little late for simply turning up at the door of the Grand Duchess’s apartments. With a sigh, Conju turns to the pages on duty. “Someone needs to run ahead, and I do mean run, to warn the Grand Duchess I am coming with a letter from his Radiancy.”

The three pages look between them, and one stands up and dashes off. Conju says to the remaining pair, “Inform the kitchen and wardrobe that Sayo Mdang has had to depart urgently, and his Radiancy will need to be engaging in much magic. As a consequence we will be reducing the schedule.”

Another page departs in the opposite direction. The remaining page looks to Conju with interest and a little anxiety. “Commander Omo has already informed Saya Kalikiri,” says Conju, with sympathy for the young man, “Anything else can be handled in the morning.”

He looks relieved. Conju hopes the rumours will not be too terrible. He walks sedately after the first page, nodding to people he knows that he passes. What the rumours would be if the Groom of the Chamber was seen hurrying to see the Grand Duchess, he does not want to even imagine.
