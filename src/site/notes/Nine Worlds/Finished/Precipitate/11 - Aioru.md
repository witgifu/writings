There is a flurry of activity when Saya Kalikiri returns to the Offices of State from the Imperial Apartments. She passes out documents into waiting hands, with a few words. “Please check the spelling,” and “Just do your best,” and lastly just, “Sorry.”

Aioru is left empty-handed. This is a mild surprise, as he had three days in a row of pained grimaces. These were accompanied by memo drafts that are one long run-on sentence. Even through his bosses' tidy shorthand, he can feel the Emperor's anxiety.

Of course, his Radiancy is not the only one who is anxious. Everyone in the Offices is, all the way down to him. Ever since Aioru arrived at the Palace, he has valued Sayo Mdang's careful comments and sage advice. The fact that the same man inspired him to come, makes it even stranger to be here when Sayo Mdang is missing.

Today, Saya Kalikiri's grimace is accompanied by a gesture to follow her into her office. Where she closes the door.

“I have two unenviable tasks for you, Aioru,” she says. When he goes to take out a pen and notebook, she shakes her head.

He raises an eyebrow in query. “Is this about Sayo Mdang, ma'am?”

“Indirectly,” she says, she moves behind her desk, but stays standing. “I need you to dig up an excuse for his Radiancy to visit the Vangaveye-ve, urgently.”

“Urgently, as in you need the information quickly, or as in the Emperor needs to leave immediately?” asks Aioru, hands itching for his notebook.

“Both,” says Saya Kalikiri, with a sigh, “Probably by the end of the day.”

Aioru's eyebrows shoot up. He turns to leave, if that's all the time he has he will need to—

“Aioru,” says Saya Kalikiri, and Aioru turns back with an awkward smile. “When his Radiancy leaves, he will need to take a secretary with him.”

Yes, of course. His Radiancy would usually take Sayo Mdang, leaving Saya Kalikiri here. But with Sayo Mdang gone… but she would have said 'me', if she had been expecting to go. Oh.

“Yes, ma'am,” he says, and swallows.

“If you're not in the Offices here, make sure to leave a note of where you have gone so we can send a page to fetch you, if required,” she says. “Is there anyone where you live who could pack for you?”

“Yes, ma'am,” he says.

“Wonderful,” she replies, with evident relief, “You have permission to ask the pages for assistance, or the guard if you require. But, you can't tell anyone where you're going.”

Aioru nods. That's not so unusual, field audit teams often can't say where they're going, nor anyone aiding in legal investigations.

Saya Kalikiri finally lets him go. Aioru rushes to his desk, and writes a quick note to the family he lives with. Another he leaves on his desk, saying he's heading to the Archives. Then he walks briskly downstairs, via the page station.

He starts with the office of the Common Weal, because that's what he knows best. It's also where he's most likely to find a complaint worth investigating, or a funding application in need of personal attention. Or at least marginally plausible.

There are *some* options, in the reams of complaints about noisy neighbours. He starts making notes; something about a fish-farm, an enquiry on turtle conservation. The local orchestra has received a number of grants, perhaps they could organise a concert?

What he finds as he goes deeper into the office's records, is rather more worrying. The account books for the office look forged. They're too tidy, too nice. In Solaara they wouldn't usually bother reviewing a provincial office's account books, unless the office was over budget. But they’re not, they are instead exactly one valiant under, every quarter. An audit team should be sent out. 

Inspired by the account books, Aioru has a look through the Provincial budgets. It’s slightly odd that they seem to have been approved without a single revision. The content looks perfectly fine, it’s just unusual. Even the most experienced members of the Council of Princes will need something tweaked. The only way he can think this would be possible was if the Princess was somehow convincing someone inside the Offices to make them. 

The budgets have him curious about what else the Princess is up to. Aioru starts looking through the accounts of the Princess’s palace. Then he heads to a completely different part of the Archives, looking for local periodicals. Finally, he digs out copies of the town council meetings for Gorjo City. It's not conclusive, but it's painting a picture. Not a nice one. In other circumstances, he might write to the secretary in the office of the Common Weal for confirmation. But with the potential corruption there, he doesn’t want to risk alerting anyone.

Then he stops. He’s been asked to find a reason for the Emperor to dash to the Vangaveye-ve. *If* Aioru is right, this is exactly what he was sent for. 

The quarter bell rings, he isn't sure what hour. He has been ignoring the bells, thrilled by the chase and discovery. It has been fun, on his own, in the Archives. When he leaves, and explains what he thinks is happening, it is going to become stressful. Aioru takes a deep breath, and returns all of what he’s pulled out to the re-shelving cart. Then he gathers his notes, and walks back quickly to the Offices.

Saya Kalikiri is still at her desk, he can see. He walks past his own desk, only slowing slightly to sweep the note that he is in the Archives into the waste paper basket. A pair of well-packed bags are taking his chair.

He knocks on the door frame to Saya Kalikiri's office. “Come in,” she says, and then looks up. “Close the door,” she adds, when she sees who it is.

“Success?” she asks, with a smile.

Aioru is sure she can read it on his face, but he says, “Yes, ma'am,” anyway.

“Will we need to send anyone? More than you, that is,” she asks, pulling forward a notebook.

“Yes. A prosecution team, and an audit team. I assume there will be Guards coming anyway,” says Aioru.

Saya Kalikiri leans back in her chair. “Corruption?” she asks softly.

Aioru nods, “Possibly also treason, ma'am.”

Her eyes narrow, “This isn't about Sayo Mdang is it, you know his Radiancy is…” She stops, Aioru is shaking his head.

“Who, then?” she asks, confused.

“Princess Oriana,” says Aioru, quietly.

“Shit,” she says, taking Aioru by surprise, “Sayo Mdang is much better than I at talking to his Radiancy about this kind of thing.”

She taps her pen on the desk, thinking. Aioru waits.

“You could—” she begins, at exactly the same time he says “I could—.” They both smile.

“You first, please, ma'am,” says Aioru.

“You could talk to Lord Conju,” she expands, “He knows his Radiancy's schedule, and when might be best to inform him of your… suspicions?”

“Yes, ma'am,” says Aioru, “We would need to seek direct evidence in the Vangaveye-ve. Unless the Princess confesses when asked.”

Saya Kalikiri shrugs, “She might, you never know. But that is for his Radiancy.”

“I will find Lord Conju,” says Aioru. 

Saya Kalikiri lets out a sight of relief. “I'll round up an audit team, and see who is available in the prosecutor's office.”

Aioru takes the back stairs he had learnt as a page. If he had been sent to the Emperor, he would be presenting himself at the formal entrance. As he is just consulting with the Groom of the Chamber, he can use other doors. There are, of course, still *guards* on the back door. But they don't stamp their spears. 

“I need to talk to Lord Conju,” says Aioru, “Urgently.”

The guard on the left says, “Work or personal?” His colleague immediately elbows him.

“Ignore the idiot. After Lord Conju broke up with his last boyfriend, this fool has been trying to milk everyone for gossip on who is next,” says the elbow-er.

“I will take that as a compliment on my maturity, then, and not a slight on Lord Conju,” says Aioru, “Probably don't let it get back to him that you think he'd be dating people half his age.”

The elbowing guard laughs, and opens the door. The other one blushes furiously, and says, “Um. Thanks. Sorry.”

Aioru shakes his head as he scurries through the back rooms. An enquiry with a kitchen hand suggests that Lord Conju will either be found in his workroom, or attending his Radiancy in *his* magical workroom. 

When Aioru finds the Groom of the Chamber's room empty, he steels himself, and turns back. Approaching down the hall is Commander Omo.

“Lord Conju is not in, Commander Omo, sir,” says Aioru, trying not to be flustered, “I was looking for him myself. I—” Aioru stops, unsure how to explain what he has learnt.

“You found a reason for his Radiancy to leave urgently for the Vangaveye-ve?” asks Commander Omo.

“Yes, sir,” says Aioru, “Possible treason. Probable, even.”

“Not Sayo Mdang?” asks Commander Omo.

“No, sir. The Princess.”

Commander Omo gives Aioru and uncomfortably long, silent, look. “That,” he says, eventually, “Will keep him busy.”

They walk together, in silence. Towards the closed door with two guards, behind which the Lord Magus is doing magic. Hand signals pass between the Commander and the guards, which Aioru doesn't know. The door opens a tiny crack, signals pass to the inside guards. By the time Commander Omo and Aioru reach the door, Lord Conju is slipping out.

The Groom of the Chamber looks at Commander Omo with one brow raised, and one hand with spread fingers. The Commander looks at Aioru, with a smile. Lord Conju lets out the tiniest huff.

“Thank you,” he says to Aioru.

Aioru, uncertain what conversation just happened between the Commander and Lord Conju, bows. “I am not sure that it is due, my lord,” he says, awkwardly, “But I did find something that will likely require his Radiancy's attendance in the Vangaveye-ve as soon as possible.”

“Unless you are about to tell *him* that the Wide Seas has fallen into the sea, with all of Cliopher's family and Rhodin,” says Lord Conju, taking a deep breath, “I hardly think it can be worse than simply leaving with *no* reason.” He pauses, and adds, “And of course, it is good that you found whatever it is now, and it won't be continuing.”

Aioru had rather hoped either Lord Conju or Commander Omo might do the actual talking. It's not that he feels quite the same way as Saya Kalikiri, who believes the Emperor is, in fact, a god. He has been in the Offices long enough to see the Emperor _around_, and has even once been the recording secretary at a meeting the Emperor chaired.

None of that is not the same as telling the Emperor that the ruling Princess of Sayo Mdang's province has likely been committing treason for years. Possibly because she doesn't like how much influence Sayo Mdang has. Everyone knows how much affection the Emperor has for his Hands.

On the other hand, his Radiancy has never once been known to lose his temper. At least, not since the Fall, which is the vast majority of his reign. Even in the face of terrible disasters, he has been the calm centre of the storm. However bad the news they have to share, his Radiancy has never even given a page an angry look.

Aioru nods. He can do this, and really when he thinks about it, he has to. There isn't even a report to present. If Lord Conju or the Commander did attempt to present the problem, they would both be just regurgitating what Aioru said.

Commander Omo gives Aioru's shoulder a small squeeze. The doors open again, a crack, and Aioru can hear the inside guards announce, “Commander Omo and Sayo Aioru.”

His Radiancy must signal for them to be allowed in, because the doors are opened. Commander Omo walks in front, and salutes at the appropriate distance. Aioru steps up next to him, and then folds down into an obeisance. His Radiancy quickly waves Aioru up.

“Are you about to tell me, Ludvic, that Rhodin and Oginu have left Gorjo City?” asks his Radiancy, picking up his teacup.

“Yes, my lord,” says Ludvic, stoically.

“I know, I saw them. They're on a boat with one of Cliopher's relatives,” says his Radiancy.

“Yes, my lord. Also, one of his friends, who knows the way to the portal,” says Ludvic.

His Radiancy nods, and turns to Aioru. Without thinking, Aioru looks him full in the face. He looks — tired. Worn. Aioru is used to seeing his Radiancy with Sayo Mdang, both of them vibrant, bouncing ideas off each other. Or in court, distant and resplendent. Not tired, nibbling on a biscuit, and sipping on a cup of tea. As if both were being forced on him, when all he wanted to do was sleep.

“What have you for us, Sayo Aioru?” his Radiancy asks, serenely.

“My lord, I was asked to do an audit of the Vangaveye-ve,” he begins, perhaps slightly less than completely truthfully, “It is very hard to prove a negative, my lord, but it would seem likely that Princess Oriana hasn't published a single one of your proclamations since her ascension.”

His Radiancy shows no response, and neither to the guards nor Lord Conju. Of course, they are all veterans of the court, they all know how to hide their emotions. Aioru is practically buzzing with tension.

“How did you discover this?” asks his Radiancy, as if perhaps Aioru has found a particularly clever answer to a riddle.

“I noticed, my lord, that while her palace accounts included many expenses related to balls and parties, there were none for public audiences. Not all Princes choose that venue for publishing proclamations, but it is common. So I looked for articles in the local papers and magazines, of public events where they might have been published, and perhaps some mention of the content. I found none.”

Aioru takes a breath, and tries to relax. He is just reporting what he has found, as he might in the early stages of an audit.

“Some provinces, especially where Shaian is spoken widely and literacy is high, simply post the proclamations in public buildings. But I could find no evidence of that in the papers either. Not even the Gorjo city council minutes mentioned them, even when they were discussing issues directly related. I looked especially for when the plans for the Sea Train to reach Gorjo City were proclaimed, but also for your most recent proclamations, my lord.”

His Radiancy nods. “Very thorough, thank you Sayo Aioru,” he says, evenly, “Have you informed Saya Kalikiri?”

“My lord, I told her I thought it possible that Princess Oriana would be charged with corruption and treason, and warned her to organise a prosecutor and an audit team,” says Aioru, now feeling on firmer ground.

His Radiancy nods, and turns to Commander Omo. “Prepare the warrant, we will seal it as soon as it is ready,” he says, “We do not want any of her people leaving unexpectedly.”

 The Commander salutes, and leaves the room. His Radiancy turns to Lord Conju. “Cavalier, we are afraid this is going to be rather an imposition. We must go with all haste to the Vangaveye-ve and root this out. Please notify the household.”

“Of course, my lord,” says Lord Conju, bowing. Then he too, leaves.

His Radiancy turns back to Aioru, “Saya Kalikiri will stay here, we will be disrupting the government enough as it is. Are you needed here, Saya Aioru?”

“I have nothing keeping me here, my lord. Saya Kalikiri suggested I might be a candidate for your substitute secretary, while you are away.” Aioru isn't sure if this is going too far, but his Radiancy doesn't offer any indication he is upset.

“Very good,” says his Radiancy, and rises from the couch. “We should probably start preparing now, reviewing the relevant clauses of the Littleridge Treaty.”

Aioru isn't sure if that is the imperial 'we' or the plural. If it is the former, it is certainly a dismissal; if the latter, he is being asked to do overtime. Nervously, he asks “My lord?”

“Yes, Sayo Aioru?” says his Radiancy, giving no hint if he is annoyed.

“My lord, it is well past the fifth bell, would you not have other plans for the evening?” says Aioru, desperately trying to ask politely.

His Radiancy becomes, if it is possible, even more still. Aioru suddenly realises he has just told the Lord Emperor to stop working for the day. He doesn't want to take it back, and he is slightly worried that if he attempts to apologise he will end up making things worse.

“Yes,” says his Radiancy, “You are correct, Aioru, we cannot spend a week secluded and then rush away leaving behind rumours of arrests made under imperial seal.”

Startled by the conclusion drawn from his words, Aioru looks up from his Radiancy's hands, to his face. There is, perhaps, the smallest hint of a smile in his eyes.

“Not what you meant? No matter, it is still true,” his Radiancy says, and then turns to the guards, “Inform Conju and my sister I will be attending court this evening.”

But when they open the door, Lord Conju bustles in, with another elegant bow. “You wish to do this publicly, my lord?”

“Yes. Ideally, with a witness statement. Do you know anyone in Gorjo City who might be willing and able to respond in less than three hours?”

“I will think on it, my lord,” says Lord Conju. For the first time in Aioru's memory, he is looking slightly flustered.

His Radiancy nods, and then turns back to the guards. “Send a page for copies of the most recent five proclamations, plus any major ones they find lying around.”

He turns back to Aioru, “Saya Aioru, if you would please help the Cavalier with the letter, as he will be short of hands. You will probably want the proclamations to send as examples.” 

Aioru bows, but doesn't get a chance to say anything before his Radiancy turns to Lord Conju, “Please consult with the sky ship captains on what would be the *best* time to leave. And thank you, Conju. We… *I* could not do this without you.”
