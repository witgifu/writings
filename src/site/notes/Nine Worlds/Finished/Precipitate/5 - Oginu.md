The office of the Commander of the Guard is stark. Not out of any sense of minimalism, or even practicality. Mostly, Oginu suspects, it is because none of the Commanders have ever thought to decorate it. It's a shame, a poster or two would really liven the place up. 

Unable to resist, he imagines sneaking in the poster for the play ‘A Thousand Nights’. It's a farce, where a mysterious Prince from Ysthar is continuously foiled in his attempts to proposition his loyal secretary. Oginu has seen it twice, although the first time he fell off his seat laughing when one of the Prince's guards kept interrupting with status updates about a water leak.

It isn't long before Commander Omo joins him and Ser Rhodin. Oginu is pulled from his happy recollections, and stands to salute his commander. Commander Omo salutes back, and they all sit down again.

“I don't have as much information as I would like,” says Commander Omo, and Oginu's eyes widen. This sounds more interesting than the investigation into the mysterious creature stalking the gardens, which turned out to be a lost chicken.

“His Radiancy has requested you to attend what, Lord Conju and I assume, is some kind of magical working. It may also involve travel, since he has asked also for a wilderness kit and a compass. You are to attend him at the first bell of the evening, in his workroom,” says Commander Omo.

Oginu doesn't understand why he's been invited to a magical working. He has very little magical sensitivity, just enough to feel a kind of itching when the Ouranatha do their rituals. An expedition, however, makes more sense. He likes hiking and camping, and sometimes helps train the recruits when they go to the Liaau.

Ser Rhodin gives Commander Omo one of the significant looks that often pass between them. Well, at least from Ser Rhodin to the Commander, who is rather less expressive. “This is about Cliopher,” says Ser Rhodin, seriously.

Commander Omo nods, once. Oginu hadn't known Ser Rhodin and Commander Omo were on first-name terms with Sayo Mdang. He feels slightly and briefly left out. Then he remembers that this is the Commander of the Imperial Guard, his Deputy, and the Hands of the Emperor. He is out of place.

Except, apparently, that he's going to be involved with this… whatever it is. Something to do with the search for Sayo Mdang, or at least so his commander believes.

Ser Rhodin nods back, and Oginu says nothing. If the spymaster has no further questions then neither does Oginu. Ser Rhodin is the senior officer in charge of thinking. Oginu is the junior who fetches and carries and stands around looking impressive. One of his favourite parts of being in the guard, really. That and the book club.

Commander Omo dismisses them, and in the corridor Oginu decides to show some initiative, and he asks Ser Rhodin, “Is there anything I should bring, sir? And would this be on duty in the full rig?”

Ser Rhodin looks at Oginu thoughtfully, and he resists the urge to squirm. Ser Rhodin is waiting for Oginu to answer his own question. “Half, sir, since we'll be in the Presence but on alternative duties? And don't bring anything since we don't know enough?”

A nod is the only answer Oginu gets. That's fine, Ser Rhodin is probably thinking about other things, important spymaster things about the search for Sayo Mdang. He shouldn't be bothering Ser Rhodin with stupid questions where he could have guessed the answer himself.

Oginu salutes, and returns to the training yard where Ser Rhodin had found him. Being hit by a colleague with a stick repeatedly will help it feel more like a normal day.

A quarter bell before the appointed time, Oginu presents himself at the Imperial Apartments. As a member of the inner guards he isn't challenged. That has always felt comfortable, but without the leopard pelts and body oil he feels odd. As if it's one of those nightmares where he's turned up in his nightshirt.

The workroom, when Oginu arrives, is nothing like he expects. There's no Ouranatha initiates sweeping away metaphorical dust or drawing salt diagrams. He can't see a single container of dried or fresh herbs, and there's no smell of incense.

There is, as promised, the expedition kit, unpacked and precisely arranged. And then baskets of some of the least magical items Oginu has ever seen. Sure, a half-pencil might be occasionally useful, but it's about as magical as his hair comb. 

Unless, perhaps, this is a magical half-a-pencil? Nothing about the basket feels itchy, but maybe the magic is quiescent. Perhaps if you write with it, it will only let you write the truth. Maybe the paperclip disguises the documents it attaches. Or the bottle opener keyring can tell you if your drink is poisoned.

Although, now that he thinks about it, how would someone get the poison in if the bottle is sealed? Tiny needles? More magic?

Spears stamp behind him, and years of drill turn him around and pull him up into a salute, while he is still wondering how easy it is to reseal a beer bottle. His Radiancy strides in, followed by Lord Conju, Ser Rhodin, and the usual rostered guards. Ogniu drops his salute and steps carefully to the side, out of the way of whatever these great men have planned.

“I have an idea for how to trace Cliopher,” says his Radiancy, mostly to Ser Rhodin and Lord Conju, but to his surprise Oginu is included too. “If he was on Zunidh, it would be easy, and if I was on Alinor it would not be terribly difficult. But I cannot go to Alinor,” says his Radiancy, and Oginu can see the tension in him. How badly he wants to go.

"Ser Rhodin, Guard Oginu, will you go for us?" he asks.

They both salute, crisply. Even if it were possible to refuse, Oginu wouldn't. An adventure! A quest even!

His Radiancy nods elegantly, "Thank you."

“Tracking a person between worlds is, theoretically, impossible. Unless you already have a strong tracing spell on them, like a blood oath,” his Radiancy pauses, and Oginu wonders if he is about to admit he has done such a thing. But instead, he continues, “However, since we already know he is on Alinor, we have a small advantage.”

His Radiancy begins to pace around the room. It is a familiar rhythm, exactly the one he would use when dictating to Sayo Mdang. Somehow, it makes Oginu feel more comfortable, as if this day is not so different from last week.

“Searching for Cliopher can thus be broken into three stages. Firstly, finding a safe open passage to Alinor, ideally somewhere near Cliopher. Secondly, finding Cliopher. Thirdly, and not at all least important, finding an open passage back to Zunidh. 

“Not all natural portals work both ways, or are open all the time, as we can see from the two well-known portals with Alinor. The larger portal opens in both directions at well known locations, but only twice per year. The smaller opens most days, but only has a stable location at one end. Neither are ideal.”

His Radiancy is in full flow now. Except unlike when he is dictating to Sayo Mdang, Oginu needs to pay attention to what his lord is saying, and ignore the room. It is decidedly odd.

“Now, there are many natural portals, indeed the Lord of Ysthar recently posited there may be an infinite number it is only that most of them are smaller than a grain of sand. In support of this he argued that the occasions where one can smell something no-one else can, may involve such a portal opening inside ones nose.” He stops briefly, and looks at his audience. 

“Well. Never mind the theory for now,” he says, and if such a thing were possible Oginu might say he was embarrassed. “I can put a spell on something so it can track Cliopher's writing kit. And lead you to a portal to Alinor that should put you near it. I can also make another to find a portal back to Zunidh.”

“My lord!” says Ser Rhodin, “That would be… that is… thank you.”

Lord Conju looks similarly dumbstruck. Oginu has never heard of magic like that. Of course, village witches often help find lost children or animals. But not across continents, much less between worlds.

His Radiancy nods. “Now, these are going to be highly dangerous and valuable magical artifacts. If they fell into the wrong hands...”

Ser Rhodin goes slightly paler, and nods. Even Oginu can imagine how valuable portal finders would be to pirates or drug runners.

“So they should be as innocuous as possible, and not inherently valuable. Something nobody would be surprised to find or interested to take if they mugged you,” says his Radiancy.

For a moment Oginu wonders how his Radiancy even knows what a mugging is. But of course he reads all Commander Omo's reports, and is the judge of the Court of Last Appeal. Even if he's never seen one, he has certainly read about them.

All four of them look at the baskets in silence for a moment.

“My lord,” says Lord Conju, “Will you need to touch the items to spell them?”

His Radiancy takes a few moments before answering, “It would likely be easier. Possibly more reliable.”

Lord Conju immediately removes two of the baskets from the worktable, and puts them in a cupboard. The taboos make so many things in his Radiancy's life difficult. It hasn't occurred to Ogniu before that it might limit his magical workings too.

“Ser Rhodin, Guard Ogniu, please choose two items each,” says his Radiancy.

Are they *each* being given a pair of incredibly powerful magical artefacts? Surely one set in Ser Rhodin's keeping would be enough. Ogniu likes the idea of being in a legend, but less so the potential reality of having his hands cut off for possessing evil spirit talismans by some hermit in a wood on Alinor. But he wasn't about to question the Emperor.

What would he normally carry? He wasn't a habitual note-taker, or particularly into lucky charms. A handkerchief was always useful, but what if he used it for its usual purpose by accident? He couldn't imagine handing back a soiled handkerchief to his Radiancy.

Now a bit of string, that was more like it. His uncle had always had a bit of string handy, and he had thought it funny as a teen. As an adult, he had come more to appreciate the wisdom. He put the string to the side of the basket.

The next item that caught his eye was a thimble ring. It was tin, and adjustable, the cheapest possible type. He knew the expedition kit included needle and thread, but found the one time he needed it that it didn't even have a leather thimble. It might be useful, so he put it next to the string.

Just in case one of those was unsuitable for some reason, he continued rummaging in the basket. He spots a protective rubber and nearly snickers. It's a very sensible thing to carry in a pocket, and not at all to be laughed at. In the end he settles on a wooden drink token, that one might win at a bar. He puts that slightly behind the string and the thimble, since it doesn't have any use really.

Ser Rhodin has also selected three items. A small grey pebble shaped like an egg, a nail, and a whistle. 

Oginu steps back, so his Radiancy can examine their selections. He can see Lord Conju make a pained face at the whistle from behind his Radiancy, and wonders if perhaps there was a mistake and the whistle should have been in one of the banished baskets.

“Don't fret, Conju, I won't blow the whistle,” says his Radiancy, as if he can see Lord Conju's face. He can't, he's looking more closely at the pebble. Is it a particular kind of rock, Oginu wonders? He has seen gems used in schooled magic, and he vaguely recalls something about the stones of the highways of the Empire holding down the magic.

His Radiancy pulls forward the pebble, whistle, and thimble. He looks between the string and the drink token a little longer.

Gathering all his courage, Oginu asks, “Should I make another selection, my lord?” He was rather hoping he'd get the string.

“What about this bit of red ribbon, Oginu?” asks his Radiancy, and pulls out a thin strand of crimson from the basket Ser Rhodin was looking through.

“Of course, my lord,” says Oginu, as if he could say anything else. The ribbon is _almost_ as good as string, and slightly better than the drink token. If he had more hair, it would be enough to tie it back.

His Radiancy picks up the thimble, looking it over. He says something that Oginu can't quite hear, although it seemed perfectly loud. Then to Oginu's surprise, his Radiancy puts the thimble in front of Ser Rhodin. “Keep that with you for at least a day and a night, then give it to Guard Oginu,” he says, and then turns to Oginu, “If you get separated, spin it on your hand, and it will point to Rhodin.”

Oginu is momentarily stunned. “Thank you, my lord,” he manages to stammer out.

The whistle gets the same unhearable word, and is placed in front of Oginu, who picks it up carefully.

“That's the same, in reverse,” he says, and gives Ser Rhodin a searching look. The guard nods. “Those won't work if you are on different worlds, so do at least try to stay together that much.”

Unsure if this is meant to be a dry joke or a serious warning, Oginu defaults to the easy reply. “Yes, my lord.”

“For your return ticket,” says his Radiancy, and picks up the pebble. This time he closes his eyes, and Oginu can feel that weird itching that says magic is happening. They all stand there for a minute, watching his Radiancy seemingly commune with a pebble. He puts it back on the table. “That one you can spin as well,” he says.

Oginu is curious how the ribbon will work. It's the last item, and presumably the most complicated, since it has to lead them to Alinor and then to Sayo Mdang's writing kit. His Radiancy picks it up, and then ties it in a complicated knot, and puts it back down.

“Pick it up,” says his Radiancy to Oginu, and he follows the order immediately. 

For a second he considers that that Lord of Rising Stars may be playing a joke on him, as he stands there, holding a ribbon tied in a sort of weird lump. But then, he feels it. The ribbon is tugging, like someone else is holding it.

“Oh!” he says, and looks down at his hand. Nothing looks any different. “I can feel it pulling to the west, my lord!” He looks back up, and in his excitement, accidentally makes eye contact with his Radiancy. It's allowed, it's safe, Oginu knows, but he's always been careful to respect the taboos. Mostly to make other people feel more comfortable.

His Radiancy's eyes are luminous. It must be the magic, he thinks, as his head begins to ache, and he quickly looks back down at the little red ribbon. That will show them the way to Sayo Mdang, so he and Ser Rhodin can bring him back to his Radiancy's side.

"Thank you, my lord," he says again, somewhat innanely. What else can he say? The Lord Magus and Last Emperor has given them four powerful magical objects, to aid them on their quest to rescue his loyal secretary from unknown distant lands. Well, maybe slightly known. Ser Rhodin grew up on Alinor.
