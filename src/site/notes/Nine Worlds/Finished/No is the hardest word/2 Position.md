I awoke to the sound of the dawn chorus, young voices raised in worship of the Sun. The Emperor slid from the bed, and was wrapped in a robe. Then he walked over through the throng to join his priests.

Slipping out the other side, I felt my shame inside me swirling. There was no one I could ask advice of, or confide in. I was alone, one step down from the Emperor, one step up from everyone else I had ever known. 

Could the priests and priestesses tell that the union had not been consummated? Would I be subject to their half-hidden looks of pity, or scorn? They could no more censure me than I could the Emperor, but they could judge in their hearts, and look to prepare another. There would be a race now, among our kin, to be able to supply the Emperor with a new Consort.

I would be dismissed in disgrace. Or worse.

No, I told myself, I must not give in to fear. I would try again, try to please my lord. He had not liked that I was drugged, I would present myself sober.

Two ladies wrapped me in a robe, and guided me out the doors. The hallway was crowded with people hoping to catch a glimpse of me looking dishevelled. I smiled at them all.

I was escorted up a flight of stairs to a room of my own. Or, at least, a room I would inhabit for now. Covered everywhere in yellow roses; from the rugs, up the wallpaper and upholstery, to the ceiling carvings and mage lights.

Finally, behind the closed door, the ladies stripped me of the ceremonial gown. I could read nothing on their faces, as I was sure they could read nothing on mine. A shift so fine it floated down over me, and then a silk dressing gown, covered in embroidered bees. Nothing of my old life.

I was taken to a fainting couch, and tucked under a soft blanket. Then they laid out herbal tea and rice porridge laced with cream, studded with dried currants. 

I recognised the scent of the tea, I had spent enough time studying the healing arts. Pain relief, and something to dull the mind as well. Had I left a night with Shallyr, I would have drunk it without hesitation. Knowing my every move was watched, judged, and reflected on me, and now the Emperor, I considered. I left the tea untouched.

After a spoonful of the porridge, I asked, “Would you know the schedule for today?”

Both ladies had retreated to the edges of the room. I addressed neither of them directly, hoping to learn by who answered which was the senior. The both bowed deeply, but the one by the door stepped forward.

“We await the Glorious One's instruction, your highness,” she said, her voice deep. I inclined my head to indicate that of course, we were all dependent on the whims of his Radiancy. She continued, “However, her royal highness, Princess Lamissa, and Princess Anastasia have both requested audiences, as has the Lord Treasurer, Lady Jivane, and several other notables.”

Who were not notable enough to be listed explicitly, and so I wondered who they might be. I would have to push to find out, which could be risky at this early and uncertain stage. Allies was what I needed first. Protection.

“I will see my mother, and then Lady Jivane, here. I will see my aunt and the Treasurer in the Lilly Room. Unless the Glorious One is using it?” I said.

“The Glorious One is expected to spend the morning with his secretary,” she said. I wished I had a secretary.

“After the Treasurer, I assume at least one Imperial duchesses has requested an audience? Whoever is eldest, please,” I said, trying to sound firm but not demanding. “Then I will spend the afternoon on my correspondence.”

After that, I would be washed, dressed, and carried down to court. It did not need to be said. 

They both bowed again, and I turned back to my porridge. I hated it, but that didn't matter. It was traditional for expectant mothers, and since I now lived to be pregnant as often as possible, it wasn't surprising.

There was no coffee, or proper tea. I wondered if that was deliberate, who was attempting to say they controlled me. In time, I would find out.

When I had forced down the porridge, I was washed and dressed again. A relaxed cut, for receiving 'at home', but the most sumptuous fabrics I had ever known. Not that I was 'home', back in Damara. I might never be back there again. I shoved down that thought as the ladies shoved my hair into the silk turban.

Once again, I waited. It was an art form, to simply be and seem content. My hands still, my body poised, but not stiff. My smile soft, relaxed, welcoming. There were no books in the room, no magazines, no work basket. Not even a music box. It was simply a place to hold me until I was needed.

With my mother, arrived the tea. I concealed how much relief that brought me. The tea, that is. I loved my mother, of course. But all she had taught me my entire life was based on the assumption that the Emperor would do *his* duty.

I could not tell her that he had not. That I had only slept in his bed in the most literal sense. No spark of hope was in my womb, only anxiety. She would be horrified.

We had many years of practice of talking over tea. A language of circumlocutions that we alone shared.

“My heart was so full to see you yesterday,” she said, as she picked up her cup, “And now, your highness, you are a woman made as well as grown.”

*I am sorry I was a wreck yesterday. I'm glad to see you well after last night.*

“Though I have flown your nest, Mother, I have not flown so far,” I replied, closing my eyes as I breathed in the scent of a truly fine tea, “The Most Serene and Gracious One has showed me naught but kindness, and I am truly blessed.”

*I understand. You know you are always welcome to see me. He was gentle, nothing like Shallyr.*

“May the Sun and the Moon bless the Glorious One, and through him the Empire. But, your highness, is this room not charming?” She indicated the wallpaper and lights with her pinky finger.

*Thank the stars. But why are you shoved into this hideous back room?*

I inclined my head, “All the roses of Astandalas, seemingly,” I said with a smile, “I look forward to seeing the many wonders of the Imperial Apartments. Tell me, is there anything particularly fine that I should seek out?”

*I know, right? It's awful. I have no idea, nobody has told me anything. Any advice? Who should I talk to?*

“The Glorious One had the entirety redecorated on his ascension, of course,” she said, taking a sip, “There was once a fine blue vase in the informal dinning room. His Radiancy's seneschal or his groom of the chamber may know if it is still on display, and can tell you of the many other treasures. Of course, they are very busy.”

*Naturally, the Emperor is who you should cultivate first. But you might have luck with the seneschal or groom. Make sure you are careful with them, they are not your friends.*

I thanked her, and we moved to topics we could talk of more openly. Never fully openly, of course, not in the Palace.

When Mother left, and Jivane came, the tea was refreshed. Jivane hesitated, after rising from genuflecting. I gestured to the seat next to me on the divan, and she took it with a twitch of relief. I hoped nobody noticed.

Our hands reached to touch, between us, not entirely hidden from view. I could feel a slight trembling in her, and realised she had been worried. For me? About us? Both entirely reasonable.

We talked of nothing. Of the tea, of the decor, of the recent trend for over-robes fastened with tiny buttons like grains of rice. As if we might have been anywhere, or it could have been three days ago, like nothing had changed.

Everything had changed, of course. I knew that I would be watched every minute of every day I was not in bed with my husband. If I was very brave, and very sure of my position, I might offer Jivane a hug. After I had given the Glorious One an heir, and a Marwn.

We were both afraid. The Princess Consort and the Lady Magus of Zunidh, terrified of holding hands. This was the Empire. What it required of us.

I was redressed again to go to the Lilly Room. My great-aunt was admitted first, being the more senior and also the more obnoxious of the two (although it was a fierce competition). She very reluctantly offered me the courtesy I was now due, under the watchful eyes of two guards, and two attendants.

There was no tea there, nothing to fill the hands or bridge the gap. And then, unlike two days prior, it was my role to begin the conversation.

“To what do I owe this pleasure of your company, Princess Anastasia?” I asked her, denying her both the honorific of 'your highness', and the relational 'aunt'. She knew I didn't like her, and it wouldn't have surprised her that I had abandoned any pretence at affection as soon as I could do without.

“Only, your highness, that I wished to congratulate you on finally being able to do the duty you were raised to,” she said. It was no secret that she had preferred Shallyr to the still-largely-unknown Artorin.

“Thank you,” I said, performatively, “It is my highest honour to serve the Empire.”

“As it is everyone's,” she answered, and I could see that she saw me as no better than any concubine the Glorious One might have taken. But I had the blood-right to a marriage, and I, unlike her, was young enough to marry. It would be a fleeting power, as she well knew.

I inclined my head. Then I made my frontal attack. “Do you have a request for me?” I asked, softly.

She looked at me sharply. This was not how the playbook went. I should introduce a neutral topic, such as music or poetry, to establish a shared basis of agreement. Then I should transition to asking after herself, and her friends. Finally, she might be able to introduce via that discussion, some personal request, or that of one of her cronies. She could play that game very, very well. I knew she could, and I was cutting her off before she could start.

“My *dearest* niece,” she said. Nothing in my face or body changed, but inwardly I recoiled. She should know that I wouldn't actually *believe* her affection, this must be a performance for the attendants. “I would not want to presume upon your kindness, but since you *asked*… I had hoped you might consider asking your most generous lord and husband about my dower lands.”

A masterful stroke, I had to admit. It was a perfectly reasonable request to make, except that she was entirely in the wrong. The Glorious One had told her on four separate occasions in the six months he had been Emperor that he would not support her case. She was trying to become a wedge between us.

I was not out-played. “The Glorious One is always most fair and just,” I said, smiling sweetly, “And I am but new-come to be his Radiancy's Consort. If he asks of me, I would of course advise my lord and husband to listen to any new petition you might make. But to make such a request unsolicited would be to presume too far on his good graces, and I must place my duty to his Radiancy's service first.”

“Of course,” said the Princess, looking like she had been given a rock instead of a sweet, “Thank you.”

I smiled, and offered no further conversation. She rapidly withdrew, without formal dismissal. Well, now the attendants could see a little of her true self.

Conversely, the Lord Treasurer simply wanted to show how important and powerful he was by taking up as much of my time as he could. Eventually, I cut him off, and invited in the ancient Duchess of Adroon. I had never met her before, and she was rather surprised that she had been given the honour of a reply to her request so quickly. 

She and I had a pleasant conversation about literature, and she regaled me with many stories of her unusually easy childbirths. I determined to add her to my regular list of people who could be safely invited to all kinds of events, being neither too boring, nor too self-important.

“I know it has only been such a short while, your highness, but I must ask: do you find married life agrees with you? I know many young folks who rush in to marriage, and I am so pleased that you have not, but I still worry, as an old woman always does,” she said.

I admired her skill in the question, and inwardly revelled in my first chance to seed the gossip myself. “I was nervous, as must any new bride be. The Glorious One is a most generous and kind lord and husband. As he is the Protector of the People, and so he has been mine, both gentle and understanding.”

The Duchess of Adroon practically *beamed* at me. “I am delighted to hear so, your highness, and I wish you good health and happiness.” It was the traditional blessing to offer a newlywed, but not the 'obedience and fertility', that was offered to a Consort. I wondered if she knew, I suspected she did. You didn't survive to such an age in the Court without knowing.

“Thank you very much for your kindness and blessings, your grace,” I replied. A gentle and polite dismissal, which she took.
