My brother gave me that choice. He was everything I had ever hoped for in a husband, everything I had ever dreamed of in a brother, everything one could desire in an Emperor.

I could only stammer, again, that it was his choice, that I was dutiful. I did not know how to say _no_, it was not something anyone had deemed necessary to teach me.

_Many years earlier_

It was a beautiful spring morning. Of course it was, because the weather wizards had planned it, had meticulously managed the rain and winds. The sun was warm, but just pleasantly so, and the flowers in the gardens were blooming perfectly. The breeze was fresh, and full of the scent of fruit blossom.

I was standing in the centre of the main receiving room, it being the only one large enough to fit everything needed. Clothes stands, tables of cosmetics, two dozen women preparing to dress me. Chatter flowed around me, as I stood on a plinth in only a shift.

Somewhere, in one of the private rooms, my mother was having hysterics. I wished I could join her.

I was washed and blessed by a priestess of the Moon. As every hair had been stripped from my body the night before, the astringent in the water stung, and the oil tingled. I did not twitch at all, only politely bowed when she had finished.

Layers of the finest fabrics, silk so sheer it could almost be used as window-glass, were waiting for me. Piece by shinning white piece they dressed me. With great ceremony, and many special prayers. Obedience, fertility, happiness. As if the third could at all be compatible with the second.

I was painted, eyes and lips, nails and palms, feet and toes. Gold paint, sparkling against my dark skin. Was I beautiful? Nobody would dare say otherwise.

Two or three hours in, my mother had composed herself enough to emerge. “I am so proud of you,” she said. She did not say she was horrified at herself, but I knew. “The people will love you,” she said. She did not say that my husband would not. It wasn't relevant, his love for me, or mine for him. Her attendants hurried her away to dress as well.

There were mirrors all around the room, and I carefully avoided looking in any of them. I caught a reflection once, on the back of a silver-handled hairbrush. The effect was indeed magnificent, but it wasn't me. It was a confection spun around me, a work of wizardry and wardrobe. I was a dress stand, a picture frame, soon to be a vessel.

When they finished, and I was directed to the palanquin, I could not actually move by myself. Two women had to carefully and constantly arrange the lower layers to make sure I didn't trip or catch any of it. If I had been wearing even an ordinary court gown, the palanquin could have fit myself and two attendants. In this, there was barely space for me. 

When I had been little, my father had returned from court with a doll for me. It had been made, supposedly, to look like my mother, copying one of her most famous court outfits. It was nestled in a wooden box, wrapped in silk and the box padded with raw silk, until the doll was unable to move. I was that doll now. Carefully framed in the window, painted, frozen.

Outside the window, there was probably cheering. Palace staff bowing, shouts of well-wishes. I knew from the papers and magazines how much everyone was looking forward to today. Except me, and mother. I sat in my box, and smiled at everyone we passed, indifferently.

It seemed two or three breaths, and we arrived at the enormous double doors to the Throne Room. The great doors, thrown open only for the coronations and funerals of the Emperors and Empresses. I would never be either. But, in a move that had almost given the Master of Ceremonies apoplexy, the Emperor had demanded that one door be opened for me.

Out of the palanquin, and into the direct gaze of thousands of eyes. Not unknown, but never comfortable. But then, what about my life was meant to be comfortable? Duty had little place for comfort.

Just as we had rehearsed, I took tiny mincing steps. Eyes cast down, smiling, hands in front. Three steps past the doors, a small white mat was placed for my first obeisance. Formal, elegant, sleeves fluttering like cherry blossom in the breeze. At this distance, not even the optical and magical illusions of the throne room could make visible the subtle gesture to rise that the Emperor gave. But the acoustics amplified the instruction.

“Rise, cousin,” he said, in the voice I had heard him perfect over the months of his reign. The address of imperial familiarity almost seemed mocking. Not my name, or my rank, nor even my true relationship.

_Sister, sister, sister,_ rang unspoken through my head.

I did not stop at the steps to the lower dais, but aided by sixteen maidens, met in the middle of the dais the high priests. Blessings, in Antique Shaian, and prayers to the Sun and the Moon. Then the priests divided, like water before me. The last barrier between me and my intended.

Down once more, now into the full formal prostration. My prayer, my oath, to the Sun-on-Earth. Amplified for everyone crushed into the room to hear. I could feel the weight of the bindings finding me, settling on me. Hand and heart, eyes and mouth.

Over my prostrate form, the Emperor gave his prayer to the Sun and the Moon. Then finally, finally, “Rise, Consort.”

The crowd erupted behind me. Cheers and shouts, as the music began. Presumably, I stood up. I only remember flashes of the rest of the day. The rituals had that effect sometimes, particularly the oldest and strongest ones. And what could be older than the bond of marriage?

I recall a few of the layers of my costume being removed at some point, so I could move unaided. Standing beside the Emperor, as my mother made obeisance to us both. A piece of music that must have been composed for the occasion, blasted out on trumpets. A sip of ceremonial tea. A biscuit.

Stepping into the palanquin, but not out. Being dried, but not undressed or washed. A scent of sandalwood and orange sprayed on my hair grounded me a little. Did I want that? Did I want to be here?

My oldest and dearest friend, Lady Jivane, tears running down her face. She stood in front of me, looking at me, really looking at me. As few did, as even fewer would from that day on.

I looked at myself in the mirror. Draped in diaphanous imperial yellow silk, that billowed and flowed. It left little to the imagination. My hair was pulled back and wrapped tightly in black silk satin.

“Highness,” said Lady Jivane, her voice betraying neither censure nor praise.

I turned back to her, my hands tingling. Had I eaten anything all day? I felt light-headed, and slightly dizzy. It could also have been the magic.

“Take this,” she said, and handed me a glass. It was cold, thankfully, and smelt like a herbal tea. The taste was awful, salty, and sweet and bitter. I threw it back, knowing exactly what it was. Then she passed me a glass of water, which tasted delicious.

I was escorted through the rooms, full of gawkers. Then into the Imperial Bedchamber. Onto the bed, my limbs and clothes arranged according to some standard or ritual I did not know. I waited, staring at the curtains.

Spears stamped, and the Emperor entered. I could hear a crowd chittering around him. Was this to be a performance? Surely he could be granted at least the illusion of privacy for tonight. The curtains were pulled back, and I looked into his eyes for the first time.

True lion eyes, of the Emperors of old. No magic, they said, but still. A powerful symbol. He climbed in, those eyes betraying nothing of his emotions. Behind him, the curtains closed, and the crowd dispersed.

He reached out, to touch my hand I think. But then I remember nothing more.

---

I woke, to see the Emperor sleeping next to me. His glow lit up the bed, like a mage light. I was used to seeing Eritanyr glow, and so my initial reaction was to freeze in fear. As I lay there, I examined him far closer than I ever had before. Tried to calm myself by noting every way he looked different from Eritanyr, from Shallyr.

My heart rate slowed, my muscles relaxed. I started to re-inhabit my body. Slumped down slightly, from where I had been arranged. And someone had pushed a pillow under my head. 

Slowly, I crept my hand down my body. The garments had not been much disturbed. I held my breath as I crept my hand over my hip. The silk was dry and clean. Not even wrinkled up. 

Something had gone wrong.

Despite all my years of training, at the gentle hand of my mother and the cruel hand of my cousin, I let out a small gasp. I flicked my eyes back to the Emperor, sleeping calmly, nestled under the blankets. He didn't wake, didn't so much as twitch.

Had he not been able? Did he not want to? I tried desperately to think of what I could have done wrong. It must have been the medication Jivane had given me. It was supposed to dull the senses and memory, but perhaps it had been the wrong dose. Had I been rude? Appeared drunk? Had I passed out?

I wished nothing harder than that The Red Company would break into my wedding bower now, as they had my betrothal party. There was one purpose to my life, one role I was to fulfil, one place I belonged. And I had failed it. If Faleron the Blue could sweep me away now, Pali holding back the guards, perhaps I could have a future.

But no, that was a child's dream. A consort knew better. I had to apologise. The only choice was whether I should try to gently wake him, or wait until he woke. I lay there for a long while, trying to decide, knowing that if I didn't then time would decide for me. If the Emperor slept through till dawn, I risked having to make my apology in front of whatever 'inner circle' of men were to witness today's waking.

Thankfully for my nerves, the Emperor rolled over and woke up on his own. He blinked at me, those eyes of gold. A look of confusion, and then surprise.

I did my best to arrange myself in the formal prostration.

Before I could say anything, he made the gesture to raise the Wall of Silence. Then he said softly, “I think someone drugged you.”

I wanted to giggle, to burst out laughing. Did he not know? Had he not met Eritanyr's consort, and seen how her mind had been damaged? Had nobody offered anything to _him_?

Of course, I let none of that out of the safety of my mind. “Most gracious lord,” I said, automatically in the high and slightly breathy voice I had learned placated Shallyr, “Please do not blame my women, it was at my own request. I humbly beg your forgiveness for not asking your permission first. I am in all things your humble and obedient servant.”

There was a silence, which I could feel cutting into me like a shard of glass. I felt the urge to fill it, but restrained myself. There was no need to blather, and give him another reason to disdain me. I waited for a sharp fingernail to dig into my wrist, a slap across the face, a pinch to my breast. Once I had so angered Shallyr by not obeying quickly enough, that he had pulled a penknife, and slashed my shoulder.

“You knew?” he asked softly, “Why?”

What a strange question to ask. I tried desperately to find the answer he wanted to hear. “Glorious One, the medication enables obedience when an inexperienced body might automatically resist,” I said, my voice steady but my mind shaking.

“Those drugs are used—they're not—people don't take them _consensually_,” he said, struggling. 

I didn't understand. There was no framework, no training, that matched with this conversation. All I had was that, theologically, it was impossible for him to be wrong. So I said, “Yes, Glorious One.”

“Please sit up,” he said, and I could hear the pain in his voice.

I obeyed, waiting for the pain to turn to anger. Despite now being protected from the taboos, I dared not look him in the eye. It could be seen as a challenge, and I must never be a threat. For if he could touch his Consort, then so his Consort could touch him. Always, the suspicion then, that they might lash out.

“You didn't want to remember?” he asked. Another question that felt like a trap.

“I was unaware that was important to you, Glorious One, I profoundly apologise and beg your forgiveness for your ignorant servant,” I said, my own voice sounding sick in my ears.

“You don't, it doesn't, I'm not…” he stopped, and took a deep breath. Then he said, in his court voice, “We make no requirements of you, Consort. Your body is your own, to do with as you will.” 

I could not believe what he was saying. Did he believe it? What did it mean if he did? My body was no more my own than the bodies of generations of the Imperial family before me. It belonged to the Empire, and he was the living embodiment of the Empire, and so I belonged to him. 

“If you would like to sleep, please do so. We will be awoken at dawn,” he said softly. Then he slid back down under the covers. As far as I could see, he seemed to at least be pretending to go to sleep.

I lay down, attempting to silently arrange the garments correctly. Listening, waiting for him to say something else, to do something else. Eventually the long day, or the drugs, or the magic, overwhelmed me, and I slept.
