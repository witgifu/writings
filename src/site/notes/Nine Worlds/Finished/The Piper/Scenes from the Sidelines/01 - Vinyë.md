When the tsunami alarm rang at the high school, Vinyë stood up from the piano, and put down the lid. "C'mon," she told Susan, who was fourteen, "It's probably only a drill, but let's do it properly."

Quickly, they picked up their bags, and left the little music practice room, Vinyë locking it behind them. No announcement came to say it was or wasn't a drill, which was unusual. But perhaps they had changed the protocols, and Vinyë had missed the notice. That happened a lot for any staff that weren't full-time.

Along the verandah of the building the other rooms emptied out as well. Vinyë and Susan joined the tide winding their way through the buildings, and out the back gate of the school. The path passed through the edge of the suburb, and then skirted through the university. Kids joked a bit, and someone started singing. But nobody slacked off, or ducked out to go to a café instead. Everyone in the Vangaveye-ve knew how serious a tsunami was.

It was hard to find an assembly area that fit all the students, so the different years stopped at the various lookouts along the path. Vinyë was appointed to join the youngest, which happened to include Susan anyway. Once there, Vinyë helped one of the Literature teachers organise the students so they could do a quick headcount, before a proper roll call.

Once they established that everyone who should be there was, Vinyë finally had time to look out at the Bay. Everything looked calm, and she couldn't see the tide pulling back. So perhaps it had been a drill. The Navy ship didn't look to have moved, although from so far away it was hard to tell if it had anyone aboard. 

The Literature teacher sent Vinyë to check with the other year groups, to see if any of them had more information. At the middle lookout, Vinyë found the head teacher, the deputy head, and to her immense surprise, Toucan Navan.

"So, this isn't a drill then?" Vinyë asked the group.

"No," said the head teacher, at the same time the deputy said, "Yes." They smiled at each other. "It's not a tsunami," said the deputy, "But it is a threat to the students. Vinyë, would you mind passing on word to all the years to settle in? We're likely to be here for a while."

"That's ominous," said Vinyë, "Can we tell them what's happening?"

"The Navy," said Toucan, "Is threatening to take people by force."

Vinyë nearly swore, but then blushed and covered her mouth. Even people who taught individual music lessons weren't supposed to swear in front of the students. But the deputy only smiled. "Quite," he said, and sighed.

"I'm going to head to the university, where I'm sure the Student Union will be organising some relief efforts," said Toucan, "I'll try to get people up here with supplies and more news as soon as possible."

_Supplies_ was also ominous, but Vinyë decided not to comment on that. Toucan gave an amusing half-salute to the head teacher, and then jogged back down the path to the city.

Vinyë did as she was asked, after all she was paid to work for the entire day. It wasn't like there were many music lessons happening today. By the time she'd visited every group once, a messenger had come with a transcript of the announcement. The head and the deputy both looked sick reading it.

"No fucking way," said the deputy, and didn't apologise when several nearby students looked at him. "I didn't just get told to go in from lunch," he said with a grin, "You're allowed to swear when it's justified." The students looked at him in awe.

The head teacher shook his head. "I'll collect everyone fifteen and under, and head back to the school," he said, "I'll dismiss them from there."

"What about the kids with older siblings?" asked Vinyë, knowing that many of them did.
