Unsurprisingly, he hadn't made it through the book by the time the noon bell rang, cutting through the silencing spells. Marking his place with the dust-jacket, he stood, and waited for someone to fetch him. It was the secretary this time, still looking harassed. Nervous, he offered her a slightly shallower bow than he had Lady Mairel, although he didn't even know if she was a noble.

She pursed her lips, and Cliopher assumed he had got that wrong, but he had no indication as to how. “Come along, Cliopher,” she said, and silently Cliopher did as he was told. They went past the galley, and what was presumably the mess hall, right to the back of the ship. Cliopher wiped sweaty palms on his robes. He might know nothing about Astandalan etiquette, but he knew where the Captain's Table was on a ship.

Indeed, the secretary opened the door and stepped inside to a room that sparkled. Every wooden or brass surface was polished, and reflected the mage lights hung from the ceiling. This ship was large enough, and important enough, that _regular_ officers didn't dine with the Captain and the Admiral, who were both here. Around the table were three others he hadn't seen before, Cliopher thought perhaps the most senior mages, or the First Mate. Lady Mairel was at the foot of the table, and the secretary completed the set.

Cliopher took the empty chair next to Lady Mairel, trying to ape the secretary as he sat down. He was at least not immediately corrected, although Lady Mairel did not greet him. As he looked in horror at the quantity of different utensils and glasses before him, he realised he should have started with the chapter of the book on dining first. Also, that if the secretary was at this table, she was surely not _just_ a secretary, but someone with rank and position in her own right. 

Admiral Longren gave the blessing to the Sun-on-Earth, and the meal was served. Cliopher resolved to say nothing, and simply concentrate on copying the way everyone else was eating and drinking. He was proud of himself for spotting one tricky case where the men picked up the tiny steamed buns with their hands, while the ladies used eating sticks.

When the meal was over, he silently followed Lady Mairel out of the room. She stopped in front of the door to the room he was kept in.

“How many mistakes did you make, Cliopher?” asked Lady Mairel, turning to face him.

“I do not know, my lady,” he said, looking down.

“Hands out,” she said, primly.

His heart rate increasing, Cliopher held out his hands.

“Offering the Duchess of Nune the incorrect bow,” she said, and struck his hands. That must be the name of the secretary. Cliopher had got it very wrong, he should have given her a deeper bow. Although how he was supposed to know she was a Duchess, he had no idea.

“Not bowing to Admiral Longren on entering the room,” and struck again.

“Twice touching the table with your hands,” strike.

“Not offering any conversation to your neighbours at the table,” strike.

“Not being self-aware enough to know how many mistakes you made,” strike.

“There,” she said, “That's done. You may put your hands down, Cliopher. Now don't cry, showing emotion unintentionally is very bad form.”

Cliopher blinked, and tried to breathe shallowly to stop himself from crying. It was hard, his hands _hurt_. But he didn't want to be hit again, even if it seemed absurd to hit someone for the natural response to _being hit_.

“A Earl is hosting a ball, and has invited the Marchioness. Who has higher precedence, the Marchioness's wife, or the Earl's mother?” asked Lady Mairel.

“The Earl's mother?” he offered, tentatively.

“Not unless she has inherited a higher rank of her own, in which case she would be known by it,” replied Lady Mairel, briskly.

Cliopher subconsciously cringed back, expecting to be told to hold out his hands.

Instead, she tutted. “More emotion, Cliopher? You must show better control. You won't be punished for incorrect answers, only incorrect behaviour.”

There was an awkward pause, and then Cliopher quickly said, “Thank you, my lady. Sorry, my lady.” And added a bow. It seemed bowing was always appropriate.

“The first of the purification rituals will begin shortly, one of the mages will come to do it. You are being granted the rare privilege of an accelerated program, so do make sure to follow their instructions _precisely_,” she said, and gestured him into the room again.

Cliopher followed her direction, and bowed to the closing door. He knew the aristocracy were very big on their rituals, and he supposed that it wasn't appropriate to sniff at them any more than he would at his own shamans. Rubbing his aching hands, he sat down on the bed, and opened the etiquette book where he had left off.

The ship's bell had yet to ring again when the sound of a key in the lock had him scrabbling to mark his place and stand up. Not wishing to offend anyone else, he bowed just as low as he had to Lady Mairel, and said nothing.

The mage looked around the room, and said, “It's not big enough.”

“I'm sorry,” said Cliopher, nervously, “…my lord?”

Sniffing, the mage said, “I'm Senior Specialist Mage écuyer Exa, you may call me 'sir'.”

“Thank you, sir,” said Cliopher, hiding his hands behind his back.

Mage Exa rolled his eyes, and said, “There's a permanent spell circle in my room, we'll have to do it there.”

“Yes, sir,” said Cliopher. When Exa strode off down the hall, Cliopher hurried to keep up. His mind was absolutely bursting with questions, and he was having to bite his lip to stop them slipping out.

“In!” said Exa, holding the door open, and Cliopher rushed in, before Exa slammed the door behind himself. The room was very slightly larger, and looked much more lived-in. The sheets were rumpled, there were books on a high shelf, and the open cupboard door showed a trunk full of smaller boxes. There was also a complex geometric shape carved into the wood of the floor.

“Stand in the corner, and strip down to your underclothes,” said Exa, pointing to the far corner behind the sink.

“Yes sir,” said Cliopher, carefully stepping between the carved lines to the other side of the room. Pulling off the outer layers of his clothes, he folded them, and placed them on the bed for want of anywhere else to put them.

Exa got out a jar of oil, a salt cellar, and a stem of dried rosemary. He plucked leaves from the rosemary, and carefully placed one at eight distinct points in the diagram. A few grains of salt went into the middle of some triangles. Then Exa made a sharp beckoning gesture.

Cliopher stepped into the centre of the circle, carefully avoiding touching the lines, or any of the spell ingredients. Exa dipped a rosemary leaf into the oil, and then wiped it down Cliopher's forehead, nose, lips, neck, all the way down to the band of his pants. Standing as still as he possibly could, Cliopher wondered what this was all for.

The tiny leaf was dipped again, and then brushed against the outer corners of his eyes, the underside of his nose, and his lips.

“Stand still,” said Exa. Cliopher wondered how much _more_ still he could be, until the mage clarified, “This might pinch a bit, _do not move_, or you will mess it up, and I'll have to start anew tomorrow.”

“Yes, sir,” said Cliopher quietly. Then Exa began to chant.

The mage was wrong, it didn't pinch. It was _painful_. Cliopher felt like all his skin was being scraped at once. Even the scars on his feet, which usually felt much less, stung like the rest of him. It rolled like the waves on a beach, a little higher this time, a little lower that time, but always coming back.

He tried to stay as still as he could, forcing himself to try to recall sections of the etiquette book. When that became too hard, he recited the Lays in his head. He might never have reason to sing them again, not in Language, not even in Shaian, but he could still remember them.

“You can step out, now,” said Exa, eventually.

Cliopher opened his eyes, and on wobbly legs, carefully stepped out of the diagram. Exa patted him on the bottom, and Cliopher flinched at the unexpected contact. Exa sniggered, but only said, “Someone will fetch you for the next one, you can take yourself back, boy.”

“Thank you, sir,” said Cliopher, with a bow. He picked up his clothes, trying not to transfer any of the oil. Then he stumbled back up the hall to his room. He shut the door behind himself, and tossed the clothes onto the floor as he leant back against the door. It was possible, he told himself, to do this thing. Surely, surely, it was possible. Lady Mairel seemed to think so, seemed to have some kind of investment in him.

Tears ran down his face, unchecked. Lurching forward, as unstable as if had never been on a ship before, he stumbled to the wash basin. With soap and cloth he removed the ritual oil, and then dried himself, and put the clothes back on. His skin was still tender, his hands still sore, yet he sat down on the bed, and opened the etiquette book again. It *had* to be possible to do this.
