At supper was a stranger, in addition to both Lady Mairel and Exa. The silver-robed person could only be a priest-wizard. Cliopher bowed to Lady Mairel, and waited to be introduced.

“My lord wizard,” said Lady Mairel, with what Cliopher judged to be a bow to minor nobility, “This is my ward.”

Cliopher noted he wasn't even granted a name, nor was he given the name of the wizard. Perhaps the man already knew, or didn't care. Cliopher bowed, slightly lower than Lady Mairel had done, as befitted a commoner to a minor lord. Then he wondered if that had, in fact, been correct. If the maid had been told he was a lord, should he have bowed to the priest-wizard as an equal? Surely not?

He supposed, as he sat down after all the others, that he would only find out if Lady Mairel corrected him later. 

“You do not think he is ready yet?” said the wizard, to Lady Mairel.

“My lord wizard,” she said, placatingly, “Today he began dance lessons, and he was so far behind on regular court manners, I have only just started on how to behave in the Presence.”

“What about you, mage?” said the wizard, “How much more time do you need? The Glorious One is not particularly patient.”

“As you know intimately, my lord wizard,” said Exa, deferentially, “It takes seven weeks to complete the accelerated schedule, we began four weeks ago. Three remain.”

The priest-wizard turned back to Lady Mairel. “Then you have three weeks. I suggest you use them wisely.”

“Of course, my lord wizard,” she said, and when the wizard looked away she gave Exa a look of rage.

After ignoring Cliopher the entire meal, the priest-wizard gave him a piercing look as he stood up. “I want to check your work, Exa,” he said.

“Of course, my lord wizard,” said Exa, with a small bow. Without turning to look at Cliopher, Exa continued, “Boy, get yourself ready and to the workroom.”

“Yes sir,” said Cliopher, “My lady, my lord wizard.” He bowed to all three, walking carefully out of the room. Then he walked very briskly, but definitely did not run, down the hallway to his room.

He was standing in the middle of the ritual circle when the wizard and the mage arrived. With the wizard there, Exa did not seem inclined to touch Cliopher. The wizard, on the other hand, very _nearly_ touched him. As if he was checking Cliopher's clothes, although he was bare-chested. He plucked and brushed at the air around Cliopher, humming softly.

“Very tidy work,” said the priest-wizard, finally.

“Thank you, my lord wizard,” said Exa with a bow.

The wizard left, without further word or gesture.

Exa hugged Cliopher from behind, around the chest and pinning Cliopher's arm's to his side. “Very good,” he whispered, his lips brushing Cliopher's ear, “You're going to get me a promotion, I think. Would you like a reward for that?”

Cliopher had no idea what kind of reward Exa could possibly offer him that he would want. It wasn't as if he could skip a ritual, and it seemed unlikely that a request to stop touching would go down well. “No, thank you sir,” said Kip quietly, trying desperately not to stiffen against Exa's grasp.

“Pity,” said Exa, letting him go slowly, “Ah well, you'll learn soon enough.”

The next three weeks were a blur of activity. He was constantly exhausted, but did his best to hide that and his anxiety. There were rituals, lessons for etiquette, dance, singing, oboe. Fittings for clothes, trials for cosmetics. 

A fastidious young man, dressed entirely in white, looked over every bit of his skin. Then subjected Cliopher to various treatments to remove every spot, mark, or scar. 

The man nearly squealed when he saw Cliopher's feet. “You'll need a mage for those,” he told Lady Mairel, stiffly.

Then Exa was summoned, and examined the scars. To Lady Mairel's relief, and Cliopher's secret disappointment, he said, “I can probably hide them.”

Cliopher had earnt those scars. They were proof of his hard-won traditional knowledge. That nobody here had any use for. He would never claim the greater fire dance before his community and family now. There were only court dances.

Dutifully, Cliopher went to the magic room and let Exa spell his feet. Afterwards, they felt the same, and he didn't look at them. It might only be an illusion, perhaps it would fade over time. Anyway, he had another etiquette lesson; they had reached the chapter on formal orgies.

---

The final ritual happened twelve hours before Cliopher's presentation. Ten o'clock at night.

He arrived five minutes early, and stood in the circle. Over the weeks, he had worked out that he could step beside himself, during the rituals. It never actually hurt any less, but that way he could not think about Exa's hands, or gaze, or words. Not now at least, which meant he wouldn't react, and Exa wouldn't escalate. Or threaten to report him.

When Exa was done, Cliopher staggered back to his room. His eyes felt dry, his ears rang, his teeth pulsed. When he closed the door behind himself, all he could see was Exa's face as he painted Cliopher with oil.

It was over, he tried to tell himself. Never again would he need to see Exa, to be near him, to smell him.

He needed to wash, he would feel better when he was clean. Cliopher scrubbed off the oil, lathering himself with lotion to hide the smell. Then he crawled into bed. He would be up at dawn to prepare, he needed to sleep, to rest his aches and hope they would be at least reduced by morning. As he curled under the blanket, he reached out for his island, that last tenuous link to home.

It was gone. They had taken him from his home, his family, his people, his world. Made him abandon his efela, his accent. He was no longer Kip Mdang, no longer of Tahivoa, no longer bore the scars of his learning. And now, finally, they had torn him away from Loaloa.

As he fell asleep that night, he didn't remember his mother singing Ani's Lament at his departure. But the way she had wailed at her own mother's funeral. 

---

The dawn light was soft when Cliopher woke. A light knock at his door, that he knew now to be the maid. She led him to a bathroom, where the man in white supervised Cliopher being stripped, washed, anointed. Cliopher couldn't help remembering how the family had prepared his grandmother's body. No tui blossom for him, just endless scents of rose. No living flowers, just embroidered ones.

The bells rang, and rang, and rang. Cliopher lost track as he was moved from room to room. Wrapped in many layers, that were pinned and arranged just so. Designs were painted on his face, heavy jewels hung from his wrists and neck. Pearls and coral, and the bright aqua of a lagoon.

For the first time since he had arrived, Cliopher left Lady Mairel's apartment. Down the hallways, down the stairs. People stopped, and stared, and bowed. Cliopher had no idea if that was for him, or for Lady Mairel, or someone else who was with them. People might have said things. He wasn't really paying attention.

There were guards outside the doors, he vaguely noticed their chests glistened, and he wondered why. Was it so people couldn't grab them, like wrestlers in Jilkano? 

A crowd around them, but he was placed in a queue. There was music, inoffensive and light, perhaps a lute. Names were called, and he stepped forward. Closer to the unmistakable glow at the front of the room. But he was not the third son, come to seek the fire of civilisation in exchange for his name.

"Cliopher Madon, tithe from the Wide Seas," came a clear voice.

Just as he had practiced so many times, Cliopher stepped forward to the same place as the others had before him. Eyes only on the Lord Emperor's hands, he lifted his robes so they didn't bind. Dropped gently to both knees, placed his fingertips on the floor. Lowered his upper body to the exact correct angle, and waited.

"Oh he's _funny_," came a voice, from just above where Cliopher was looking. "Do you think he knows the Fire Dance like the Seafarer King, Melissa?"

Cliopher moved not a muscle. He had not been addressed, had not been asked. It didn't matter that Elonoa'a had been paramount chief and not a king, and had been a Kindraa so hadn't known Aōteketētana. That knowledge belonged to Kip Mdang, not the man he was now. Waiting patiently to be told his fate by the Sun-on-Earth.

"If such legends were true, Glorious One, would not the Wide Seas send dancers to your court as well as pearls?" said a soft, high voice, "Yet, is he not sweet, your Radiancy? Truly a pearl of great price to ornament your Illustrious Self."

The hands of the Emperor were so dark, it seemed impossible they should shine. Yet they did, and with every slightest movement the gem-encrusted rings cast sparkles and rainbows around. Now, they finally made the gesture to rise to standing.

Cliopher did so, keeping his eyes only on the Glorious One's hands. His expression trained carefully into a slight smile.

"Why don't you take him as a pet?" said the Lord of Rising Stars, "To entertain you until our wedding next month, princess."

Princess Melissa replied, "His Radiancy is most generous to his servant. I would not wish to deprive the Glorious One of any good thing, but I receive with gladness whatever my sovereign grants."

The gesture of dismissal. Cliopher went down again, until his forehead was a hair from touching the ground. Then he rose, and stepped backwards the requisite number of paces until he was allowed to turn slightly. His own hands resting gently in front, eyes still on the Lord Emperor's sparkling ones. But now someone else stepped forward from the queue, and the process moved on. The rest were all simply newcomers to the Palace of Stars, mostly the teen or young adult children of various noble families.

Cliopher waited, and observed. The Emperor Shallyr reclined on an asymmetrical couch, his left hand resting on the head of a young woman who knelt behind him. His right, on the low arm of the couch, to give direction. A good gap beside his feet, and there was a low, padded stool, where another woman sat. She had the features of the Imperial bloodline, and impeccable posture, despite the ridiculous seat. Princess Melissa.

As soon as the queue had been drained, the Lord Emperor stood. "We are tired," he announced to the room. Everyone, including Melissa and Cliopher, dropped to their knees. Flanked by guards, and wrapped around the young woman, the Glorious One left through a side door.
