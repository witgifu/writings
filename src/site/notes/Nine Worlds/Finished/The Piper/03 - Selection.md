It was dawn. Nieleni and Kip were on the roof of the union building. They'd ended up only drinking a small amount of rum each, deciding that they didn't want to ruin things by turning up drunk, or hungover, or even slightly tipsy, at the palace gates.

Kip looked out over the city, trying to imprint every building in his mind. His mother and sisters were going to be furious with him. But he wanted his last morning in the city, his last free morning, to not be full of his mother yelling and crying. If he wasn't selected, he would apologise.

Perhaps it was the dark thoughts, or the tears, that clouded his sight. Because it was Nieleni who said, “Do you know that old man down there? He's been staring at you for ten minutes.”

Kip looked down, expecting to see Uncle Lazo, or maybe one of the Varga elders, who'd come looking for him.

“Buru Tovo!” said Kip, and dashed back through the door and down the stairs. He didn't notice Nieleni following behind him.

“Did Basil find you?” said Kip, as he ran through the doors, to grasp Buru Tovo's arms.

“Eh?” said Tovo, “No, but I was coming anyway. Who is this?”

Kip turned to see Nieleni looking at them wistfully. “This is Nieleni,” said Kip, “She's the other person who is going to… have you heard, Buru Tovo, what's happening?”

Tovo sighed, “Hard not to, it's all anyone in the city is talking about.”

“Are you going to tell me to go home to see Mama and Uncle Lazo?” asked Kip, dejected.

Tovo looked him over. “No,” he said gruffly, “You know your own mind boy.”

“Thank you,” said Kip, tears pooling in his eyes, “If I don't see them… can you tell them...”

“They know, boy, but I'll tell them anyway,” said Tovo. He turned to Nieleni, “Any word you want to send to Linaroa?”

Nieleni looked startled at being addressed. “Tell grandpa I'll write as soon and as often as I can,” she said quietly, “And tell my brother to listen to grandpa, and that I love him.”

“I will,” said Tovo, “Now let's get you both up to the palace before anyone tries to burn it down, or something even more foolish.”

They walked together, the old man and the two young people. Nobody paid them much attention. There was lots of gossip, lots of people trying to pretend it was a normal morning. They spotted a few teens sneaking out of windows, or in.

One of the teens was the first to realise who they must be. As they passed the back of a warehouse, they snuck past and whispered, “Thank you.” Then a grandma blew them a kiss from a window. Before long they had a small group of children trying to sneak along behind them. Then they walked right past the chess club, who turned to form a sort of honour guard.

By the time they reached the palace gates, there was a small, silent, crowd behind them. The gates were shut, and the two guards were on the other side, looking nervous. Finally, Ginger worked their way through the crowd, and up to join Tovo, Nieleni, and Kip at the front.

“Would you like to address the crowd?” asked Ginger, to Buru Tovo. 

He shook his head. “Let's wait, and see what happens. You got a calming speech in your pocket?”

Ginger grinned. “I'll do my best, you let me know if you want crying eyes or rocks thrown or whatever.”

Tovo snorted, but smiled. Then he walked up to the guards by the gate. “They coming soon, Inji?” he asked the left-hand one.

“I don't know, sir,” said the woman called Inji, shuffling her feet, “They wouldn't tell us anything. I think they're worried we'll turn on them if things get ugly.”

“Got *some* sense then,” said Tovo, and returned to Kip and Nieleni. Ginger was watching the crowd, not the palace.

Just as the crowd was starting to get restless, and well after the appointed time, the palace doors opened. What looked like nearly every solider on the ship marched down. The Admiral somewhere in the middle. It was a stupid move, if they wanted to try to stop a riot breaking out, thought Kip. It was bringing a sword to a fist-fight.

Tovo and Ginger turned to the crowd, and started talking gently to people. Kip couldn't hear what they were saying, but the group behind them did seem to thin a bit. Perhaps they were asking people to leave; or maybe sending out messages.

Kip and Nieleni stood perfectly still, watching the gate. The squad of soldiers opened it, but didn't march out. There were some scraping noises, and the Admiral's hat, followed shortly by his face, popped up in the middle of them.

“Are there any volunteers?” he said, slightly horsely.

Kip and Nieleni both stepped forward. “Us,” said Kip loudly.

“And who are you?” said the Admiral, and coughed.

“Nieleni inLinaroa,” said Nieleni, boldly, “I'm twenty.”

“Cliopher Mdang,” said Kip, “Twenty-one.”

“Come forward,” said the Admiral, and a small gap was made in the soldiers. When Kip and Nieleni stepped forward again to walk through, a rumble went up from the crowd. The soldiers ignored it.

Both of them went forward. Behind the wall of soldiers, the Admiral was standing on a fruit box, and next to him was a harassed looking secretary. “Can you spell those names for me,” she said, holding a clipboard and a bottle of ink in one hand, and a pen in the other.

Kip and Nieleni spelled out their names, and then went through the criteria with the secretary. Once, she just waved a strange bronze rod at them, which Kip assumed was some kind of magical device, possibly medical. When they got to the question about singing, she made them both sing a bit of the Imperial anthem. She noted down what musical instruments they played, their grades, she even looked inside their mouths to check for fillings.

All the while, the Admiral looked them over, like someone thinking of buying a boat. 

When they had finished with the paperwork, the secretary said, to the Admiral, “All done, sir, they both qualify.”

The Admiral paused for a moment. Then he said, “Turn around, both of you.”

They both turned, to look out at the crowd. It flowed down the slope, and despite Ginger and Tovo's attempts, it seemed to be getting both larger and more restless.

“I could take both of you,” said the Admiral, from right behind Kip. 

He jumped, he hadn't noticed the Admiral stepping down from the box. He was too close, uncomfortably close. Kip swallowed. He could tell how carefully balanced the situation was. The people of Gorjo City had rocks and knives. The Navy had spears, canon, and battle mages. It would not end well.

“But a riot would be _so_ messy,” whispered the Admiral, “And the paperwork is tiresome.”

Kip and Nieleni stood perfectly still, looking out. Breathing slowly, trying to be calm.

“So,” said the Admiral, placing a firm grip on Kip's shoulder, “I'll take the boy. People get more emotional about girls.”

Nieleni turned around to face him, “Why you—”

“Oh dear,” said the Admiral, “Manners! I *am* glad I didn't pick the girl. I hope you're more teachable than your friend, boy.”

Kip turned his head slightly to look at Nieleni. “Don't,” he whispered, “please.”

She bit her lip, and said instead, “Sir, will you let him say goodbye to his family first?”

“He's had all night, if he wanted to do that,” said the Admiral, releasing Kip, “Now you're not allowed to take anything with you, boy. So if you've got a treasure in your pocket from your sweetheart, or a lucky charm, give it to the girl.”

Shaking, Kip emptied his pockets. A couple of coins, a handkerchief. He reached up to touch his obsidian pendant, as he handed over a library receipt. 

“Would a necklace—” Kip began.

“Not a stitch of clothing, not a hair on your head,” said the Admiral.

Letting the tears fall now, he reached up and undid the string he had spun. Winding it carefully, he put it into Nieleni's hands. “Give it to Buru Tovo,” he said, thickly.

Nieleni nodded. She was crying too.

“Right!” said the Admiral, “I want to get to Kavanduru before the month is out, so let's get moving. Go back to your friends, girl.”

Nieleni walked out of the ring of soldiers, holding her hands out with Kip's efela. As soon as she left, the soldiers closed ranks. One of them called out, “Forward!” and the cordon started moving down the street.

Kip walked with them. Part of him still didn't believe this was real. The Navy ship had only arrived the day before yesterday. How could it already have upended the world? Upended his world?

They were half-way through the city, when he heard his mother's voice. He stumbled, almost falling into the soldier behind him, who gave him a dirty look. She was singing the _Lament of Ani_. All through the crowd, people started picking it up. Above it, soared his mother's wonderful soprano. 

Kip couldn't see, the tears wouldn't stop. His nose was running, his eyes were aching. He stumbled on, until someone grabbed his shoulder and started steering him.

He could feel the path beneath his sandals changing. Cobbles, to boards, to the stone of the edge of the harbour. The soldiers and the Admiral didn't stop. They took him out along the big dock, where the largest ships berthed. The one that had to be dredged regularly so they could. Up a steep ramp, surprisingly wide so that soldiers could stand either side of him. He felt his sandal slip over the edge at the top, and he turned around, and waved.

Let them think he went excited for adventure. Rather than devastated, with the song of his mother's sorrow ringing in his ears.
