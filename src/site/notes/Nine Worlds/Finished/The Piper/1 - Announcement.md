The Navy ship sailed into Gorjo City harbour on a lovely Monday afternoon. On their way home from school, children pointed it out to each other. Had it comes recruiting sailors? Probably. Many people could sail in the Vangaveye-ve, although few had the desire to join the Imperial Navy. The pay might be worse on the merchant ships, but the contracts were shorter.

Everyone still remembered Aurelius Magnus coming to ask Elonoa'a to show him the way to Colhélhé. So even if they had little success, nobody resented the Navy trying. It was good to be remembered as seafarers.

The captain, or whatever fancy word they called themselves, paraded up to the Palace, and that was expected. It was fun to see the sailor's uniforms, agreed the children. And the captain's hat was sure to have many paper imitations made in the upcoming weeks.

It wasn't until dusk that rumours started flowing down from the Palace. And not in the chattering of children, or the smirks of young men. In the dark looks shared by the elders, and the whispers of servants to trusted messengers.

By full dark, every inch of space in Lazo's shop was taken. Even the broom cupboard had two people standing in it, with the door open. All eyes turned to the tanà when he coughed.

“I've sent Basil to fetch Tovo. But it will be at least a full day,” said Lazo, looking around the elders. Everyone looked worried. Kip, perched on a corner of bench, hadn't yet heard why, but it seemed incredible that anything could happen that would cause every lore keeper and apprentice in the city to be summoned. What news had the Navy ship brought?

“My niece at the palace,” said Lazo, over the mumbling that had broken out, “overheard that the Navy is here to collect a special tithe. For the ascension of the new Emperor, Shallyr.”

“Wasn't a special tithe for his father,” said one of the oldest elders, who had been given one of the chairs.

“No,” said Lazo, seriously, “And it is highly unusual for the tithe collectors to come with a Navy escort.”

“There's another two ships waiting outside the ring,” said one of the apprentices, “I was up on Mama Ituri today, and they're hiding just on the horizon. Could be trading vessels, but...”

Everyone nodded, or looked seriously at their neighbours. What tribute had the new Emperor requested that he thought *three* Navy ships would be required to collect it from the peaceable Vangaveye-ve? Was he going to *conscript* them into the Navy?

“That's all we know. Under the circumstances, if he requests pearls, or foamwork, or aholo cloth, we should probably be glad,” said Lazo. The Navan looked angry, but even they had to agree. Their work could be redone, even if it took generations. If he demanded the *weavers*, well.

“The zamà have already quietly sent their apprentices out of the city,” continued Lazo. There were a number of grimaces. Battle mages were much in demand in the Empire. None had ever gone from the Vangaveye-ve, that Kip recalled, but perhaps the Navy had decided force might work where generous pay offers had not.

“There will be an announcement tomorrow at the palace gates. That's all we know for now, anything else is speculation,” said Lazo. Immediately there was a rumble of discussion. Some wanted to send away everyone between ten and fifty. Others wanted to try to send spies into the palace to learn more. A few wanted to storm the palace, but they were quickly shut down by the rest.

Kip wished he could talk to Basil, but of course it was much more important to fetch Buru Tovo. Press-ganging sailors seemed like the most logical choice to Kip, he didn't think it likely that the Navy would have been sent if they wanted musicians or pearls. Perhaps, if they had somehow detected a very powerful mage. They would have to wait and see. Personally, he was determined to be at the gates tomorrow, eight o'clock class be damned.

Slowly people drifted out of the meeting, muttering and whispering. No real decisions could be made, because they didn't have enough information. But at least it would help stamp out some wilder rumours. Kip had heard someone whisper something about the City of Emeralds declaring independence, which was ludicrous. Zunidh had been wholly part of the Empire for centuries.

At last, it was just Kip and Uncle Lazo.

“Will you go, tomorrow?” asked Lazo, as he closed the front door.

“Yeah,” said Kip, jumping down from the counter. “I hope nothing… I hope Buru Tovo gets here soon.”

Lazo knew what Kip wasn't saying. “I'll keep out of it, I think,” he said, “If you're not building barricades or trying to talk your friends out of rioting, will you come by after?”

Kip smiled, “Of course.” He also wasn't sure what he would be doing if they started seizing people who looked like sailors. Perhaps he and his friends should try to get academic robes from the student union, so it was clear what they were. In theory students were supposed to wear them to lectures, but they were too hot, so people only put them on for formal occasions.

The next day, as Kip stood with Toucan by the palace gates, he was regretting the costume choice. Ghilly, Cora, and Bertie had headed to their classes. Toucan had a free morning, and a truly spectacular set of kingfisher-blue formal robes.

There was a crowd, but it was smaller than he had expected. He could see most families and guilds represented, but they had sent just one person. And someone who could run fast, with the message.

As scheduled, a group walked down from the palace to the gate. A little wooden podium had been set up earlier. The crowed gave them plenty of space, as the man with the most impressive hat took the stage.

He coughed, and fiddled with a magical amplification device.

“Admiral,” whispered Toucan into Kip's ear. He'd learnt the rank insignia for one of his law subjects, presumably. Kip nodded, but didn't say anything. The group around the podium seemed to made entirely of Navy personnel. If the Prince knew what was happening, he didn't want to be involved. Or possibly he was too drunk to walk down.

Finally, the Admiral coughed again, and it was loud enough a few people covered their ears. “His Most Glorious and Illustrious…” (the official titles on a proclamation were always long and dull) “… Emperor Shallyr, has decreed that all provinces of all worlds under his Glorious Dominion, shall, in celebration of his accession, provide a special tithe. In accordance with that, I, Admiral Longren, have been appointed to collect from Zunidh, and to select from her children those best suited to serve as ornaments to his court and person, …”

Kip kept half his mind on the proclamation, and whispered to Toucan, “Run to the high school, tell them to do a tsunami evacuation drill.”

Toucan nodded, and slipped off. He wasn't the only one, the crowded was thinning rapidly. There was no way Gorjo City, or the Vangaveye-ve, was going to stand by and let the Navy remove even one child.

“… Those who would place themselves under consideration should return here tomorrow. Aged between sixteen and twenty-one, male or female, unmarried. Of good education, having received band six results at their last standard examination. Without physical defect or medical problem, including glasses or prosthetics, … ”

The long list of criteria would have made Kip sick due to the discrimination, if it weren't for the overwhelming sense of anger that they would take any Islander away. Kip's heart was beating so hard he could feel it in his fingers.

“… If no suitable candidate presents them self tomorrow, we are authorised to,” and here the Admiral paused, and finally looked a little sheepish, “to collect for the Glorious One's use every sixteen-year-old, by force.” He rustled the parchment a little, and then added, “The Wide Seas is ordered to provide one person. See you tomorrow.”

He got down from the podium, surrounded by Navy spears. Kip turned and bolted through the city towards Lazo's shop. *He* was between sixteen and twenty-one. So was Toucan, Ghilly, Cora… 

Could they hide all the sixteen-year-olds? For how long? More kids would turn sixteen, should they hide all those aged fifteen? He didn't want to think what would happen to them in Astandalas the Golden. Even the Vangaveye-ve had heard rumours of what Prince Shallyr had been like. Without the hand of his father restraining him, he had already shown he was prepared to hurt his own people, with this preposterous demand.

Kip pulled open the door to Lazo's shop. “It's bad,” he said, and started recounting from the beginning. 

By the time he finished, Lazo was looking grey and leaning on the counter for support. “Were there any other criteria?” he asked, his voice shaking.

Kip recited the list in full again, and then he went behind the till, and grabbed a pencil and paper. He wrote it out, double-checking, “Yes, I think that's it.”

Two elders came through the door, looking scared. “Toucan did the right thing, telling the high-school to evacuate,” said one of them, with a sigh.

“They want hostages,” said Lazo.

“One hostage,” corrected Kip, “Who can sing, dance, play music, is academic, very fit, no magic, … shit.”

“They want all the sixteen-year-olds,” said Lazo. 

Kip bit his lip, looking over the list. “No, there's… not a lot of people who would qualify… but…” He tried to remember the names from the papers who had got top marks. “I would.”

“No,” said Uncle Lazo, firmly, “They're not taking a single person. Not you, not anyone.”

“There are three Navy ships,” said Kip, “With canons, soldiers, spears, mages. Do you propose we hide all the teenagers in the jungle? Forever?”

“Kip—” said Lazo, but Kip twisted past his outstretched arm.

“I'm going to the university,” said Kip, leaving the list behind on the bench. “We'll see how many of us qualify,” he turned back to Lazo, whose face was twisted with grief, “We're adults, we can decide for ourselves.”
