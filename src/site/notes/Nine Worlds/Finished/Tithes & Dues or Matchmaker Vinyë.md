“Will you be able to join us?” Vinyë's mother asked with careful neutrality.

Kip sighed. “Possibly. I shall have to see what’s on my calendar now.”

“Now?” Quintus asked. “You seem far too organised not to know your schedule.”

Kip cut the starfruit into precise segments, controlled tension evident in every line of his body. “I would be, usually, but things are topsy-turvy at the moment … I was ahead on my work, you see, in anticipation of your visit. These are all things I would not normally be obligated to deal with.”

“Because of the Glorious One being—”

“Yes.”

“You care very deeply for your lord,” Eidora said in a softer voice.

“Of course,” said Kip, his glance straying to the note. He looked so unbelievably tired, despite the sleep in.

“You’ve never thought of getting married,” Quintus said. There was a slight inflection at the end, that could almost have made it a question.

Kip looked at him blankly.

“Not even with … I suppose it would be complicated but … ”

Kip flushed as he realised what his cousin was implying. “No. But also: no. That’s not why … That’s not what …”

“It would be understandable,” Eidora said.

“He is my lord and my friend,” he said firmly. “That’s all. Quite apart from the fact that it would be edging into blasphemy.”

Quintus seemed disinclined to drop it. “But still, you never have—”

“No.”

Into the short silence that followed his sharp retort, Dora looked up from where she was industriously drawing a picture of the Liaau to ask: “What haven't you done?”

Kip struggled with his temper—and deep emotions. Vinyë wished he would just let them out, rather than bottling them up. It couldn't possibly be healthy. “Thought of marrying my lord.”

“Lord Artorin? Why not? He's very nice.”

“What do you want, Kip?” Vinyë asked.

“Now?” he laughed wearily. “Right now I want the time to spend with my family, but unfortunately past ambition is present work.”

Vinyë gave Quintus a speaking look, and he inclined his head slightly. Kip didn't notice, watching Dora still. Their mother watched Kip. The conversation turned again, but only to other difficult topics. It made Vinyë sad to see their mother press Kip, and him respond only with polite evasions. What had happened to the Kip who sparked like lightning?

When Kip left to talk to the Glorious One, she pulled Quintus aside, out of the hearing of anyone else.

“We need to do something about *them*,” she said, knowing she didn't need to specify who she meant.

“I admired your boldness in just walking up to Melissa Damara and introducing yourself,” said Quintus, a smile spreading on his face, “But I'm not sure that is a good idea with the Glorious One. And you saw how my hinting at Kip went.”

“They're just too _polite_ to each other. Neither of them is going to say anything out of fear the other would take offence. If I'm invited to perform without mama, I am _definitely_ going to say something.” 

Quintus rolled his eyes.

“You're not going to try and stop me, are you?”

“No,” said Quintus seriously, “I know once you've got an idea like this you can be unbelievably stubborn. Like any good Mdang.”

Vinyë grinned. It was true, it was why they could butt heads so easily. She paused, a memory welling up. “Do you remember what we did to Nora?”

“When we enlisted all her friends to help? I don't think Conju is likely. Have you seen how he acts here? I've never seen someone be so … formal. And Bertie and Cora and so on back home aren't about to come here and stage an intervention.”

“I was thinking of Ludvic,” said Vinyë, “Or possibly Rhodin, although he's probably quite busy too if he's also doing Ludvic's job.”

They were interrupted by Zemius. “Dora's set on going to see the animals again, are you coming?”

“I think I'll pass,” said Vinyë, giving Quintus another significant look.

“Mmm,” said Quintus, “I might see what books Kip has. Apart from the law code, I think I might have to be quite desperate to read that.”

Zemius shrugged. “Suit yourselves,” he said, and went to collect the others. Vinyë and Quintus wandered the rooms, looking at Kip's various objects and art, waiting for the sound of the rest of the Mdang clan to leave. Once they were sure it was quiet, they went to find Ludvic.

As Vinyë had guessed, after a stressful night, he hadn't decided to visit the gardens either. Instead, they found him standing in the dinning room, looking out the window.

“Ludvic?” asked Vinyë, tentatively. She wasn't quite sure if she should have called him 'Commander Omo', but formality like that would probably set the wrong tone for the conversation she wanted to have.

The guard turned from the window, to look from Vinyë to Quintus and back. He didn't say anything, but waited expectantly.

Tired of skirting around Kip's sensitivities, Vinyë just came right out and said it. “It's clear that Kip loves the Glorious One, and that it's mutual. Why don't they _do_ anything about it? Kip said the Lord Emperor doesn't need to marry for an heir, so why _not_ marry for love?”

Ludvic waited before answering, as if he was carefully piecing it together. But all he said was, “Many reasons.”

Quintus shook his head. “They are both smart, I think if they thought the other really wanted it, they could work it out. Since, as we found out, Kip built and runs the government for him.”

The smile Ludvic gave them both was genuine. “Yes.”

“You've never tried to give them a bit of a shove?” asked Vinyë, baffled.

“No,” said Ludvic. “I must be careful with his Radiancy's great trust in me.”

That wasn't _quite_ the firm denial that Vinyë had half-expected. It seemed that Ludvic wasn't willing, or perhaps able, to join their plans. But perhaps he was giving his tacit permission.

“Of course,” said Vinyë with a smile. Then she changed the subject entirely. “Perhaps with everyone gone I should take the chance to practice some of the new music I acquired in the city.”

The next time she was called to play for the Glorious One, she was going to _have_ to say something. Actually, perhaps, just in case, she should write him a note _asking_ to come and play again.

Pen, paper, and ink, were easy enough to find in Kip's study. Tully looked slightly shocked when Vinyë asked how to send it to the Glorious One, but took the note from her and promised to find a page to do so.

Not wanting to stand around fretting to see if there was a response, Vinyë did as she had suggested she might, and turned her mind to the new music. It was challenging enough that if she wanted to get it right, she really couldn't think about much else.

She heard the gentle rumble of Kip and Quintus chatting, but put it out of her mind. If Quintus was trying with Kip again, she wished him luck. Well, perhaps it was easier to talk about such things with your noticeably unmarried cousin than your widowed older sister. Who had someone—well, not that she'd told Kip yet—but there might be someone else for her.

A couple of hours later, Tully found her with a return note. The Lord Emperor would of course be delighted, etc, etc. She was to attend immediately.

Changing as quickly as she could, Vinyë tried to think of potential approaches she might make. How could she bring up the issue naturally? If only it had been their first audience, where the Lord Emperor had wanted to do nothing more than talk about Kip the entire time.

She presented herself to the frankly ridiculous series of guards and doors at the entrance to the Imperial Apartments. It had become less imposing the more times she'd been invited, but no less silly. Finally, she was admitted to the Emperor's bed … room seemed to small a word. Bed hall?

When she rose from her curtsy, Conju had quietly placed a chair for her. One could play a double-bass or a violin standing, but a cello was simply the wrong size. Unless one propped it up on a box or something, but she'd never had reason to try that.

“Play whatever moves your heart, Saya Vawen,” said the Glorious One. He looked so small in the giant bed. Vinyë gave another small curtsy.

Then, taking her seat, she began playing the most famous romantic pieces she knew.

After spending a good while covering her repertoire of love music, much of which was from operas, but she also threw in some popular ballads, Vinyë took a small pause.

“Glorious One,” she said, trying not to hesitate, “Might I ask you a question about my brother?”

He smiled, “Of course, although no doubt you know him better than I.”

“I know he loves you very much,” she began, and now she had come to the point, she hesitated. What could she ask? “And he has never married. Is he… I cannot think why, Glorious One. Unless there is someone he is waiting for?”

Vinyë couldn't parse the blankness on the face of the Glorious One. Had she perhaps overstepped some line? Was this something that wasn't talked about in Solaaran culture?

Nervously, she began to blather. “Only he has introduced us to his friends, Conju, Rhodin, Ludvic, and none of them seem to have that kind of relationship. I know, when he was younger, he always talked of finding someone...” She trailed off, eyes flicking to the guards, and the door where no doubt Conju waited out of view.

There was a slight cough from the bed that might have been covering a laugh.

“If you are asking, Saya Vawen, if I have secretly proposed to my Lord Chancellor, I have not,” said the Glorious One, sounding as formal and dignified as always.

“Why not?” said Vinyë, and then clapped her hand to her face in shock at her own temerity. Managing to hit herself in the face with her bow in the process.

“Glorious One, I am so sorry, I should not have presumed, that was wildly inappropriate,” said Vinyë, trying to regain her professional demeanour.

“Perhaps we should rest, Saya Vawen,” said the Glorious One, slightly coldly.

Not wanting to embarrass herself further, Vinyë bowed, and took herself out of the Imperial Apartments. She couldn't help wondering if perhaps the Emperor wanted to, but for some reason it wasn't allowed. There were very many special rules around him, even after the Fall, when he no longer had to marry a close relative.

She was this slightly surprised to be interrupted by Rhodin in one of the public hallways. He bowed, and she bowed back despite the awkwardness while carrying her cello.

“I wish you the best in your endeavours,” he said, and moved on past her. Confused by the elliptical statement, she didn't manage to respond before he had gone.

Rhodin hadn't been one of the guards in the room, or one of the others she had walked past. Yet that *almost* sounded like he had overhead what she had said. And *approved*.

The next day when the family went into Solaara, Vinyë begged to be able to claim Kip to herself. And so they had a pleasant and private walk through a garden.

“Kip,” said Vinyë, not sure how to brooch the topic.

“Vinyë,” said her brother back, in exactly the same tone.

They both smiled and laughed, much as they had as children.

Vinyë appreciated the break in tension. “You said that before the Fall, the Emperor would have had to marry his sister or a cousin. But after, now, he's going to retire? Go on some quest for an heir? So could he marry whoever he likes?”

Kip quirked an eyebrow at her. “Only the expectations of a world hold him back, I suppose. And his own preference, of course. Not everyone wants to marry, you know. Look at Quintus.”

With a smirk, Vinyë replied, “Don't look too closely at Quintus, just because he's not the type to settle down...”

“Like Rhodin,” said Kip, “I don't even bother learning the names of his paramours.”

“Exactly,” said Vinyë, “I suppose the Emperor could have a harem if he was that way inclined.”

Kip didn't laugh at that, but pursed his lips. “He wouldn't, the memory of Eritanyr still looms too large.”

“Eritanyr had a harem?” asked Vinyë, softly.

“Yes,” said Kip, “Of teenagers. You can understand, I hope, why his Radiancy wants nothing to do with it.”

Vinyë's mouth dropped open, and she struggled to understand how that could have happened. How it could have been *allowed*. “Oh, oh dear. Is he… celibate because … Oh Kip! How awful for both of you.”

“He's never let the priest-wizards purify anyone, not even attendants like Conju,” said Kip, “He has always been very careful. Of course, it would be a great honour for anyone if he did change his mind.”

“What would you say if he asked?”

“Yes, of course.” She saw a flash of longing pass over his face.

“Have you asked him?” said Vinyë, gently.

Kip shook his head. “There are some things that are just… No.”

“What's the worst that could happen?” tried Vinyë, “He says no, and it's a bit awkward for a while?”

Kip looked at her, and raised both eyebrows. “Being torn apart by wild beasts. That's the official punishment for high treason, although the last few cases were commuted to beheading.”

It struck Vinyë again just how very strange and foreign Solaara and the Palace of Stars were. That someone could be tortured like that for just asking a question.

“But he loves you,” she protested.

“And I am very thankful and mindful of my lord's great regard, and would not wish to upset him.” That was Kip's court voice and face. He had closed down, and she knew from their Mama's attempts that pushing further now wouldn't help.

After dinner, she took Quintus aside and admitted her failures.

“They just shut down, I've never seen two people so determined not to see what's right in front of their faces!”

Quintus sighed. “What are you going to do now, then? Forge notes between them like this is a school crush?”

Vinyë thought about that for a moment. It would be tricky, it was clear that kind of note would be highly unusual between them, as they mostly talked face-to-face. While she _might_ be able to copy her brother's handwriting, she didn't know where she'd even get a sample of the Glorious One's hand.

His eyes going wide, Quintus said quickly, “I was joking! I was joking! After what we heard about Kip and corruption I don't want to know what kind of trouble we'd be in for if we tried something like that.”

“I don't think it would work anyway,” said Vinyë, “It's not like we can sneak a bit of paper into the Glorious One's maths textbook.”

“Please tell me your next idea doesn't involve breaking into the Imperial Apartments,” said Quintus, seriously.

“Not at all,” she replied tersely, “I think you should try to enlist Rhodin's help. I'm going to try Conju.”

“You know Conju only likes men,” said Quintus, teasing.

Vinyë swatted at him. “Get away with you, not like that. If *you* end up in Rhodin's bed for any reason, leave that bit _out_ of anything you tell me. Or Kip. Or Mama.”

Quintus winked at her, and sauntered off.

It took a couple of days for Vinyë to have the opportunity to corner Conju. Finally, she managed to do it one day before lunch.

“I wanted to ask you, Conju...” she said, as walked past.

“Yes, my dear?” he said, and stopped with a smile.

“About Kip and the Glorious One?” she tried, testing the waters to see if he would shut her down.

But Conju laughed. “Have you been reading those secretary romance novels? I've tried a few, but I found them rather disappointing and contrived.”

“There are *novels* about Kip and the Glorious One? Do they know? How are they not… illegal?”

“Oh well they all *claim* to be an ancient Emperor or a Prince, you know, plausible deniability. But Cliopher dismantled the censorship office centuries ago. His Radiancy removed nearly everything from the banned works list.”

“Has Kip read any of them?” asked Vinyë curiously.

Conju sniffed, but in an aristocratic way that almost made Vinyë want to giggle at it.

“I don't believe so,” he said, “I think he reads government reports for pleasure. I've tried to get him to see reason, but he claims they are 'fun' and 'interesting'.”

Vinyë snorted. “Perhaps if I could trick him by putting one in one of those folders.”

“If you do,” said Conju, with a conspiratorial look, “I shall be delighted.”

“And the Glorious One?” asked Vinyë, “Has he…?”

“His Radiancy has never asked for any. I do try to supply a small selection of modern literature in his personal library.” He looked pensive for a moment. “Perhaps it is worth trying one or two. One never knows, and sometimes it can be relaxing to read something frivolous for a change.”

That wasn't *quite* the reason Vinyë wanted the Emperor to read the books. But, well, she was hardly going to argue the point.

“It is indeed,” she said instead. “I shall have to try to get one of those report covers from Tully.”

They went into lunch together, smiling.

Afterwards the family decided to go into the city for some shopping. Vinyë pled a need to practice, and stayed behind. She got an eye roll from Quintus, but nobody else commented.

Once everyone was gone, she approached Tully again.

“I have an idea,” Vinyë said, leaning on the door frame.

The young friend of her son arched her eyebrows and folded her arms. “Does this involve Gaudy?”

“No, not unless you think he'd be helpful. I was thinking of playing a joke on my brother.”

“What kind of joke?” asked Tully, warily.

Vinyë wondered what Kip had told her of what they had got up to as kids and teenagers. Or, what Kip and Basil had got up to. That pair had been the trouble-makers, more than Vinyë, who had been old enough to know she would always be the one told to 'behave her age' if she had.

“You know the secretary romance novels?” she asked, smiling.

“I suppose I should probably deny it, but yes,” answered Tully.

“I was thinking of trying to disguise one as one of Kip's reports. I was wondering how far through he'd get before realising.”

“That's… brilliant. I will absolutely help with this,” said Tully, pulling open one of the drawers of her desk. “Report covers, papers, and ink are easy enough to come by, but it's going to take _ages_ to copy it all out.”

“Oh, we don't need to copy the whole thing,” said Vinyë, “Just enough to look like a normal report length. If he makes it to the end then I'll give him the book.”

“Do you mind if I ask the others? Zaoul's got the twisty mind for coming up with a believable report title, and I happen to know Eldo has access to a good library of them so we can pick one.”

“Do I want to know why Eldo has a collection of romance novels?” asked Vinyë, who very much did want to know.

Tully sniggered. “Well, they're not _his_. It's his father. I guess Prince Rufus finds them funny? Or relaxing? I don't know. I always thought he was jealous of Lord Mdang's close relationship with the Lord Emperor.”

“Maybe he's trying to get tips from them?” asked Vinyë, grinning now.

Tully shuddered. “I'll coordinate the others, and let you know when it's ready.”

“Thank you,” said Vinyë, “This is going to be _amazing_.”

It was all organised in a time that Vinyë found incredible. They had the whole thing ready in two days, including a spare full copy of the book. When Gaudy realised who was behind the idea, his eyes went wide with surprise.

“I thought this was Tully's idea, or perhaps Cousin Quintus?” asked Gaudy, confused.

Vinyë smiled. “I've been known to play a prank or two on my younger brother. I'll trust you all with the report version, and I'll hold onto the novel. Perhaps I should read it.”

“Mama!” said Gaudy, shocked, and then more quietly added, “Of course you can read whatever you like. I suppose it's just not something you like to think about your parents reading. Imagine if you found grandmama reading a salacious novel.”

“Oh no,” said Vinyë, feigning shock. “I probably would have recommended it to her. Solaara is rubbing off on you, my boy.”

Gaudy gave Eldo a painful and sympathetic look. That young man was clearly less certain about helping his friend's mother pull a prank on his boss. Yet he seemed resigned to Tully's enthusiasm.

Under *Imperial Navy Food Service Plan* and *Employment and Workplace Relations Ministry Budget Statement* they placed *Tax Services (Code of Professional Conduct) Amendment*. Zaoul reassured them all that the first two reports were from the previous week, and “Lord Mdang will remember, and not reread them.”

Vinyë took the novel, _Tithes and Dues_, to the sitting room to read. She sat down with the book, a cup of coffee, and planned to while away a pleasant afternoon. 

The cover showed a dark-skinned man, if perhaps not quite as dark as the Glorious One or Zaoul, sitting on a throne. He was, for no obvious reason, wearing the Solaaran-style outer robes with no tunic, so all of his chest was visible. It wasn't clear if he was wearing trews, for the man in front covered the lower half of his body. At the front was a young man, staring at the reader. To Vinyë's eye he didn't look particularly _Islander_, although he did have the right general colouring. He was dressed in an Alinorel scholar's robes; possibly the illustrator hadn't wanted to use the Service uniform for more _plausible deniability_, and no other reason. She couldn't quite decide if she was thankful or not that the man's portrait was from the navel up only.

It was, she admitted after two chapters, on the better side of trashy romance novels. The plot was contrived, but the characters were well-written and didn't suffer from any of the usual things that made her huff with derision. Nobody was described as having 'pure animal magnetism', for example. It also didn't seem like anyone needed to be a professional contortionist in the kissing or sex scenes. She decided it was probably worth finishing.

When Kip arrived at breakfast the next morning, his face was flushed.

“Have you been exercising?” asked Aunt Oura, who hadn't been brought in on the plan. Gaudy very carefully only looked at his pastry.

“No,” said Kip carefully, “I probably should. I try to make some time for dancing, but, well.” He sighed and sat down.

“I was thinking we might go to a bookshop, mama?” said Vinyë, “I am sure they have some things here we don't get in Gorjo City. Perhaps Dora would like a picture book?”

Dora bounced in her chair, nearly knocking over her glass of juice. “A book about a rhino? A baby rhino?”

“If you'd really like to see _all_ the books,” offered Kip, clearly trying to distract himself, “I could take you to the Imperial Library in the Palace. It has one of every book published in the Empire before the fall, and on Zunidh since.”

“That's too many books!” said Dora, “What if they fell?” She illustrated the point by toppling sideways off her chair.

“The librarians wouldn't let that happen,” said Kip, “You'll see.”

That flushed face was all the evidence Vinyë got that the fake report had any effect on Kip. She asked Gaudy if any of his friends had seen what Kip had done with it. As far as any of them could tell, he'd put it in his writing kit and not taken it out since. It was a disappointing result.

Similarly, Conju reported no evidence the novels in the Emperor's little library had been touched.

Perhaps they would have more luck after Kip and the Glorious One retired.
