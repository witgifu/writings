“I am… Most decomposed,” said his Radiancy.

Cliopher looked at him in sympathy. He recognised the tension in his lord's body as if in a mirror. It didn't happen often, thankfully, but it was very uncomfortable when it did. He couldn't remember the last time he'd been able to get relief in the usual way.

“I find playing complex music helps, my lord,” he offered, “Although sometimes I have had to resort to a cold bath, or even ice.”

Conju gave Cliopher a brief confused look before turning back to their lord. Cliopher sensing that Conju wanted him to make himself scarce, bowed, and went to his own room. 

As he drifted off to sleep, he wondered if the music he could hear was his own imagination or his lord's harp.

Finding himself *decomposed* when he woke in the small hours (what an excellent word for it), Cliopher got up. He fitted his oboe together, mind full of the Moon's perfect white hands on his lord's night-dark skin. Not wanting to wake anyone else, particularly his lord, Cliopher simply rested the reed on his lips, and went through the fingering. 

By the time he had finished three pieces he was feeling more his usual self. He put the instrument away, cleaning it as his teacher had drilled him. Then he went back to bed, and slept peacefully until the dawn chorus.

Cliopher had just finished dressing when there was a knock on his door. He was surprised to find Conju there.

“I'm sorry, does he need me?” asked Cliopher, automatically reaching for his writing kit.

Conju shook his head, and raised an eyebrow.

Now Cliopher was confused. Had he missed an appointment? Did he agree to do something this morning and forget? Was there something on his face?

“I am not sure of a delicate way to discuss this,” said Conju, and Cliopher could see the signs of suppressed tension.

“Come in then,” said Cliopher, and shut the door behind him. He indicated for Conju to have his choice of the desk chair or the bed. Something flickered on Conju's face at the bed, and he took the chair.

“Cliopher… Last night,” said Conju, looking at his own hands, “You sounded like what happened to his Radiancy is a common problem for you? That you can't climax on your own?”

“Not common, thankfully,” said Cliopher, “But not uncommon.”

Conju looked at him, pain clear on his face. “Have… Have you talked to a doctor?”

“Once,” said Cliopher. He hated seeing doctors, so it was only when the (unrelated) fungal infection on his toe had gotten painful that he'd gone. “They said it wasn't unknown, although it was mostly limited to people with certain blood issues. They couldn't find any cause.”

“When did it start?” asked Conju, his voice soft like Cliopher was very ill. Cliopher found it slightly amusing, but kept his face calm.

“Not long after I had the bonebreak fever,” he said, “I assumed it was simply a long-term effect.”

Conju's eyes went wide. “That was… Cliopher. Have you… been unable… for *centuries*?” Even the idea had rattled him so much he seemed to have lots the ability to form sentences.

“I've never had much of a sexual appetite,” said Cliopher, clearly much less embarrassed than Conju expected him to be, “I know you'd be climbing the walls, Conju. For me, it's on an occasional inconvenience.”

“Not even with someone else?” Asked Conju. When Cliopher shook his head, Conju looked stunned.

“No wonder you never…” muttered Conju, deflated. “Did you ever get checked for a curse? Or any other reason?”

“It's fine,” said Cliopher, smiling, “I just do a bit more oboe practice than I might otherwise.”

Conju sighed. “If you ever want someone to go with you to the doctor, or help you find one you're comfortable with, you know you only have to ask.”

“Thank you, Conju,” said Cliopher, sincerely. Next time he did have a medical problem, maybe he would ask the other man for advice on which doctors were the best.

Offering a blatant change of topic, he asked, “Now, breakfast?”

After breakfast, his Radiancy invited Cliopher to come snorkelling. Together they admired the life of the lagoon, pointing out particular fish or interesting coral.

Again, they sat on the sandbar to rest. It seemed just as perfect as that first time. Cliopher wished dearly he could take this moment back to the Palace, to share with his lord during the long work that was to come.

“Cliopher,” said his Radiancy, quietly.

“Yes, my lord?” said Cliopher, automatically, if perhaps slightly languidly.

“Conju is worried you might be cursed,” said his Radiancy, carefully.

Cliopher twisted, to look at his lord more fully. “Yes, my lord, he did say. But I have no reason to believe it is anything more than the unfortunate long term effects of a bout of illness.”

“You may say no, Cliopher, and I will not mention it again, but if I examined you magically you could be sure. It would put Conju at ease,” said his Radiancy.

Cliopher didn't mind, he trusted his lord completely. He didn't think there was much magic on him, perhaps some remnants of old healing magics, or traces of the work he had done as the Hands of the Emperor. “If it will make Conju happy, then of course, my lord.” He smiled warmly.

“After lunch, perhaps?” asked his Radiancy.

“Of course, my lord,” said Cliopher, “Thank you.”

After lunch, Cliopher returned to the common room to find his lord and Conju preparing for a trance. Quickly, Cliopher bowed and walked over.

“Take a comfortable seat, Cliopher,” said his Radiancy, “Hopefully this won't take long, but…” He trailed off, and looked out the doors.

Cliopher looked at Conju, who clearly had no more information. “Of course, my lord,” said Cliopher, and sat on one of the squishy armchairs. “Should I close my eyes? Or do anything?”

His Radiancy gave a small smile, “Just relax, perhaps imagine you are floating on the lagoon.”

Obediently, Cliopher closed his eyes, and lent back in the chair. It wasn't a hard thing to imagine, as he had been doing it only this morning. The wide blue arc of sky, with a scattered haze of clouds, drifting gently by. The lap of the water on his skin.

Eventually he was roused from this pleasant drifting by his lord's voice.

“Cliopher, have you been involved in any rituals with the Ouranatha where I wasn't present?” his Radiancy asked, his voice slightly horse.

“Many, my lord,” said Cliopher, trying to quickly wrench his thoughts back to Palace life, “Although none recently.”

His Radiancy took the cup of tea Conju provided and sipped it. “Could you identify any pattern?”

“They started after the high priests were informed I was to be appointed as your Hands, my lord,” said Cliopher, wishing he had his old appointment diaries to confirm. “They were more frequent in that first year, my lord, and since then perhaps one or two per year.”

Ludvic and Rhodin were giving each other tense looks behind his Radiancy. If the high priests had been up to something nefarious and the guard hadn't noticed, that was very bad.

Cliopher hastened to clarify, “I never felt any ill effect from any of them, my lord. They were usually quite short.”

His Radiancy put down his teacup, and breathed slowly. “I know it does not in any way excuse what has been done to you, Cliopher, but I want you to know that I had no idea, and if I had known, I would have ordered it stopped immediately.”

“I don't understand, my lord,” said Cliopher, watching his lord's hands as he pressed the nail of one perfectly manicured thumb into the quick of the other. “Is this related to what Conju and I discussed before? Did the Ouranatha place a curse?”

The very idea seemed absurd, yet that was what his Radiancy has looked for.

“That depends on one's perspective. I would consider it a curse, but so much of the magic that bound the Empire together was similar,” said his Radiancy. Cliopher wondered if he was taking refuge in abstract academics to avoid saying what he felt.

“They placed an Imperial binding on me, my lord? To what purpose?” asked Cliopher. It didn't sound like the bindings his lord had described before, he had no trouble in his work or thoughts. But perhaps he would have noticed? Was that a part of it?

His Radiancy looked Cliopher in the eyes, but then closed them. As if it was Cliopher's gaze that weighed him down.

“My lord, if it has not impeded my work all these years, it does not seem to have been a problem, perhaps it can be forgotten. Unless it poses some future danger, my lord?” offered Cliopher, trying to give his lord a way out of a conversation that he was clearly finding painful.

His Radiancy said, “Please leave us, everyone.” The on-duty guards saluted, and moved to stand outside the door. Ludvic said “Of course, my lord,” and he and Rhodin went out onto the verandah and out of view. Conju bowed, and picked up the tray, moving towards the dining room.

“Unless?” said his Radiancy, sounding more anxious than Cliopher had ever heard, “Cliopher, I know Conju is your friend. If you would like his support.” Conju paused, and looked back.

Cliopher still didn't understand what he was supposed to be worried about. “I'm fine, my lord,” he said.

Conju gave Cliopher a significant look that he didn't quite understand, but left.

“Cliopher,” said his Radiancy, when the last footsteps silenced, “The Ouranatha purified you, and bound your body as… as… an Imperial concubine.”

Cliopher blinked, trying to understand what this meant. The first thought he had was that Conju would have noticed immediately, if it had happened to him.

“I cannot imagine you will want to stay, knowing that has been done to you. I am not sure if it will be possible to remove, although I will try everything I can,” said his Radiancy, looking at the teacup, “Anything you want, Cliopher, anything I can do, please ask.”

Cliopher took a deep breath. “May I stay, my lord?” he asked, in a small voice. His Radiancy hadn't ruled it out, but perhaps he didn't want Cliopher around anymore, knowing he was able to touch his lord without risk of the taboos.

“But Cliopher,” said his Radiancy, “How… How can you stand to? Surely you would hate me, you can't…” He seemed unable to complete this thought.

Shrugging, Cliopher said, “It has been mildly inconvenient, but my lord, it was before I came into your service that I last had dreams of having children. I am more troubled that they did so without your consent.”

“Without—my—Cliopher…” his Radiancy looked up, finally, his eyes damp. “They should have asked _you_.”

“Well, yes,” conceded Cliopher, “But if you had agreed, I don't think I would have seen a problem with it.”

His Radiancy dropped his head into his hands. “That is _why_ I never allowed them to purify anyone, which they knew, so they didn't ask.”

Cliopher tried in vain to parse what his lord meant. “You wouldn't allow them to purify anyone who agreed?” he asked, eventually. As far as he knew, nobody had been purified at all. Well, except now for himself.

“If I allowed someone to be purified, then anyone, everyone, would have to agree to it also,” said his Radiancy, to the floor. “They would not have had a choice.”

“I wouldn't have said yes if it had blinded me, or if I had been required to never see my family, my lord,” said Cliopher, frankly. “But if I was already blind, or had lost my family in the Fall, then those would not have been such a great sacrifice.”

There was a time of tense silence, interrupted only by the birds.

“My lord,” said Cliopher, “If you are worried that I can touch you without consequence, of course I would not impose my presence on you. But please, believe me when I say, it has not been a sacrifice, not even something I made much note of.”

He thought for a bit, and then added, “Although, I would be more comfortable if you reformed the Ouranatha. I can't imagine Conju would feel the same way if it happened to him.”

His Radiancy took a shaky breath, swallowed, and then looked up at Cliopher. “He is very concerned for you.”

Cliopher let out a small huff. “I did tell him it was fine,” he said, “But we are talking about the man who dates every unsuitable man he can find, so…” Suddenly he realised he had just said that to _his lord_, and not Rhodin. “I mean, uh,” he added, “Conju is of a romantic disposition. My lord.”

The smallest smile flitted across his Radiancy's face. “He is, isn't he,” he said softly. He rubbed his fingertips along the edge of the chair. “If you genuinely want to stay, Cliopher, I would never send you away. I trust you completely. But it may not be possible to unwind the magic on you until I am also unwound from the taboos.”

“Well then,” said Cliopher, with a smile, “I will stay. And if you ever want someone to hold your hand, or scratch an itch between your shoulder blades that you can't quite reach, let me know.”
