In the end, it was Conju who went with Fitzroy to the hospital. Both Kip and Conju agreed it needed to be someone who was willing to bully Fitzroy into staying and waiting, and neither of them quite trusted Ludvic or Rhodin on that account. Kip said that if Fitzroy and Conju weren't back by sundown, he'd go and swap with Conju.

Of course, Fitzroy argued the entire way that he didn't need to go. Conju didn't actually dignify Fitzroy's argument with a response, but just handed him another bandage when he soaked through the first.

Just as they got to the doors, Fitzroy said, “It's not like it'll get infected, I'm a mage!”

Conju just sighed, and opened the door.

They were greeted by a friendly guide. “Hello! Can I help you?” the young man asked.

“Fitzroy has grated the tip off his finger,” said Conju, waving at his friend, “I had assumed grating cheese was a safe activity, clearly I was wrong.”

“It is safe!” argued Fitzroy, “I've seen Dora do it just fine!”

Conju didn't bother to argue that Dora was much less likely to get distracted than Fitzroy. He felt the point was obvious.

The guide smiled at the two of them, and pointed to a corridor to the right. “Follow the orange line on the floor, and the nurse will triage you.”

They followed the orange line, and Fitzroy only tried to leave once before they got to the nurse. There were two people in front of them in the queue, and of course Fitzroy recognised one of them. A young woman from the local library, who had been bitten by a parrot who had wanted her fish sandwich. It was a good thing for Conju's patience that the line moved fairly quickly. 

The nurse turned a smile on them and asked, “Name?”

Conju looked at Fitzroy, who blinked twice. “Uh,” said Fitzroy, “Fitzroy Angursell?”

The nurse raised an eyebrow at him. “Not sure, dear?” he said gently, as if perhaps Fitzroy might have a head injury or something else more serious.

“Well,” said Fitzroy, but didn't say anything more.

Conju rolled his eyes. “Sometimes government paperwork prefers he puts in his birth name, sometimes it doesn't matter.”

The nurse nodded, suddenly more understanding. “Fitzroy Angursell is fine,” he said, and wrote it down, “And how old are you?”

Fitzroy grinned. “Depends who you ask. I'd say about 67, but I've also been informed I'm 1037, so take your pick.”

The nurse was less surprised by this. “You were born before the Fall? And travelled,” he said, smiling, “It's not so uncommon. I'll put down 67. And what brings you in today?”

Looking a little sheepish, Fitzroy held up his left hand. His pinky finger was wrapped in a lump of bandages. He wiggled it a little, as if it might be a puppet.

“He cut himself grating cheese,” said Conju, deciding that Fitzroy's flare for the dramatic was likely to be holding up the queue. 

The nurse nodded. “About how long ago? And what have you done for it so far?”

“Less than an hour ago,” said Conju, not giving Fitzroy time to answer, “We washed it with clean water and bandaged it, but after it soaked through two wrappings we came here.”

The nurse made a note of this, and then directed them to the seating area to wait. There was a good collection of other people there with minor injuries. A child who had a scrape down their face, but was now running around despite their father's attempts to keep them seated. An elderly woman who kept telling the young man with her about her various near-death experiences.

People were seen relatively quickly, which was a relief. Although nobody was surprised when a young man hobbling on what was almost certainly a broken ankle (that angle was not natural) was hurried through directly.

Mobi Nga had been a nurse for nearly fifteen years. He'd worked all over the Ring, the Sociable Isles, and even around Jilkano. Across all those years and places, he'd built up a good sense for trouble. That was part of why he often got the triage desk. He could frequently spot the “just a twinge” that was something much more serious, or the person who was likely to get belligerent.

When Fitzroy came in (had he really renamed himself after the legendary folk hero?), he got the feeling something was off. It wasn't being brought in by a friend for an injury he claimed was minor, Mobi would have lost track of the number of fishermen brought in like that each day. It wasn't changing his name, especially for someone who was so clearly not local.

Rather, it was something in the way the other man answered for him. Something like a spouse, or a child, or a carer. Which he could have been, of course (although child looked unlikely). Fitzroy also seemed to struggle sometimes with eye contact. But — well, it was only a feeling. He had been wrong before. Nevertheless, he put Fitzroy's card to the top of Group Three, and wrote “T74?” at the bottom.

The queue continued. Some time later, there was a gap, and Mobi grabbed a coworker to hold the desk while he went for a coffee and toilet break. On the way back, he saw a healer putting Fitzroy's card into the 'Community Follow-up' box. He quirked an eyebrow at her, and she returned a nod and a sigh.

Sometimes, Mobi wished his instincts weren't so good.
