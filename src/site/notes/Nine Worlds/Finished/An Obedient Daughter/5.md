Four days later, I sat on a golden chair, in the Lilly Room. What staff remained in the Tower had done their best in that little time to repair me. I left the decisions about what medications I should take to the professionals, and did as I was asked. No longer told, of course. Suggested, begged, pleaded. Only Jivane dared to actually tell me, and I clung to that lifeline of humanity.

Spears stamped, and I gestured to admit the guests for this audience.

"His Highness, General Prince Benneret Halioren; His Grace the Honourable Halioren Lord Leaveringham," said the guard on the left. Ser Yovian, who should have been training recruits, and had been pressed back into his old uniform. 

In came the General, in *his* full dress uniform, followed by his great-nephew, who looked rather more nervous. The General saluted, and his young namesake made a very well-practiced obeisance. Mentally, I calculated how quickly one could travel from the court of Rhondé to Astandalas the Golden. Just enough time, barely, if the message to them had been sent at nearly the same time I was told.

There had _not_ been enough time for the General to come from the front, and he had not been at the meeting on my first day. An ally then, possibly Lord Thalassior, had notified them, and they had come as fast as they could. But the Prince had come, not with one of the princesses or ladies of the family, hoping to get them appointed to my household, but with a child. 

I gestured for them both to rise, keeping my gaze firmly on the painting behind. It was important to practice, while making a mistake would only be awkward rather than permanently disabling. Six days until the coronation. It was amazing how quickly that could be arranged, given how long it had taken to plan my wedding.

Considering carefully which title to use to address him, I turned to the General first. "Your highness," I said, "Your grace, to what do we owe this pleasure?"

A life of training, and years chained to _him_, had taught me control. So I kept my surprise contained, when 'General Ben', one of Astandala's heroes, dropped into the full formal prostration. As did the young duke.

"Glorious One, I have come as a representative of our family, to beg for your mercy," he said, and I could hear only the voice of one who had defended his men against _his_ repeated attempts to decimate them for the army's losses. 

"The Kings of Rhondé have ever been our loyal subjects," I said, carefully, "We know of no reason to doubt you, Prince Benneret, Duke Halioren."

"In the reign of your uncle, Glorious One, there was a disagreement between the Emperor Eritanyr and his younger brother," said Prince Benneret. I knew that, and I knew why, although I thought nobody else left alive did. It had been so many years ago that my mother had told me. When I had first learnt I had an older brother. I wouldn't look for him, if nobody else knew. But perhaps someone else _did_ know.

"The Emperor's brother left. Many thought he had left the Empire entirely. Emperor Eritanyr forbade anyone to talk of him, Glorious One," continued the prince, his voice perfectly court-formal, "He went to Rhondé, to the court of my mother. He had a daughter, who married my nephew. This is their son, most merciful and illustrious lady."

"Our cousin's son," I said, carefully. Acknowledging the relationship was dangerous for the boy, but it must be what they had actually sent him to achieve. "We see no reason to uphold Eritanyr's decrees about his brother. There is nothing to forgive. Please rise, both of you."

They both did so, with a fluidity that spoke of long training for court. "My eternal thanks, Glorious One, for your generosity towards our family," said Prince Benneret, "I had only thought to plead for the lives of the people of Rhondé, and did not imagine that you could be so merciful."

That, I knew, was not at all true. They had protected their secret through most of Eritanyr's rule and all of Shallyr's. They could have continued to live in obscurity, perhaps pretending to be an illegitimate offshoot of the Imperial family. And so, there must be some reason to admit to the truth now.

With a twist in my heart, I saw it. Had I not been thinking to reach out to those more distant relations who had fled the Empire? And here was a close relation. The King of Rhondé had seen the chance to cash in his mother's bet. I would be the last of my line, that was clear. Here was a healthy boy, ready to be trained to the position of Emperor.

I wondered how much of the rest of the royal family of Rhondé knew. I didn't even know if the boy's mother lived, or if she had any more children or siblings. "Is your mother still with us, cousin?" I asked, turning to young Halioren. There was no way he was the source of this plan.

"Yes, Glorious One," he said, his eyes carefully on her hands, "My grandfather died before I was born." He omitted mentioning his father, or grandmother, or siblings. Perhaps it was best that everyone else be kept out of the spotlight. Perhaps it was even best if I did not know. If the Ouranatha ever wanted to make another Marwn, any sibling or cousin of his would be the prime candidate.

Was he young enough that I could keep him hidden away, for his own safety? "And how old are you, cousin?" I said, once again granting him the familial term. Prince Benneret must have be thrilled, to hear me repeatedly agree to the relationship, even in this private setting.

"It is my fifth birthday today, Glorious One," said Halioren. I doubted that was true, but it had been a long time since I had seen many children, not since I had been one myself. It was also terribly convenient for it to be _today_. However, if that was what he had been coached to say, then clearly the Kind of Rhondé had a reason. Interesting that they had picked the boy, and not the mother.

He was not, thankfully, old enough to be betrothed or married. I would not make him my Consort, that would clearly be a waste. We had time to find a suitable distant relative for him to marry, hopefully one he could at least get along with.

"We welcome you to our court, cousin," I said. The same formal words that my uncle had spoken to me when I was nine. Perhaps I couldn't give him his freedom to choose, to love, but I would give him whatever I could. At least he would not be a child watching his family's debauchery, learning to become inured to it.

"My eternal thanks, Glorious One, for your extraordinary kindness to me and my family," said young Halioren. It was no kindness. If I had been able to send him home, to the life he had before his family had dragged him here to be my heir—but that was a past choice. If they had not taken the risk now, and I had found them later, would I have been forced to punish them? Possibly at least some token amount. Always, I had to balance two unknown evils, and choose the lesser.

Additionally, I knew that this was a trade. Prince Benneret had come and offered the support of the Kind of Rondé, his army, and his own, not insubstantial, political weight. It was an offer I could not afford to refuse, given the discussion I had so far with the other generals. They were used to doing as they wished, ignored by _him_. That I even wanted to know what they were doing was clearly a shock.

"Cousin Halioren. We shall grant that you be known also as the Grand Duke of Damara on Zunidh, and all the rights and property associated with that," I said. That was the title of my own father, which presumably by right went to my older brother. "Since you are our nearest relation," I added, carefully again ignoring my brother, "We also grant that you may be known as Prince, and as our heir."

I couldn't tell if Prince Benneret was surprised by this declaration; I thought possibly. An acknowledged heir was a risk, a rallying point for those discontented with the status quo. It seemed unlikely to me that those who had conspired to place _me_ on the Imperial Throne would then turn around and replace me with a boy. Not unless I showed signs of madness, or pregnancy. And if they came for me, would I defend myself? I was, in all things, but an obedient daughter of the Empire. If they didn't want me, I wouldn't stand in the way.

I did manage to catch the young Halioren's astonishment. Clearly, he had not been coached to expect to be made heir. What had he expected, then? To be made Consort, to a barren Empress, him aged five? Possibly, as Prince Benneret had said, he only was told to expect to receive an audience and a welcome to Court.

Now he would need to move out of the suite his great-uncle occupied. Presumably the apartments that belonged to the Grand Duke of Damara were empty. I did smile inside to think of him attending court from the same home I had. Would it be a home for him? There would be so many strangers; how could I keep him safe?

"If your mother, our cousin, would like to attend court, she would be most welcome. But we understand that she may prefer a quiet life, and would not wish to disturb her if that is the case," I hoped his mother _would_ come and help raise him. Yet I understood that it might not be safe, or that she might have other children. Having to choose could only be painful. 

Turning back to the Prince, I offered him another gift. "We know you will be very busy with other responsibilities, General Prince Benneret, but we would appreciate your assistance in appointing our young cousin a household. If there is anyone from Rhondé who would be suitable—family, tutors, staff—please send for them."

He bowed deeply, acknowledging what I treasure I was giving him. Letting him surround the Heir with people of his choosing. At some point, when I had people I trusted to spare, I would have to insert some to keep an eye on the boy. Currently, I didn't have enough people to even fill my own senior household.

"We will hold a court ceremony granting your titles and rank at the first auspicious occasion following our coronation," I said, wishing I had a trustworthy secretary I could have brought to this audience. "We will instruct the Ouranatha to notify you of the date."

The General and the boy accepted this as their dismissal. I hoped I had made the right choices, knowing I had condemned this little boy to the same prison as I lived in.
