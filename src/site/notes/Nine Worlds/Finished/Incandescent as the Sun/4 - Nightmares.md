Sleep was precious to Conju. Most days he was up before dawn, and in bed after midnight. He tried, when he could, to snatch a morning nap. Depending on his Radiancy's schedule he could sometimes get an afternoon nap as well. So to wake up at the third bell of the night was unpleasant.

It wasn't the temperature or his own dreams that had woken him. No, he could hear a whining, almost whistling noise. Reluctantly, he put his slippers on and shrugged into his dressing gown, and went to investigate.

No doubt Zala would be here in moments, and scold him for getting up rather than going back to sleep. But if it was a burst pipe or a stray piece of magic he wouldn't be sleeping through it anyway.

As he walked down the hallway, it became clear that the noise was coming from Sayo Mdang's, no, Cliopher's bedroom. Conju stopped by the door, listening.

Cliopher was crying. Conju couldn't make out much of what he was saying, most of it was too quiet or jumbled. But then, he screamed. Not loudly, but high and so very long. Conju was about to open the door, when it broke off with another sob.

“My lord, please,” came Cliopher's strained voice, “My lord, please, it hurts.”

Horrified, Conju backed away from the door. Zala hadn't yet appeared in the corridor. Conju shuffled back to his bedroom quickly.

By the time he had got into bed, he head heard Zala open Cliopher's door. He hoped she knew what to do, because Conju didn't.

---

Next morning, Conju made the difficult choice to leave the Imperial Apartments once his Radiancy had sat down to breakfast. His lord not called for Conju at breakfast since Cliopher had been preparing the reports. Yet, he still felt uncomfortable leaving.

He reassured himself, as he slipped quickly through the back corridors, that his duty was his Radiancy's happiness. If anyone was now, or had recently, hurt Cliopher, his Radiancy would be upset. If his Radiancy learnt that Conju knew, and did nothing, then of course Conju would be in disgrace.

From what gossip Conju had gathered that morning, it seemed quite likely that Cliopher had put someone's nose out of joint. The question was, did the nightmare represent recent events? He would have to see if he could get more details. Like who Cliopher had worked for before his Radiancy.

Conju's first quest, however, was to check that Cliopher was well enough to work.

He found Cliopher in the library, finishing a Broc Banner novel. It wasn't particularly good, in Conju's opinion. He'd bought it on the recommendation of a friend, and hadn't bothered with any others.

“Good morning,” said Conju, smiling politely.

Cliopher looked up from the book, smiling politely back. “Good morning, Conju. Did you need me for something? Is there a change in his Radiancy's schedule today?”

Conju had thought hard about how to broach the topic politely. If Cliopher had appeared particularly tired or run-down, that would have made a good entry point, but he appeared perfectly normal.

“I left a crème behind that I was experimenting with,” said Conju, “but I thought, since I was here, I would ask if you heard a strange sound last night? Something like a whistle? I wondered if perhaps it was someone in the gardens.”

Shaking his head, Cliopher said, “I don't recall hearing anything, sorry. I think I slept quite heavily, I don't remember anything at all.”

Well, Conju could hardly quiz Cliopher about a dream he claimed not to remember. He wished Cliopher well, and headed back to the Imperial Apartments, determined to find an answer from somewhere else.

---

There were so many stories about Cliopher, that it seemed impossible to sort fact from fiction. Had he really quit his position to travel around the world and prove the Wide Sea Islands hadn't fallen into the sea? How many heads of departments had he offended by correcting their statistics during important presentations? Was it true he had once submitted an annual report as long as he was tall?

In fact, there were so many people he *had* offended at one point or another, it seemed easier to assume it was everyone of note. 

The next question then, was who might use intimidation or violence against members of the Service? There were many stories from Astandalas of heads of department who used direct physical violence on their commoner staff. In fact, it seemed to be a better tool of control than Internal Security, since someone sent there might not come back, or might not come back whole enough to continue their work.

That was depressing enough. But then, when asked about the current situation, Conju received only silence. Was this because nobody would pass on such information to a noble? 

One night at Court, Conju turned to his newest source. 

“I have been most frustrated of late, by a story that has all the particulars and none of the names,” said Conju, with his eyes fixed on a cluster of preening young men.

“One of the many old legends of court? The maid with a leopard, or the duellists on the stairs?” asked Ser Rhodin, watching the same group.

“Nothing so amusing, I'm afraid. Someone was taking their frustration out on their secretary, who was heard begging for mercy,” said Conju, his tone light but his heart twisting.

“Such things—” began Ser Rhodin, but Conju held up a hand.

“Were commonplace before, yes. But Princess Indrogan runs a tight ship. Her highness would never stand for such a waste of resources, and the one detail attached to this tale is that it's recent.”

“Why not just tell her then?” asked Ser Rhodin, politely.

“Without names? Her highness would, with good reason, dismiss it automatically. But what disturbs me most is I am not hearing denials, just silence. It is as if my friends do not trust me with this answer,” answered Conju, letting a bit of his frustration come into his voice.

Ser Rhodin turned to Conju with an assessing look. Conju pretended not to notice, watching a group of dancers. Ser Rhodin looked at him for so long it was feeling very uncomfortable.

“Come, Lord Conju, you are usually more perceptive than this,” said Ser Rhodin, finally.

Conju gave him a scathing look.

“He's been walking all over the Palace, covered in bruises since starting his latest appointment. And it's very obvious why no-one would want to tell the Groom of the Chamber. Especially when he's also said to be your latest beau.”

Conju couldn't think of an immediate reply that wasn't the kind of denial that would make this worse.

“If it's any comfort, the chatter favours you as the cause. The guard have received at least three anonymous domestic violence tip-offs.”

With carefully honed restraint, Conju did not cover his face with his hands and groan. He did let out a gentle sigh. “Thank you for your honesty, Ser Rhodin.”

“Oh, I wouldn't have tipped you off if I didn't know how Sayo Mdang was actually injured. However, you might want to take him on a date in Solaara, so he doesn't seem quite so much like a prisoner.”

“We are *friends*. He is staying with me while he heals,” said Conju, acerbically.

“Yes, but you know a good story wins every time. You'd probably have better luck claiming you were father and son, or had joined the same cult.” Ser Rhodin managed to say this completely deadpan, which Conju found impressive.

“Fine, fine, I'll ask him to go somewhere _as friends_,” said Conju.

“You could bring him to court. You could dance together! It would be so cute. That would give people something to talk about, you hardly ever dance,” said Ser Rhodin. In Conju's opinion, Ser Rhodin was now getting far too invested in this idea.

“Surely there is some restaurant in Solaara that is the place to be seen?” said Conju, trying to distract Ser Rhodin with his favourite topic. It worked. Ser Rhodin suggested several places. At least one of them sounded clean enough that Conju might actually consider it.

Conju still worried about Cliopher's nightmares. Was it about his Radiancy? Or was it actually about someone else? Conju hadn't been there when Cliopher had broken the taboo, hadn't seen Cliopher until some days later. How injured had he been, exactly?

The doctor was unlikely to explain, she'd already felt she was overstepping by asking Conju to help. But perhaps either Cliopher, or Ser Ergaz might say more about what happened.

---

Conju managed to corner Ser Ergaz as he came off shift. It did feel a little like the guard was trying to avoid him, but Conju pressed past that.

“I wanted to ask, Ser Ergaz, what the guards' opinion of his Radiancy's mood has been of late. Has it improved, do you think?”

Ser Ergaz looked mildly relieved, and Conju realised that the other man had thought Conju might blame him for the rumours swirling around Cliopher. “Much improved, thanks to your intervention, Lord Conju. We are all very relieved.”

“Excellent, thank you Ser Ergaz… were you there, when Sayo Mdang first was injured? I was wondering how widely the information might have spread.”

“I was, Lord Conju. I escorted Sayo Mdang to the Infirmary. He initially tried to return to his rooms, you can imagine how that might have gone. But I persuaded him to seek medical attention. 

“When he was initially injured, only his Radiancy, Sayo Mdang, Guard Omo, and Guard Sergei were in the room. I was on the fourth antechamber, so I did not see or hear the, ah, inciting incident. Guard Sergei explained it to me, when he sent me after Sayo Mdang.”

“Thank you, Ser Ergaz,” said Conju, and then with a small smile added, “And if anyone does happen to ask you, feel free to say that Sayo Mdang had an accidental magical injury. And he is recovering under the care of his _good friend_.”

Then Conju bowed, and left before Ser Ergaz had a chance to respond.

---

A few nights later, Conju was woken when Cliopher screamed. This time, he did not hesitate. He was slightly worried the sound might travel beyond his rooms, since the magical soundproofing had yet to be repaired fully after the Fall.

Conju threw on his dressing gown and slippers, and did not hesitate at his own door or Cliopher's. The lights were turned low in the hall. When he opened the door to Cliopher's room, moonlight cut through the room.

The light from the window lay directly across Cliopher's face, and Conju wondered if it wasn't that which had triggered the dream. Cliopher was whimpering, and as Conju approached he could see Cliopher was sweating and tangled in the sheets as well.

“Cliopher,” said Conju, uncertain what volume was enough to wake him without scaring him further, “Cliopher, everything is fine. You're dreaming.”

Groaning, Cliopher tossed in the bed.

Nervous, Conju reached out a hand. “Cliopher, wake up. It's just a dream,”  Conju said gently, as he gripped Cliopher's shoulder.

With a gasp, Cliopher opened his eyes, looking wildly around. Conju leaned in closer, in the hope that Cliopher might be able to see his face. He still wasn't sure how far Cliopher *could* actually see. “It's Conju, Cliopher. You're safe. You've just had a nightmare.”

Cliopher took a deep breath in, and let it out very slowly.

“That's right,” said Conju, awkwardly, “You're safe, Cliopher. You're staying with me, remember?”

Blinking slowly, Cliopher said, “Conju? Oh… did I… I'm sorry if I woke you up.”

Conju sat on the edge of the bed, and patted Cliopher's arm. “That's alright, Cliopher,” he said, and then decided to try probing, “You were crying out, begging for someone to stop hurting you. That's not… nobody has been hurting you, have they?”

Cliopher hesitated for a moment before answering. He *had* just woken from a terrible dream, so it could just have been because he was dazed. But Conju wondered if maybe it was because he had to come up with a lie.

“No, not like that,” said Cliopher, “It was just a nightmare, just a mix of everything.”

Conju patted his arm again, before he stood up. “Well, this bright moonlight on your face probably didn't help,” he said, in his usual voice, “I'll close the curtains, and then hopefully you can have a more peaceful sleep the rest of the night.”

“Thank you, Conju,” said Cliopher softly.
