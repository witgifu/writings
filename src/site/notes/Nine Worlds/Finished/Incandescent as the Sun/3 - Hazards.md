Cliopher would not admit it to anybody, but by the time the fifth bell of the afternoon rang, he was exhausted. Every meeting was a trial of remembering names from voices, of navigating spaces. He had tried, as far as was possible, to do what he needed by writing. But too many people in the Service saw putting something in writing as a kind of threat.

Now that Lord Conju had told him about the magic, he could see how much easier it was for him to not collide with people or structures in the central spaces. That was a relief, because those spaces also held so many more people. Thus, when he arrived at the entrance to the Alinoral wing, he had no idea who in the crowd would be the person Lord Conju had sent.

He comforted himself that even if his sight was working, he wouldn't have known. There were a great many people in the Palace that he did know, at least by sight if not by name (or alas, voice). However, most of them were either guards, or members of the Service. Few of the Tower staff had reason to know him.

“Sayo Mdang?” said a prim voice, “I'm Zala, Lord Conju sent me.”

Before Cliopher had a chance to reply, another older voice added, “And I'm Imyan, Zala brought me along as well, in case there's anything up high.”

Cliopher smiled at them both, “Thank you for helping me,” he began, but Zala interrupted.

“Not at all, Sayo Mdang, it's our job. Now, I understand your rooms are at the end of the wing? Shall we make our way now?”

They kept Cliopher between them, and it was a pleasant walk back. They each were pushing some kind of trolley, seeming very prepared for the task. He chose not to interrogate them about their work.

Imyan spent the walk explaining her complicated extended family in Solaara. Some of them lived in the Palace, some who worked as day staff and lived in the town, and some who worked and lived in the town. They had all aged at different rates, and so Imyan spent her free time visiting her brother's great-grandchildren, who had children of their own. Cliopher found it fascinating.

For the first time since his injury, he was reasonably sure, he did not walk into that infuriating statue. He did bump two doorways with his elbows, but he was almost used to doing that by now. 

Arriving at his rooms, he let them both in. Cliopher had a sitting room, a bedroom, and a bathroom. For one person it was perfectly comfortable, especially when he took most of his meals in the refectories. He did have a little kitchenette in his room, but not being much of a cook, he tended towards things that kept well and could be prepared quickly.

“Shall we start in the bedroom, Sayo Mdang?” asked Zala, “You know where everything is, so if you just direct us, that will probably be easiest.”

Cliopher found the process very strange. Of course, having lived in these rooms through the Fall and its aftermath (a year, or a hundred?) he knew where everything was without being able to see. Yet, when Zala and Imyan moved them into the boxes they had brought, it seemed to take a different amount of time than he expected. He had more books and notes than he remembered. And yet, surely he owned more clothes that weren't his uniform?

Zala recognised his oboe case as she pulled it down from the shelf. “You play an instrument, Sayo Mdang? I will treat it carefully.” That offered some relief. He told them of his oboe, and his sister's dedication to her cello.

He had a small box of treasured mementos. Cliopher was grateful that when Imyan took it down from the shelf, he did not hear her open it. He had no desire to explain the shell that had floated to him on the dawn, or the promise efela Ghilly had returned.

As he directed them, Cliopher realised how impersonal his rooms were, even after so many years. Back home, he had at least the old wanted poster of Fitzroy Angursel, and the hanging his sister had made. Of course, it wasn't like he could easily have things sent from home. Not when the Palace had been in Astandalas, and even less so now. Yet he hadn't even acquired a cheap print to liven the walls. Did that say something about how he hadn't ever really belonged here?

Even with the two women working very hard, it took two hours to pack everything he needed. True, he didn't take any of the furniture. Nor did he take any of the kitchen things. He did take the mug he had bought from the potter in St-Noire.

When they walked back to the centre of the Palace, Cliopher felt guilty that he hadn't really done any of the labour. Yet, what could he have usefully done? He would have only got in the way. It was painfully humbling.

---

The door to Conju's rooms was not as impressive, of course, as the doors to the Imperial Apartments. Still, the door was carved with a large leafy oak tree. Zala opened it, and motioned Cliopher inside.

It wasn't as if Cliopher didn't know what the rooms of the nobles were like. In the early period after the Fall, when they were looking for survivors and supplies, he had visited them. But it was somehow different in the light, and when you weren't just hoping to find candles or food.

Lord Conju's first room was a public reception room, decorated primarily with wall paint and carved panelling. And yet, here was a carpeted runner, and a heavy plush bench. The mage lights were in glass acorn sconces, he saw as he passed one closely, thankfully walking too slowly to hit it.

As Zara showed him further in, down to the private rooms, Cliopher became more and more anxious. How could he possibly return such a favour? It was abundantly clear that not only did Lord Conju have staff under him in the Tower, he employed staff here in his rooms as well.

Before the Fall, Cliopher had known of a few people who had exchanged bed-warming for noble patronage. It was probably more common than he knew, if people were discrete. Is that the kind of arrangement Lord Conju was expecting? Cliopher braced himself.

The first test, he thought, was where Zara led him. They passed a number of doors, which Zara named, dinning room, library, reception room. Then, “This will be your room, Sayo Mdang,” she said, and opened a door, “My lord's room is further down the hall on the other side.”

His own room, and without a door directly into Lord Conju's. That was a relief. Zara's statement also confirmed what Cliopher had suspected, she worked for Lord Conju directly, and not for the Tower.

Then, Zara began describing the room. Cliopher felt embarrassed that this was something he needed. He supposed he could walk around slowly, and look at everything up close. It *was* easier to have someone give him some anchors to the blobs of colour, light and dark, that he saw around him.

Two plush chairs near the fireplace, a desk, and chair by the window. The other side of the bed, the door to the dressing room, and beyond that the bathroom. Zara walked around as she explained, so Cliopher could tell from her voice and movement where exactly those things were that she mentioned. Bookcases around the fireplace, carpets under the seating area and bed, a chest at the end of the bed.

They walked through to the dressing room, and the bathroom, with Zara explaining. Then Zara said, “Now, Sayo Mdang, my lord asked me to make sure you ate something before he arrived. So let me show you back to the dinning room. Imyan and I will unpack for you.”

Cliopher tried to protest that he should help. But both women refused, and promised to make sure everything was placed in as much the same way as it had been in his own rooms. Embarrassed, Cliopher accepted their assurances, and meekly followed Zara back to the dinning room.

Just as Cliopher sat down, a bowl of soup appeared from his right. He turned in surprise, but couldn't hear anyone. “Thank you,” he said awkwardly, hoping whoever it was hadn't already left the room. The soup was delicious, laced with warming spices and cheese. 

---

The next day, Cliopher noticed he was up and ready for work far too early. Without the long walk from his own rooms, he actually had a spare bell. Standing in the hallway, ready to head out, he realised this was likely to be a regular gap in his schedule. Nobody else would be willing to have meetings at the second bell of the morning. Or, indeed, the fifth bell of the afternoon.

He decided to peruse Lord Conju's library. You could always get an interesting impression of a person by what books they kept. It behooved him to learn a little more about his host.

It was a happy quarter-bell that Cliopher spent perusing the collection. There was a haphazard collection of novels, a very orderly selection of the standard history texts, and a perhaps not-very-surprising selection on the Imperial Household.

His interest piqued, Cliopher selected one of the latter, and took it over to the chairs. Cliopher swore silently as he tripped on the rug, and sprawled across the chair. The chair was padded, but the ends of the arms were wood, and seemed to be the perfect height to hit him in at the edge of his thigh muscle. He sighed, and gathered himself into the chair properly, to read.

He hadn't had any problems with the rugs in the bedroom he had been given. In the library, though, there were layered rugs in similar colours. He supposed it gave the room a cosy atmosphere. Maybe in Astandalas had been necessary to insulate against cold damp. 

Perhaps he should just read in his room, rather than in Conju's library. But Cliopher wasn't sure if the books were allowed to be removed from the room. And anyway, he was in the chair now. 

The book, on the constructions of the Imperial Baths, was surprisingly informative on how central government budgets were managed under Empress Anyoë.

---

Lord Conju, Cliopher noticed, often ate at odd times. He had told Cliopher on the first day, that he didn't have breakfast until the third bell. Although it took Cliopher a few days to realise that was because his Radiancy was then occupied with Cliopher, freeing Conju. Similarly, if his Radiancy was not eating with the Court, Conju would not have dinner until after their lord had left for his evening entertainments. For both Lord Conju and Cliopher, lunch tended to be something grabbed quickly between whatever they had on that day. 

So it was a few days before they sat down for a meal together.

“Good evening,” said Lord Conju, when Cliopher arrived in the dinning room, “His Radiancy is dinning with the court tonight, so I thought I would join you, Sayo Mdang.”

“Oh, please call me Cliopher,” said Cliopher, and blanched. What a stupid thing to say! Lord Conju was an aristocrat, and one of the most important people at Court, with the ear of the Emperor. And, Cliopher still wasn't clear on what Lord Conju expected of their relationship. Did he even want to be friends? Why had he taken Cliopher in? “I apologise, that is very forward of me,” he added, looking at his place setting rather than the other man, “Sometimes I forget…”

To Cliopher's immense relief, Lord Conju replied immediately, “Call me Conju, then. Have you heard there's a playhouse opening in Solaara?”

The conversation was light, and congenial. Conju clearly had an incredible mind for detail, maintaining a mental map of Court relationships that Cliopher found impressive. Cliopher suspected that, for Conju as for himself, his work was his calling.

Whenever Conju spoke of his Radiancy, there was a clear and intense admiration. Something Cliopher was most used to from those ardent adherents of the Imperial Cult. Yet surely, Conju more than anyone on Zunidh, knew that his Radiancy was a man. The person who built up and took down the sparkling armour of his dress and cosmetics, who washed him, who possibly even helped him on the toilet. 

Then Cliopher looked at his own hands, barely visible to him on the table. Didn't he have evidence that if his Radiancy was a man, he was a man like no other? Cliopher had been nearly blinded by casual eye contact. He was still slightly surprised that he hadn't been executed for that.

Conju and Cliopher removed to the drawing room together. The younger man took what was clearly his accustomed chair. Cliopher moved towards the one opposite.

Just as he had earlier in the library, Cliopher tripped on the edge of the layered rugs, and tumbled into the edge of the chair. Ashamed and embarrassed, he said “Sorry Conju, I must have drunk more wine than I thought.”

Conju was quiet, and Cliopher wished dearly he could see the other man's face. He rearranged himself into the chair in a more decorous fashion, sure his cheeks were burning.

“You had one small glass, Cliopher, that can hardly be an excess?” said Conju gently.

Cliopher didn't know what he could say to this. Should he double down on the lie, and claim he hardly drank, and one glass could make him tipsy?

“There really are too many rugs in here, and in the library,” said Conju, “It made sense in Astandalas, but in Solaara they are too easy to catch with the edge of one's sandal. I will ask Zala to change it. Do let her know if you find anything else like that Cliopher.”

Without further comment, Conju began telling an involved story about a courtier called Ser Rhodin, the man who had found the safe path to Solaara. It seemed to involve a surprising variety of tacos.
