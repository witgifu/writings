Kip woke up in _excruciating_ pain. It was impossible to tell what the time was. The room had no windows, and the lighting was always the same through the night. The gentle glow of the Emperor. Or was it the taboos that glowed, around the Emperor. He'd never really been clear on that—

Another wave of pain ripped through him, and his was unable to suppress a gasp. To stop himself yelling, or perhaps even screaming, he shoved his wrist into his mouth. The Emperor needed to sleep. Surely whatever this was would stop soon, he just needed to breathe through it. Then perhaps run to the toilet and take the biggest shit of his life.

That was his only reference point, the distress he'd had when giardiasis had ripped through his school. As the pain ebbed, he was able to think a little more clearly. It perhaps reminded him of other people's description of appendicitis. He hoped it was just something he ate, and not something that would require medication or even surgery.

Biting his wrist stopped him from yelling, but it didn't stop him from groaning. With the next wave of pain, his Radiancy rolled over, and slowly blinked open his eyes.

“Cliopher?” he whispered, “What is it?”

“Ngh,” was all Kip could reply, as his vision went grey, even though his eyes were closed.

“Cliopher?” whispered his Radiancy, more urgently.

Kip tried to breathe long and deep, tried to get himself under control so he could reply. He needed to reply, his lord asked a question.

“Don't know,” he gasped, “Stomach cramps?”

“Cliopher needs the doctor,” said his Radiancy, clearly and loudly enough for even a dozing attendant to hear. The guards were surely now on alert.

Two attendants pulled back the curtains on Kip's side of the bed. Gesturing for him to leave. Of course, he shouldn't continue to disturb his Radiancy's night. He had a bedroom of his own, he should go there and wait out whatever this was. Slowly, he began to wriggle his way over.

“Stay here,” said his Radiancy, “The doctor can come to you.” He emphasised his point by gently holding onto Kip's shoulder.

The attendants both bowed, and as if it had been their intention all along, began to arrange the room. A small table was brought up, next to the bed. A tray, with cloths and bottles, a jug of water and two purified glasses.

Kip dropped back into the mattress, and looked over at his Radiancy. “My lord,” he whispered, “You should rest. You needn't—”

“I would be up worrying anyway,” said his Radiancy, dismissing Kip's concerns.

It wasn't really clear how long it took for the doctor to arrive. When Kip could focus, he noticed a knot of attendants, and wondered what they were saying about him. Then he looked at his Radiancy, and tried to smile to convince his lord that everything was going to be okay.

“Dominus Lewin,” announced the left-hand guard, as the gentleman bustled through the door.

Of course, he first stopped to make his obeisance to the Emperor. Only if it had been an urgent illness of his Radiancy could such a thing be skipped.

“Dominus, Cliopher woke up with stomach cramps not half an hour ago,” said his Radiancy.

The physician approached the bed on Kip's side. “Has he had any other symptoms recently, Glorious One?”

“No, my lord,” gasped Kip, clenching at the sheets.

“He had headaches on and off for the past few months, and has gained a little weight,” said his Radiancy.

Kip should have been surprised by this, he hadn't noticed either. The weight gain was a little embarrassing, it was probably because he hadn't been dancing enough. Although, if this pain continued, he wouldn't exactly be eating anything.

Dominus Lewin bowed, “Thank you, Glorious One. May I examine him?”

“Of course,” said his Radiancy.

From his bag, Dominus Lewin pulled a number of different instruments. If Kip had been in less pain, more focused, he would have been interested to ask what they were. As it was, he could barely stay with it enough to follow the physician's instructions.

“Any initial impressions, Dominus?” said his Radiancy, sounding tense to Kip's attuned ears.

“Glorious One… The only similar case I have seen was a major infection of a tropical parasitic worm.”

The idea was almost as horrifying as the pain. It also seemed unlikely, given the strict dietary regulations Kip shared with his Radiancy. Yet, as the next wave hit, Kip couldn't help thinking of a giant mass of intestinal worms working their way out of him.

“Oh stars,” he gasped, “My lord, I need the toilet.”

Rather than taking him out of the room, the attendants delivered a chamberpot. It was yellow, covered in bees, rimmed in gold. Kip tried not to think about it. He just rolled out of bed, and pulled down his sleep pants.

Normally squatting gave him no troubles, having grown up in a community where it was just as common as sitting on chairs. With what felt like a turd the size of a watermelon in his arse, he needed to grip onto the bed for support.

His Radiancy reached over, and placed a hand on Kip's, his eyes full of concern. Attendants and the physician hovered. Kip thought he should have felt embarrassed to be having a bowel movement in front of such an audience, and in the Imperial Bedroom. Instead, all he felt was a desperate hope that soon this might be over.

He panted, and strained, and finally decided to abandon any dignity he might have thought he had left, and yelled. His body was on fire, he was sure that after this he wouldn't even have legs, much less be able to walk. All that was left of him was this urge to push this monstrosity out of him.

With a final yell, and a slippery thunk, it passed out. The only sound was Kip panting, trying to get himself back under control. His body still felt awful, ripples of cramps running through him. But he was sure that whatever it had been, the worst part had gone.

Kip pulled himself up a little, but the physician said, “My apologies, Glorious One, I think I should examine more closely…”

That was probably fair, Kip thought. He did feel like he might have split some skin. Obediently, he squatted down and lent forward over the bed.

“There is, ah, still more to come, Glorious One,” said the physician.

Kip winced. That wasn't what he wanted to hear. Behind him, one of the attendants did something with the chamberpot. Had Kip already filled it? It had felt like he might have, but surely not—

Another wave of cramps struck, and he did indeed feel the need to strain again. This time, while it was painful, it didn't seem nearly as hard. Panting, Kip looked up at his lord.

His Radiancy was frozen. A perfect serenity.

Through the haze of pain and relief Kip tried to smile, offer some reassurance that he was okay, his lord need not worry. It was nearly over.

“Glorious One,” said Dominus Lewin, “Congratulations, you have a son.”

A wrapped bundle was placed on the bed by one of the attendants. As they would have offered food, or a book. Kip, baffled, stretched up and lent closer. Was this some kind of bizarre joke? Why had the physician said that?

“Glorious One, your concubine is bleeding. He has torn some skin and possibly some muscle. I would recommend stitches.”

This seemed to break through to his Radiancy. “Of course,” he said, “Help him up here so that you can work.” He slipped backwards on the bed, not touching the bundle.

The bundle let out a cry. Without thinking clearly, Kip scrambled into the bed and gathered it up. Looking down, he saw a familiar nose and two golden eyes looking up at him, from a face as dark as he had ever seen. In perfect, if slightly wrinkled, miniature.

“How?” said Kip softly, “I don't have… where did you even come out of?”

His Radiancy was back to painful serenity. Kip realised he had broken the rules, again, by addressing the baby. That was followed by a much greater concern.

Quietly, and only once, his lord had confessed he didn't want children. Partly because of the family history of too-close relations. But most strongly because he couldn't bear to know he would be cursing them to the throne.

“My lord,” said Kip softly, “I am so very sorry. I swear by the Sun and the Moon I had no idea this was even possible. If it would please you, denounce the baby and me, and I will take it away.”

Something was happening with the doctor and Kip's nether regions, but he ignored it. Would Loaloa be far enough that the Empire wouldn't reach out to take the child? Would he need to run, to hide him? Kip wasn't sure he could make it, if the Imperial Guard and the Ouranatha didn't let him go.

Kip needed to stay with his lord, though. How often had his Radiancy whispered into his ear, as they made love, that Kip was the only good thing in his life. Would Kip deprive him of the little joy he had?

Or was that now always broken? If Kip's body could do this once, what was to stop it happening again? Would his lord want to risk the chance of a second child? No, Kip knew him. He would deny himself to prevent another. Lock away his heart, and become just the Serene and Holy One. Never again be Kip's sweet lord.

“Cliopher,” said his Radiancy, “If we denounced you for adultery, you and the baby would be executed.”

Kip's breath caught. Execute a *baby*? Simply because one parent was incorrect? Kip was willing to die for his lord, had known it was likely that he would. Instinctively he held the baby, his lord's son, tighter. Not the child, they couldn't have the child.

“If we acknowledge him… A suitable foster family would need to be found,” said his Radiancy, his eyes closed.

Kip reached out a hand to grasp his lord's. His mind scrambled, trying to think of a solution. Who could protect his lord's son, and would understand the dangers? Kip's family would raise him with joy and love, but it would never be allowed. No, it would need to be a great noble, to be entrusted with even an illegitimate Imperial child.

“Melissa,” whispered Kip. Then said more clearly, “The Grand Duchess of Damara, my lord.”

“I couldn't—” began his Radiancy, but he cut himself off, “Yes.”

The tiny boy began to cry in earnest. Kip tried to think of what he knew of newborn care. There was something about having the baby touch the mother's skin, he thought. His nightclothes were not made for access from the top, so he used two hands to hold the tiny face up to his neck. They would very quickly need…

“A wetnurse, my lord?” he asked, tentatively.

“Yes, one must be found immediately,” said his Radiancy. Two attendants hurried out of the room. Kip hoped they would also be getting things like clothes, and clean sheets for the bed.

The physician appeared again in Kip's sight. 

“Glorious One, if you would not wish to permanently damage your concubine, we would recommend abstaining from penetrating him for eight weeks,” he said.

Kip was used to this kind of talk. As if he was no different from a cup, that might be thrown away if it chipped. He knew that was not how his lord thought of him, that he did really love him, as kip loved his lord. But this was what was expected, what was allowed.

“Thank you, Dominus Lewin,” said his Radiancy, “And our son?”

“Perfectly healthy, Glorious One,” said Dominus Lewin.

Anxiously, Kip considered all the things he would have done differently if he had known he was pregnant. How he could possibly have suspected, he had no idea. The doctor still hadn't mentioned how it was even possible for him to have given birth. Yet, the evidence that it *was* somehow possible was snuffling into his neck.

*Magic?* mouthed Kip, to his lord. His Radiancy's eyes flicked to the physician, and Kip took the hint that now was not the time to discuss it. Which meant, likely yes, and likely a faction within the Ouranatha. They didn't know if Dominus Lewin was a part of the Court that continuously grumbled for the Emperor to beget an heir. Who would now divide into those who were thrilled, and those who discounted the child immediately.

“The child is not to be announced,” said his Radiancy, “At least not until we are sure he will live.”

Everyone bowed. The attendants began spiriting things away for cleaning, the physician hovered awkwardly.

Kip had just reached the same thought as his lord. Even though child mortality was a lot lower than it had been in generations past, still. It was quite possible the baby had some congenital issue that was not immediately obvious. Of course, the rumours would be harder to control. It was mostly in the interest of Tower staff not to spread it, but the courtiers, government functionaries, and most importantly the priest-wizards, would have no such constraints.

It would be best if they could spirit the child to Melissa as quickly as possible.

Kip could not stay with both the baby and his Radiancy. It would probably not even be a choice. Melissa would take the little one, for his protection. Kip would stay in the Palace, for if the child was to survive, he needed Kip to be seen to be in favour. How long, though, could he remain so in truth? His Radiancy waved this thought away. “I would rather give that to you,” he said, moving closer, “There will be so much to do.”

As soon as the wetnurse arrived, it was likely that neither Kip nor his lord would hold the baby for a long time. Possibly the next time they would even see him would be for his first court appearance at nine. They should savour what time they had now.

Then he brought their baby down, unwrapping his tiny head so they could both look closely. He was asleep now, tiny hands balled into fists, pulled up against his chest. There was a name that would fit. _Fitz_, son of, _roy_, the king. Did he dare? _Fitztorin_ would probably fit his Radiancy's family naming tradition better. A name was probably all he was going to be able to give the little boy. He wouldn't make him a toy vaha, as his father had. Wouldn't teach him to sail, or even to light a fire. Nor would his Radiancy get to give him more than the correct gifts of an Emperor. Nothing meaningful.

“What's the Antique Shaian word for _star_, my lord?” asked Kip, softly.

“Aster, like the flowers on Alinor,” said his Radiancy, just as softly, running his hands along Kip's arms.

“Aster,” echoed Kip, “Aster… Damara?”

“Yes, if I am to claim him,” said his Radiancy with a sigh, “Although the nose tends to give us away.”
