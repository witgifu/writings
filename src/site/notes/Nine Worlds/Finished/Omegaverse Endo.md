Cliopher gripped the edge of the table, carefully keeping his court face impervious, although his focus faded. He needed to remain here, just for another hour. He could ride this wave of pain and nausea and then turn the sails and hold until the end. It wasn’t the first time he’d had to work through the first day of a heat, although that had been many years ago. It probably wouldn’t be the last time either. The timing was simply unfortunate, it had come a day early and clashed with the last day of the council of princes.

All Cliopher's skill was focused on forcing himself to smile politely, as the world went grey around him.

But then it slowly came back, as he knew it would. No doubt the smirk on Prince Rufus’ face was because he thought he’d made some unassailable point, which Cliopher had entirely missed. Breathing slowly, in through his nose, out through his mouth, Cliopher tried to act like he was only being derisive rather than holding on to his awareness by the skin of his teeth.

“It’s clarified in the appendix,” he said, managing to sound dismissive rather than angry.

Cliopher could admit that his management of the discussion through the rest of the bell was not up to his usual standards. The notes were sufficiently complete, however, which was his primary duty. As it was the last session, it was mostly circular arguments about issues that would need to be presented again next time.

At least, he was reasonably sure that was what hat happened. When his Radiancy finally called an end, it was only his tone that let Cliopher know what was happening. The words didn't quite make it through. Perhaps one of the few times he saw the advantage to all the pomp and ritual, he could predict what would happen even if he wasn't quite on the top of his game.

Usually, he would have followed his lord back to the tower for a debrief and perhaps a lunch. Not a good idea today. Thankfully Rhodin was on duty with his Radiancy today. Cliopher signed ‘unwell’ to his friend, a knot of anxiety twisting inside him. Hopefully that would suffice to explain his absence.

His lord must have seen the gesture, however, because he said to his guards, “It has been a trying few days, we shall rest this afternoon.”

Cliopher nearly cried. He knew it was just the hormones, but no alpha had ever… well, of course his lord was not just *any* alpha, but still. Such an unexpected kindness. Cliopher struggled into the obeisance as his lord departed, and then hurried out the door himself.

Four weeks earlier, Cliopher had sat in his uniform across from a new doctor. He had practically begged to be given another suppressant script. But there had been new research that had linked them to bowel cancer, on top of the heart problems they had been known to cause for decades. The doctor had declared the medications too risky. Had recommended peppermint tea for the nausea, headache powders for the pain, and to take some time off. After all, wasn’t it Sayo Mdang’s recent reforms that had given everyone paid sick leave?

As Cliopher dry heaved into a garden bed, he cursed the doctor. There were just enough sick days that if the average omega never had any other reason to take sick leave, they’d be fine. Cliopher was neither average, not completely immune to all the illness that went around the Palace. He would need to work through at least some of this.

Not now though, he thought, as he stumbled down the back paths of the gardens. Trying to stay in the shade and not be seen. Now he needed to say clear of alphas and get to bed. Then he could collapse into a shivering wreck.

Somehow, he made it back without seeing anyone. Probably without being seen, but that was harder to judge. Hopefully, if there had been anyone, they hadn't realised who he was.

When he finally made it into his room, into his bed, he burrowed under his blankets and pillows. As he flitted in and out of awareness of the world, he dreamt of his sister Navalia comforting him through his terrifying first heat. Singing to him, helping him to drink. No judgement, just love.

When the heat hit him in full, sometime in the evening, nobody was there. No friend, no family. He cried, alone, begging for the alpha that couldn’t be with him.

Cliopher blinked his eyes open. There was a scent, familiar, although not what he craved. It was like smelling something deliciously savoury when you wanted nothing but sweets. Unfortunately even the thought of food made his stomach roll and cramp. He reflexively curled into a ball.

“Cliopher?” came the voice that smelled of smoke and amber, “Our lord sent me to check on you—”

Cliopher tried to make a sound, but all he could manage was a pathetic whine.

“What kind of nest is this?” said his friend with a tut, “Really you’d think it was your first time.”

There was no way Cliopher could explain he hadn’t had time, had been so busy, had been caught unawares. Plus, it was his first time in a long time. First time he’d been unmedicated at all since he’d sailed through the Wall of Storms. When he'd been sailing, for months, or years, at least he could listen to his body. He could prepare, and wait it out, could access what natural treatments he knew.

Here, he was unprepared, untreated. If he managed to crawl out of his room for anything, alphas would be drawn from everywhere. In Astandalas, an unspressed omega was an invitation to all. In Solaara it would, thankfully, only result in scorn. Of course in Astandalas doctors handed out suppressants without question.

Cliopher wasn’t sure, right now, which he preferred.

“I’ll get you some things,” said Conju, and disappeared again.

Time passed in dollops, of searing bodily hunger, and quiet pained exhaustion. Cliopher sweated through his uniform, which he hadn’t even managed to remove. Then the damp made him shiver, but he couldn’t remember how to undress.

In his core, was the voice that said his alpha would help. Would know. Would care for him if he could only reach out—.

Smoke and amber returned. More pillows, more blankets. He set up two small warm mage lamps, and turned off the main light. Unwrapped a bundle of sex toys and set them on the side table. Finally, he lit a stick of incense and sat down on the bed.

Cliopher sobbed, and curled away from him. Wrong smell, wrong wrong wrong.

“When you are better,” said Conju gently, “We will find you a new doctor. If I have to get Rhodin to find a quack we can bribe, so be it.”

Cliopher tried to put what Conju was saying together, but he was struggling. Wasn’t it Conju who had said it wouldn’t be so bad? That he was probably remembering it being worse than it was? Hadn’t he himself tried the ‘natural’ way and found he preferred it?

“I had forgotten,” continued Conju, “It has been a long time for most of us. I had forgotten that some of us took suppressants not just for the comfort of others. I should have listened more carefully.”

Cliopher scrambled to the edge of the bed, and dry heaved onto the floor. He wished Conju would just come out and say what he meant. With a spinning head, it was hard enough to just parse the surface meaning. Then a wave of need hit him, and he was writhing all over the bed, bitting the pillows and twisting the blankets around himself.

If Conju said anything else before he left, Cliopher missed it.

Some time later, Cliopher realised he was no longer nauseous. Not hungry, he knew it would be days before he was hungry. But at least if he drank some water he might not immediately vomit it back up on himself. He looked around.

On the floor, tucked by the bed where he could reach it, but probably wouldn’t knock it over, was a ceramic jug. With a straw in it, like a giant mug. Cliopher bent over and took a sip.

It wasn’t water, but something sweet and vaguely herbal. Also, wonderfully cold, which must be some kind of charm placed on the jug. At this point it was the greatest work of magic Cliopher had ever known. He took another sip. Since his stomach was still sore from cramping, he knew he needed to take it slowly. What he wanted was to slurp down until the jug was empty.

Well, no, what he *wanted*… but there were many things he couldn’t have. That were impossible. 

He should focus, while he had this moment of clarity, on looking after himself. Removing his disgusting uniform, for one. Possibly trying to use the toilet. Slowly drinking the liquid in the jug. There would be, at best, a bell of respite.

In the bathroom, he looked at the medicine cabinet. If he was keeping down water, he could try a painkiller. It was always a trade-off. The headache powders could only take off the very edge. He had some stronger medication he’d been given when he broke his arm… which helped with pain, but made him even more nauseous.

Deciding not to risk either on an uncertain stomach, he stumbled back to bed. It wouldn’t kill him. Nobody died from a too-strong heat.

Cliopher wondered vaguely who Conju was getting to make the trip out to his room. It made him uncomfortable to think that they were seeing him in this state, but at least Conju knew how to choose people who were discreet. No gossip about Cliopher’s state would be spreading around the Palace.

For someone had to be visiting. The jug had been refilled, and a bowl of chopped dried fruit had appeared. Even if he couldn’t manage chewing, it was small enough he could swallow it whole. The incense had been replaced, and the uniform he’d left on the floor had disappeared.

Part of him wanted to cry with gratitude, part with shame. His room was a mess, his bed even worse. One of the pillows was probably beyond repair, and the sheets were stained with sweat and other fluids. He should have had rags, or protective sheets. It would probably take weeks for the pheromones to dissipate. No doubt he’d get angry looks from the cleaning staff who would have to be careful who they sent.

At some point Rhodin visited. “I’ve found you a new doctor,” he said gently, “Although they said there’s not much they can do this time.”

Cliopher grunted. Rhodin must have been around Ludvic and his Radiancy, because there was something of the other men’s scent on him. It was intoxicating, and he wriggled closer to breathe it in.

“Conju sent me with… ah… he said this might help,” said Rhodin. From a bag on the floor he pulled a small hand towel.

Cliopher whimpered. That was the source. The aroma was divine, majestic, wonderful. He dug his fingers into the mattress, trying to stop himself from grabbing it. 

Then his mind finally registered what the smell was. It was wrong, they shouldn’t do this. That was stealing, surely. And if it wasn’t, if his Radiancy knew what Cliopher was, knew what Cliopher wanted… No, there was no way his Radiancy would have approved.

His lord hated being worshipped. Hated being a sex symbol. All his guards were alphas or betas. Nobody in heat was allowed in the Tower, not even Conju.

Why was Rhodin taunting him like this? Was it a test? That would make sense. Cliopher was head of the Service. Strong heats like this made him vulnerable. What if it had been someone else? Could they have used this against him? Could they even have extracted information from him?

He wasn’t sure, the very idea terrified him. Next time he would lock the door, and make sure only Conju had the key. Or maybe the guard could take him into protective custody.

With a groan, Cliopher forced himself to move back, and away. Pushed himself as far away as he could, and smothered his face into one of the most disgusting part of the sheets.

“Sssorry,” he mumbled, unsure if Rhodin could hear him.

“That’s okay, if you don’t want it. Conju was adamant it would help. Is there… someone else?”

“Noooo,” whined Cliopher into the sheets. Breathing heavily, he managed, “Not betray… my lord.”

There was a pause, and Rhodin shifted uneasily. “His Radiancy gave permission,” he said eventually.

Cliopher looked up, uncomprehending. Baffled. How did he even know? Oh of course, how foolish. His Radiancy would have been able to smell it in the Council of Princes. Perhaps the others had as well. The shame bit into Cliopher again. He should have called in sick that morning. It was irresponsible. They could have easily accused him of using his state to manipulate the Council.

“His Radiancy asked Conju if there was anything you needed, if there was anyway he could help,” said Rhodin, still clearly uncomfortable with the whole situation. Trying to tuck it behind his professional demeanour, which was obviously a challenge.

Slowly, the words seeped through Cliopher’s addled mind. His lord, not Conju, had sent this. As a gift, to help him.

Cliopher’s eyes welled with tears. This time he let himself do what he wanted. He scrambled back to Rhodin, and snatched the towel, hugging it like a child with a comfort blanket.

“Th-thank you,” he mumbled. A happy fog descended on him, as the smell filled his mind. It didn’t stop the rolling pain in his lower back, or the stomach pain, or the ache across his scalp. But it was soft, and warm. And contained the love of his friends, and concern of his lord.

“Rhodin,” he managed, after a little while of simply basking.

“Yes, Cliopher?” said the other man, and Cliopher was slightly surprised he was still there.

“Thank you,” said Cliopher, “Sorry.”

“Don’t worry about it, Cliopher,” said Rhodin, “Have you had anything to drink recently?”

Cliopher couldn’t remember, and shrugged.

“Let’s fix that next then,” he said, and helped Cliopher drink from the jug. 

He didn’t mention the way Cliopher was rubbing his face on the towel, although he did look worried when a cramp sent Cliopher curling back into a ball suddenly.

“Maybe I should ask the doctor to visit anyway,” said Rhodin, gently, “Perhaps she can recommend something more.”

Cliopher shook his head. There was nothing, he knew. He’d kept abreast of what little research was done. Short of knocking him out for a week, which no doctor would be willing to try. The only other reliable treatment was prevention. 

Or… Cliopher smothered his face in the towel, trying not to think about the other treatment. The recommended treatment. He didn’t want it, he’d never wanted it, and his lord was untouchable anyway. It probably wouldn’t even help much, and his lord was so kind and generous even if… 

Even if he could. Why would he possibly want to be around such a useless wreck of a man.

Cliopher balled up the towel, and cried into a pillow. Another wave of cramps overwhelmed him, and he lost track of time again.

“I had heard rumours that coming off suppressants could cause blowback,” said smoke and amber.

“Nonsense pedalled by idiots,” said a voice Cliopher recognised, which smelled of antiseptic and warm cotton.

“Then…” said Conju, uncertainly, “Has he always…?”

“Probably,” she said gently, “Estimates vary wildly. Because it’s so stigmatised.”

Cliopher wanted to say it wasn’t that bad, really. He’d heard of someone back home who had gotten stuck in heat, and after nine months had overdosed on painkillers. Nobody had been sure if it should have been ruled an accident or a suicide.

Instead, he said, “Doctor?”

“Yes, Sayo Mdang? I can offer you some stronger painkillers? I’m worried you’re not drinking enough.”

Cliopher shook his head, “Morphine makes me vomit.”

“Ah,” she said, “Yes that does happen to some people. Which will make the dehydration worse, and that’ll feed back into the cramps and pain.”

“Mmm,” said Cliopher. He already knew all of that. Had been told it by every doctor he’d seen since he was a teen.

“I’m sorry,” she said gently, “You’ve probably heard that a lot.”

Cliopher didn’t waste energy replying. He crawled over to the edge of the bed, and took a few sips of the drink. Then he fell back onto the bed and looked up at the ceiling. 

“Would you cut it out?” he asked.

The doctor sighed. “It’s only effective for about a third of patients,” she said, “It would make you infertile, it usually causes sexual dysfunction, it’s very risky.”

Cliopher tried to breathe, tried to think clearly. He had arguments, good arguments, for why it was worth the risk. Why he didn’t care, that he didn’t need those things anyway, and if it did work he would be *safe*. Instead, all he could say was, “Please.”

“Not now, Sayo Mdang,” she replied, “But if we go through all the risks together, when you’re well, then yes.”

“Thank you,” said Cliopher, and he let the tears run freely down his face. Someone had listened, finally. Or perhaps it was just that she had seen the horrific state he was in. He didn’t care.

She might change her mind later, she probably would. He didn’t have the energy to try to make her swear.

“What does…” began Conju, but cut off his own question.

“A surgical intervention to reduce the hormones—” began the doctor.

“Castration,” interrupted Cliopher. 

“… a little crude, perhaps, but yes. Not entirely unlike,” said the doctor.

“Oh,” said Conju, and Cliopher pushed his head up a bit so he could see the other man’s face. It looked horrified.

“It’s different, for omegas,” said Cliopher. Then he remembered he was talking to Conju, and amended, “Well, it’s different for me, anyway.”

Conju showed no sign that he believed Cliopher, but it was equally clear he didn’t want the conversation to continue. Cliopher had no interest in forcing it. He was exhausted.

He rolled away from them both, pulled the hand towel up to his face again, and tried to go to sleep.
