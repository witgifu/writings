When next morning you bade Cliopher to request the Ouranatha release their hoards of salt to the usage of the people, Cliopher forbore comment. But you looked at his face as he noted your precise words in his ever-efficient shorthand. It was tired, stretched.

"What interesting report was it that kept you up so late, sayo Mdang?" you asked, allowing just a hint of levity to your voice, and deciding for once you dared to ask a personal question. It was dangerous for you to ask questions, but perhaps something of my wild magic yesterday had infected you.

"My lord?" asked Cliopher, obviously confused by the change of subject. Then he realised he had been asked a direct question by you, the Sun-on-Earth, and that was not the correct response, "My sincere apologies, my lord, if my performance is suffering today. I was expecting someone to call on me last night; they did not come. I will endeavour to improve, or if you would prefer I can find a substitute for today." A small shadow of fear slid over his face.

You should have expected that. You were not supposed to care about the welfare of your staff, your household, except as it inconvenienced you. You were not supposed to actually *like* them, want the best for them.

"Sayo Mdang, you are, as always, performing admirably," you said, trying to smooth over the situation. I wanted to ask, joking, what kind of person would dare to stand up such an excellent man. But you did not. I had already put our foot in it enough.

The awkward moment was interrupted by a knock on the outer door. The outer guard passed Ludvic a note, and after glancing at it Ludvic passed it on to Cliopher. You paused in your pacing.

Cliopher took it with shaking hands. His face showed the ghost of a pure terror that you had only seen on his face once before, at the end of his very first day as your secretary. He scanned it quickly.

Then his usual manner returned. "A granary outside Solaara has collapsed," he said, in his business-like way, "They are asking if we can supply any labour and possibly store some of the grain until a replacement is built."

You waved your hand, "Of course. We should assist in any way we can. Ask the guard captain if he might send out the trainees to help. We should have plenty of magically controlled storage space."

Cliopher dutifully noted this down. Then he wrote out a quick letter, presumably to your guard captain. He passed it, still damp, back to Ludvic, who passed it back through the doors.

"Were you expecting such news, Sayo Mdang?" you asked, trying to clarify the reason for the fear you had noticed earlier.

"No, my lord," said Cliopher, reorganising the papers on his desk, "but any news deemed worthy of interrupting your Radiancy is not usually good news."

This was, unfortunately, true. It was not a common occurrence, but it also was not one that usually gave Cliopher cause to fear. You had seen worry, resignation, disappointment, frustration. Not fear.

You resumed pacing. Brushing your fingertips across your desk, trying to decide if you (if I) dared to probe further.

Cliopher was waiting to resume taking dictation.

You tried to piece together the information you had. Cliopher was expecting very bad news, possibly related to the visitor who never arrived last night. The news was so bad that he was willing to believe that your time might be interrupted for it. 

He could have a lover (and why would he not, he was attractive) and they might have come to some injury or misfortune that had prevented meeting Cliopher last night. If they had not been found before Cliopher was due to start work, he might have asked to be sent word. But then, they would not interrupt *you* for that message. It would be left with the pages outside, and Cliopher would get it when you dismissed him (however unfair that was).  

Who would be visiting Cliopher in the night, and sending notes into the Imperial Apartments during the day?

You paused, and looked full in Cliopher's face. You had an idea, and you didn't like it. You didn't want to believe it possible, that your staff could still be afraid, that they still *should* be afraid. You needed to know.

"Who were you expecting last night, Sayo Mdang? And what did you expect that note to say?" you asked, trying to soften the imperial serenity.

Cliopher looked up, oh so briefly, and then back down at his desk. Then he stood up from his desk, came around to the centre of the room, and at the prescribed distance performed the formal obeisance. I cringed, you were serene. We needed to know.

"My lord," he said, a little of the fear returning to his voice, "the draft of the report I prepared for you yesterday was stolen. It is entirely my fault, I should not have let it out of my sight until I was sure it was destroyed or securely archived. I humbly beg your forgiveness, most merciful lord. Given the topic, and guessing who might have taken it, I was expecting the Ouranatha to order my execution for treason, my lord."

He did not look up, but placed his forehead down on the marble floor.

You breathed. In, out. In, out. In, out. You were Most Serene, Protector of the People. And your secretary was terrified he would be executed for losing a report. The chains of the Empire bound you still, and endangered everyone near you.

It wasn't an unfounded fear. If you had told them, on that first day, the Ouranatha would have ritually executed him for looking you in the eyes. And if they had read that report a week ago, they might have trumped up some charges. Perhaps, if they had stolen it, even now there was a risk of them trying.

Even after the warning you gave them last night.

Without further thought, I began placing every magical protection I could think of on Cliopher. He was taking risks, enormous risks, for you. We must do everything possible to protect him in turn.

"Cliopher sayo Mdang," you said, while I wove a net against eavesdropping, "You have our full pardon for misplacing that document."

Cliopher finally lifted his face from the floor, although he still would not look at you. You could see the glint of tears in the corners of his eyes. "Glorious One, this unworthy servant gives you his most sincere and heartfelt thanks for your most generous mercy."

My heart twisted. I fitted a magical cloak against minor poisons around his shoulders. "Furthermore," you continued, "We informed the Ouranatha last night that we would be more closely overseeing their activities. They have been acting too often in our name without our permission. The Empire is dead; kidnappings and secret executions should have died with it."

You gave the signal for Cliopher to rise. He hesitated, but eventually rose and resumed his seat.

"Guard Omo, make sure the guard captain is aware that someone may have been stealing our documents from our secretary. If they have not simply been misplaced or misfiled, it is a serious security breach," you said, and resumed pacing.

Ludvic Omo saluted, and then passed the word through the door.

I put a small finding charm on Cliopher, so if the Ouranatha did try to steal him, I could trace him quickly.

"Make a note for tomorrow to review the laws pertaining to the Ouranatha's role as a justiciary power," you said to Cliopher, who was cleaning his pen.

When he was ready, you resumed dictating about the distribution of salt.
