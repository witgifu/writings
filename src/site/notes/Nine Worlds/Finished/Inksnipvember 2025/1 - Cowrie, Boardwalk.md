Kip was sitting up against one of the pillars that held up Gorjo City. It was low tide now. Kip wouldn't be here at high-tide, the water still made him nervous. But at low tide he could watch the water gently pushing seaweed against the rocks, without fear. 

The water turned over a bit of the seaweed that looked like an _efela_. He liked that kind, it was fun to pull apart into all the little segments. Kip went down to the water to pick up the strand. Under it was a real cowry. Not one of the little ones, the size of his fingertip. A big one, almost the size of his hand. But more than the size was unusual. There was a pattern on the shell, of squiggle-edged brown shapes, made of horizontal lines. 

Kip tried to remember where he'd seen a similar pattern. A grass skirt? A wall painting? Then he remembered the map in his classroom. That was drawn like this, squiggly land, made of horizontal lines. Thick lines for mountains, thin lines for the coast. Turning the shell around in his hand, he squinted, trying to see if the shape on the shell looked like any island he knew. He couldn't quite place it, although he was sure if he took it up to the classroom, it would match exactly with one of the islands in the Ring. 

"Kip!" came a voice from above him. 

Surprised out of his thoughts, Kip dropped the shell. That was Baru Tovo's voice. If his mother had talked to Uncle Lazo, and Lazo had sent Baru Tovo after Kip... But Baru Tovo had never yelled, Kip had never seen him even angry. 

"I'm under here!" Kip called out. 

"Get up here boy," said Tovo, "I'm not going to go crawling in there after you." 

Kip looked down, to grab the shell again. He couldn't see it. He swung his head around frantically, looking. He'd wanted to ask Baru Tovo if there was a name for that kind of pattern. But it was gone. Annoyed at himself for loosing it, he stomped back out, to see what Baru Tovo wanted him for.
