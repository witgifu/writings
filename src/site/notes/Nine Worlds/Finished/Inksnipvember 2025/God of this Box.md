The Sun bent his light and power down on the Sun-on-Earth. “You agreed to give up your false claim to godhead, child of mine.”

Fitzroy tried not to cringe, tried to bring his old serenity back. He was out of practice, and the Sun was burning hot.

“Look,” said Fitzroy, feeling a bit miffed, “I told them to stop. But then I abdicated, so it’s not like I can just arrest them all for treason.”

The Sun thought about this for a moment, and Fitzroy took a deep breath as the heat lessened slightly.

“I will make you a god in fact then!” said the Sun, grinning. “We shall give them what they want.”

“Wait! No!” protested Fitzroy, but the Sun ignored him.

“You are now the god of that box,” said the Sun, pointing. “Enjoy!” And with that, he vanished as quickly as he had come.

Fitzroy looked at where the Sun had pointed. It was Kip’s writing kit. The writing kit Fitzroy had enchanted, and probably held drafts of at least six hundred years of legislation.

Fitzroy sat down hard on the bed. He was going to need to convince Kip to clean out his writing kit.
