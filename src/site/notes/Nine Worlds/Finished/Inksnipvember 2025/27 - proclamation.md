Jullinar arrived at the Chancellor's siege lines on a cold, autumn evening, as the low clouds turned to mizzle. The town of Marston had once been a cheerful place, but with the seemingly interminable hostilities, it had become a grey and grim sort. 

Leaves filled the public fountain, and the only open signs were the two inns. Although, looking through the window of The Red Goat, Jullinar thought 'open' might be generous, it seemed to be exclusively used to host a calvary garrison.

She walked on to the second, The Drum. Here, at least, there was an elderly man sitting under the awning, smoking. If he had been in the army, he was too old for service now. Jullinar smiled at him, and he smiled back.

"G'd evenin', Saya," he said, his accent thick and local, "Y' be lewkin' fa' a brotha' or fatha' 'ere abouts?"

"How did you know?" replied Jullinar, giving him a sheepish grin, although she was doing nothing of the sort.

"'Ain't th' first time I 'eard such and won't be th' last, eye," he replied, and winked at her, "Stay clear o' th' Goat, there's a good lass. As' Mavin inside fo' a room, an' if ye don't have the coin 'e can give ye laundry to do. Army always needs washin' done, eye."

"Thank you, Sayo," said Jullinar, and headed inside. 

There was just one person in there, polishing the brasswork. He put down his cloth when she closed the door, and stretched. The common room was small, but very clean. Next to the bar was a collage of peeling posters. The top one was an official proclamation.

"There's a new Emperor?" said Jullinar, without thinking.

The man, presumably Marvin, gave her a confused look. "About two months ago, yeah," he said, "Where you been at, miss?"

"Oh. Um," said Jullinar, wishing as always that she was as good as Fitzroy at spinning stories, "I've been travelling about that long. Mostly in the east, I guess news spreads slower there."

*Artorin Damara* the poster called the new Emperor. Jullinar wondered where they'd found him.
