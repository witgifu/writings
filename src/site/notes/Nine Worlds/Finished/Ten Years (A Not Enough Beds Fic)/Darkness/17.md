Outside the throne room, Kip handed his lord up into the palanquin. He looked unbelievably tired, and Kip wondered if there was any way to make sure he got to the dressing room before his Radiancy. The palanquin had to take the ramp that spiralled, but on foot he could take the stairs.

Two Imperial Guards peeled off the group surrounding the Emperor, and came over to Kip. Unable to repress it, he flinched. His lord had promised guards, these were his guards, they were not here to arrest him. Technically, now he was a Prince, they couldn't arrest him, only confine him to quarters.

Suddenly, Kip realised that most of the fears that had haunted him since he had arrived at the Palace could no longer touch him. The Imperial Guards, the Department of Internal Security, the Ouranatha. All would have to apply to the Emperor for a writ, none could take him away, or torture him. Even if the Emperor gave them a writ, they could only fine him. Even for high treason, he would be beheaded quickly.

The cage kept him safe.

He did not wait to stew in these realisations, but strode off quickly up the stairs. Conju very nearly had to run from the main door to catch up.

“You did so well, highness!” Conju said, nearly out of breath, “Everyone is talking about how the Emperor clearly adores you.”

“Thank you, Conju,” said Kip, aware that they were still being watched. He did not let up the pace, and it did not allow much breath for talking. He envied the guards, whose short kilts did not need to be carefully managed to avoid tripping on the stairs. Kip kept an eye on Conju, who was not in the heavy court robes for the first time.

Kip did not pause at the doors to the Imperial Apartments, but was relieved to see the guards no longer followed him and Conju. “I want to help his Radiancy, when he arrives,” he said quietly to Conju.

“Of course, highness,” said Conju, with warmth, “We'll just get you upstairs and out of this—”

“Conju” said Kip, unusually emphatic, “You can take anything that you can carry with you, but I'm not going upstairs until his Radiancy dismisses me.”

Conju looked thoughtful for a moment. “If you would hand me your rings then, highness? And the circlet? I will send down your attendants.”

Kip pulled off the rings as quickly as he could, and bent his head so Conju could remove the careful pins that held the circlet in place. Then he went to stand beside the door to the Emperor's dressing room. Conju hurried off, somehow managing to hold the circlet respectfully while climbing the stairs in court robes.

Not long after Conju vanished from Kip's vision, the guard's spears stamped to announce the return of the Emperor. When his Radiancy gave the gesture to rise, Kip did so, and saw his lord looked just as tired as when they had parted. And the night before.

Again, Kip offered his arm for support, and the Emperor took it gladly. It was harder to help him undress, while Kip was still covered in stiff fabrics and jewels himself. But despite seeing Lord Hari hovering, obviously wanting to help, Kip studiously ignored him. He was determined to see his lord to bed first.

When his Radiancy was finally dressed for sleep, Kip helped him into the Imperial Bed. His lord looked at him for a moment, and Kip wondered if he was about to be asked to come in with all the costume on. He probably couldn't sleep in it, apart from any damage that might do, it would poke him the whole night. But he could sit on the bed and keep watch.

“Come back when you're dressed for bed, Cliopher,” his Radiancy said.

“Thank you, Glorious One,” said Kip, and bowed as the attendants drew the curtains closed.

Off to the side, in the Imperial Dressing room, Lord Hari was finally able to divest Kip of all the court costume. Two of the other attendants also helped out, and Kip wondered if that was a debt he now owed them, or Lord Hari. They even had the same sleep outfit prepared that he had worn last night.

“Thank you,” he said to the three of them, when they were done.

“Our pleasure, your highness,” said Lord Hari.

“Thank you, your highness, for helping with his Radiancy,” said one of the others.

Kip wondered if they were being polite, or if it genuinely was easier to get the Emperor to bed when Kip helped. He knew he didn't follow their preferred way of doing things. But he also didn't have to worry that he might accidentally brush skin, which probably made removing his cosmetics a lot faster.

Feeling much more comfortable, Kip returned to the bed. The attendant pulled back the curtain, but before Kip could perform his obeisance, or even bow, his Radiancy gestured for Kip to approach. He did, kneeling on the bed as the attendant closed the curtain again.

“Thank you, again, Cliopher,” said his Radiancy.

Kip bowed where he was keeling, wishing sincerely that his lord felt able to give him constructive and detailed feedback. “Thank _you_, my lord, for your forgiveness, patience, and protection. I only wish there was more I could do in return.”

The Emperor patted the bed next to him. “Try to get some sleep, please Cliopher.”

Obediently, Kip crawled over and wriggled under the blankets. Staring at those golden eyes, he crept one hand across the covers, just over halfway between them. “Sweet dreams, my lord,” he whispered softly.

A hand bumped up against his. Kip pressed the side of his hand against his Radiancy's hand.

“Good night, Cliopher,” said his Radiancy, and closed his eyes.

Kip watched him, as he fell asleep. Wondering, traitorously, if this tired, scared man, this Emperor, this mighty lord whose touch burnt and gaze blinded, felt just as imprisoned here as Kip.

It was some days after Kip's introduction to the court. Kip was in his front room while his Radiancy was participating in some minor rituals. He was trying to read an apparently famous romantic epic poem. But it seemed to actually mostly centre around the protagonist's horses. One more metaphor for how _long_ a horse's neck was, or lustrous its mane, and he might throw the book. Well, maybe not throw it, but put it aside with an angry look.

“Your highness,” said Ser Rhodin, as he came through the door and bowed.

Kip wasn't sorry to be interrupted, “Yes, Rhodin?” he asked, waving the footman over.

“I have an update on my investigations, highness” said Rhodin, looking unusually stiff.

At this, Conju looked up from his embroidery as well. Both he and Kip waited for Rhodin to continue, but Rhodin seemed to have frozen in place.

“Yes?” said Kip, wondering what was wrong with him.

“I may have jumped to conclusions too early previously, highness,” he said, not making eye contact but looking at the empty chair between Kip and Conju. “The group that meets with Lord Igel does not appear to be a conspiracy. Or not primarily a conspiracy. They seem to be a literature and tea club.”

Conju raised one eyebrow in query, “Do you mean a wine and pornography club?”

“No, my lord, highness. They read popular novels and drink tea,” answered Rhodin, now looking even more stressed.

“But it was definitely Lord Igel's signature on those order forms,” said Kip, suddenly glad they hadn't shared any of their suspicions with his Radiancy.

“And I still believe the head chef administered it, highness, because...” said Rhodin, and took a deep breath, “He was trying to get me to sell him information. He wanted to know if there were any foods you particularly disliked. So… so he could put his Radiancy's medicine in it.”

Conju sighed, gently. “And you told him that his highness doesn't like…?”

Rhodin coughed, “Ah, the tofu dish with the dried chillis and pepper all over it?”

“Which he said wouldn't work,” completed Conju, “And so he asked you to find out other dishes, yes?”

“Yes, my lord, highness,” said Rhodin, now looking slightly less stressed. Clearly he had expected Kip or Conju to be irate that he had apparently sold them out.

“Did the chef give any reason why his Radiancy needed medication?” asked Kip, curious.

“No, highness,” said Rhodin, “I was hoping… to exchange another food preference for that information. And then your highness and his Radiancy could avoid it?”

Kip shook his head, “If we avoid it entirely, it will be obvious. Do you know anything more about the medication? Is it just the same erotic stimulant?”

“There has also been a mild sedative delivered, highness, in addition to more of the stimulant,” said Rhodin, more confident.

Kip looked between Conju and Rhodin. “I will need to talk to his Radiancy,” he said, softly, “For now, Rhodin, please put the chef off.”

“Of course, highness,” said Rhodin, and bowed again.

Picking up the book of poetry again, Kip flipped it to a random page. He didn't read it at all, it just provided good cover for his thinking. Conju could probably tell, but would hopefully be too polite to say anything.

How could he get a private word with his Radiancy? The only space where they weren't watched was the Imperial Bed, where his Radiancy, understandably, just wanted to sleep. At every meal, there were attendants and guards. Kip didn't want to invade his Radiancy's one truly personal space, his private office. Maybe if they both woke up early? Yet that seemed unlikely when his Radiancy had so little time in bed.

Then, even if he found time, how could he raise the topic? It wasn't proper for him to raise any conversation. Would he just try to look anxious, and force his lord to ask him if something was wrong? It wouldn't be entirely dishonest, he was anxious about his Radiancy's household _continuing_ to drug his lord.

He might just have to try to take advantage of whatever opportunities arose. His least favourite form of plan.

Frustrated with the bad poetry, and anxious that Lord Igel and the chef might give up and just drug both of them, Kip put the book down. He strode over to the spinet in the corner of the room, and pulled out the sheet music for the very tedious beginner exercises. There was something in the way his hands moved up and down the keys that made him feel, not relaxed, but focused, in a way that didn't allow his thoughts to bother him.
