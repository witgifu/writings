Face washed, and in a clean uniform, Kip started his day. Aware of Conju's admonition that he had overworked the pages and undersecretary, he tried to complete as much by himself as he could.

With the invitation to breakfast, Kip no longer had time to meet any of his new staff until at least lunchtime. He wasn't sure if that was a relief or a worry. There wasn't time enough even to think about it properly.

Rather than working upstairs, he took a corner of the desk in the library. He skimmed through the overnight reports, adding anything of note to the cover sheets. As he was adding in the last of the missing references, the bells chimed eight o'clock.

Kip very nearly swore. Both he and the reports were now late. He walked quickly, definitely not running, to the breakfast room.

The Emperor arrived at the same time as Kip. Unable to perform a proper obeisance holding the stack of documents, Kip bowed low over them instead.

“Good morning again, Cliopher,” said the Emperor, with a polite smile.

“Good morning, Glorious One,” replied Kip, with a matching smile. He entered the room behind his lord, and placed the reports in the tray reserved for them.

The Emperor took his seat, and there was also a place set for Kip. Today's breakfast was fried rice, which the attendant served from a common dish into their two bowls. With it came tea for the Emperor, and coffee for Kip.

“You should try some of this, Cliopher,” said the Emperor, lifting his cup, “the fragrance is most enticing.”

The attendant did not wait for Kip to agree, but poured him a cup. Kip had only ever had the very cheapest tea before. He breathed in the scent appreciatively, although he didn't really know what he was supposed to be smelling. He sipped it, and smiled politely. “Indeed, Glorious One,” he said.

Over the course of the breakfast, he made sure to drink the entire cup.

The Emperor read over the reports, as his mostly ignored breakfast cooled. Kip had been carefully matching him bite for bite. 

Concerned, but hiding it, Kip turned to the attendant. He offered a polite smile as she curtsied. “Would it be possible to get some toast and marmalade, Lady Enoié?” he asked, in his most polite and cheerful voice, “I'm afraid after a rich dinner this is not entirely agreeing with me.”

Lady Enoié curtsied again. In what Kip was sure was not enough time, a rack of toast and three pots of spreads appeared on the table. Kip took a slice, and mindful of his previous complaint, spread it thinly with marmalade.

The Emperor seemed to take no notice of this exchange. However, when he finished the current report and turned to the next, he too took a slice of toast. Inside, Kip sighed with relief. If the Emperor didn't feel able to ask for what he needed, even something as small as a breakfast that he liked, perhaps that was something Kip could help him with.

They finished their breakfast in silence, before removing to the official study.

Nothing changed in their work that morning, except that the Emperor continued to call Kip by his first name. He was not entirely surprised when he was invited to lunch. There was a selection of savoury pastries, which Kip carefully split to make sure none were eaten by the Emperor alone.

They had a pleasant and polite conversation about the different forms of poetry over the Empire. It was a topic about which Kip did not have much knowledge, but the Emperor clearly did. Kip dutifully tried to elicit his lord's opinions.

After lunch, the Emperor had a meeting with his generals, that Kip was not invited to. He gathered his work and headed upstairs to his desk.

There, he opened the door to find Lord Conju in the middle of discussions with two people Kip didn't recognise. 

“Lord Cliopher!” said Conju, standing up and bowing. As per Conju's previous instruction, Kip carefully did not bow in response. Even though it felt exceedingly strange.

“May I please introduce Ser Rhodin, a candidate to be your footman, and Dame Hollis, a secretarial candidate.”

Kip blinked, looking them both over. Ser Rhodin was the picture of a court dualist, although not currently wearing a sword. Dame Hollis was dressed as a textbook-perfect Fifth Degree Secretary.

“Oh, um. Hello?” he said, dragging his mind back from the work he had been planning to tackle.

Lord Conju rolled his eyes very dramatically. Kip bit back an apology, he didn't want to get in trouble twice in a row. “I'm afraid I'm new to this position and still finding my feet. Please, don't let me detain you.” Then he escaped to his desk.

He managed to get through the very urgent items before Lord Conju interrupted again.

“Lord Cliopher,” said Conju, with a slight tone of apology, “Would you be able to join us?”

Reluctantly, Kip closed the report he was reading and joined the other three at the comfortable armchairs. “Do you have any particular questions for Dame Hollis or Ser Rhodin, Lord Cliopher?” asked Conju, as Kip sat down.

“When did you join the Service, Dame Hollis? Where do you work currently?”

She smiled politely. “Six years ago, sir. I currently work for the Treasury.”

“How long have you been there?”

“A year, sir. I was transferred from Agriculture.”

“And who did you bribe for that transfer?”

Conju let out a small huff of disapproval. Kip ignored him. Bribes were often talked of openly in the service. It was the people sent to Internal Security who were only whispered about.

“Nobody,” she said, although clearly not offended, “Even if I wanted to, my family couldn't afford it. I send most of my pay home for my sister's care.”

Kip decided he didn't need to pry into that any further. “Good,” he said, and proceeded to quiz her on the most recent Treasury statistics. After a few minutes, both Lord Conju and Ser Rhodin were looking very confused. Both Kip and Dame Hollis were smiling.

“I'll take the position, sir,” she said, after she finished explaining the methodology for estimating historical exchange rates.

“Excellent,” said Kip, “Can you start now?” 

Conju rolled his eyes, but Dame Hollis laughed brightly. “Yes, sir, since it's you.”

Kip didn't quite understand what she meant by that. But he returned to his desk and loaded her down with reports to validate. “You can use this desk for now. I suppose I should find us an office somewhere in the lower levels, Lord Conju?”

“Yes, Lord Cliopher,” replied Conju tartly, “We will find somewhere more suitable later. Come back and talk to Ser Rhodin.”

“Please do not ask me anything about exchange rates, Lord Cliopher,” said Ser Rhodin, looking every inch the polished courtier.

“What exactly does the role entail?” asked Kip, with a glance back at Dame Hollis to see her buried in a document.

Conju smiled slightly, “Fetching and carrying, handling messages, negotiations with the lower Tower staff for supplies, serving meals. Standing around looking impressive when you're out of the Tower, to discourage miscreants.”

“The pages handle messages. And the Emperor said he was going to arrange guards?” said Kip, confused.

“But there are places it's not politic to take guards,” said Ser Rhodin, “And perhaps places you need to send someone who can defend themselves.”

Kip wondered if this included defending other people from him. But not even the Emperor's own guards had interfered last night. Kip sighed. “Fine, fine. If you're satisfied Lord Conju, I really need to get some documents to the pages.”

“I can take them, sir,” said Ser Rhodin, with a small smile.

Pursing his lips, Kip went to the desk and pulled out the papers he needed to send. Silently, he handed them to Ser Rhodin. The man jumped up, and slipped out the door, with the athleticism and grace of a noble trained to fight.

“So,” said Conju suggestively, “How did your night go Lord Cliopher? The gossip is _wild_. Did you really—”

Kip interrupted him. “I need to ask you something in private, Lord Conju.”

“Not a problem!” said Dame Hollis from the desk, “I need to visit the library. I will leave my finished work here, sir, if you are occupied later.” She got up from the desk, taking a small selection of the reports with her, and headed out.

When she had left, and her footsteps had faded, Kip turned back to Conju. The younger man looked his usual elegant and polite self. Kip did not want to have this conversation. He thought Conju had gone above and beyond for him, and seemed to genuinely care.

If Conju's family had been in the close circles of Emperor Eritanyr, how much had that influenced the young man? (As much as the Palace, the Empire, had made Kip into someone who fell asleep proud of his repeated rape of his Emperor? Or had that always been inside him?)

“Did you ever visit court under Emperor Eritanyr?” asked Kip, his voice neutral.

“No,” said Conju, relaxed, “I was twelve when he died.”

“But you said your father was the Emperor's friend? Did he not take you along?” Kip was trying very hard to keep any emotion out of his voice.

“No,” said Conju, more firmly, “My father did all he could to avoid court after Eritanyr came to the throne. When he did have to go, he never took me or my siblings. He said Eritanyr was no longer the same person.”

“How did he describe Prince Eritanyr, then?”

Conju looked a little concerned now, “Oh, I don't know. A bit of a party boy maybe? But what prince isn't spoilt? He wasn't like Shallyr.”

Unfortunately, at this moment the Ser Rhodin opened the door. He looked over both of them. “Should I stand outside looking impressive?” he asked, “Or maybe downstairs?”

Kip was still watching Conju closely, as the other man recovered himself. “Why don't you go an introduce yourself to some of the other tower staff, Ser Rhodin,” he said, with a passable imitation of nonchalance.

They both listened as Ser Rhodin's footsteps retreated as well.

Kip took a deep breath. He needed someone else he trusted. More than himself. His only real doubt about Conju had been his family connection to Eritanyr.

“Someone drugged the Emperor last night.”

“That's madness, nobody would do that,” hissed Lord Conju.

“Somebody did,” insisted Kip, “And it is only by the _extraordinary_ kindness and completely unwarranted grace of the Emperor that I have not been summarily executed.”

“*Fuck*,” said Conju with unusual passion, “Are you okay? Is the Emperor recovered?”

It didn't escape Kip's notice that Conju asked after him first. It was sweet, if completely ridiculous.

“It wore off in a couple of hours. I'm fine. I think I will be taking meals with the Emperor until I work out who or how...” Kip trailed off, his thoughts a frantic tumble. “Do you know when the Emperor is next dinning with the court?” Kip asked, tentatively.

“Tomorrow evening,” said Conju, and then suddenly connected the same information Kip had. “I will handle your presentation, don't even think about it Lord Cliopher.”

Kip could feel the panic rising. “Under the circumstances, of both the short notice and my offences, please don't assume I will be given any rank, Lord Conju. I didn't draft a proclamation this morning.”

“I will talk with Lady Ylette immediately,” said Conju, “We will prepare options.” Then he bustled out of the room.

Alone, and no longer able to bury himself in a mountain of work, Kip slumped down into the seat. How could the Emperor face him over every meal? 

He must do his best to be as correct as possible. No slips into informality, no speaking unless spoken to, no offering his own views or opinions. He would be like the Emperor's golden eating sticks. An elegant tool, but only truly remarkable because of who used it.

Kip went to the bathroom, and scrubbed himself thoroughly. He wanted to remove his own skin, as if somehow he could blame it for his behaviour. Or punish himself by doing so. He could remember the feel, the taste…

He threw up, thankfully into the sink. Once he had stopped shaking, he rinsed it down, and washed himself again.

The ritual soaps sat on the shelf with the special cloths. If he didn't do it, perhaps the threat of burning alive might stop him from being so stupid. But if it didn't stop him, he might hurt the Emperor as well as himself. Crying with the shame, he completed the ritual cleanse.

Someone had put his dressing gown in the bathroom. Kip wrapped himself in it, and went to bed. There he lay, staring at the ceiling, plunged into the dark churning riptide of his guilt.
