The dawn, as always, came too early. However, Kip was pleased that he was woken only then, and that his lord had not had any nightmares. As the sounds of the staff and observers arriving grew louder, Kip slid out from under the blankets to kneel on the bed. An attendant pulled back the bed curtain, and the dance of the day began.

When Kip returned to his rooms, the door was opened by an attendant, a Lord Hari.

“Good morning, your highness,” said Lord Hari, bowing Kip into the room.

Kip suppressed a sigh, and walked in. If he hadn't been able to get Conju to stop calling him 'highness', it seemed unlikely he'd convince anyone else. It wasn't worth distressing Hari over, who looked about the same age as Conju.

“Would you like to refresh yourself, highness?” asked Hari, in a way that Kip suspected meant he would be judged for saying 'no'. Thankfully, unlike yesterday, he wasn't trying to finish any work.

“Yes, thank you Hari,” said Kip, and followed the younger man through his bedroom (would he ever sleep here at night again? Possibly not), to the bathroom. Here Hari had laid out everything. Kip carefully did _not_ give Hari the chance to undress him, shedding his nightclothes and grabbing for the wash cloth.

Once clean, and dressed in a fresh uniform, Kip returned to the main room. For lack of anything better to do, and trying not to slip into anxiety again, he went down to check if the pages had anything new.

There was a surprising number of dispatch boxes addressed to him. He had been hoping to get some instructions, and maybe a copy of the oath he needed to memorise for this evening. However, it seemed from a quick scan through, that he had been sent _everything_ about his new rank, by everyone.

The Master of Etiquette had sent not just the oath, but the full order of the ceremony. The Master of Ceremonies had sent instructions on where and when to enter the throne room, where to stand, and what else was on the order for the evening. 

However, it wasn't just about the ceremony. The Minister for Trade had sent the most recent reports (many of which he had already read) for the Wide Seas. The Survey Office sent maps, the Tithe Office sent budgets, the Ministry of Works sent lists of Imperial properties.

It was as if they were expecting him to actually rule. Which was ludicrous. The Vangaveye-ve had a Prince, the Islolates, the Sociable Isles, and the Line Islands had an Imperial Duke each. None of them really _ruled_, at least not from the perspective of the islanders. They were tolerated, but largely ignored.

By the time he returned to his desk, he had sorted the documents into things he needed today, and things to deal with later. Some of them had sparked a hope in him that he might get to visit home one day, which he tried to firmly squash. The chances were slim, many of the high aristocracy lived in the Palace of Stars as absentee landlords.

Today, Kip needed to memorise an oath. He always did better at memorising when he could put a tune to the words. Since he was very sure he did not want to be caught accidentally humming _Aurora_ or _Donkey Ears_ in the Throne Room, he settled on a children's song in Language, one Basil had taught him. It didn't fit very well, but it would do.

```
 By the Sun and Moon, 
 I will to the Lord Emperor, 
 	be true and faithful, 
 loving all which he loves,
 	shunning all which he shuns, 
 according to the laws 
 	and order of the Empire. 
 Nor will I, in word or deed, 
 	do anything unpleasing to him.
 To him I submit myself,
 	and choose his will, 
 	even unto death.
```


Kip had never sworn an oath like this before. Oh, he had sworn to the Emperor when he had joined the Service. But that was different, it was much more like a work contract. This oath was a binding of his whole life. Could he declare it honestly?

He had already done things unpleasing to his lord. Terrible things, horrifying things. Surely his continued presence, having to see Kip day in and day out was unpleasing to his Radiancy? How could it not be?

Yet, this was what his lord had instructed him to do. His Radiancy hadn't been forced to ennoble Kip, he had chosen to. What could Kip do but obey?

It wasn't as if he wanted to make his lord upset, or distressed. It did make it hard that he wasn't supposed to ask what his Radiancy wanted (although he had tried), and that his Radiancy seemed to feel unable to ask.

Kip shook away these thoughts, and went back to memorising all the things he would need to know for this evening.

---

The morning work was otherwise uneventful. Kip endeavoured to try to read his Radiancy's mood, but he seemed perfectly serene and in control.

The first half of their lunch together, his Radiancy was completely silent. Kip worried, unsure if his lord was only tired, or if he was displeased with Kip for some reason. He was almost shaking with tension, when his Radiancy finally spoke.

“I hope you have received instructions from the Masters of Etiquette and Ceremonies,” said his Radiancy, not making eye contact.

“I have, Glorious One, thank you. I am very much unworthy of the honours you are bestowing on me, your servant, most gracious and kind lord,” said Kip, the words tumbling out of him as if pushed by his anxieties. “If there is any way I can help, if there is any service or function I could perform for you, my lord, any way I might ease your day or night, please tell me.”

Kip put down his eating sticks, and looked up through his eyelashes at the Emperor. 

“Only that you would tell me if there is anything you need, Cliopher,” said his Radiancy.

Kip looked up properly, unsure how to respond. His lord's face was calm, but his eyes were worried. How could Kip reassure him? A dozen hollow platitudes fizzled through his mind.

Finally, he dropped his gaze to his plate again. “Glorious One, I would not mention it but that you asked me to, it seems such a small matter, but I would like to write to my family, and I only have one stamp.”

There was a small pause, and Kip feared he would be told he really shouldn't write to his family anymore. That his duty to his lord should break his family ties, or that he was too important for them now.

His Radiancy finally spoke, “Of course, you must write to your family, Cliopher. We will send a memo to the Postmaster General once you have a signet. Until then, we will seal your letters so they may arrive promptly and without interference. Add them to the rest of the documents for our seal.”

Kip nearly fell of his chair with surprise. Letters with the Imperial seal would be sent by magic, and not be subject to censorship. And it sounded like his Radiancy would make a similar provision for Kip's own seal.

“Thank you, again, Glorious One, for your generosity,” said Kip, and looked at his Radiancy with a genuine smile. To his astonishment, the Emperor smiled back.

Flustered, Kip asked, “Do you write to your sister, my lord?”

He immediately wanted to kick himself. How dare he ask such a personal question. What if he didn't? They were not known to be enemies, but they were also not known to be friends. And Kip was his secretary, he knew the Emperor hadn't written to her recently, because he would have scribed the letter.

“Occasionally,” said the Emperor, “we only met at my coronation, we did not grow up together.”

Kip needed to stay on topic, but not ask another personal question. He also shouldn't talk about his own family. Oh, gods above, what could he say?

“Did you study music as a child, Glorious One? I know you are an ardent patron now,” said Kip, just before it became awkward. Technically, this was a change of topic, but Kip hoped it passed.

“We did,” said his Radiancy, graciously, “We have always had a great love of music.”

To his relief, Kip was then able to ask his Radiancy something about his favourite styles and composers. Unlike with poetry, he felt much more confident that he knew something, even if he felt other members of his family would have known more.

After lunch, Kip returned to his rooms, hoping to get some work done. When he opened the door, everyone looked up and bowed.

“Your highness!” said Conju, walking over, “Excellent timing. Let's do a final fitting, and then get you into a bath.”

“Ah,” said Kip, with a sigh, “Dame Hollis, can you take these then?” He handed over the reports reluctantly.

“Don't worry, highness, I'll get this all done for you. And I'll check in with the pages later in case any more have arrived,” said Dame Hollis, taking the reports and starting to sort them.

Then Conju whisked him onto the little stand, and he closed his eyes. As soon as he did, his mind drifted to the memory of that smile on his lord's face at lunch. What could he do to elicit more of them, and fewer anxious glances?

He had thanked his Radiancy just before, and received only a polite smile. So it wasn't that. Did his lord have a particular interest in the postal system? Or was he hoping to learn more about Kip's family?

He wondered how he might direct a conversation towards either topic. So far he had done a pretty poor job at simply participating, despite Conju's careful teaching. He would have to practise more.

Suddenly Kip realised Conju and Lady Ylette really only needed his body from the neck down. “Dame Hollis? Could I get you to take some notes please before I forget?” he asked, twisting to try to look at her.

“Of course, highness,” she said, and then after the sounds of quickly shuffled papers, she added, “Please, go ahead, highness.”

Kip began rattling off research questions, reports he thought were overdue, minor process reform suggestions. Everything that had been collecting in his head over the past few days. He reached the end and added, “Did you need any of that repeated or clarified, Dame Hollis?”

“Not at all, highness. And I must say it is so pleasant to take dictation for someone who knows what they are doing. You were so easy to keep pace with,” she said. Then somewhat uncertainly, she asked, “Did you mean for me to answer all of this today, highness?”

“Oh my, no. That has to be more than a week of work, plus some of it would need authorisations. No, no, I just wanted to get it out of my head before I forgot any of it,” said Kip, with a smile that Dame Hollis probably couldn't see.

“Oh, thank goodness,” she said with a small laugh. Kip spotted Conju giving her a disapproving look. “I should be able to start on one of these research questions, highness. I will ask Lord Rial to handle checking for these missing reports.”

It was, Kip admitted to himself, sad to delegate the parts of his job he enjoyed most. But it wasn't like he could ask anyone else to attend court for him, much as he wished he could.

“Are you going to be at court tonight, Lord Conju?” said Kip, with a shiver of rising fear. 

“Of course!” said Conju, with a smile that eased Kip's heart, “All your household will be there to see you.”

Kip blushed. “You don't have to come if you don't want to,” he said to the room, feeling awkward. Surely some of these people had family or friends they'd rather be spending their evening with.

“Of course we want to,” said Dame Hollis, and there was a chorus of agreement from Lady Ylette, Ser Rhodin, Sir Buana, and Conju.

Smiling back, slightly confused, Kip said quietly, “Thank you.”

Lady Ylette peeled off the layers he was currently wrapped in. “That's all we need, your highness,” she said with a smile, dismissing him.

“Would you like a rest now, highness, or after your bath?” asked Sir Buana. 

Kip noticed that neither the nap nor the bath were optional. It reminded him of how people gave choices to very young children. He tried not to feel resentful, given that the bath was required. The six hours of sleep he'd gotten last night wasn't too bad.

“Bath first, I think,” said Kip, “I'm too jittery to rest at the moment.” He stepped down off the pedestal, and followed Sir Buana to the bathroom.

A full bell later, Kip was snuggled up in his own bed. He was alternating between anxiously reciting everything he needed to remember for the evening, and mentally composing letters to his family. Neither of which fulfilled what he was supposed to be doing.

After a good few cycles, he decided to give up. Wrapped in his old dressing gown and slippers, he wandered out into the main room. 

Everyone was still there, chatting away quietly, presumably so they didn't disturb him. They stopped when he came in. He wished they hadn't. It had sounded like a warm, friendly conversation.

“Is there anything you need, highness?” asked Ser Rhodin, standing up.

“No, no,” sighed Kip, “I just thought if I should do something useful rather than lying in my bed imagining everything that could go wrong.”

“Would you like to practice, your highness?” asked Conju, gently.

“Yes please,” said Kip, “if it wouldn't get in the way.”

Everyone re-arranged the furniture, to give Kip the longest space possible to walk. They put a chair at the end to stand in for the throne. It was, Kip admitted to himself, kind of fun to play act in his dressing gown. If he imagined he was Elonoa'a greeting Aurelius Magnus as he often had played with his cousins.

Although, that had been a meeting of equals. There could be nobody equal to the Emperor in the throne room of the Palace of Stars.

After Conju was satisfied with Kip's practice, they began to dress him. Once again, Kip mostly ignored what they were doing. He recited his oath in his head, and then when that was starting to lose all meaning, he recited the part of the lays about Elonoa'a and Aurelius.

Suddenly, everyone stepped back and smiled at him. Kip blinked at them. He could feel the weight of everything he was wearing, and he realised he hadn't ever asked what this was going to cost. Hopefully it could at least be used for many court appearances.

“Would you like to see, highness?” asked Conju, admiring him.

“Oh,” said Kip, “I suppose so, I'm sure you've all done splendid work.”

Conju rolled his eyes, and Ser Rhodin carried in the full-length mirror from the bedroom. He managed to carry it so that Kip couldn't see anything of himself, until Rhodin put it down in front of him.

Layers and layers of fabric enveloped him, cut and arranged to show the other layers beneath. Most of it was silk, but the top was sky blue ahalo cloth, attached to a net of copper wire that had a tiny pearl on every join. 

There was a coronet on his head, a thin band of gold with rubies and pearls. There were rings of rose gold with rubies and sapphires. But what took Kip's breath away was the double-strand of fire pearls, which hung around his neck and down to his belt. 

He tried to reassure himself that most of this was just borrowed from the Treasury. It had been gathered from hundreds of years of tithes and gifts. Even the ahalo cloth was a tithe-good. And in blue or red it could never have been made into a garment for his Radiancy.

“Oh,” said Kip, struggling to put together a coherent statement, “I look like a prince.”

Lady Ylette smirked, “Yes, that is the general idea, highness.”
