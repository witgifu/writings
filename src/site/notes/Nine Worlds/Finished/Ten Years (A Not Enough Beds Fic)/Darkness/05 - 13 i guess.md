The interview ended up happening in the small drawing room, where Kip sometimes wrote letters of an evening. Lord Igel told Kip to wait there after lunch. So he took some notes to the desk there, to write up into draft documents.

At precisely two in the afternoon, Lord Igel opened the door. After the tiniest pause, when Lord Igel must have been making some kind of mental calculations, he made introductions.

“Sayo Mdang, may I introduce the Cavalier Conju enazo Argellian an Vilius. Lord Conju, Cliopher sayo Mdang.”

He bowed to both of them, and left. This was the first time Lord Igel had ever bowed to Kip, and he was worried by this change. 

Lord Conju was on the short side, but dressed incredibly elegantly. Kip thought Conju was probably a couple of years younger than himself. He wondered why Lord Igel had picked this particular young man.

“Good afternoon, Lord Conju,” said Kip, only slightly before it became awkward. He abandoned his writing materials at the desk, and walked over to the more comfortable chairs.

“Good afternoon, Sayo Mdang,” said Lord Conju, and bowed in what Kip was sure was a specific way that contained some extra meaning. He walked over to stand next to the other chair.

They both paused again. Finally, Kip gave up trying to work out what he was supposed to do and just sat down. He didn't know, he wasn't going to work it out by staring at Lord Conju.

“Okay,” said Kip, “I know I got that wrong, Lord Conju, what should I have done, and why?”

Lord Conju seemed slightly taken aback, but he carefully sat down opposite Kip. “When Lord Igel introduced us, Sayo Mdang, he introduced me to you first. That means he judged you to be the higher ranked person. So, Sayo Mdang, it is up to you to set the level of formality and lead the discussion.”

Kip blinked a few times in honest surprise. “Oh,” he said, thinking it through, “I did know that, I've just never been the higher ranked person before. Um. I'm not actually sure that I *am*.”

Conju artfully raised one eyebrow. “Do you doubt the judgement of one of the leading people of one of the preeminent families amongst the Imperial Court, Sayo Mdang?”

“Oh,” said Kip again, feeling like this might be becoming somewhat of a refrain, “Um, no? But I'm pretty sure he would have said my rank was about equal to one of the pages a few days ago.”

“What are the determents of rank, Sayo Mdang?” asked Conju, relaxing slightly back into his chair.

Here Kip felt he was on stronger ground. He recited from the set exam text, “Titles, family, military rank, ordination by the Ouranatha, higher degrees granted by universities, membership of some professional organisations.”

“And?” said Conju, waving for Kip to continue.

“I suppose, marital status comes into it as well?” said Kip, uncertainly, now trying to gather information from what he had seen in the past year, “And maybe magical power?”

“Position,” said Conju, looking closely at Kip, “Is also important. And its importance is determined by the rank of the one who gave the position. What is your position, Sayo Mdang, and who gave it to you?”

Kip paused for a moment before replying, “The first concubine of the Lord of Rising Stars, chosen on the occasion of the celebrations of the tenth year of his reign. Also the Private Secretary.”

Conju looked a trifle smug at this. “Quite,” he said, “Which puts you at least equal with an Imperial title. You will probably be introduced to the court as Lord Cliopher, First Concubine.”

Kip sighed. “I'm not…” he waved his hand, to try to express what he could not put into words.

“You are not what, Sayo Mdang?” said Conju, testily, “Not chosen by the Lord Emperor? Not the only person in five worlds who can touch him without being burnt alive? Not the only one who dare look in his eyes without being blinded? Not his favourite? If you were only the Emperor's secretary, you would not be living *in the Imperial Apartments*. It does not matter if he takes you to his bed or not.”

Kip blushed furiously. It mattered to *him*, he had been appointed to do a job and he was failing spectacularly.

Conju realised his mistake, “I mean to the court, to the people outside the Tower. It isn't their business. Which is not to say that they might not be tactless and *ask*, but you shouldn't tell them.”

Mollified slightly, Kip relaxed. “All right then. Lord Igel said you've done this before, Lord Conju? How does it work?”

Conju snorted. “There is *no* precedent for a companion to the First Concubine, Sayo Mdang.”

Kip tried to imitate Conju's nonchalant posture and raised eyebrow. “Did Lord Igel give you no instructions? I had asked the Glorious One...” 

This got a quickly suppressed look of alarm from Conju, before he answered the first question.

“Lord Igel said I would spend the first half of every day on ritual purifications and training in the ways of the Tower. Then afternoons and evenings following you around, Sayo Mdang, and making sure you do not embarrass the Emperor.” Conju gave Kip a very sceptical look. “What did you request, Sayo Mdang?”

Kip bit his lip, he did not want to confess his failings so quickly. Looking at Conju's hands, he said, "Remedial instruction."

There was an awkward silence.

It was Kip who broke it. "I do not know what the Emperor wants from me, Lord Conju. I have been summoned to his bedchamber twice when he is unwell, and once to deliver a book. I have dined with him once. The only work I have been able to do for him that he seems to appreciate is as his secretary. I had to beg to let me give him a scalp massage to help him sleep. I have not been sent for in more than two weeks, and I am worried."

As he spoke, Kip got quieter and faster. Desperately he suppressed the tears that wanted to come. Why was he being given a court introduction when it seemed so clear the Emperor had no interest in his work? Seemed to find the very idea repulsive?

"He could have anyone on five worlds," said Kip, in almost a whisper, "he has been presented with the best the Ouranatha could find."

Kip took a deep breath, and continued in a flat tone. 

"I was a last-minute choice for their selection of 'exotics'. The Glorious One picked me out of the lineup and appointed me as undersecretary. I was promoted to secretary when he became frustrated with those previously appointed to that position. Lord Igel has made it clear to me that this is some kind of fantasy, or game. But it is not one where I understand the rules. I only know the price of failure."

Conju looked Kip over, appraising but not unkind. "Are you asking me to teach you how to seduce the Emperor?"

Kip blinked. "I don't know." Maybe Conju was working for Lord Igel, but it wouldn't really matter if he knew how desperate Kip was. He had been terrible at these power games since he had arrived in the Palace.

He took another deep breath, and shared the truth that had haunted him since being chosen.

"The priest-wizards taught us that anything purified and used has been made sacred and must be set apart. If discarded it must be ritually destroyed. I don't know if it's true, but it is certainly within their power."

Conju pursed his lips, looking tense. "Do you know if the Lord Emperor or Lord Igel are aware of this?"

Kip twisted his sweating hands in his robes. "I don't know if Lord Igel knows. The Glorious One does know the rules, but I think does not see how they might apply to me. His Radiancy offered to send me home to my family. I begged to stay, but I could not correct him."

"Of course you couldn't," said Conju with a nod. He thought for a moment. “Well then, let us start with seeing if we cannot regain the Emperor's attentions. Dress, conversation, manners... Do you dance, Sayo Mdang?"

"I was taught a selection of the most popular court dances," said Kip, unwilling to bear any more of his heart.

"Excellent, one less thing to learn then," said Lord Conju, with a polite smile.

“When should we start?” asked Kip, trying not to let too much hope spark.

"We may as well begin now, let's have a look at your wardrobe," said Conju, standing up.

The corridors were empty as Kip showed Lord Conju up to his rooms, and through to his bedroom. He opened the door of his wardrobe to show the small collection. The fake finery, a few uniforms, a sleep shirt, and a dressing gown.

Conju stared at the contents for a moment. "That's it? You haven't anything stored elsewhere? Do you know if anyone is working on your court dress?" he asked, his voice carefully polite.

"That's it," answered Kip, "I haven't heard anything further about being presented at court. Lord Igel may not think I need to know."

More quietly he added, "The Glorious One may be waiting for me to demonstrate improved behaviour."

Conju stared at the wardrobe, and Kip wondered what he was thinking. Did he not believe Kip? Or did he have other ideas about what was happening?

"Let us leave the wardrobe for now then. We shall work from the assumption that the presentation has not yet been scheduled, and you need to encourage the Emperor to do so."

With relief, Kip closed the wardrobe door.

Conju tapped his lips with one, thin, elegant finger. "Is there anything that you think might have pleased him?"

Kip thought back. "When I begged to be allowed to stay, he described me as the 'most competent secretary' and said I had been courteous in all my actions. He repeated the complement at dinner. At dinner, he also complemented my skill with eating sticks. I think he did like the scalp massage."

Taking a deep breath, Conju closed his eyes, "When you are working together *every single morning* have you noticed if there are any particular times he look at you? Any parts of you he looks at? Have you noticed when he has made eye contact?"

Blushing, Kip shook his head. "I'm sorry, Lord Conju, I haven't noticed anything like that."

"Pay more attention tomorrow then," he said, a little sharply, "and since you're the one person who *can* make eye contact, do try and take advantage of that."

That was the exact opposite of what Kip had been doing. However, he seemed to have been pretty much equally bad at being forward and being demure, based on the Emperor's reaction. It was worth making eye contact, if Lord Conju recommended it.

"Do you know anything of the Glorious One's preferences?" asked Conju.

Kip sighed, "Nothing useful. He's very particular about his dress. And while he doesn't ever request dishes, his favourite breakfast is flatbread and marmalade, because there's not usually much left if he's given that."

Drumming his fingers on the arm of the chair, Conju said, "I already knew the first one, but the second I did not. Gossip is currency in the Palace, so don't share that around without good payment first."

A gift of marmalade was the only possible way Kip thought someone might be able to exploit the breakfast information, but he refrained from commenting.

"You were in that fancy-dress costume when the Glorious One first chose you?"

Kip couldn't see where Conju's mind was going. He desperately hoped he was not expected to walk around the apartments in that outfit. He nodded, curtly.

"Show me the obeisance, and then how you stand and move to sit in your chair," said Lord Conju, standing up to watch Kip more carefully.

Returning to the main room of the suite, where his desk was, Kip arranged himself. Then he dropped gently to his knees, bending forward to place the tips of his finger on the floor, and bowed his head. When Conju gave the signal to rise, Kip did so, pulled out his chair, and sat down.

Conju made him do it again, and watched from another angle. The third time he started making corrections.

"Lift your robes just a little more, yes, we want just a peek of your sandals," he said, and walked around and behind Kip. "Sweep them forward just a little more as you fold down, good, and head is perfect."

Kip had to wait until Conju was back at the front, and indicated for him to rise. "Good, when you stand up I want you to make eye contact with a small smile, then lower your eyes respectfully."

Kip did as he was told, and then moved towards the chair. "Now, when you sit down, where is the Emperor, relative to your desk."

"Most often, the Glorious One would be about where that lamp is, Lord Conju."

Conju went and stood beside the lamp. He squinted, and stood on tip-toes. "Is your desk there any larger?" he asked.

"It's wider, but not as deep," replied Kip.

Conju arched his eyebrows. "Can you poke the tips of your toes out from the desk? Or is the back solid."

"It's not solid," answered Kip, now thoroughly confused.

"Excellent," said Conju. "When you pull your chair back, stand to the side."

Kip did so, now thoroughly confused. There was correct etiquette for pulling out his desk chair?

"As you pull your chair out, use your other hand to sweep and smooth your robes from your back to your front." Conju watched Kip try and follow this direction, and tutted.

He came over and moved Kip's hands and robes to demonstrate. Then he let Kip do it on his own. "Yes, that's it," said Conju, and Kip blushed at the praise.

Conju tilted his head and tapped his nose. "You do blush beautifully dear," he said, "Now, what are the rules about your uniform."

It was at least a half hour of grilling on every detail of the dress rules and regulations for the Service. Hem length, permitted jewellery, hair styles, cosmetics, alterations to the standard pattern. At the end, Kip felt like he'd just sat an oral exam.

Finally, at the end, Conju asked, "If the hairstyles are so strict, why is yours longer?"

Kip sighed. "The priest-wizards wanted me to match the picture in the Atlas of Imperial Peoples for the presentation. I was given an exemption. It had to be sealed by one of the high priests."

"But they didn't let you keep the necklace? Isn't that man wearing a necklace of pearls or something?"

Unable to suppress his fear, Kip twitched. In a small voice he answered. "I was permitted to wear my own *efela*."

Conju looked suddenly more interested. "Do you still have it? Can I see it?"

Reluctantly, Kip pulled his *efela ko* out from under his robes. Conju trotted over to inspect it. Kip tried to keep his hands down on the table, and not clutch at it protectively.

"It means a lot to you?" asked Conju, perceptively, "And you'd rather it was hidden?" 

Unable to gather his words, Kip nodded mutely. At home, he was proud of his studies, his people, his hopeful future role as tanà. Now that was likely gone forever, he desperately wanted to hold on to his one part of his heritage, and keep it private.

To Kip's relief, Conju nodded and did not ask further questions. Silently, Kip tucked his *efela ko* back under his robes.

"I am going to get some supplies," said Lord Conju, "While I am gone, practice the changes I showed you. You need them to be automatic."

"Yes, Lord Conju," said Kip, standing up again as the other man went to leave. Lord Conju bowed to Kip, and Kip attempted to guess the correct degree to bow back. Since this got a sigh from Conju, he had clearly got it wrong.

It was hard to be sure, without Conju's critical eye watching him, that he was doing it correctly. Eventually Kip moved back into the bedroom, bringing the desk chair, so that he could practice in front of the mirror.

Lift, swish, sink, wait, rise, smile, smooth, sit.

With the mirror, Kip could see some of what Conju was aiming for. The changes to how he handled his robes made them alternately flutter and pull smooth against him. He had never considered that it was possible for his uniform to be elegant.

Kip hummed as he tried to make the movements flow naturally.

After a little while, he decided that was probably enough practice for now. He took the chair back to the desk, and retrieved his work from downstairs. This time, when he went to pull the chair out and sit, he was careful to follow Conju's instructions.

When he went to find some supper, he got the opportunity to try the other change. As he turned into the main hallway, the Emperor stepped out towards the Study, and presumably evening court. Kip saw a glimpse of black shot with gold, and the sparkle of gems. As he had practiced, he dropped down, making sure he was out of the centre if the Emperor changed direction.

There was the tiniest gesture from the Glorious One to rise, and Kip did so. He looked up, but the Emperor had moved on. Kip admired the sparkling sweep of his train, and then lowered his eyes until the doors closed behind him.

The Emperor was busy, Kip reminded himself. He tried not to think about the "good morning" and "good evening" he used to receive. Not feeling hungry after all, Kip returned to his rooms to finish his work and practice some more.
