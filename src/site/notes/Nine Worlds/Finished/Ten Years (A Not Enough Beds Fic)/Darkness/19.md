Kip felt like he was trying to lead a conspiracy himself. He passed word to Rhodin that he didn't like marzipan. By the time he returned from lunch, there seemed to be a council meeting in his rooms.

They all stopped talking when he entered, and rose to their feet. Dame Hollis nipped forward, and slipped the stack of documents from his hands. The rest subtly arranged themselves to have Lord Conju in front.

“Your highness,” he said, and they all bowed.

Kip waited to see if there was anything more, looking from Rhodin, to Ylette, to the surprise guest of the Tower's priest-wizard, and back to Conju. They all said nothing. Kip realised they were waiting for him to speak, possibly out of genuine diffidence (shudder), or out of not wanting to admit what they had been up to.

“Any news, Lord Conju?” he asked, formally.

“They are planning to administer the stimulant tonight, your highness, and if that goes well, then the sedative next week,” answered Conju in the same tone.

“Oh, sit down, everyone,” said Kip, waving at them and slumping into a chair. “Tonight, because today is afternoon court. But did you find out why they want to do it at all?” he asked, turning to Rhodin.

Everyone arranged themselves, but sat nervously on the edge of their seats.

“They… find his Radiancy easier to manage. It has been a regular solution when… 'the burden of the crown weighed heaviest',” said Rhodin, nervously. “But if they drug you by accident instead, they are worried his Radiancy might do something drastic.”

“Like executing Lord Igel,” completed Kip, “But if they give me the stimulant by accident, rather than the sedative, they think there will be fewer problems.”

“It is a test, highness,” said Rhodin gently, “Of yourself, but also if I can be trusted not to tell you.”

Kip nodded. If his Radiancy could do a passable performance of making Kip eat the marzipan, it would probably warn them off further attempts. It should also not give Rhodin away. Of course, it would be even better if he could make it look like his Radiancy had eaten a little as well.

“Have you been able to get grapefruit of some kind purified? Or find an alternative antidote?” he asked, worried. He knew it was very hard to do fresh fruits, they tended to go mouldy. Event citrus and apples.

The priest-wizard coughed gently, “I have a small amount of a grapefruit confection which contains boiled juice, highness.”

“Hopefully that will work, if needed. Thank you,” said Kip, with a smile. “I don't want to be caught with it, so Conju or Rhodin can you try to deliver it?”

They both nodded, still looking tense.

“I'm surprised they think you wouldn't tell me, and I won't tell his Radiancy,” said Kip.

Rhodin grimaced. “Highness, while the Tower staff are, for the most part, entirely loyal to his Radiancy, many of the guard are not. We might risk inviting a coup.”

“And it is hard to clean out the guard, without the support of the Groom of the Chamber,” finished Conju. 

Kip looked between them, “But even if he isn't charged, his Radiancy would be perfectly entitled to dismiss him without cause.”

Nobody said anything.

“Ah,” said Kip, “What compromising evidence do they have?”

“Your highness,” said Conju, his voice steady, but he was refusing to look Kip in the eye, “Lord Igel showed me a letter he claims is from your fiancé. If you do not 'stay in your place', he will give it to his Radiancy.”

So Ghilly had written back. Presumably if Lord Igel thought it could be used to control him, Ghilly had told him to come back home, and that she still loved him. When had she written? It took a long time for post to make it to the Vangaveye-ve and back, although it did travel faster than Kip had.

“I can guess what is in the letter,” he said quietly, “It is probably genuine.”

Everyone looked thoroughly shocked. 

Kip tried to explain, “You know the Ouranatha don't care if you're single when they select candidates? Well, I was engaged to a woman back home. I wrote to her to break it off, but she presumably wrote back to try to convince me otherwise.”

“Highness?” said Conju, softly, “You aren't afraid of being accused of adultery? You know it would be treason?”

Kip grimaced, “I am aware, but if his Radiancy considers the letter adultery, then I am condemned.” He took a deep breath. “Every day, I live only by the generous mercy of his Radiancy. He has been unwaveringly kind to me, despite my failings, for which he could already have me executed. I have seen nothing that makes me think he would condemn me for being loved by someone else,” answered Kip. 

He did not say that he did not actually *know* what his Radiancy's response would be, but that a potential charge of treason wouldn't stop him. After all, Kip had already done worse, and seemingly been forgiven. He owed his lord the truth, even if it came at his own expense.

“Highness,” said Conju, interrupting Kip's self-recriminations, “Lord Igel could simply share the letter with a few key individuals, or even publish it. The rumour, backed by the right people, could ruin you, and undermine his Radiancy if he does not act.”

“Additionally,” added Rhodin, “Lord Igel might be wanting to test if you could be bought.” 

Kip twitched, “I would never—”

“Of course not, highness,” protested Conju, “But, Lord Igel knows very little about you, and a lot about the senior players in the game of courts.”

“If Lord Igel just did his job properly,” Kip began, but sighed, and gave up on the argument.

Conju rolled his eyes, “Well, you should prepare for court.”

Kip looked around the group as he moved to the space that had been designated for dressing him. “If any of you are concerned for yourselves or your families, please, do not feel that you have to stay.”

An hour later, Kip awaited his Radiancy in the hallway. He was uncertain if his lord would want his company this afternoon, but he wanted to provide his lord and easy way to request it if he did. The comment from the night before about how dull his lord found court still burnt in Kip's heart.

His Radiancy emerged from dressing, followed by Lord Igel. Kip carefully performed his most elegant obeisance, aware of Lord Igel's eyes on him. When he was gestured to rise, Kip put on his mildest, most polite smile. He looked only at his lord.

As Kip had hoped, his Radiancy reached out a hand. Kip bowed over it, as if he were kissing it, but carefully kept his lips away. His lord had not invited such an intimacy, so he would only pretend.

“You are truly the image of your divine forefather, Illustrious One,” said Kip softly. He suspected he was confusing his lord, but this performance was not for him.

His Radiancy said nothing, but did not release Kip's hand. Much as they had the night before, they walked to the palanquin together. His Radiancy entering first, and when his hand was not released, Kip following after.

As soon as Kip was inside, his Radiancy released his hand.

“My apologies, my lord, for taking liberties,” whispered Kip, as he knelt on the floor at his lord's feet.

“It seemed to us,” whispered the Emperor in reply, “that you meticulously took no liberties.”

“I tried, my lord,” said Kip, relieved that he had not accidentally made contact. He looked up now, into the golden eyes.

Kip waited patiently, wondering if his Radiancy wanted conversation now. What topic should Kip choose, if his lord offered none? Perhaps he could ask what his Radiancy knew of the people at court? Or would that be gossip, rather than conversation? He mentally compiled a list of those he and Conju had practiced which he felt most confident with.

When they arrived, Kip climbed out first, and then offered the Emperor his hand. His Radiancy took it, gently, and together they entered the Throne Room. A lilting piece of cello music was already being softly played. It was one of those he had heard his sister practice.

They sat down, and Kip decided to be brave, and asked, “Do you know the name of this piece, my lord? I have heard it before, but if I knew then I cannot now recall.”

His Radiancy did know, and they had a few minutes where Kip was on the comfortable topic of music. He knew, however, that here in view of all the court, would be his only chance to speak to his lord of what he had learnt that day. So when he should probably have asked about the composer's other works, he committed his social faux pas.

“My lord,” he began, casting his eyes down to his Radiancy's beautiful hands, “Lord Igel and the head chef intend to administer the stimulant tonight. They suspect that I know, but think I will not tell you, because they have a letter they believe is evidence I have been unfaithful. I swear to you, I have not, my lord, and also that I would never withhold anything from you.”

There was an awkward silence. “Is the letter a forgery?” asked his Radiancy, quietly.

“It is likely genuine. It is from the woman I was engaged to marry, before I was selected as a candidate. I wrote to her once I was made a candidate, to break the engagement, and received no reply,” said Kip, tensing his entire body to stop himself from shaking. “Since you have done me the honour of choosing your humble servant, Glorious One, I have written only to my mother and sister, by the letters you have seen.”

His Radiancy's hands stayed perfectly still, his body perfectly poised. “Engaged?” he said quietly.

“My sincerest apologies for not telling you sooner, Glorious One,” whispered Kip, trying to keep his face as calm and unreadable as his lord's was. “But please, my lord, do not eat the marzipan tonight. Give it to me and send me away to deal with the effects alone. Or… if I am not there… please send for the Tower's priest-wizard to destroy it.”

His Radiancy said nothing more. Kip did not dare. He kept his eyes firmly on his lord's hands, expecting the flick of dismissal. It did not come.

Kip had thought his lord would believe him, would forgive him. But perhaps this was just an easier, less embarrassing, crime to punish. No one would question a charge of adultery where there was written evidence.

Two fine singers joined the string quartet. His Radiancy did not dismiss Kip.

A table of refreshments appeared. Kip wordlessly offered his lord the fragrant cup of tea that has been poured. His Radiancy ignored it, but still did not dismiss Kip.

As Kip knelt there, he realised his lord probably would not want to cause a scene, by summoning the guards here. Nor would he want Kip to have any chance to disappear. Kip would have to wait for his arrest. Was the letter to his mother still in the desk? Hopefully Conju would find it and send it on.

It was agonising, the wait. He dared not move, or look at the court. They were two statues, in this shrine. A freeze-frame from a parable play, the Good Lord and the Unfaithful Servant.

Finally, at a time that was really the middle of the evening rather than the end of the afternoon, the court session drew to a close. His Radiancy stood, coming alive, and held out his hand to Kip. Surprised, Kip took it and bowed over it. Then the Emperor turned, and Kip accepted being led away.

Kip kept his eyes down, and tried to think only of walking. Not of the warm, soft hand in his.

They walked through the door, but the Emperor did not drop Kip's hand. Briskly across the room, but there was no word from the Emperor, nor circling of guards. Into the palanquin, and Kip almost tripped in surprise. He caught himself enough to kneel, but not in the elegant way Conju had taught him.

He didn't understand. Why had his lord turned so cold and silent, if he was not angry with Kip? Finally, when the guards lifted them up, Kip dared to look at his lord.

His Radiancy was blinking away tears. Kip did not know why, and he was afraid to ask. He squeezed his lord's hand, in what he hoped was a reassuring gesture.

“Cliopher,” whispered the Emperor, “I am so, so, sorry. You were *engaged*.”

“My lord, please do not blame yourself,” replied Kip, “I was too selfish to break it when I left home. If the reply had not been withheld, I would only have responded more insistently.” He squeezed his lord's hand again, in lieu of being able to offer a hug. “I forswore nothing when I vowed myself to you, my lord.”

“Cliopher,” whispered his Radiancy, but said nothing more. He was not looking at Kip, but staring at the curtains.

“My lord?” said Kip softly, stroking his lord's hand with his thumb. Suddenly Kip wondered if someone had already administered a drug. Could they have given him something slow-acting at lunch? His Radiancy had not had so much as a glass of water since then, that Kip had seen. Or had they found some other way to administer it?

“My lord?” repeated Kip, more insistently, “Are you feeling unwell? Sleepy, or light-headed? Hot?”

The Emperor closed his eyes, but did not answer. Kip waited, anxious. He didn't want to cause a panic by raising the alarm in the public hallways. But neither did he want to wait the agonising minutes until they were safely back in the Imperial Apartments.

He rubbed the back of the Emperor's hand. It still felt warm, normal. He dared to slide his fingers up to see if he could feel his lord's pulse. It was fast, but even. Increasingly terrified, he said again, “My lord, please stay with me, I will send for the doctor as soon as we arrive.”

“We are fine, Cliopher,” whispered his Radiancy, still not opening his eyes, “Only tired.”

Kip did not quite believe him, but it was of course treason to say so. “Of course, my lord,” he said instead, “Shall you have dinner, my lord? Or would you rather go to bed?”

“We would not want to cause undue distress to our staff, we will have dinner as scheduled,” said the Emperor.

“Of course, Glorious One,” said Kip. He did not say it would be far more stressful for the staff to find out later that their Emperor had been hiding some illness. But he was quietly thankful there would be no guests tonight.

Kip and the attendants undressed his Radiancy, and then dressed him in what passed for comfortable clothes for the Emperor. Lord Igel was conspicuous in his absence. The confluence of an ill Emperor, a missing Groom of the Chamber, a dinner without guests, and a known plan to drug the Emperor, felt like more than a coincidence.

The attendants also redressed Kip, and he took the chance to turn to Lord Hari. “Please ask Lord Conju to send Ser Rhodin down to collect some messages,” he whispered. The other man did not question the request, only bowed his acknowledgment.

Kip knew there was every chance Ser Rhodin wouldn’t come, or Conju wouldn’t understand. And of course he had told them all they could leave if they were afraid, he wouldn’t judge them if they took that option. The air felt like a gathering storm, heavy and humid, despite the spells Kip knew kept it perfect at all hours.

His Radiancy picked at the late dinner, and so of course, did Kip. His lord said nothing, and looked as perfectly serene and composed as he had at court. Even his eyes seemed dull, normally the one place Kip could spy some of the emotion within.

The final course was served, a beautiful plate of curls of cheese, with marzipan fruits. His Radiancy examined the display, his eating sticks raised to make a selection. He pulled out a beautiful miniature apple, and the bottom fell out of Kip’s stomach. He lent over, aiming to bite it from his lord's eating sticks, which he hoped he could pass off to the watching staff as coy rather than weird.

But his Radiancy was faster, and popped it into his own mouth. Kip looked into his lord's eyes and saw the sudden look of fear. Had his Radiancy forgotten? Been spelled or drugged into forgetting? Kip saw one last chance.

He lent forward the last hand span between them, and kissed his lord firmly. Eyes closed, unable to face the look of betrayal that surely must be filling those beautiful eyes. He slipped in his tongue between his lord's teeth, and sucked. A small lump of marzipan slipped into his mouth, and he swallowed quickly. He had been too late.

Immediately, Kip broke the kiss. He slid out of his chair, and into the full formal prostration on the floor. “Your servant begs your forgiveness for his unconscionable forwardness, Glorious and Illustrious One,” he said to the beautiful marquetry.

“Rise, Cliopher,” said his Radiancy, his voice serene.

Feeling ill, Kip rose to kneeling. His lord gave him a small, polite smile, but his eyes were full of pain. He picked up another apple with his eating sticks, and pressed it to Kip's lips. Keeping his eyes on his Radiancy's, Kip took it and swallowed.

He had perhaps a quarter of an hour before it started to take effect, depending on the dose. He still needed to get his lord safely to bed, and himself as far away as possible, unless he could get hold of the antidote. And there was no sign of Conju or Rhodin, nobody else he was sure of.
