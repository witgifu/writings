There was a knock at the outer door. Should Kip answer it? Would one of his new staff do so?

He sat up. The door seemed so far. He heard it open, and footsteps. A knock on the inner door. Yes, he should answer that.

He stood up, and walked forward a few steps. The door opened anyway. Ser Rhodin poked his head around the door.

“The Emperor is asking for you, sir,” he said.

This sent a shock through Kip. He needed to dress. Someone had laid out both his uniform and one of the new outfits on a stand. Kip hesitated, looking them both over.

“Would you like help dressing, sir?” asked Sir Rhodin.

Kip shook his head. “What should I wear?”

“The Emperor is in the official study, sir.”

Kip grabbed the uniform. It also had the advantage that he could put it on much faster, having more practice. He smoothed it out, and pulled on his sandals.

Then he raced out, and down the hall. He must not keep the Glorious One waiting.

Kip entered the study, and dropped into his obeisance. Technically not required, but it seemed prudent when the Emperor had needed to send someone to find him, and he had been in bed. 

When the Emperor gestured for him to rise, Kip automatically put on his polite smile. Swallowing hard, he immediately dropped his eyes from the Emperor's own. The Glorious One waved for Kip to take his usual seat, and he did so.

Pulling out a pen and paper, Kip readied himself for taking dictation. The Emperor turned, and began his usual pacing route.

“A pronouncement, from the Throne of Glory, et cetera,” began the Glorious One, in his usual smooth cadence.

Kip's pen very nearly skipped, but he managed to hold himself steady. Was this the order? He flicked his eyes to the guards, but they seemed to be their usual attentive selves. Not preparing to take Kip away.

“Having been presented to Us on the occasion of the celebration of ten years of Our reign, and having performed all his duties with care and excellence, and having, with diligence, won Our regard, We hereby announce the accession of Cliopher Mdang of Zunidh to Prince of the Wide Seas of Zunidh. Dated today. Please leave it on our desk to seal. That will be all, thank you, Cliopher.”

The Emperor did not look at Kip. He turned again in his pacing, and made straight for the door of the private study. When the Glorious One closed it behind himself, Kip finally let out the breath he had been holding.

The guards took up their position outside the door. Kip scribbled 'prince of the wide seas' onto the corner of his paper, and tore it off. Then he got up and walked over to the doors to the inner rooms. 

Opening them and looking through, he was relieved to spot Ser Rhodin. The man was somehow contriving to make standing in a hallway look like a perfectly reasonable activity. Kip caught his eye, and waved the paper scrap. Ser Rhodin took it with a bow. “For Lord Conju,” Kip said, and shut the door again.

As he wrote out the official version in chancery script, with the gold-flecked ink on parchment, he felt like he was watching himself from above. It was eerie, but it did seem to keep his hands steady. When he finished, he waited for it to dry, staring at the jewelled nightingale.

Was this document his sentence? To be imprisoned for life, however long that might be. To be set apart, to be called 'highness', and bowed to, and covered in silks and jewels, and be watched by jealous eyes everywhere he went. To be the so-called prince of a place he would never again see. Would he forget his own name, if the Emperor didn't say it? Did the Emperor ever forget his, when nobody ever used it to his face?

Kip had promised not to kill himself. He had placed his life in the hands of the Glorious One, to do with as he would. If this was his choice, Kip must accept it graciously.

Leaving the proclamation in the tray on the Emperor's desk, Kip walked out of the imperial study for his rooms.

As he climbed the stairs, Kip tried desperately to think of how he could uncover who had drugged the Emperor. It seemed that at least one of the kitchen or serving staff needed to be involved. But what could they gain? The only person he could think of who might stand to gain anything was Lord Igel.

It was just so risky. Lord Igel was one of the Emperor's closest confidants. Why would he risk his life?

It was possible, Kip conceded, that Igel believe he was doing it to help his lord. If the Emperor had been genuinely ill the first night. Perhaps Igel thought to be seen as the enabler of the relationship, in the Emperor's eyes. Maybe he thought that Kip could be easily manipulated, or could be used as a distraction.

Or perhaps Lord Igel just thought his lord needed a good fuck.

Kip opened the door to his rooms, to find them a hive of activity. Lady Ylette was directing Ser Rhodin, Lord Conju, and another attendant who he thought was called Sir Buana.

“Your Highness!” said Conju, spotting Kip first. Everyone stopped what they were doing, looked at Kip, and bowed low. Kip stood there, back against the door, and looked at them all.

Then he bolted for the bathroom. He made it in time, although he only dry heaved. His muscles still ached from earlier.

“Are you ill, highness?” asked Conju from the doorway.

Kip heaved again, bringing up a little stomach bile. “No,” he said, “it's just nerves.”

“That's alright,” said Conju, softly, “it's normal to be nervous the day before your wedding.”

Sliding down the wall to sit on the floor, Kip looked up, dazed, at Conju. He was right. When the Emperor ennobled him tomorrow, Kip would make oaths of loyalty and obedience. They weren't wedding vows, but they weren't so different. And Kip was a concubine, not a spouse, so of course there would be no need for reciprocal vows.

He wanted to laugh hysterically. To curl into a ball on the floor and cry. To confess his crimes to Conju too, so the other man wouldn't look at him with soft pity, but would recoil back like he should.

Kip got up. “Did you need me for fittings?” he asked, wiping his face with a damp wash cloth.

“Yes, I'm afraid we do, highness,” said Conju, “I'll also need your signature on the request to the Treasury.”

“Please don't call me that, Lord Conju. I'm not… it's not...” said Kip, watching Conju in the mirror.

Conju looked worried.

“I'm sorry, forget what I said. I didn't mean it's not real — I wrote the proclamation out myself, the Emperor will put his seal on it before the hour is up — I'm just... overwhelmed.”

Kip pushed himself away from the sink, and went out into the main room again. There he carefully read over the request letters (not that he was entirely sure what a 'parure' was) and signed them.

He stood on the short pedestal Conju led him to. Then he closed his eyes, and let them do as they wished. His uniform was whisked away. Layers were added, and taken off. Paint applied to his face, and removed. His hair was brushed, and combs stuck into it. He let the feeling wash over him, ignoring individual touches. Kip was asked to kneel, to sit on a chair, to bow. To smile. He did as he was told, feeling like a doll in someone else's puppet show. 

Until finally he realised everyone had stepped away. He looked down. They had dressed him in a similar outfit to yesterday, but different shades of red. He started to shake, collapsing down into a squat. Wanting to wriggle out of the clothes, but unwilling to smear everyone's work on his face and hair. 

How could he go to the Emperor dressed as he had been yesterday? As if he was proud of his crimes, as if he was flaunting them? As if he might repeat them? Kip could feel the nausea rising again. 

There was a hand on his shoulder, and a cool damp cloth on the back of his neck. “Please,” said Kip, hoping it was Conju, “Please, is there anything else I could wear? Please...”

“Shhh,” said Conju, rubbing his shoulder. “Of course, highness. We only thought you would want to celebrate your triumph—”

Kip twisted around, grabbing Conju's arm. In the tiniest possible whisper he breathed into Conju's ear, “He was drugged, I should be dead.”

Realisation dawned in Conju's eyes. Presumably in the rush to prepare for tomorrow, he hadn't thought much about the implications of adding the rumours he had heard to Kip's assertion. Conju gave the tiniest of nods, and Kip slackened his grip.

“There will almost certainly be other guests at dinner tonight,” said Conju to the room, as he helped Kip to stand again.

Lady Ylette nodded, “Something a bit less obvious then?”

To Kip's relief they replaced the two outer layers. An over-robe that buttoned by the shoulder, and a mantle that buttoned to the robe and could be removed with it. Both in shades of red, but with a sky-blue ribbon trim. There was probably jewellery, Kip tried not to think about it.

“What time is it?” he asked the room. He hadn't noticed a bell ring since lunchtime.

“Half five, highness. Dinner will be at six in the formal dining room,” said Ser Rhodin. Both Lady Ylette and Conju gave Ser Rhodin unimpressed looks. “Sorry, Lord Conju, Lady Ylette, for not realising this was information you needed. The guests tonight are the Lords Magi, of Ysthar and Alinor.”

Kip sighed. “I hope I get some time while his Radiancy is at court to get some work done.”

The Emperor arrived just after Kip, already dressed in court costume. Kip admired the gold paint around his eyes, and wondered who, apart from himself, would ever see it. Even the attendants who had applied it would only get to see it with the Emperor's eyes closed.

The Lord of Rising Stars approached, and Kip dropped into his obeisance. The Emperor bid him rise, and Kip followed him into the dinning room. It was, unsurprisingly, larger than the small dinning room they had eaten in before. Far larger than was necessary for the two tables and four dinners clearly expected.

There were two square tables of dark wood, one inlaid with a sun-in-glory pattern in mother-of-pearl. Four matching chairs, one gilt. The ceiling was strung with soft mage lights, none of them bright, but together making the room light enough. Looking more closely, Kip saw they were indeed also forming a sun-in-glory.

They were only left alone for a few moments, before the guards stamped their spears. The Emperor turned, and indicated to admit the expected guests. Lady Jessamine and Lord Thalassior Barred walked in, dressed beautifully. They both glinted with jewels; the Lord of Ysthar in blue diamonds and deep sapphires, the Lady of Alinor in pink diamonds and grey pearls.

Kip felt dowdy by comparison. But then, he reasoned, these were two of the greatest wizards in the Nine Worlds. They probably looked impressive in their nightshirts.

Both guests both made flawless obeisances together, and the Emperor gestured for them to rise. “Cliopher, may I introduce Lord Thalassior Barred, and Lady Jessamine. Lords Magi, this is Cliopher Mdang, Prince of the Wide Seas of Zunidh.”

Kip gave the shallow bow, that Conju had informed him not half an hour ago, was correct for him greeting the Lords Magi. When both of the Lords Magi bowed in response, he assumed he hadn't done too badly.

The next step, was sitting at the table. At the sun-in-glory table were two settings, one with gold eating sticks, the other with silver. The other table had forks and spoons. Kip tried to keep his face in a polite smile. He was seated at the right hand of the Lord Emperor. _Above_ the Lords Magi. 

Between this and the introductions, he was suspecting the Emperor was saying Kip outranked the Lords Magi. It seemed absurd. He would have to check with Conju.

The Emperor sat first, then indicated for his guests to join him. Since no higher deities were present, it was the Emperor who began the discussion. “I hear you had treatise with the Good Neighbours recently, Lady Jessamine?” came the serene voice from next to Kip.

They were presented with cubes of smokey grilled meat, with a tangy orange sauce. Our of the corner of his eye, Kip watched the emperor pick up his eating sticks, and take a piece. The signal that Kip could do the same.

Here he was, it would seem, having dinner with at least two, possibly three, of the greatest experts on magic in the Empire. As far as Kip knew, the Emperor didn't have any magic of his own, but he did seem very well-informed. At least to Kip, which as far as he knew could mean that the Lords Magi were simply humouring the Emperor.

Eventually the conversation drifted to minor gods and spirits of place, and Kip felt he at least understood some of what was being said. The courses came and went, Kip trying to make sure he ate no more or less than the Emperor beside him.

Lord Thalassior Barred finished a long explanation of the comparative difference between water sprites on Ysthar and Voonra just as desert was served. Kip eyed the fried buns on the serving platter. There was no obvious pattern to how they were assigned between the four of them, but Kip fretted. 

“Do they have water sprites in the Vangavaye-ve, Cliopher?” asked the Emperor, biting his bun in half.

In his surprise at being addressed, Kip almost dropped the bun he had been nibbling on, but managed to make a passable impression of putting it down instead.

“I have heard of them appearing in high springs, Glorious One, but I have never encountered any,” said Kip.

“And are they described as more like those on Ysthar or Voonra?” the Emperor inquired again, as he reached over to swap his half-eaten bun with Kip's.

“Neither, Glorious One, they are said to resemble Tui flowers, which I believe only grow on Zunidh,” said Kip. The Emperor's face was serene, but there was a twinkle in his eyes which must have been just for Kip.

Kip looked back at his plate, and finished the bun the Emperor had started. He wondered what the Lords Magi thought of their Emperor's behaviour. Perhaps it might be considered flirtatious?

Eventually the last plates were taken away. Kip looked up hopefully at the Emperor, wondering if he would be dismissed soon. Although he had tried hard to maintain the neutral polite smile, something in his face must have given him away.

“I'm afraid Cliopher has other duties to attend to, and will not be joining us at court tonight,” said the Emperor, as the bells rang eight.

Kip got out from the table, bowed again to the Lords Magi. Then he performed his obeisance to the Emperor, and sidled out of the room with as much haste as he thought he could get away with, and not be called *unseemly*.
