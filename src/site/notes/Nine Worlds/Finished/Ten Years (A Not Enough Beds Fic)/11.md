Kip reflected that he should not have asked a question of the Emperor. It was probably poor etiquette to ask such a personal question. As a bonus he had managed to ask a question that the Emperor clearly found awkward.

They both picked at the coconut rice without eating much. It was delicious, but the sticky texture felt like he was eating glue, and his nerves were congealing it in his stomach. Kip felt intensely aware of every move of his hands, and could imagine his mother sitting next to him telling him not to fidget. 

He wished he dared try and introduce another topic of conversation. But he knew that the higher rank person was to set the topic, and since the Emperor had clearly closed the previous topic of music, Kip was at a loose end.

There were plenty of questions Kip wanted to ask, all of which he was sure were inappropriate. Most of all, he wanted to know what the Emperor wanted of him. When they were working together in the study, Kip felt comfortable. It was a job he knew how to do. It also seemed like the Emperor felt comfortable. Certainly, it had not been particularly difficult to improve on the work of the Emperor's previous secretaries.

The Emperor clearly did not want Kip in his bed. That was fine, Kip was perfectly happy with that. He would rather not be asked to administer medication again, either, although he wasn't sure what he would do if the same situation happened again.

What did dinner tonight mean? Had the Emperor requested this? Was Lord Igel setting this up? Kip was reasonably sure that the personal secretary to the Lord Emperor was not a position where one ate at his table. Or, for that matter, lived in his apartments.

He wanted to write to Basil, and ask his advice, but that could be a dangerous letter to write. Someone might still be snooping on his mail (he was sure they had been during the training, all his mail had arrived slightly rumpled). Although, what question would he even ask? All he could think of was to write what had happened, and ask if there was anything he could do differently in future.

After the Emperor dismissed him, Kip went back to his rooms and the desk there. He pulled out a sheet of paper, and tried to think of what he could write.

> Basil,
> 
> I told you how I have a new position now that my 'special project' has wrapped up. It's going quite well, my lord seems to appreciate my work, which I must say is a nice change. One of my co-workers (he's more senior than I, but I don't report to him, you know how it goes) is behaving a little oddly. Or two of them are, it's hard to explain.
>  
> I think my first co-worker thinks the second is flirting with me, and is trying to get us together. The second does not seem to me to be flirting, and seems very careful to maintain a professional distance. However, he seems to be doing nothing about the first co-worker's actions. 
>
> I have not worked out how (or if) I should ask anyone about this. Perhaps I am overreacting, and I should just wait and see what happens. At least the work is interesting and varied!
>
> I know I haven't written to Ghilly recently. This new position was quite a surprise, and everything hasn't been confirmed yet, so I don't want to let *everyone* know until it's quite sure.
>
> All the best, Kip

He read over it again, sighed, and then burnt the letter in the fireplace. If anyone other than the Emperor *had* actually flirted with him, they could get in big trouble. He didn't want the Tower staff or the guard under suspicion. He wrote out the first two sentences again, but didn't know where to go from there. 

After half a bell without progress, he went back to the pages to collect anything that had come in since lunch. Back at the desk, he shoved aside the troublesome letter, and began making notes on the reports. If he was quick, he might be able to get a request to the pages for cross-references from the archives before the midnight bell.

---

The next day was pleasantly without surprises. He worked with the Emperor in the morning, and in the afternoon recorded a series of surprisingly informative budget meetings that required the Emperor's sign-off. 

In the evening, he reviewed the material from the archives, and was able to find some of the missing context from some of the recent reports. He diligently added in to the next round of morning reports for the Emperor.

At the start of the morning work the next day, the Emperor asked Kip to check over the budget for the Tower.

"It's not that we suspect anyone of mismanagement," the Emperor said, pacing as usual, "but we suspect that habits may have ingrained inefficiencies. Such as items being unnecessarily purified."

"Of course, Glorious One," replied Kip, and wondered if this was a test of his skills following the extra notes he had added this morning. It would require careful negotiation with the Tower staff to get the records he needed. He doubted Lord Igel would look kindly on the Emperor's pet asking to audit the books.

It took Kip every spare minute he had for two days to gather and read the records. Then another three to read through the stack of references he requested the pages fetch. Finally, he spent a very productive afternoon and evening synthesising and double-checking the work.

It wasn't until he put his pen down just before midnight that he actually looked at what the results meant. There was a minor note that ritual beeswax candles were being purified, even though they were supplied purified from the Ouranatha's stores. That seemed like the kind of small oversight the Emperor was expecting him to find, and it did not give him cause for concern.

More worrying was that Lord Igel had employed his own son and was paying him an inflated salary. Additionally, it was not clear what exactly Lord Igel's sone was supposed to be doing, or where he worked. Everyone seemed to assume he worked in a different part of the Tower to them.

Finally, there was the conclusion that the Tower was spending a _lot_ of money on Kip. All the things that should have normally come out of his salary, from uniforms to postage, were being paid for by the Tower. His salary, at least, was still coming from the Service. 

He was also causing them additional costs in purification. All his clothes, linens, and towels had to be washes specially. All his dishes, too. They had mostly been feeding him leftovers from what was served to the Emperor, but that was not always available. 

It was mildly embarrassing. The only benefit he could see from the arrangement so far, was that Kip hadn't been blinded by his persistent habit of looking into the Emperor's eyes. Of course, it was the Emperor's prerogative. Perhaps now that it was done, the Emperor might think it was useful to have one member of his household who could touch him if needed.

The midnight bell rang, and Kip decided to check over the short report one more time in the morning. Then he would simply include it with the morning reports for the Emperor. Kip's job was to record accurately, and report clearly. Even about himself.
