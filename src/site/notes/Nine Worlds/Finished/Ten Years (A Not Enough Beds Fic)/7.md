Kip looked up into the darkness where the ornate white and gold ceiling was, and tried to will himself to sleep. He knew it wouldn't work, it hadn't worked in the cupboard room he'd had as a page, or the dormitory he'd been moved to shortly after being selected. It hadn't worked any other night in this sumptuous bed.

He had just worked himself to exhaustion when he'd been sleeping at his desk. It had been such a relief to have a job he was vaguely qualified for again that he hadn't minded so much. Working so hard also kept his mind from other things.

Here, now, he had a schedule. Lord Igel had been very clear that once the midnight bell had rung, Sayo Mdang was to be in bed unless summoned, and to stay there until the dawn bell. Igel had even sent someone to check on him twice, which seemed excessive.

But he did still have a job, Kip reassured himself. Working directly with the Emperor. He even (somewhat) had someone reporting to him.

The Emperor did seem to be kind. Or wanted to be kind. It had been clear, from the moment Kip arrived, that the Palace was not a place where kindness was valued. Rank, and the power and money associated, were what was valued here.

Kip's lack of discernable rank clearly bothered Lord Igel, and all the other Tower staff. They seemed to have decided he was a sort of useful pet, an indulgence to their Emperor that kept their Emperor happy. This rankled, but Kip was careful not to talk back. Igel had made it clear he was happy to report any infraction to Internal Security.

Before Kip had time to spiral into his nightmares about Internal Security, he realised there was a noise coming through the floor. Since the Tower had so far been absolutely silent from midnight until the choir began at dawn, Kip found this strange.

The sound started as a kind of groan, erratic and deep. Then the pitch rose and rose until it became unmistakably a scream. Punctuated only occasionally, when whoever it was gasped for air.

Kip didn't think about his schedule, or the rules, or even putting slippers on. Someone was screaming directly under him, and he had to try to help.

Perhaps if he had been more awake, he would have thought a little further and realised there was only one person who could be screaming. Or remembered when the guard had shown him the layout of the Tower, and known what room was below his.

Heedless, Kip barrelled down the stairs and around the hallway, following the terrifying sound. He only stopped when he saw the Imperial Guards in front of the door. 

He stopped, looking between the two impassive and impressive men. They seemed unmoved by the sound coming from the room they were guarding. Kip didn't dare ask for admission.

Lord Igel appeared from the other direction, a sumptuous blue and silver dressing robe streaming along behind him. His eyes narrowed when he saw Kip.

Belatedly, Kip bowed to Lord Igel, and started slowly stepping backwards. Kip suddenly remembered he should not have left his bed.

The door opened before Lord Igel had a chance to chastise Kip. A face popped out between the doors, an aristocratically shaven head dripping with sweat. "Apologies, Lord Igel, the Glorious One is moving too much to allow us to administer the medication."

Kip took another step back, his bare feet soundless on the marble floor. He should not be here. If the Emperor was seriously sick, Lord Igel would have no reason to keep him around. Much less honour the Emperor's promise yesterday. The Emperor was Kip's only protector.

Lord Igel turned from the door, back to Kip. Igel paused, and in the dimly lit hallway at night, Kip 
wanted to do nothing more than run. He suppressed the urge, and bowed again.

“Come here, Sayo Madon, perhaps you can be useful,” he said, his voice perfectly neutral, his accent that of the highest court nobles. Kip could never tell what Igel was thinking or feeling, if he was angry or happy.

“How may I be of service, Lord Igel?” Kip replied, trying his best to produce the court accent himself. 

“You have been following all the rules?” Igel asked, snidely. Through the gap between the doors came another terrified scream, followed by a moan, and then the much quieter sounds of someone flinging themselves around in bed.

“Yes, Lord Igel,” answered Kip, deferentially. It seemed pointless, since it was abundantly clear to Kip that the Emperor only had interest in Kip for his secretarial skills. But Lord Igel had said to, and any slip up could technically be construed as treason until he was formally sent away.

Lord Igel motioned to the guards to let them all in. The guards nodded, and opened the doors wide. Igel strode forwards, offering only a perfunctory nod to the bed.

Kip stepped forward, nervously. Strictly speaking, he thought he should make a full obeisance, but it was clear the Emperor was in no state to signal anyone to rise. He made a slightly deeper bow than he had to Lord Igel, but twitched upwards suddenly when the Emperor screamed again. 

Kip trotted over to join the circle of attendants.

“Sayo Madon can administer the sedative,” said Lord Igel, “he won't be in any danger, and then we can all get back to bed.”

“No… no, no,” came a distressed whisper from the bed. Kip turned, and saw the Emperor tossing and turning, illuminated by his own glow. Then the Emperor stopped, his back arching, and screamed again.

This was clearly beyond the usual sort of nightmare that anyone might have. Kip wasn't sure why the Emperor hadn't woken himself with his own screams.

One of the other attendants put a gold-and-crystal vial onto the side table. Gesturing at it, he said to Kip, “The Glorious One needs to drink the whole thing, don't let any of it spill.”

Kip picked up the vial, and bit his lip. He had a good idea of how to make the Emperor choke on it, simply pour it down his throat as he screamed, but no clear idea how to get him to drink it. Never mind the risk of flailing limbs.

Trying to ignore the audience, Kip climbed up onto the enormous bed. The Emperor was whimpering and whipping his head from side to side. Kip shuffled forwards on his knees, and then stopped, a hand's breadth from the Emperor's head.

He looked so vulnerable, despite the glow, the opulent bed, the sheets covered in tiny woven suns. His eyes were scrunched tightly closed, his breaths short and shallow.

“Glorious One—” whispered Kip, but the Emperor cut him off.

“NO!” the Emperor yelled, raising up his head briefly, before dropping it down.

Kip wasn't sure if it was just a coincidence, what the Emperor said. “My lord, please, you need to take your medicine,” he tried.

“No, no, no,” whimpered the Emperor. Kip could see tears running down his face. What was he so afraid of? What horrors was he watching?

“Please, my lord, it will help,” said Kip, holding the vial out, but still stoppered. He didn't want the Emperor to toss and knock it from his hand.

The Emperor continued to make small, inarticulate sounds. Distressed, angry, scared. Kip's heart was aching for him.

The Emperor let out a sob, and Kip was reminded of his sister Vinyë's nightmares. Kip was sure those dreams were of their father dying, although Vinyë would never say. Kip used to hold her, and call her 'papaya', the nickname their father had used when she was a little girl.

Kip had no idea what the Emperor's childhood nickname would be. Had his parents even raised him? He knew from novels that aristocrats often had their children raised by servants. What pet name might a servant give their imperial charge?

“Savelin,” breathed Kip, as close to the Emperor's ear and as quietly as he could, “You need to take your medicine.”

The Emperor groaned, but seemed to relax slightly.

Kip, trying to suppress that he was shaking with fear, slid his left hand under the Emperor's head. He took a deep breath. The skin was soft and smooth, if clammy with sweat. But Kip had not burst into flames.

He lifted the Emperor's head slowly, while flipping the lid of the vial with his other hand. “Savelin, please open your mouth,” he breathed.

To Kip's immense surprise, the Emperor did as he was told. Kip gently poured the pink, shimmering potion, down the inside of the Emperor's cheek. The Emperor flinched once, and Kip quickly stopped pouring. The Emperor closed his mouth and screwed up his eyes, as if the taste was bad.

“I'm so sorry,” said Kip, just above a whisper, “There's only a little bit more.”

The Emperor opened his mouth again. Kip poured the last of the potion out, and then gently lowered the Emperor's head. He pulled his hand out from the pillow.

Without warning, the Emperor rolled over to face Kip and grabbed the hand still holding the empty vial. He pulled it towards his chest, hugging it to him like a child with a favourite toy.

Kip struggled for balance. He was kneeling, and the Emperor had pulled his arm forwards and sideways across Kip's face. It would be easier if Kip could lie down, but he could not believe Lord Igel would tolerate that kind of behaviour. Instead, Kip bent down, in the full formal prostration.

Had anyone ever done that while the Emperor cuddled their arm? Kip thought probably not, but with a hundred Emperors, maybe they had.

“Good,” came Lord Igel's voice from somewhere behind Kip. “Sayo Madon, stay where you are until the Glorious One releases you.”

“Yes, Lord Igel,” said Kip quietly, partially muffled by the bedspread.

There was the sound of the bed curtains being pulled closed, retreating footsteps, and the door closing. Then, silence. Kip wondered who had stayed. The guards surely would still be here. And at least one attendant, in case the Emperor woke and asked for anything.

The Emperor made a small sniffling noise, and a tiny whimper. Kip wondered if the medication, the sedative, actually stopped the dreams or if it just stopped the Emperor screaming. He hoped that it did help.

Kip wished he was more comfortable. Or that he could sleep. As the rush of fear drained out of him, he was feeling very tired.

He began by reviewing his tasks for the next day, and then the rest of the week. He needed some documents from the archives, which hopefully the pages could find. Two draft letters needed to be confirmed, and then sent on. He should summarise the report from Alinor on cheese production, it was fascinating but about half of it was background history.

The Emperor whimpered again, a little louder.

With a big family, Kip had often been roped in to help entertain, look after, or sometimes just hold, various younger cousins. So it was almost unconsciously that he began to hum a lullaby.

The tiniest of smiles appeared on the Emperor's face, and he relaxed his tight grip on Kip's hand. Kip couldn't imagine the Emperor knew the lullaby, since the only words for it Kip knew were in language, not Shaian. But maybe it didn't need words, maybe lullabies everywhere were simple soothing tunes.

Kip stopped when he finished the song. A few moments later, the tension returned to the Emperor's body. Kip sighed internally, and hummed another.

He'd run out of lullabies and children's songs, and started on the Lays. And then, at some point, he must have rolled slightly onto his side, and fallen asleep.

Kip did not wake up to the choir, as he had done since moving to the Tower. Nor did he wake up to attendants flinging wide the bed curtains. He woke when the Emperor flung his arm, rolling him over in the process.

It was a very sudden waking. Kip did a rapid assessment of where he was and why, as his memory of the early morning returned. Panicking slightly, he rolled himself back over, and crawled back on his knees into the full formal prostration again.

He could hear the Emperor's rapid breathing over his own.

“Are you,” said the Emperor, taking a deep breath, “Are you well, Sayo Mdang?”

Kip peeked up to look at the Emperor's hands for any signals. They were clutching the sheets very tightly. “I am fine, Glorious One, I apologise unreservedly for causing you distress.”

To Kip's shock, the Emperor's breathing slowed almost instantly. Kip peeked again, and the Emperor's hands were in his lap. He made the signal to rise.

Kip lifted himself up to kneeling. It did not seem right to sit cross-legged, to squat, or stand, on the Imperial bed. 

“What happened last night, Sayo Mdang?” asked the Emperor, his voice perfectly neutral but only the tiniest of whispers.

“You were shaking and screaming, Glorious One,” whispered Kip in reply, “Lord Igel wanted to give you some medicine, but nobody could get close enough to administer it. So they asked me to, Glorious One.”

The Emperor closed his eyes. “And after that?” he continued, in the same whisper.

Kip swallowed. “You grabbed on to my arm, Glorious One. Lord Igel instructed me to stay until you released me.” He was sure he should probably add some court gesture of apology or something, but he did not know the appropriate ones. He compromised by adding a small bow.

The Emperor flicked his hand in the gesture of dismissal. Kip performed his obeisance from where he was on the bed, and then shuffled off backwards. 

He watched the Emperor as he went, and as Kip's feet found the edge of the bed, he saw a single tear roll down the magnificent serenity. Kip pretended he hadn't noticed anything, as he slipped off the edge of the bed and ducked under the curtain.
