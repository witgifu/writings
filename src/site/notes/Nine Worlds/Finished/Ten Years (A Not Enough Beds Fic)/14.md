The Grand Duke of Eldizorka, third of that name, actually stood up from his chair when the footman let Conju in. This was unusual, as the Duke was very much aware of his rank, and careful to preserve every sign of it. Great nobles with grand titles passed through many generations, like Conju's mother, could afford to relax the rules as they chose. The Duke had a lot of money, and a grand title indeed, but was acutely aware that his family had only been raised to it quite recently.

Conju noted this different behaviour, but changed nothing about his own actions. He bowed to precisely the correct degree for an unmarried Cavalier of Ysthar greeting his patron, a Grand Duke. Then he waited patiently to be told why he had been summoned. He could guess from the Duke's actions that it was either very good or very bad news.

“Well,” said the Duke, looking Conju up and down in an assessing way.

Conju smiled politely, since that was not a question, nor a statement waiting for a response.

“I must say, you do quick work, Lord Conju,” said the Duke, and waved to a servant to pour wine, “Lasara said yes, of course.”

There was no one who Conju could immediately think might have offered to marry Lasara. After all, they had only been at the Palace of Stars for a month. That seemed very fast, even for an engagement that must be mostly about family connections and strategy.

Conju chose not to admit his ignorance. “Thank you, your grace,” he said, bowing again, “but surely most of the credit must go to the young lady?”

The footman handed both of them fine crystal glasses of dark red wine. The two noblemen sat down on the very fashionable, if somewhat slippery, satin-upholstered chairs. Conju took a small sip of the wine, savouring the heady flavours.

“You introduced Lasara to the eldest grandson of the Elector Prince of Zangoraville, yes?” said the Duke, raising one eyebrow.

Conju inclined his head, “I did, your grace, but it was up to the young lady to make the impression.” The lordling in question was one of many seemingly interchangeable young men that Conju had a nodding acquaintance with. It was only polite (and one of the reasons for his position) to introduce his charge to all of them who had rank equal or above hers.

The Duke snorted into his wine glass, in a rather undignified fashion. “To be perfectly frank, I suspect his father has gotten tired of his worst habits, and ordered him to settle down. If Lasara is the eligible girl he could stand to wed, well, that's fine with me.”

It was, Conju had to admit, an excellent match. The Elector was from an ancient and distinguished family. But the enormous cost rebuilding after the highly destructive fires four or five years ago had left them short of cash. The Grand Duke had money, and was willing to settle a lot of it on his daughter, for the right match. This, it would seem, was going to be that match.

“Congratulations to them both,” said Conju, raising his glass very slightly, “I assume Her Grace will be planning the wedding for next summer? Here in Astandalas?”

There was a small pause, before the Duke responded. “The girl's father wants his son married as soon as may be,” he said, not meeting Conju's eyes, “my wife will have to content herself with a wedding in Zangoraville in two months time.”

Well. That put rather a different spin on things. That kind of timeline usually meant that the couple had somewhat _preempted_ the wedding. Or there were debts due very soon that needed to be paid. Given that Conju had been Lasara's escort, he rather thought the first option unlikely.

The final piece of the puzzle clicked in Conju's mind. He was out of a position. Or he would be, as soon as the Duke's family headed to Zangoraville to finish the arrangements. The stipend the Grand Duke had paid Conju was very generous, he could certainly stay in Astandalas a little while longer if he wanted.

Did he want? It was a question for later, when he was not being closely watched by his very-nearly-former patron.

They talked of minor court gossip for another quarter bell. Conju, carefully polite; the Duke, giving off a faint whiff that he felt awkward about the whole situation. Once Conju judged he had spent enough time not to cause insult, he excused himself with a vague mention of a prior arrangement.

At the front door, just before the footman opened it, the majordomo caught Conju's eye. She smiled slightly, and handed Conju an envelope. Conju pocketed it without reading. The majordomo said, “Thank you also from all of us, Lord Conju. I believe Lord Igel is likely to reach out as well.”

The Groom of the Chamber to the _Lord Emperor_. Of course, he was the eldest child of the Elector. The warning rather surprised Conju, but if the majordomo said so, then it was likely true. He nodded his thanks for the message, and returned to his own apartment.

His valet opened the apartment door, and Conju stepped through. Then he flopped, artfully, onto the divan, and pulled the letter from his pocket. It was, as he expected, a formal notice saying his services were no longer required.

He was about to toss it onto the table, when he saw that it was more than a form letter. It also made note that a final deposit had been made in his stipend. It noted the amount, Conju had to read it three times. Surely that was an error? They must have put an extra zero on the end. He would check the letter from his bank when it came.

Regardless, it was still generous of the Grand Duke to pay him an extra month of stipend. It would mean that Conju could at spend the rest of the season at the Palace. Maybe make some friends of his own, or find another position.

Conju decided to celebrate his new lack of responsibilities that night, by simply enjoying himself at the party he had been invited to. And if he brought someone back to his rooms afterwards, well, now there was only his valet to complain.

He waved sleepily to Roan? Rome? Rojan, that was it, as the other young man got up our of Conju's bed. Rojan gave him a cute wink.

“Where you off too so early?” asked Conju, rubbing his head into the pillow.

“Afternoon shift at the Tower,” said Rojan, collecting his tunic from the stand where Conju's valet had arranged his discarded clothes.

“What time is it then?” asked Conju, wiggling to sit up and look out the window.

“Nine. I need to be washed, fed, and dressed, and be at the Tower by eleven.” Rojan pulled on his outer robes, gave Conju a quick kiss on the top of his head, and left.

Conju mused over getting up. He didn't have anything in particular to do today, but he did feel the need to brush his teeth. Maybe he should write to his mother.

The problem was solved when Conju's valet came in. “The Tower has sent a page for you, my lord,” she said, eying the clothes she had prepared for him.

“The Tower?” asked Conju, and he was relieved that his voice didn't squeak. He threw off the bed covers, and slid out of bed.

“The message was from Lord Igel, my lord, he didn't provide any further information,” she replied. Conju could tell she disapproved of this high-handed behaviour.

“I better not leave him waiting,” said Conju, before splashing his face with the warm water she had set out for him.

Conju arrived at the Tower half a bell later. Given the rush, he was reasonably happy with his personal presentation. He would have rather had more time with his cosmetics, but it was still morning, so less was acceptable.

Since he hasn't been in the Tower before, Conju asked one of the pages for direction. He thought it unlikely he should attempt the main entrance, since he had been summoned by the Groom of the Chamber and not the Emperor. This proved correct, when the page showed Conju through a concealed staff entrance, and to what must be a meeting room.

The page left Conju alone in the room. It was slightly cramped, with no windows, indicating it as very much a working room. It was furnished with a table, a central magelight, and a few mismatched chairs. 

Shortly after, Lord Igel arrived. He was dressed plainly, although Conju could tell the fabric was very fine. Given the man's job was to garb the glory of the Emperor, perhaps he preferred to fade into the background.

Conju bowed deeply, indicating both his respect for Lord Igel's rank, and for his position. Lord Igel responded with the appropriate acknowledgement. They both sat at the table.

“Thank you for coming, Lord Conju,” said Igel, “I am hoping to collect more information on my son's new fiancée.”

“Of course, your lordship,” replied Conju, “I understand this is quite sudden. I have known the family for some years, and been the particular confidant of Lady Lasara since she was brought to court.”

“I shall be blunt, Lord Conju, do you think she is capable of the position to which she aspires?”

If that was blunt, Conju wondered what his usual manner was. But he understood the implied question.

“She has been well-educated, has impeccable manners, enjoys socialising and country pursuits. She plays the lute tolerably. She and the young lord met at a garden party. They have played three games of cards together, and shared seven dances. I have escorted her home every night, and she has never been excessively under the influence of drink.”

Conju stopped, waiting for further questions. If he had been given a drink, he would have sipped it. Apparently in the Tower he did not even rate a glass of water.

Lord Igel tapped his index finger on the table. “Good,” he said, clearly thinking something through. Conju wondered what his true concerns about the match might be. The couple seemed to at least get along tolerably well, which was generally the best start that most arranged marriages could expect.

“Are you interested in a new position?” he asked, scrutinising Conju's appearance more closely.

This question took Conju by surprise. He wasn't aware that Lord Igel had a daughter. Perhaps he had a ward? Or a more distant relative he was sponsoring at court?

“I would certainly consider it, your lordship. Do you know of one?” he asked, diffidently. He didn't want to insult Lord Igel, but Conju was concerned about those money rumours. If they were true, then he didn't want to be trapped escorting some idiot girl around, and having to ask his own parents for money when Lord Igel couldn't pay.

“Possibly. There is a young man,” (Conju almost interrupted Igel, but managed to restrain himself) “who is already… engaged. He is in need of… education… and… companionship.”

Conju tried to parse what Lord Igel was, and wasn't, saying. It was very confusing. Why would the young lord's parents, or impending in-laws, not hire a deportment teacher? They practically hung around the Palace like flies, trying to pick up students. 

Why did the young lord need a companion? Was he prone to fighting or gambling, and required someone to keep him on the straight and narrow? It seemed like more trouble than Lasara had been.

Lord Igel must have sensed some of Conju's hesitation. “The young man has risen in rank quite unexpectedly. His… intended… is already fully established. It will need to be discreet. I do not suspect the young man of any ill habits, he has spent the past months closeted away. He has not even been presented at court.”

This was even stranger. Surely a young man engaged to a noble who was already head of their household would be common gossip? Certainly if it was anyone in Lord Igel's circles. And yet, Conju couldn't even think of anyone who was *eligible*.

“Would I be able to meet the young lord first, your lordship? Or his intended?” asked Conju, trying to fish for more information about whom this might be.

Lord Igel pursed his lips. “I will need to discuss it with his intended first. It would be a position in the young man's household, but of course not arranged by him. If they said yes…”

Conju could complete the assumption for himself. It would be an offer he would not be able to refuse. If his name was put forward, he would be obliged to accept. Conju was a younger son, yes, but he was hardly friendless, so it would have to be someone his family could not afford to offend. 

Had Grand Duchess Melissa got pregnant with her cook? Who else could—

In a sudden flash, Conju finally put the pieces together. He put his hands under the table to hide the shake he could feel building. He was a loyal son of the Empire. His younger brother and sister had both joined the Armies of Astandalas to serve. Conju was no fighter, had no magic, no unusual skills at all. He had never expected—

“You are known to be discrete, and fastidious, Lord Conju,” said Lord Igel, leaning forward, “Both qualities that would be valuable in this position.”

Conju looked up from where he had been gazing at the marquetry in the table. Unsaid by Lord Igel, was that Conju knew how to play the game of courts. Members of his family had been close to the Imperial family before.

If he wanted. If the… young lord's intended wanted.

“_If_ I say yes, your lordship, and the position is confirmed, what happens then?” asked Conju, trying to emphasise the conditional without sounding rude.

“You would begin at once,” replied Lord Igel, immediately. Then after a pause, added with a wave of his hand, “it would come with a generous stipend, and cover reasonable expenses.”

Conju nodded his understanding. He did not honestly think he could say no, or even pretend to hold off Lord Igel. He did not believe in the gods, or fate. But if the Empire called, if the Emperor asked, if he was *needed*…

“I would be most honoured,” he said, and bowed in his seat, with the gesture to match.
