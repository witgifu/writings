That evening, you sat on your golden self on your golden throne. Serene, luminous, perfectly poised. Inside, your thoughts were racing.

It was a good time for thinking, court. Not if you needed to reference any documents, or might want to dictate notes. Not if you wanted to be *comfortable* while you thought. But if you wanted to be uninterrupted, it was at least three hours, often as many as six, of needing to do nothing more than sit still and watch benevolently.

After ten years, you had, or so you thought, rather perfected the art.

Tonight, however, if anyone had dared look in your eyes, you were certain they would have been able to see the currents of concern. Of course, the only one who could, currently in the Palace of Stars, was the one causing this concern.

Sayo Mdang. Possibly now ensconced in the Primrose Rooms. If you had your magic, you could have stretched it out to test if anyone was there. To reassure yourself that of course, Lord Igel had understood Sayo Mdang was your undersecretary, and nothing more.

Magic, however, could not be the answer. It was buried, chained, curled within a dark corner of your soul. You had spent ten years like this, with the open wound bleeding.

And so, and so. You would have to be patient, and wait for the opportunity to ask the right question, of the right person. Or for the whispers and rumours to swirl so loudly you could hear them even from up here. Eleven ells from everyone.

Your thoughts continued to tumble. You could do nothing, at least now, and so you worried.

Surely someone had explained to Sayo Mdang that you had never chosen anyone. That, presented with any temptation your priest-wizards could find, you had resolutely and politely refused to participate. The priest-wizards must have spread their displeasure when they had realised that expressing it privately had no discernible effect on you (it had effected you, it had made you more resolute).

You had seen the pride and courage in Sayo Mdang's gaze, so rare in Astandalas, and never before directed at you. His certainty in who he was, despite how he was being treated as an object for your assessment. They had not broken him, in all the months of purification, of etiquette lessons. Not even when they forced him to wear his customary dress to make him fit the exotic expectations.

When he had been given nothing more than a desk, and perhaps a single new uniform, was he surprised? Was he embarrassed? Had those looks he had given you in those two days been silent admonitions? You thought not. At the time, it had felt like he was piercing your mask. That he could see that you were pinned here, like a butterfly specimen. *Emperor sp. Artorin, grown in captivity.*

If he was now in the Primrose Rooms, was he angry? Did he think you had walked past him for a week, and decided that yes, you would have this one after all? Was he waiting to be summoned to your bedroom by your attendants or guard? You felt light-headed. Were your guards and attendants waiting as well?

When you had caught him in the library, what had he thought you were there for? For the document? Or for Sayo Mdang? You had stepped into the room with him. Had you missed any signs of fear, or disgust? You thought not. You had thought he was uncertain. Embarrassed to have the document you needed. Worried that he had gone where he should have not.

But, you had not looked too closely. You had wanted to make him feel more at ease (make yourself feel more at ease). You might have missed some clue, some tiny tell. That he believed the relationship had changed, that the appointment as undersecretary was make-work, and his main service was now expected elsewhere.

Did Sayo Mdang have a partner, a lover, a spouse, somewhere? In the Palace even? Did they know? Had he written them a note, a letter? Oh, you could hardly imagine how anyone could write such a thing. To try to say that they would be untouchable, almost as untouchable as you. That if Sayo Mdang spent any time alone with them, even if only to try to explain what had happened in private, it would be automatically considered adultery. And so treason.

Should you hope that Sayo Mdang had no one like that, so that his heart might not be broken? Or that he did have someone, and that he could hold on to that flame? Tend that light and warmth against the dark of Astandalas, called golden.

You should send him home. Surely it would be better to find some role, some position, in the Wide Seas. He was trained in the Service, those skills would be transferable. Perhaps he could be put in charge of tithes for the province, or given a senior position in the household of the Prince. Perhaps that would be enough protection to not be haunted by the disgrace of being sent away.

What you wanted, more than anything, was to be able to ask him. To ask, and to be sure that you were being given an honest answer. You could believe that he might genuinely want to stay. That having worked hard to get into the Service from the very edge of the Empire, he would be willing to endure the firestorm of rumours, knowing they could not last forever. (You could more easily believe that he would want to leave.)

But you could *not* believe that he would be able to answer honestly. And so, you could not ask. It would be cruelty on top of cruelty to be asked a question where you knew you could not tell the truth. Where you must, perhaps on pain of death, lie through your teeth, and offer your own body as a tithe to the Emperor.

It was disgusting. You were disgusting. You had sat so long at the centre of this web of corruption and cruelty. Your body shone like a light, but your soul was tarnished, your heart was beaten down into submission. Perhaps doing better than Eritanyr, Anyoë, or Lodyr, but still utterly failing.

There must have been some way to prevent this.

You sat there, serene, golden, luminous, consumed by guilt and fear.
