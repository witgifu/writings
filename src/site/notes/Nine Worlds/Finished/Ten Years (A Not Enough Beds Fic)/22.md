Kip finished writing up a couple of memos. That done, he separated his notes into those things he would need to pass to the undersecretary and those he could handle himself. Pleased with his afternoon of work, he chatted briefly with his fellow secretary, before heading back to his rooms.

Lord Conju was waiting there for him. Still dressed as he had been to petition the Emperor. When Kip came in, he let out a huff of breath, as if he had been worried it might be someone else.

“Thank you, Lord Conju,” said Kip. He wanted to greet Lord Conju like he would his friends back home, if they had just won a competition at a festival. But knew that was wildly inappropriate. He did his best attempt at the correct court bow and gesture instead.

Lord Conju nodded, and didn't correct his form. So it was either passable, or Conju was too tense to notice it was wrong.

“I hope,” said Lord Conju, “that I never have to do that again. Also, I suspect I just made Lord Igel into an enemy, so that will be interesting.”

Kip paused, one hand holding a sheaf of notes. “Oh,” he said, “I'm sorry I hadn't thought about that.”

Lord Conju rolled his eyes. “Of course you didn't,” he said tersely, “so it's a good thing I did. Given that I seem to have the favour of the Glorious One through you, I should be fine.”

“You're sure?” asked Kip, still worried. He realised that an enemy like Lord Igel could mean a slow poisoning or quick knife. Even if Lord Igel wasn't directly involved, someone else might do it to curry favour.

“His Radiancy granted you permission to wear the jewels of the _Imperial Consort_, Sayo Mdang,” said Lord Conju, with a sigh, “He can't marry you, but he's doing everything he can up to that.”

“But why,” said Kip, very quietly, gripping the papers tighter, “should the Sun-on-Earth… want me…?”

“I don't know,” said Lord Conju, and then added sarcastically, “Are you particularly talented in bed?”

“We haven't… I'm not… I told you…” said Kip, rather incoherently. He dropped the papers onto the desk, spoiling their careful organisation. Shaking slightly, he gripped hard on the edge of the desk, and fumbled to pull out the chair. “What does he want, Lord Conju?”

“What has he told you?” asked Lord Conju, finally starting to relax into his chair.

Kip closed his eyes, trying to remember the exact words.

“When the Glorious One first told me he would appoint me as Hands of the Emperor, he said, 'We would place on you no obligation. If you would stay, we would ask nothing more of your service to us than to continue as our secretary. We wish our court to know you have our protection, esteem, and favour.'”

Lord Conju made a sort of high-pitched squeak.

Kip opened his eyes to see Lord Conju sitting forward and tense again.

“Is that… a problem, Lord Conju?” asked Kip, suddenly worried. “I thought he was just trying to say that he wasn't going to ask me to be the usual sort of concubine. The training was interesting, from an anthropological perspective, although given the history I hadn't expected to need to put it into practice. Not that he's asked that, or even hinted, quite the opposite. I had wondered if maybe he had an issue, they did cover that a bit in the training, you know Eritanyr was sometimes too drunk, but they taught us about stimulants—” Kip shut his mouth, suddenly realising he'd been babbling.

“I'm glad they taught you _something_, even if they apparently didn't bother teaching you court manners and language,” said Lord Conju, who seemed to have regained his composure, “The Glorious One seems to be *courting* you, Sayo Mdang.”

Kip sat there for a moment, blinking. “But, I'm engaged,” he said finally.

“You are WHAT?” said Lord Conju, “To whom? Why didn't you talk to them when you were selected?!” He seemed suddenly incredibly angry.

“Ghilly, back home, we dated in uni—” said Kip, anxiously.

“Did you run away to Astandalas and not tell her? This is serious! What have you done?” said Conju, his voice rising. He was gripping the chair with his hands like he was going to break it, his knuckles white.

“No!” said Kip, trying to explain, “She knew I was leaving! I told everyone. Ghilly agreed to wait until I came back, I…”

Conju took a deep breath. He closed his eyes, slowly calming his face and relaxing his body. “You aren't going back, Sayo Mdang,” he said eventually, his voice cold.

“I know,” said Kip, very quietly. “It didn't seem right to put it in a letter.”

Conju huffed. “True, plus your mail is no doubt being watched. You could be accused of treason for not breaking it off when you were first selected.”

“I haven't told any of my family or friends,” said Kip, looking at the floor, “They wouldn't understand. They would come here and try to bring me back.”

Both Lord Conju and Kip sat in silence for a while. Kip, wondering if he would ever be able to tell people back home what had happened. Would he just ignore their questions of when he was coming home? Would it be safer for them if he just stopped writing altogether? Or would they then be determined to come and find him?

Perhaps he could write, and tell them that he would not be able to come home for many years, if ever. It was going to be painful to write. To give up his dreams of a home and family with Ghilly, of dancing at the greater Singing of the Waters, of being taná.

He had looked at that portrait of the Emperor, in Saya Dorn's house, and thought their fates were intertwined. He had not understood what he would have to give up for the Emperor. Would he have chosen differently, if he had this foresight?

Now, he realised how small this cage was. That if he lost the favour of the Emperor, for any reason, at best he would be imprisoned in a monastery. The Emperor had offered him a way out twice. Should he have taken it? It would have meant crushing different dreams, going home in disgrace.

It was too late, now. It didn't matter.

“I'll write,” said Kip eventually, “And tell Ghilly, and my mother and sister, that I'm not coming home. And that I'm sorry I have to tell them by letter.”

Lord Conju nodded. “I will ask around and see if I can find some suitable candidates to be your valet. Try to keep out of sight of Lord Igel, please, Sayo Mdang.”

“Yes, Lord Conju,” said Kip, thinking that shouldn't be too hard. Lord Igel seemed to want as little to do with him as possible, even before today.

Kip finished the letter to Ghilly, wishing he had better words, more words, truer words. But it was late, past midnight, and he should be in bed. Perhaps tomorrow he would find the words.

He slowly drifted into anxious dreams, where his mother was annoyed at him for bowing incorrectly to a giant parrot who moved into his old bedroom.

He woke, again, to a tapping at his door. It was one of the attendants from last night. “Hello, Lord….?” said Kip, adjusting the belt on his dressing gown. He joined the young man in the hallway.

“Sir Lirianote,” said the young man, “I'm only a baronet, my lord.”

Kip stumbled slightly at the return address. Trying to ignore it, he asked, “You couldn't wake the Glorious One?”

Sir Lirianote blanched, “My apologies, my lord, you said to fetch you if the Glorious One became distressed. We have not tried waking him.”

“Thank you, Sir Lirianote,” said Kip, realising his slip up, “I did, and I would rather be called on right away.” They hurried together down the stairs. Kip needed to be more careful about what he said. Even implying a mistake could be seen as a judgement. He was lucky nobody was around to overhear.

They reached the doors to the Imperial Bedchamber. The guards opened one of the doors, and once again Kip slipped in. The second attendant was standing beside the bed, ringing his hands in the dim light.

Kip bowed deeply to the bed, and then moved over to the worried attendants. He nodded to them both, but said nothing. The bed curtains were closed. Kip could hear soft sobs coming from behind them.

“Do you want the medication, my lord?” whispered Sir Lirianote.

Kip shook his head. If he could avoid drugging the Glorious One, he would. The Emperor certainly seemed to be in no danger from simply crying.

Walking as quietly as possible, Kip approached the bed, and then peeked through the curtains. The Emperor was lying on his side. He looked to be sleeping normally, except for the sobbing.

Kip slipped behind the curtains, and shedding his slippers, climbed up onto the bed. He shuffled forwards slowly on his knees. When he reached the Emperor's side, he began to hum softly.

The Emperor's eyes flew open.

Kip froze. He should drop into the obeisance, or full prostration, but there was not enough space. He would have to move backwards. He bowed his head as low as he could in the position he was in.

“Cliopher,” whispered the Emperor, a breath only audible because Kip was so close, his head bowed down. “Cliopher, I am so very sorry. I did not want any of this. I am so scared.”

“It was just a dream, Glorious One,” whispered Kip in return, “Everything is fine.”

The Emperor closed his eyes tightly, in a look of intense pain. Without thinking clearly, Kip began humming the lullaby again. He reached up a hand, and stroked the Emperor's arm.

The Emperor's eyes flew open wide again, looking at Kip's hand. Kip wondered belatedly if the touch, such a small thing for anyone else, was unwelcome. But the Emperor didn't ask him to stop, or move away. So Kip kept humming and stroking.

Tears began to leak from the Emperor's eyes. Now that he was awake, he made no sound as he cried. Kip wondered how often he cried, alone, behind these curtains. Worried that if he made a sound, he would frighten his attendants, or be drugged to sleep. The Emperor of five worlds, who could have anyone executed on a whim, crying silently in his bed.

“I am sorry, Cliopher,” whispered the Emperor, as tears streamed down his face. “For waking you up. For trapping you here. And now they will try to force more people to be purified. And the nobles will abuse their staff and say they are copying me. I am so sorry I couldn't explain. People are always watching and listening, and I dare not trust anyone.”

He finally seemed to run out of words. The Emperor, the Lord of Rising Stars, Protector of the People, Sun-on-Earth, lay there illuminated by his own glory, terrified.

Kip did not stop stroking him. A hug seemed too far, even now. Kip did not know what to say to the Emperor's extraordinary confession. “Nobody?” he whispered.

The Emperor closed his eyes, and took a deep breath. Kip could see him pulling back from his emotions, closing them off, becoming the Emperor he was expected to be. Surprised and worried, Kip grabbed the Emperor's shoulder, tightly.

The touch seemed to shock the Emperor back out. “Only you,” he breathed. 

Kip relaxed his hand, and resumed humming and stroking. Thinking through the problem, trying to ignore that the Sun-on-Earth had just said he trusted Kip alone. What could he offer? 

He could help to improve the civil service. To do audits, root out corruption, find people who could be trusted. Perhaps he could do the same in the Tower? Watch, and listen. His first audit suggested that, apart from Lord Igel, nobody was openly corrupt. Maybe Lord Conju or the future valet could help work out who actually cared.

The issue of their relationship… what tale could they spin? Deep in Kip's heart was the word he treasured, his secret hope. But that was not for now. Nobody at home understood, how much less would the Empire. He needed a story, a pattern, known over all the nine worlds.

He realised he was humming Aurora. The Emperor's face was a mixture of surprise and delight, despite the tears twinkling on his cheeks. The Emperor also knew the banned epic? He didn't seem upset that Kip did. Technically, only the words were treason, but that wasn't a technicality Kip had wanted to test.

“Perhaps, Glorious One,” he whispered, “We could offer a different, better, story.” He took a deep breath. The Emperor had offered Kip his truth, Kip could be brave in turn.

“Where I am Tenebra, come to rescue Aurora, trapped in a tower of nightmares and corruption.”

The Emperor started crying again, but this time his eyes were full of hope. “Thank you, Tenebra,” he breathed. He reached out with his right hand, slowly, as if waiting for Kip to jump backwards. Gently, he placed it on Kip's shoulder. “Thank you for coming to find me.”
