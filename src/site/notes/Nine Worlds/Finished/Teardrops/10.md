You did not see Sayo Mdang at court. Well, you had not told him to attend, you had wanted him to choose. He had chosen not to. Giving people choices meant accepting when they said no. Perhaps another time. It had been exceedingly dull, and you hadn’t even noticed the new piece of music. It must have been quite derivative.

That night, you had been scheduled to have dinner with the Lady Magus of Zunidh. But a landslide had required her attention, and you had this morning excused her (ordered her) to see to her own duties first. So you walked to the small dinning room, expecting to be eating alone.

That would be nice. Your stomach always felt worse when the nightmares bothered you more. Possibly it was a side effect of the medications they insisted you take. It wasn't a question that was worth trying to ask.

If you sent away a dish uneaten, whoever you were eating with was also obliged not to touch theirs. So you had mastered the art of playing with your food and eating just enough tiny fragments to make it look like you had consumed more than you actually had. Then your guests could enjoy the meal. 

Perhaps the kitchen staff weighed the plates as they went out and came back, to check on you. It wouldn't be surprising, you knew your medical team worried that you didn't eat enough. Not that they would say so to your face. Or that you could explain to them that you had low level nausea for every hour since you had been made Emperor.

Ten years, twelve days, twelve hours.

Your guards opened the door to the dinning room. And there, standing by the window, still in his uniform: Sayo Mdang.

You absolutely could not continue to put off talking to Lord Igel. The message you had tried to send him this morning had clearly not been received correctly.

Sayo Mdang looked towards the door as it opened, and saw you framed in the doorway. Your eyes met, and he must have seen in them some of your annoyance at Lord Igel, as you saw a flash of fear across his face. Then it was out of your view, as he lowered himself to the floor.

He rose when you gave the signal, and took a seat when you gestured at the table. It wasn't until the attendant pulled out your chair that you actually looked at the table setting. 

There was only one table, when usually you would have been separated. Both place settings had matching china, glasses, and eating sticks without forks or knives. The only difference you could see between the two place settings was the chairs.

You had paused a moment too long, and cursed yourself internally. Your irritation was with Lord Igel, not any of the attendants here tonight. You assumed your seat gracefully, and signalled for the meal to be served.

They served you both from the same dishes. Your hands wanted to shake, so you carefully gripped them together under the edge of the table. It would be fine. You had spent hours holding Sayo Mdang's hand, and as far as you knew he hadn't had so much as a scratch or a blister from it.

You had never read up on what anyone else had to do to maintain the purifications, once complete. It had not been important; your sister was safely in Damara, and you had carefully moved anyone they purified from your orbit.

Presumably, they were effectively subject to some of the taboos. You would need to find out exactly, and work out what was a requirement and what was Lord Igel throwing his weight around. The Ouranatha would probably not want to tell you, at least not unless you demanded answers. They would want to keep the knowledge as their own power, claiming they had it all well in hand.

There was, however, someone here who *might* answer honestly. Running on a terrible night of rest (how much worse had Sayo Mdang's been?) you would need to be extra careful in how you asked. You had terrified the poor man this morning, and spooked him again just before.

You began with a complement. "We have very much appreciated your work these past few days, Sayo Mdang, you are a most excellent secretary."

Sayo Mdang demurred, but you could see him blushing slightly. He also relaxed a little bit.

The first dish was served, greens sautéed in a garlic oil. Reasonably confident that eating sticks are unusual on Zunidh, you complemented Sayo Mdang's dexterity with them while eating the oily, slippery, stems.

"I learnt up the skill here, Glorious One," he said, "The priest-wizards were most insistent, they had us practice with glass marbles."

"That sounds very difficult," you offered, hoping Sayo Mdang would expand.

"We treated it as a game, Glorious One," he said, with a small smile, "It was fun trying to compete for a bowl of marbles. It was more fun than practicing bows, or the theoretical classes."

"Oh yes," you said, desperately wishing you dared to ask a question directly, "The priest-wizards are very dedicated to their theory. I would imagine it could make for some dry lectures."

"The geomancy expert was interesting, and he did the only explanation of impurity transfer that made sense to me. But then, I have no magic, Glorious One. I'm sure the relations of powers and stars made sense to someone who had magic or understood Old Shaian."

Sayo Mdang had finished his greens. As you were focused more on conversation than food, you were surprised to see you had eaten at least half. You paused, and put your eating sticks down, the signal to serve the next course.

"We have studied the philosophy of magic and Old Shaian; it doesn't get much more logical if one does understand. It remains mostly memorisation. There is some logic, such as cooking purifying foods. But for example, a new pair of eating sticks would take the longer purification rite if they were metal, the shorter if they are entirely gold or silver. If they are wooden, it is better that they are destroyed after use."

We both looked more closely at the eating sticks. It would be impractical for them to be solid gold, of course, but yours did appear to at least be covered in gold plate. Underneath they were perhaps hollow silver. Sayo Mdang's appeared to be silver.

"Wood has to be destroyed after use, Glorious One?" asked Sayo Mdang, tentatively. It sounded like he had something in specific in mind, and you wondered what it might be.

"Only if it has entered the mouth, and it is on a specific list, which includes eating sticks. If it included all wooden objects, we would be replacing a lot of chairs," you said, and tried a small smile to invite him into the joke.

The next course, noodles in a peanut sauce, was served. Sayo Mdang twisted the eating sticks in his fingers. You could see he wanted to ask another question. Eventually, he gave in.

"Woodwind instruments, Glorious One? Would they be destroyed? That also seems very wasteful." He bit his lip, presumably to stop himself from making further comment. He did not look you in the eye, but applied himself firmly to the noodles.

You considered the question carefully, mentally running through the lists. "A silver or gold flute would be fine, of course. A clarinet or a recorder might be a problem. For an oboe or bassoon it might be possible, since they have a replaceable double reed... they are none of them mentioned explicitly."

The resignation was clear in Sayo Mdang's eyes. They had probably lazily said he would have to give up almost everything they personally didn't see any value in. What prized instrument had the Ouranatha taken off him? You shouldn't be surprised, but you were.

You had managed to acquire a small harp for your private study. It wasn't *my* harp, but it was *a* harp, and it was pleasant to hold it and practice trills. I didn't dare play any of my own music, where the sound might carry, but I could at least play something. Even if your attendants were careful to treat away any sign of rough skin afterwards.

Sayo Mdang offered no further comment. 

"Those rules should only apply to the Emperor, surely," I said, thinking it through, "Anyone else should be able to use it once purified initially. It would only be if the Emperor played it that it would have to be destroyed." It was awkward, referring to yourself in the third-person, but at least it was clearer than saying 'we'.

He raised his head, and his eyes lit up. I was relieved that I had found the right answer. Hopefully they had not destroyed his instrument (surely only someone who never played would do such a thing). Maybe it had been locked away, or given to a friend for safe keeping.

After all, he could hardly have missed noticing that everyone else purified by the priest-wizards had been sent away with gifts and endowments. He probably expected to retrieve his instrument. You really had ruined his life. It was the least you could do to make his imprisonment with you as pleasant as possible. You might have to risk a direct question.

"We used to play the harp. Do you play an instrument, Sayo Mdang?" you asked, signalling to remove the noodles. They were replaced with tiny cubes of rice, each topped with a square of sweet and sticky grilled eel.

"The oboe, Glorious One, but not very well. I'm afraid I never put in sufficient time to practice," he said, awkwardly, pushing the cubes around somewhat randomly, "Do you not play the harp any more, Glorious One?"

Ah. You had used the past tense, and Sayo Mdang had caught you. "It is considered by some to be below the dignity of the Emperor to play music. We may only patronise others."

You could not say how much you loved music, how much you needed it. How you despaired of the dull and predictable works your court produced. That you preferred the adorably enthusiastic children's choir to the technically perfect but soulless performances you were usually surrounded by.

There was pity on Sayo Mdang's wonderfully transparent face, as he looked you in the eyes again. He could see through the bars of your gilded cage, behind your habitual mask. If only— 

You ate a cube absently, and considered that perhaps you were approaching the problem of the taboos the wrong way. Obviously, Sayo Mdang had not been given clear instructions, and was having to trust Lord Igel. As your secretary, however, he should have full access to the archives and libraries. You could simply ask him to write a report that would need to include the relevant information.

Dessert was spent in companionable silence, as you mentally drafted possible research questions.
