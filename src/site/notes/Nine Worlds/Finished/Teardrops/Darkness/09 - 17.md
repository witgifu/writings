Kip rushed furiously through all the work he needed to do. If he was to be having dinner with his lord, he couldn't trust that he would have any more time before tomorrow morning. 

When he had done everything he could at his desk, he hurried into his bedroom and threw on a clean uniform. Then he headed down to the pages and undersecretary to hand out and collect work. Thankfully, while he wasn't a great analyst, the undersecretary had excellent handwriting. He could be trusted to write up anything Kip didn't have the time for. His summaries were also acceptable.

As Kip thumbed through a document in the little library, he hoped that 'new staff' would include an analyst, as well as a researcher. Then he found the statistic he needed, and walked briskly back up to his rooms.

Thankfully, Lady Ylette didn't seem to mind working around Kip's desire to read reports while she fitted clothes on him. Kip was slightly pleased he was allowed to be distracted, he didn't really want to look too closely at the fine linens and silks she was working with.

The afternoon went by far too quickly. Kip put down the last report reluctantly when he finished it.

“Is now a good time for explanations, Lord Conju?” he asked, as he stood up and stretched.

“Of course, Lord Cliopher, where would you like to start?” replied Conju, smiling politely.

Kip surveyed the room. Lady Ylette had departed, taking most of her work with her. She had left behind some finished pieces, mostly in looser styles that she had been able to run up quickly. Kip suspected there was also some magic involved.

Kip tried to think of what was at the root of all the questions he had. Why were the Tower staff treating him differently? Why was he expected to treat _them_ differently? What was the significance of still being in the imperial bed when the Emperor left it? Was Lord Igel in trouble? If so, would Lord Igel being in trouble spell trouble for Kip? Who would assign him staff? What kind of staff?

Eventually, Kip sat down opposite Lord Conju. As he did, he realised he had done the same move of his robes that Conju had taught him to do when sitting at his desk. Would that change as well?

“What has changed, what will change, and why?” said Kip.

“Oh, I see, we're starting with the easy questions,” replied Conju, deadpan. 

Kip smiled back. When he said nothing more, Conju smiled slightly. “Let's take it in chronological order then, shall we Lord Cliopher?”

“Thank you, Lord Conju,” replied Kip, now grinning.

Conju rolled his eyes. “It's all about perception. You spent an entire evening, intimately sequestered with his Radiancy, and managed to convince him to eat more than he usually would.”

With difficulty, Kip refrained from interrupting to correct the other man. He'd already told Conju what had happened. There was no point debating people who weren't even in the room.

“Then he took you to bed. You spent an entire night with him, and when the morning rituals began, he allowed you to be seen. Before that, you were a public secret. If you had been dismissed yesterday, you could have been quickly forgotten. It was assumed you had no influence. Now, you are no longer secret, and are assumed to have _significant_ influence.”

“And so I can't have breakfast in the kitchen?” asked Kip. He understood that there might be political ramifications. Although privately he thought that he had more power at the secretary's desk than in his lord's bed.

“Now the staff want to _court_ your good opinion. They want to be sure you speak well of them to his Radiancy. If, say, you speak well of someone who Lord Igel had given negative report, who would the Emperor believe?”

Kip huffed. “If they want my good opinion, they should let me eat in the kitchen. It's faster, and less wasteful.”

Conju actually let out a bark of laughter. “They also don't want you listening in on their gossip.”

A big sigh was the only complaint Kip allowed himself about that. He liked the gossip. It had made him feel like he was part of a workplace, or a home.

“That was prior to lunch. With lunch, things have changed again. Not only were you invited to a private meal with the Emperor _on the same day_, the Emperor stormed out of that lunch to berate his Groom of the Chamber. That marks you as _more_ powerful than Lord Igel.”

Kip quietly doubted this assertion. Lord Igel was still in charge of the Emperor's schedule, almost all of his staff, even his menus. If Lord Igel resented Kip for this, if he saw his star falling because Kip's had risen, he could be dangerous.

“Then, the Emperor appointed you a household, a wardrobe budget as part of his, and gave you access to the Treasury. Eritanyr never did anything like that for a concubine, they were firmly under the management of the Groom of the Chamber and his wife. Not even his *consort* had access to the Treasury, she had to apply to him to have anything taken out.”

“Oh,” said Kip, very quietly. Had his lord intended this? He must have known, surely?

"Quite. What happens next enters the realm of speculation. It is _expected_ that the Emperor will grant you to a senior Imperial title. Duke, or possibly Prince.”

Kip sank into the chair. What would he tell Basil? What would he tell his _mother_?

“Power without rank makes people nervous, Lord Cliopher. That's why all the staff are starting at you, why no one knows how to address you. Most of them probably haven't called anyone _sir_ since they left the control of their tutors.”

“The Emperor still called me Sayo Mdang, not sir or lord or anything else.” said Kip, quietly. Then he added, “Except once, last night, and once at lunch, when he called me Cliopher.”

Conju rubbed his temple with his hand. “That is the kind of thing that is very important that you tell me. Who heard?”

Thinking back, Kip wasn't sure exactly. “The first time, I think nobody? Maybe the guards, but the Glorious One was whispering. The second time it was the guards and the staff serving lunch. Possibly more people outside the room. Sorry, Lord Conju.”

Conju took a deep breath. “Do you know his Radiancy doesn't even call his own sister by her first name? He addresses her as 'your grace'. He called his mother 'your highness'. As far as I know, he has never called anyone by their first name.”

The bells rang for six o'clock. A formal dinner would start at eight, a less formal one at seven.

Lord Conju quietly swore, the first time Kip had ever heard him do so. “Let us continue this conversation while you get dressed, Lord Cliopher,” said Conju, standing up.

There were a surprising number of layers. A pure white tunic that reached from his collarbone to his ankles, with long white sleeves. A shorter dark magenta tunic went over it, with three-quarter sleeves. Finally, a long and sleeveless crimson vest. The vest had a single closure at the front, just below his bellybutton, and slits up the side that reached his waist. The tunics, being looser, billowed out through the slits and gaps in the vest.

While Conju was showing Kip how it was supposed to be arranged, he explained further. “The first appointees to your household should arrive tomorrow, and then I can go back to the position I was appointed to, rather than trying to do _everything_ for you,” he said acerbically.

“Sorry, Lord Conju,” replied Kip, tugging on the back of the vest.

“Not your fault darling,” replied Conju, lightly, “Your new wardrobe will take some time. Garments like these can be made up quickly, but it could take months to get the embroidery done if you needed a formal Varoonan waistcoat.”

Kip looked slightly startled. Of course such things were made for the Emperor. It felt strangely dissonant that someone might do the same for him.

“I have seen the Treasury catalogue kept in the Tower,” continue Conju, “Of course it will need your seal on the request to actually get any of it sent up, and you don't have a seal yet. We'll get to that. For now, we can work with the things I found for you earlier.”

Letting out a small sigh of relief, Kip smiled. He didn't want to think what kinds of ridiculous jewels were kept in the Treasury. From Baru Tovo, he knew that the largest flame pearls formed a part of the tithe given by the Wide Seas.

Conju put Kip in the same jewellery as the night before, and the same cosmetics. “We don't want you to look too different, dear, the clothes are enough for now. His Radiancy will know his orders have been followed, but you will still look like _you_.”

Quietly, Kip didn't think he looked at all like himself. But he didn't say anything. His lord had asked for this, Conju had chosen it. It would be rude to imply either of them had done anything other than excellent work.

“Who is being appointed to my household? What even _is_ a household?” asked Kip, now slightly worried he would wake up to a group of strangers looming over him.

“Three personal attendants,” said Conju, with a small smile, “—you don't need to look so scared, Lord Cliopher, they work in shifts—a footman, a household manager, a wardrobe manager, and a private secretary. We'll see how that goes, you might need more.”

Conju held up a new pair of sandals. The leather had been dyed red, and they had little coppery studs that glinted. Kip reached out to take them, but Conju swatted his hand away, and put them on for him. This made Kip feel rather like a small toddler, who didn't yet know how to do up a buckle.

“And they're all coming tomorrow?” said Kip, not at all reassured.

“The personal attendants I pulled from the lower ranks of Lord Igel's staff, so you should have at least seen them before. The first will arrive at eight tomorrow morning, which was as quickly as we could get the rosters adjusted.”

Kip did feel a bit of relief at that. In his audit he had at least very briefly seen everyone. And arriving at eight meant he would already be up and finishing the morning reports before breakfast.

The sandals were stiff. Well, that was always true of new sandals. Kip hoped he would be allowed to wear his old comfortable ones sometimes.

“Lady Ylette had kindly agreed to be your wardrobe manager,” added Conju, putting away Kip's uniform.

Kip smiled genuinely at that. Lady Ylette seemed much less formal and stiff than many of the senior members of the Emperor's household. She had laughed at his joke, and seemed confident and competent.

“It seems odd,” ventured Kip, “for me to have a private secretary when I _am_ a private secretary?”

Lord Conju waved this concern away. “Firstly, you'll need someone to manage all the tedious mail you're going to get. Not personal mail, just people who want to make themselves important. I'm sure you've seen the same come to the Emperor.”

Indeed, Kip had. He knew most of them were filtered by the Palace staff and sent form responses. They couldn't do that with the nobles though, so Kip got those. In the first week he had noted the replies the Emperor dictated to them, and started preparing blank versions he could offer the Glorious One after reading the letter.

“Secondly, you are wearing that poor undersecretary and the pages out. They need to sleep and eat too, you know.”

A wave of guilt flooded over Kip. In his rush to take as much as he could off the shoulders of the Emperor, he'd been throwing a large part of it on others. Partially that was because he couldn't leave the Tower. But it was very wrong of him to exploit them like that.

He crumpled onto a chair. “Sorry,” he said, miserably.

“I will find some people for you to interview tomorrow. If you need more than one, that's fine. Look at me, Lord Cliopher,” said Conju, suddenly stern.

As instructed, Kip looked up. Conju was smiling slightly, not frowning.

“I spoke to your undersecretary, Lord Rial, and honestly he's been watching you far too closely. He thinks you're absolutely splendid, and asked if there was more he could do to help. The pages are rotated regularly, and work in shifts anyway.”

Kip let out a slow, deep breath. Lord Conju had been partially truthful, and part teasing. Kip would be okay, Lord Rial would be okay, they had a plan to fix it. It hadn't got too out of hand yet.

“Now,” said Lord Conju, when Kip finally looked back with a smile, “Let's see you do your obeisance in the new robes, and then do some more conversation practice until it's time for you to go downstairs.”
