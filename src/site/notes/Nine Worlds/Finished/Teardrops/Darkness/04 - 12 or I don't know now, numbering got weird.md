Kip had maybe, in the end, managed to please his Emperor. He could only hope, as he walked back to his rooms, that the Emperor would take seriously his request for training. He did not want to continue this trial-and-error approach. Even if it seemed errors might not have him immediately dragged to his execution, they were still unpleasant.

At his desk, the next day, he had a pleasant time taking dictation for various replies, requests, and letters of all kinds. As the morning came to a close, and Kip was mentally planning how he would spend his afternoon, the Emperor stopped pacing. Kip looked up, carefully, only to the Emperor's hands again.

“We think it would be productive for you to learn more of the workings of our household, Sayo Mdang,” said the Emperor, his voice calm and even, “To that end, please audit the finances.”

“Of course, Glorious One, it would be an honour,” replied Kip, with as much of a bow as he could from his seat.

The Emperor moved to leave, and Kip slipped out of his chair, and performed his obeisance unnoticed behind him. He wasn't sure if this was a bad sign, or simply that the Emperor was very busy today. Kip tried to keep his spirits up by dividing his notes for the undersecretary, and thinking about how he would approach the audit.

He would have to start with Lord Igel.

Lord Igel was to be found, according to the kitchen staff he asked nervously, in a small room off the Emperor's dressing room. Kip approached the open door cautiously, and saw Lord Igel carefully examining a tray of tiny jewels.

As he arrived at the doorway, Lord Igel looked up. Kip bowed, and waited to be addressed.

“Yes?” said Lord Igel, with a sigh.

“My lord,” said Kip, not making eye contact, and trying his best to be respectful, “the Glorious One has requested that I become more familiar with the working of his Radiancy's household by auditing the tower finances.”

There was an ominous silence. Kip wondered if he should do something, perhaps apologise, or run away and pretend he'd never said anything.

“Very well,” said Lord Igel, “the ledgers are maintained by the clerk, downstairs next to the seamstresses.”

Kip bowed again, with a mumbled, “Thank you, my lord.” He made a hasty exit.

As promised, the clerk was indeed to be found behind the unlabelled door next to the one marked “seamstress”. Since the door was shut, Kip knocked. It was opened by a harassed looking woman in the robes of the upper secretariat.

Kip bowed, (he had the feeling he was going to be doing a lot of that during this audit) and said, “Excuse me, my lady, would you be the clerk?”

“Yes,” she said testily, “Who are you and what do you need?”

“I am Cliopher Mdang, my lady—” he began, but she interrupted him.

“Oh yes, his Radiancy's new pet. I'm Lady Luccia. What has he asked you to do?” She didn't roll her eyes, but Kip suspected that was only because it would have been considered gauche.

“I have been asked to audit the tower finances, my lady. His Radiancy believes it will be educational.”

“Be my guest,” she said, with a smirk. She opened the door further, revealing a small office, with floor to ceiling shelves on every side. In the middle was a desk, with a mage light. There was one chair, which was clearly the clerk's, and a small step-ladder to reach the higher shelves.

“This year's logbook is on the table,” she said, with a clearly false smile, “I'm very busy, so I won't have time to help you. No records may be removed from this room.”

“Of course, my lady, thank you,” replied Kip, with another bow. He thought there was a good chance she believed he would give up quickly. She didn't know Kip's backup plan had been to become an accountant.

Kip opened the logbook to the start of the year, and scanned down the page. Every receipt, every order, every delivery, was logged. It was beautiful, and he suddenly had a lot more respect for Lady Luccia. Well, provided it matched the filed documents anyway.

He spent a very happy afternoon comparing a random selection of the logbook records to their filed counterparts, and then a random week's individual records to the account totals. If Lady Luccia looked at him oddly for flipping a coin to get a suitable source of randomness, he didn't notice.

It would have been easier with a desk, or at least a chair, of his own. But he made do with the corner of Lady Luccia's desk and the step-ladder. He made notes as he went on what to follow up on.

Lastly, he pulled down the staff pay register. He looked at Lady Luccia out of the corner of his eye to see if she would object. She did look up briefly, but went back to reconciling orders with delivery slips.

It was humbling to see the salaries of the senior household spelt out. And yes, there he was as well. Apparently, acting as the Glorious One's secretary entitled him to the pay of a fifth degree secretary. It was likely, he thought, that this was the least anyone had been paid in that position for a long time. And indeed, yes, the undersecretary was rated higher and paid more.

Well, that was not why he was here. Were concubines paid? He thought they should be, but of course such work was often undervalued. Perhaps the noble ladies kept by Eritanyr had been given a stipend. Class no doubt played a large role.

The payroll did give him a excellent view of the structure and organisation of the Emperor's household. For a start, it was much larger than even he had realised. Many of the positions were shift-work to ensure there was always someone available and alert. Kip drew up his best guess at a chart for the organisation of the household. 

That was all he thought he could learn immediately from the records. “Thank you very much, my lady, your recordkeeping is a delight,” he said with a bow. This earned him a look of approval, although still tempered a little with disdain. Kip collected his notes, and bowed himself out of the room.

Starting at what appeared to be the very bottom of the organisation, Kip began interviewing. He tried his best not to be too much of a distraction or obstruction, and was careful to make sure he never kept anyone back late. 

It was a large organisation. From the junior wizard who purified every incoming item that needed it (which was many), to the apprentice seamstress making nightshirts. The junior staff were all willing to explain what they did. Each level up, people became more haughty and less willing to talk.

Every single one of them at least had a courtesy title. The two middle-aged men washing dishes were both younger sons of minor lords, who had been picked up for their steady hands and sense of magic. In fact, *steady hands* were a noticeable common thread.

Only the Master of the Cloth of Gold no longer had steady hands. Since he didn't work directly in the Presence, he said it was more important that his hands were smooth, to look after all the delicate fabrics.

It was, as the Emperor suggested, very educational. Kip learnt a lot more about the taboos, and the Emperor's known likes and dislikes. Unfortunately for Kip, all the Emperor's known opinions were about fashion.

He wondered if he should talk to someone about expanding his wardrobe. The only clothes he had were the secretary uniforms, and the mocking imitation of Islander finery. Or did the Emperor prefer his uniform? If he was very particular about fashion, perhaps that included wanting to see Kip in his uniform.

There was only one person on the payroll he hadn't been able to find. That was Lord Igel's son. Nobody could remember working with him, although a few people had seen him in the Imperial Apartments on occasion with his father. He did _not_ want to bring this up with Lord Igel, and so he resolved to simply note it in his report conclusions.

It took the better part of two weeks to interview everyone, verify records in the archives, and write up the final report. He kept it brief, after all the point of the exercise had been in the work, not the results. Kip slipped it in the middle of the next set of morning reports after it was finished.

In those two weeks, Kip had not been summoned to attend the Emperor at all. Their time together had consisted solely of his scheduled secretarial duties. Kip tried to imagine this was because the Emperor was well, and otherwise busy. But knowing now the opinions of so many of the Tower staff, that he was simply an idle fancy of the Emperor that would be put aside soon, he worried.

He was still trying to be as correct as he knew how. Not making eye contact, trying to copy the court accent, suppressing his urges to make suggestions or ask questions. Trying to anticipate anything the Glorious One might ask of him.

It was, then, very pleasing to Kip that the Emperor chose to mention the report on the same day it was delivered.

“We have read your report, Sayo Mdang, it was very thorough. Write a note to the appropriate person about the candles. We will handle the other conclusions with the appropriate parties.”

Kip ducked his head to hide his smile at the complement. “Of course, Glorious One,” was his only reply. No doubt, the wizard would be happy to have one less thing in his “urgent” inbox.

Four more days passed, and Kip began to wonder again if he had indeed fallen from his Emperor's favour. He did not know how he could approach him, if he even could. The Glorious One had said he would think about what Kip had said, what Kip had offered. But he had said that in the semblance of privacy that was the closed curtains of the Imperial Bed. Perhaps the answer was not possible to give elsewhere.

Kip did consider, briefly, trying to hide in the Imperial Bed. But he suspected that was the way to get on the wrong side of the guards, if not the Emperor, and get a quick trip out of the Apartments and this life. 

A few days after he had delivered the report, Kip began spending more time in the hallways. It was, perhaps, slightly subconscious, and slightly not. He would think of something he needed to check in the library, or a note he needed to leave for the pages, at exactly the time he thought the Emperor might be in the corridors.

Kip wasn't sure if the Emperor noticed him. He was always careful to drop into the obeisance if the Lord of Rising Stars did happen to pass him. Once he noticed Lord Igel giving him an assessing look, but that could have been for any number of reasons. All of which only added to Kip's worries.

Four days later, at the end of a session of listening to petitions from various nobles, the Emperor turned to Kip.

“We understand that you have not left the Tower since being admitted, Sayo Mdang?” he asked, perfectly neutral and serene.

Kip swallowed any number of explanations or protests. Was this a request that he remove himself? Would the Emperor send him away? He decided to be honest, but short. “Yes, Glorious One. The priest-wizards were very explicit.”

The Emperor began pacing. “It is not required to maintain the purification. Even we leave, to attend court, and rituals. You may leave if you wish. But… for your safety…” 

There was a pause, and Kip's first thought was that the Emperor was going to suggest not wandering the halls of the Imperial Apartments. Of course, the Glorious One had noticed. Although, surely they should be _safe_. It might be improper for Kip to be treating the Imperial Apartments like his home, rather than his workplace. But surely they were safe?

Continuing, the Emperor added, “It would best if you had a guard as escort. And if you were presented at court first, so that our courtiers know you have our favour.” The Emperor stopped pacing briefly, and breathed slowly. He kept his face averted, and Kip couldn't see hear any emotion in his voice.

That sounded like the Emperor intended to make Kip's position public and permanent. Kip was confused, but recognised the Emperor was paying him a great honour. He slipped from his chair, and fell into the full formal prostration.

“Your humble servant can offer no thanks equal to such a gift and blessing, most Glorious and Generous Lord,” he said, knowing that he should properly give a short speech of adoration. But he was conscious that the Emperor had a busy schedule, and that he was not a great orator.

The Emperor gestured for him to rise, and Kip did so. He moved to return to his seat, but the Emperor continued before he sat down.

“Then it might also be helpful if you had a companion.”

One hand on his chair, Kip was intensely aware that he was not _supposed_ to ask questions of the Lord Emperor. And yet, he could not stop himself. “A friend, Glorious One?” he asked.

“Not necessarily, although we would be pleased to hear if you had made friends. A companion would be someone of similar age and rank, who can go where you do. In your case, Sayo Mdang, they would be able to help with court etiquette. We know ourselves that it is peculiar and strange on first encounter.”

Was this the answer to his request for remedial instruction? Perhaps. Or at least, it might provide the instruction the Emperor thought that Kip needed. He could also see the wisdom in having someone who could also go to court, or whatever other public places the Emperor required him to go.

“Thank you again, Glorious One, for your most kind consideration” he answered. 

The Emperor nodded. “Lord Igel has a candidate. He will arrange a meeting, and you can let him know if the candidate is acceptable or not.”

“Thank you, Glorious One,” replied Kip, wondering what kind of person this would be, and what he might have to teach Kip.
