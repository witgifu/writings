Dame Hollis really had done a wonderful job. Kip lightly edited her cover letter, and set the morning reports in the breakfast room. Lord Rial had also finished writing up all the letters and memos. Kip wrote out two draft replies to items in the morning reports, slipping the plain versions in with the report, and the chancery hand version into his own notes.

That was, it transpired, all the work Kip had to do. Could do. It took him less than an hour. The bells rang nine, as he stood up from his desk, lost. Now that he didn't have anything to do, to keep himself busy, the anxiety and guilt came washing back over him.

He took two steps, stopped, with his head in his hands. Why had he made the rash promise to find out who had drugged the Emperor? He had no idea, or he had too many ideas. How would he find proof? It wasn't like they'd leave an audit trail…

Well, perhaps if they were particularly stupid, they had. Would the door to the record office be locked? If someone had ordered the drugs as part of a usual delivery of supplies, it would be recorded there. He walked over to the door, but turned around when Conju politely said, “Excuse me, your highness?”

Kip had just managed to suppress the urge to turn around and look for someone else when Conju said 'your highness'. He would have to get used to it, even though it made his skin crawl.

“I'm sorry, Lord Conju, did you need me for something?” he asked, as he tried to recall if medical orders were filed next to ritual or cleaning supplies.

“I was just wondering, your highness, what your plans were for the rest of the evening?” said Conju, standing by the comfortable chairs. Kip scanned the room, and saw Ser Rhodin using tiny brushes to clean something at the dinning table.

“I wanted to check something in the Tower archive,” said Kip slowly, “Before his Radiancy returns. Then I suppose I should go and wait for his Radiancy.”

“I'm sure Ser Rhodin can fetch what you need from the archive, highness” said Conju, brightly.

Kip smiled back, “Nothing is allowed to be removed from the room. Do you know anything about double-entry bookkeeping, Ser Rhodin?”

Ser Rhodin looked up from his work, blinking, “Sorry, your highness, I know nothing about accounting.”

Kip gave Conju a small shrug. The other man looked less than pleased. “Ser Rhodin, please escort his highness,” he said finally.

Ser Rhodin quickly put down his tools. “Of course, Lord Conju,” he said, and strode over to join Kip. Kip wondered silently if _he_ had been appointed a household, or if _Conju_ had.

As if he had every right to do so, although unsure if he did, Kip strode down to the lower levels of the Tower. Ser Rhodin trailed him silently. They passed three attendants, who all stopped what they were doing to bow to him. It grated on his nerves.

The archive, it transpired, wasn't locked. But that was because Lady Luccia was still there. She looked up, startled, when he opened the door without knocking.

“My apologies, Lady Luccia,” he said, with his best polite smile, “I assumed you would not still be working. I was hoping to reference some recent costs to confirm a budget claim by the Ouranatha.”

Lady Luccia smiled, but she looked tired, “You're still working too, Sayo Mdang?” she asked, but didn't wait for an answer before adding, “Be my guest.”

Ser Rhodin didn't come in, and didn't correct Lady Luccia. Kip appreciated both. He scanned the shelf for the relevant archive box, pulled it down, and flipped through the files. He had to look through each receipt line by line. Most deliveries were what would be reasonable in any home or workplace, like headache powders.

Then he found the receipt with his Radiancy's usual medicine, unhelpfully labelled 'Preparation per Domina P, PRN'. Two other items were on the same docket. The first was a common mild stimulant, often abused by university students. It really wasn't much stronger than three cups of coffee. Below that, the most common erotic stimulant.

Kip flipped over to the purchase order. Yes, there was the authorising signature. Lord Igel. For a moment, he considered stealing the record to make sure nobody else could. But then he changed his mind, and simply re-filed it to the previous month's folder. Incorrect filing of records was bad, but it wasn't as bad as _theft_.

As he closed the box and returned everything to the correct place, Kip considered what he could do with this information. If it had been anyone else's name on the purchase, he was reasonably certain they could be dismissed easily. And it didn't answer the question of who administered the drug. It seemed highly unlikely Lord Igel had tampered with the food himself, someone in the kitchen or serving it was much more likely.

“Thank you, Lady Luccia,” said Kip, “It seems the price truly has risen.” She nodded at him, and he left her to her work.

Kip closed the door behind him, and Ser Rhodin gave him an inquiring look. Kip nodded back, and they returned to Kip's room in silence. He felt anxious the entire way, unsure what he should with the information. Did Lord Igel know he suspected?

Suddenly, Ser Rhodin grabbed his arm, and pulled Kip back around the corner and into a doorway. Rhodin held a finger up to his lips in the universal gesture for quiet. His eyes flicked back to the hall. 

Kip didn't argue. He could hear footsteps and voices.

“Now really isn't a good time, my lord,” said a voice Kip didn't recognise, “The commander—”

“I'll give you a note, don't worry about your commander,” said a voice Kip knew much better, Lord Igel.

“I think he's becoming suspicious of me—” said the first voice again, sounding petulant.

“Then don't act so suspiciously,” snapped Lord Igel.

A door closed behind them, and Kip could no longer discern what they were saying. Kip mouthed a 'thank you' at Ser Rhodin, who nodded, but did not yet release Kip's arm.

Some time later, probably not long, but enough for Kip to become anxious, Ser Rhodin released his arm, and gestured for Kip to continue on. The rest of the walk back to his rooms was uneventful.

“Lord Igel's little conspiracy club is meeting,” said Ser Rhodin, as he and Kip walked into Kip's rooms, “We should keep out of their way, Lord Conju.”

Kip turned to Ser Rhodin, startled. “He has a conspiracy? Do you know who else is in it?”

“I think so, your highness,” replied Ser Rhodin, like this was a perfectly normal thing, “He meets with that guard you heard, the head chef, and two of the attendants.”

“Is Rojan one of them?” asked Lord Conju, his voice calm but his hands twisting.

“Yes, Lord Conju,” said Ser Rhodin, and then paused with a grimace, “and his sometimes boyfriend, Joffrey.”

Conju closed his eyes, and took a deep breath. Then he stood up, and made a bow of first-degree apology to Kip. Who stared at him, completely poleaxed.

“Your highness, I offer my resignation effective immediately. I was recommended by Lord Igel, whose son was married to my previous charge. I have also been sleeping with Rojan,” said Conju, without looking up.

“Don't be ridiculous, Lord Conju,” said Kip, “I already knew Lord Igel recommended you. If you are in his conspiracy I'm so screwed it doesn't matter. I'm not going to blame you for not doing a thorough investigation on your paramores; it _should_ be a reasonable assumption that the Tower staff can be trusted.”

Conju looked up at Kip, with a dampness in his eyes, “Thank you, your highness, I don't deserve such forgiveness.”

“There's nothing to forgive,” said Kip tersely. How could he, of all people, stand in judgment of Conju?

“And don't break it off with Rojan,” said Ser Rhodin, “Try and see if you can find out who he knows outside the Tower, I'm sure there's more people than just those five.”

Kip nodded, and Conju looked between the two of them, baffled. 

“It's probably most important to know who of the guards we can trust,” added Ser Rhodin, and then turned to Kip, “I don't know, highness, what their plans are.”

“Well, Lord Igel signed off on the erotic stimulant order, so that's probably part of it,” said Kip, with a sigh, “But I don't understand why. There are surely easier ways to get rid of me, or control the Emperor.”

They all stood there for a moment, lost in thought.

“We will need to gather more information, highness,” said Ser Rhodin, eventually.

Kip took a deep breath, and closed his eyes. He wished, so hard, that he had better news to share with his Radiancy. “Is there anyone who you think _is_ trustworthy?” he asked, hopefully.

“Lady Luccia, the clerk. The junior priest-wizard who does the purifications. The other staff Lord Conju picked. I will continue looking, highness.”

Conju breathed a sigh of relief when Ser Rhodin mentioned he had picked trustworthy people.

“Is this something else a footman usually does, Ser Rhodin?” asked Kip, curious, “Background checks on all the staff?”

“No, highness,” answered Ser Rhodin, “But gossip collecting sometimes is.”

“Most households,” said Conju, with a sigh, “don't need to worry about a treason conspiracy.”

The bells rang eleven. 

Kip turned to Conju. “What should I wear to greet the Emperor, when he returns from court? He's usually very tired. But I should be there, I don't want him to have to send for me.”

Conju nodded, “Quite correct, your highness. Let's see what we have that might be suitable.”

Conju dressed Kip in a knee-length pure white linen nightgown, with crimson ribbon trim at the sleeves and neckline. Over it went a diaphanous coppery robe, which seemed designed to fulfil only the definition rather than function, of a dressing gown. Azure felt slippers completed the look. No jewellery, and the only cosmetic was the lip balm.

Kip went back to the little drawing room, for the book of poetry. Thinking perhaps his Radiancy might appreciate it. It hadn't occurred to Kip, until he opened the door, that he might not want to go into this room. The book was still resting on the end of the chaise lounge, where he had left it yesterday.

Holding on to the door frame for support, Kip tried to take slow, deep breaths. His heart was racing, and his stomach rolling. Images of the night before flashed into his mind, the sound of his lord's breathing, the smell of sweat.

Kip pressed his thumbnail hard into the palm of the other hand, trying to break himself out of the spiral. He forced himself to turn, leaving the book behind. There were other books, there was a small library here, he did not need that one. He did not need to go into that room.

The library in the Tower had no fiction or poetry in it. It mostly contained books on law, but also some history and natural science. Kip picked a book on botany, seemingly at random, and dove into it. Unwilling to take the chair (surely that was his Radiancy's chair), Kip squatted next to the shelves. Thankfully the shortness of the nightgown made that possible.

Not very long after the midnight bell rang, Kip heard the sound of the guards escorting his Radiancy. He hurriedly returned the book to the correct place, and smoothed out his nightgown and robe. Then he took a deep breath, and walked calmly and slowly to the door to the Emperor's dressing room.

As he had expected, when the Emperor entered the hallway, he looked exhausted. After rising from his obeisance, Kip noticed his lord also looked weak, weighed down by the many layers of fabric, gold, and jewels. All of which would still have to be removed before he could go to bed.

Instinctively, Kip reached out, offering his arm for support. Just in time, he managed to remember to leave space for his lord to decline.

But the Emperor reached back, leaning into Kip as he had two nights ago. Various parts of the costume dug into Kip, but he ignored it. There was a very slight tremor in the hand that gripped his.

How much sleep had his Radiancy missed, these past few days? Too much, Kip was sure. Perhaps Lord Igel would have usually dosed his lord with the stimulants he ordered, but couldn't today. Well, stimulants couldn't make up for sleep, they could only mask the effects, and only for so long.

Kip steadied his lord as he stepped up onto the dressing pedestal. A surreptitious scan of the room showed that Lord Igel wasn't there. Clearly he didn't value time with an Emperor ready to collapse.

The attendants approached, dressing wands in-hand. Kip made a hasty decision. Rather than stepping back, and allowing them to do their work, Kip said softly, “May I help, Glorious One?”

There was a tense moment, and Kip thought perhaps his lord might dismiss him. But the Emperor said clearly, “Yes, Cliopher.”

Smiling up at his lord, Kip began removing the jewellery, and handing it to the attendants. Kip had been taught how to remove many kinds of garments (not, alas, how to put them on), and so he had a good idea of how to disassemble his Radiancy's outfit. Still, he very much appreciated the glances and tiny gestures the attendants made to indicate what they would like him to pass them next. Everything sparkled in the light of his lord's skin.

Whenever he was able, Kip made sure his lord could lean on him, or rest a hand on his shoulder. When his Radiancy was down to just his trews, Kip ignored the attendant clearly wanting them. Instead, he reached for the one holding steaming hot towels. He gently wiped off the cosmetics on his lord's face, as his Radiancy stayed as still and solid as a statue. Kip applied the lotions to his lord's scalp, face, and hands, rubbing them in gently with his fingertips. 

Then he beckoned over the attendant holding his lord's nightgown, and pulled it on. Finally, he reached up under it to remove his lord's trews, and passed them to the attendant who had started to look quite anxious. Clearly, Kip had broken the Order Of Things. Well, Kip saw no reason for his Radiancy to stand around stark naked when he didn't need to.

After what felt like an age, the Emperor was able to step down and into his slippers. Kip helped him out of the dressing room, and across the vast empty bedroom, to the bed. The attendant pulled back the curtains, and folded back the covers.

Once he handed his lord into the bed, Kip expected to be dismissed. The Emperor needed sleep, and he looked likely to do so immediately. But he said softly, “Come, Cliopher,” and dismissed the attendant.

Kip climbed up onto the bed, shedding his slippers, and pulling the curtains closed behind him. He knelt just inside the curtains. Watching his lord, hoping for instructions.

Eventually, his Radiancy beckoned him closer. When Kip had reached his lord's side, the Emperor whispered, “Thank you.”

Not entirely sure what he was being thanked _for_, Kip blushed and ducked his head. “Please, my lord, is there anything else I can do for you?”

“Would you sleep here with me? So they don't drug me,” whispered his Radiancy.

“Of course, my lord,” said Kip, and lay down. His head carefully lower than his lord's, and with enough space between them to fit another person.

“You can come under the blankets, Cliopher,” said his Radiancy, in his usual voice, but perhaps a little sleepily.

Unsure if he should offer thanks or an apology, Kip wriggled around to follow his lord's instructions. He lay there for a few minutes, watching his Radiancy as his breathing slowed, and his lord finally relaxed.
