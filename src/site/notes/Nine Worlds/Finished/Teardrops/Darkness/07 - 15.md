Kip was leaving the kitchen, having finished his breakfast, when Lord Igel found him.

“You will attend his Radiancy tonight in the small drawing room,” said Lord Igel, looking Kip up and down, “be there by six o'clock.”

“Yes, Lord Igel,” replied Kip, wondering if he should bow, and to what degree. Thankfully the other man swept off before Kip had a chance.

With much effort, Kip put all thoughts of the upcoming evening aside, to focus on those things he needed to do before then. He had, for the first time, included a draft response with one of the morning reports. He was feeling nervous enough about that anyway.

In truth, he needn't have worried about the draft letter. When the Emperor came to that topic, he simply said “You may send the reply you included without amendment.”

Kip had a chancery hand version ready, and put it in the tray for the Imperial seal, with those from the previous day. In theory, of course, Kip could hand the Emperor the documents directly without worry. But he did not want to make the Glorious One, or anyone else, uncomfortable.

He took the chance to make eye contact, with just the desk between them. The Emperor seemed more drawn and tired today. Kip hope he hadn't been bothered by nightmares again.

---

After lunch, Kip told Lord Conju the good news. They held a short conference, and then went into a flurry of preparations.

Kip sent a page for a particular book of poetry that Lord Conju recommended. Then he finished all the work he needed to complete that day. Lord Conju quizzed Kip on conversation topics, while brandishing a pair of tweezers over his eyebrows.

Finally, Kip did his ritual wash. When he came out from the bathroom in his dressing gown, Conju had laid the outfit on the bed. One of the uniforms, a clean undershirt, sandals. A different ring, and an anklet with a beautiful red drop gem.

Kip looked across the bed. “Am I out of clean trews?” he asked, noticing the lack.

Lord Conju looked at him with one eyebrow raised, and half a smile.

“Oh,” said Kip, and bit his lip, “Yes of course.” He donned the clothes and jewellery without further comment.

The cosmetics were applied by Lord Conju, who declared that for such an evening he did not want to trust to an amateur. Kip was quietly thankful. Conju also added little brightening powder in the inner corners of his eyes, which was new.

Half an hour before he was due, Conju sent Kip on his way. Book of poetry in hand, he descended the stairs and entered the small drawing room.

Lord Igel had redecorated. There was no longer a desk, or a second chair. The Imperial Yellow chaise lounge took pride of place. To one side was a small table, and on the other side was a low, padded stool.

Kip would honestly have preferred to squat than kneel on the stool, but he understood what was expected. While he was waiting, though, he could do as he pleased. To stop himself from simply fretting, he started reading the book.

The poetry was quite good. It was from Colhélhé, and evocatively captured a sparsely-populated windswept landscape. The experience of the semi-nomadic shepherds was so different from anything he had seen on Zunidh or Ysthar.

Footsteps approached, breaking Kip out of the poem he had been reading. The rhythmic motion of the Imperial Guard. Kip closed the book, placed it on the stool, and stood.

The doors opened, the Emperor entered, and Kip folded into his obeisance. The changes Conju had made were now automatic. There was a slight pause before the Glorious One signalled for him to stand, and for a brief moment Kip was worried he had done something wrong. But then he looked up to meet the beautiful golden eyes.

The Emperor was exhausted. Under the paint on his face, rather than his usual blue-black skin tone, he looked almost green. Without thinking, Kip abandoned all the careful lessons Lord Conju had given him.

“Oh, my lord!” he said softly, and rushed to the Emperor's side. He reached out to steady the Glorious One, who looked in horror at Kip's hands, and flinched.

Kip should have apologised. He should have thrown himself on the floor, and begged forgiveness. He should have backed out of the room, and gone and killed himself in shame (although he had promised not to). He did not.

Hands steady, Kip looked again into the wan face of the Lord of Five Thousand Lands. “My lord, please let me help you,” he said softly. The eyes closed, and the Emperor gave the tiniest of nods.

Kip placed one hand under the Emperor's elbow, and one around his back. He could feel, through the silk robes, that the man inside was far too thin. Gently, he helped him to the lounge, to recline. Kip helped the Emperor lift his legs as well. There was a cashmere throw blanket behind one of the pillows, and Kip pulled it out, and tucked it around the Glorious One.

While he did this, an attendant laid out food and watered wine on the table. A cup of soup, soft bread rolls, steamed fish with a spinach sauce. The attendant had whisked themselves away by the time Kip turned his head to see.

Kip knew he was meant to go to the stool, and wait for the Emperor's request. But the Glorious One was breathing shallowly, with his eyes closed. He squatted by the Emperor and the table.

“Will you have a little water, my lord?” asked Kip, gently.

The lion eyes fluttered open, and Kip lifted the glass. Again, the tiniest of nods. Kip lifted the glass to the Emperor's lips, tipping it gently so he could sip. When the Emperor's head fell back onto the cushions, Kip returned the glass to the table.

Far too slowly, the Emperor's breathing became more normal. Kip watched one perfectly manicured finger twist in the blanket. The lion eyes were still closed.

Kip put to one side thoughts of if the guards would report his impertinent behaviour to Lord Igel. The weak man in front of Kip was only literally radiant, with the light granted by the taboos. While his silk robe was glorious, Kip had held the body beneath.

He tested the temperature of the soup with his pinky finger. It was warm, not hot. 

“Please, my lord, will you have some soup?”

The golden eyes opened slowly. Kip offered the cup with two hands. The Emperor tried to pull himself up a little. Kip carefully put the soup down, and helped his lord sit, arranging the cushions for more support.

Once again Kip offered the soup. The Emperor reached out a hand with a very slight trembling, and then drew it back. His eyes betrayed fear.

“Will you allow me, my lord?” said Kip, and lifted the soup to his lord's face. There was a flutter of eyelids, Kip thought perhaps it was resignation.

As he helped the Emperor to drink the soup, Kip wondered what had happened before he had been available. Had the Lord of Ten Thousand Titles simply collapsed on the lounge, and slept until he had recovered enough to drink and eat? Had he been given a stimulant? If he had, that could be why his lord was so thin.

When the Emperor turned his face from the cup, Kip put it back on the table. It was less than half full. He thought it unlikely a man who had struggled to drink a cup of soup would be wanting bread or fish.

“I could read to you, if you would like, my lord?” Kip suggested. If he had a better singing voice, or his oboe, he would have offered music instead. When the Emperor made no acknowledgement, Kip added, “Or shall I let you rest, Glorious One?”

“What book?” whispered the Emperor, his voice sounding so weak Kip immediately proffered the glass of water again.

As the Emperor sipped, Kip said, “Poems of Colhélhé by Brown, my lord.”

At another tiny nod from his lord, Kip put the water glass down. He stood, and walked around to fetch the book. Kneeling on the stool, he held the book in one hand, open to the first poem.

Slowly, making sure the Emperor could see all the while, he placed his other hand next to his lord's on the arm rest. Palm up, offering to hold hands, if his lord wanted. 

“A boy leaves a small house / of sea light. He leaves / the sea smells, creel / And limpet and cod.” ^

Kip continued, his voice soft, trying to catch the rhythm of the words. They felt like the wind in his hands, and the smell of the sea, but cold.

At the end of the first poem, Kip felt the smallest touch of a finger against his. He looked up at the Emperor's face, but the lion eyes were watching Kip's hand.

“Please, my lord,” whispered Kip. 

Those golden pools flicked briefly to Kip's face, and then back to their hands. Slowly, the Emperor stretched out a finger, and curled it around Kip's.

A second poem, and then a third. “Water, my lord? Food?” offered Kip, when he looked up from the bottom of the page.

Gently pleading, Kip convinced the Emperor to finish the soup, and eat some bread. Kip almost despaired of the fish, when he realised it would need eating sticks. But he managed to get his lord to eat a few bites, with Kip holding the golden sticks.

Kip returned to the stool, reading more of the poems. Once again he offered his hand for his lord to hold. The Emperor held two fingers this time.

Through the evening, the bells rang the hours. When they rang ten o'clock, Kip asked, “Would you like to go to bed, my lord?”

There was a pause. The Emperor looked to be struggling to make a decision.

“Would you like a scalp massage to help you sleep, Glorious One?” Kip rubbed his thumb up and down the two fingers holding his.

Still the Emperor said nothing, made no gesture, but his eyes were wide and pleading, looking at Kip. What did his lord want? Did he dare ask again?

“Your Radiancy, please, may I help you to bed?” asked Kip, trying to offer a reassuring smile.

The fingers holding his squeezed. Kip's breath caught the tiniest bit. He smiled more genuinely, and he thought perhaps the Emperor looked slightly more relaxed. 

Kip slid off the stool, leaving the book behind. Slowly, fingers still entwined, Kip helped his lord to stand. Then Kip held him as they walked together from the drawing room to the Imperial Bedchamber. 

Kip's heart was hammering. If Kip hadn't been there, would the attendants have left the Glorious One to sleep fitfully in the drawing room? Or would they have sent his Radiancy directly to bed, without supper? Did they have protective clothing they could use, but preferred not to since Kip was available? He didn't resent helping his Emperor, but he did resent whoever or whatever had left him in this state.

Beside the bed that was large enough to be a family cottage, Kip helped the Glorious One shed his dressing gown and slippers. Their hands had to separate to remove the dressing gown, and Kip thought perhaps his lord was reluctant. The usual bedroom attendant appeared from behind the screen to sweep both gown and slippers away, but offered nothing else. Kip helped his Radiancy to get under the covers, and the Emperor burrowed into the warmed sheets.

“My lord,” whispered Kip, “please, may I give you a massage?”

The Emperor nodded. Kip shed his sandals, and climbed onto the bed. He pulled the curtains closed behind him, and thought perhaps he heard a gentle sigh.

Kneeling by the Emperor's head, Kip began to gently rub. This was one of the things he'd learned in training, and having had the other trainees practice on him, he knew it was incredibly relaxing. He hoped that his lord could find some comfort in it.

“Cliopher,” the Emperor whispered.

“Yes, Glorious One?” replied Kip, not pausing or slowing.

“We will likely have nightmares tonight,” the Emperor whispered, and then took a deep breath, “If you would rather not be pulled from your own bed, there is plenty of room here.”

Kip had to bite his lip to stop himself from crying. This poor man, who should probably be allowed to sleep past noon (which would never happen). This kind man, who had struggled into bed, had struggled to decide to go to bed, was thinking of Kip's comfort.

“T-thank you, my lord,” said Kip. 

He continued the massage, until the Emperor's breathing slowed, and he seemed to be asleep. Carefully, he lifted his hands away. Then he shuffled as quietly as possible to the far side of the bed. Still in his uniform, he curled up to watch his lord sleep.

---

Kip woke to the sound of someone crying. Not sobbing, but a soft keening, with occasional sniffle. Sitting up, Kip saw the Emperor was once again a curled golden ball.

Crawling over, Kip saw his eyes were clenched tightly closed, and his hands twisted in the quilt. Gently, as he had last time, Kip stroked his lord's arm.

“I'm here, my lord,” he whispered, “everything is alright, my lord.” Kip continued to whisper soothingly, as he wiped away tears from that beautiful face with his thumb. He ran gentle hands along the Emperor's cheeks, his shoulders, his arms, his hands. Eventually, slowly, the Emperor quieted and relaxed.

As quiet as he could, he moved back to give the Emperor space.

Kip woke a second, and a third time, to help his Radiancy in his distress. The fourth time he woke, it was dawn.
