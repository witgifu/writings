As he poured himself a small glass of port, Conju's hands shook. He had been told by Lord Igel to report to the Tower at eight the next morning. It was happening. This was happening.

He sat down heavily in his favourite chair, staring blankly at the divan opposite. There was so much he didn't know, couldn't dare ask. 

Oh, he was confident he could help Sayo Mdang (or would he be Lord Cliopher soon?) improve his etiquette. Lord Igel's fears seemed mostly unfounded, the concubine, or secretary, or whatever he was, seemed perfectly teachable. Even if he was currently so direct it put Conju's teeth on edge.

The question he could not possibly ask anyone, least of all the one person who would know, was what did the Lord Emperor _want_ with Sayo Mdang? Did the Glorious One want a more discrete relationship with his concubine? Did he genuinely have no interest in sex, or was he unable? (after all, he had so far refused to marry)

The alcohol did help settle the shaking a bit, even if it did nothing for his whirling thoughts. 

There was a knock at the door. “Come in,” said Conju, wondering where his valet had got to.

Rojan opened the door, closed it behind himself, and lent against it. 

“What were you doing in the Tower today, Conju?” he asked, in the careful neutral court tones of one hiding other emotions.

“Good evening, Rojan, would you like a drink?” asked Conju. He noticed his valet finally appearing from the bedroom, and waved for her to get Rojan something.

“I would like,” said Rojan, not moving from the door, “to know what you were doing in the Tower. That's the second time I've seen you there. I don't appreciate being followed around, Conju.”

Conju turned his instinctive bark of laughter into a snort. “Sit down Rojan, and take the drink. It isn't about you.”

Rojan took the indicated seat, and the proffered drink, but sat stiffly. “Are you going to apprentice to the Master Embroiderer then?” he asked, sarcastically.

Conju looked directly into Rojan's eyes. He wanted to see what reaction his words would get here, as a measure for how it would be perceived elsewhere. “I've been appointed companion to Sayo Mdang,” he said, in his own very best neutral court voice.

Rojan's eyes widened very slightly. He also relaxed the tiniest bit. “Oh, keeper of the new imperial pet, are you?”

Conju let the silence hang. He carefully filed away the insult for later reference.

“It's going to be hard work,” said Rojan, eventually.

Twisting his glass in his hands, Conju lent back in his chair. “And why would that be?” he asked, as if the answer was not very important.

Rojan snorted. “He hasn't been appointed so much as a valet. We've all been having to fit him around everything else for his Radiancy. *Thankfully* he's not nearly so demanding. Did you hear when his Radiancy wanted his entire evening court costume changed at the last possible minute? The poor woman in charge of his shoes nearly fainted.”

Conju carefully noted that Sayo Mdang had no household of his own. Then he said, as Rojan no doubt expected, “I heard *something* about it, but not in so much detail…”

Rojan smiled, in the cute but salacious way, that had first tempted Conju to turn casual flirting into a more serious attempt. “Well,” he said, and launched into a blow-by-blow account, which Conju thoroughly enjoyed.

Conju's valet woke him up at six. This time it was Rojan's turn to complain about the early hour. “Wassu?” he said blearily, as Conju slipped out of the bed.

“I'm due at the Tower at eight,” said Conju, stifling a yawn. He was definitely not used to getting up this early. Plus it had been a late, if enjoyable, night with Rojan.

“Nnnn,” said Rojan, pulling the blankets over his head until only his nose and eyes were exposed.

Conju wrapped himself in the proffered dressing gown, and gave Rojan a tiny kiss on the tip of his nose. “This is going to be regular now, so if you prefer you can always go back to your own bed to sleep,” he said, teasingly. He suspected Rojan had a room somewhere with his aunts.

As he sipped the thin soup that was all he could stomach this early, Conju wondered at the ease with which Rojan had shared intimate gossip from the Tower. Was it just because they both now worked there that he felt comfortable? Or did the Tower usually spread that kind of information? Was there some system Conju would have to learn for what should and what must not be shared? Because surely if you got it wrong, you risked the Emperor's personal displeasure.

He dressed more simply for now, knowing that he would be spending the morning being chanted at by one of the priest-wizards, and wreathed in incense. He hoped he would have time to come back and change for the afternoon. Just in case, he carefully folded up another overrobe, and packed it into a small attaché case. He added some of his cosmetics, and a perfume that he thought would not clash with any lingering incense.

Conju arrived precisely five minutes before he was due, at the ritual room in the Tower. There were, of course, several such rooms in the Tower. Some for the Glorious One, and the senior members of the Ouranatha. This one was much more mundane, with a slate floor (easy to chalk and clean chalk from), and fitted wooden cabinets. No window, but clear magelights on the walls.

Conju wondered if all the rooms with windows in the Tower were the public rooms. So far that *had* been the case, but he had not yet had a proper tour. Lord Igel had told an underling to show Conju this room, and the kitchens. But there must be dozens of rooms here, if not more. A laundry, sewing rooms, somewhere for making soaps and lotions, and all the other things needed.

The junior priest-wizard arrived, carrying three bolts of white silk. “Oh good,” he said to Conju, without clarifying, “hold these please.”

Surprised, Conju carefully held out his arms to take the delicate silk. One was so thin the loose end almost seemed to want to float away. Another had a beautiful shimmer to it, almost like mother-of-pearl. He had never touched such fine fabrics, and he considered himself a bit of a connoisseur. 

Conju followed the priest-wizard into the room, and stood where he was told, still holding the silks. The wizard quickly got on with the process, drawing a complicated diagram on the floor in chalk. Then, as expected, came the chanting and incense.

His arms were getting quite sore by the time the priest-wizard stopped, and scuffed the edge of the diagram. He wasn't sure how long it had been, at least a bell. 

Conju was finally able to relinquish the fabric back to the priest-wizard. As he hurried out, the wizard called back, “See you back here in another quarter bell!”

In the end Conju spent all but the last quarter bell before noon in the ritual room. Sometimes holding things for the priest-wizard, sometimes not. Some things were clearly just also in need of being purified; Conju was reasonably certain no purification ritual required the participant to hold a pair of slippers in one hand, and a saucepan in the other.

After he was released, Conju took the time to touch up his appearance in the washroom. If the choice was between lunch and looking the part, at lest for his first day, he would pick making sure he was impeccable. Pleased with the result, he walked briskly up to the kitchen.

Here, he found Sayo Mdang, standing in a corner and eating a bowl of sticky rice porridge. Conju was surprised, he had assumed that Sayo Mdang would have lunch with the Glorious One, or at least in one of the dining rooms. Eating, standing up in the kitchen, seemed undignified.

Sayo Mdang saw Conju, and smiled apologetically. “Sorry, Lord Conju, I'm still finishing lunch,” he said.

“Actually, I was hoping to find some lunch myself,” said Conju, looking around at the staff. 

One of the kitchen hands heard him, and waved to a large platter of sandwiches, “Help yourself, Lord Conju. But if nobody's told you yet, don't take them over the line.”

Conju nodded, and looked around for the line in question. There was, in fact, a thin line of black tiles down the walls, and across the floor, carefully dividing the kitchen in two. On each side, everything was mirrored. Except that, on the no-sandwich side of the room, the dresser containing large serving platters had golden platters. The sandwich side had silver and plain white ceramic.

Sayo Mdang was standing on the gold side. His bowl was rimmed with gold, but his eating sticks were silver. Conju considered this, as he took a sandwich, and found a chair that was out of the way.

The other Tower staff seemed to be treating Sayo Mdang like his position _was_ just the Emperor's secretary, but maybe that he had some serious allergies. And yet, yesterday Lord Igel had introduced Conju to Sayo Mdang, and not the other way around. Perhaps everyone was uncertain what rank rightly applied to Sayo Mdang. For all his assertion yesterday that Sayo Mdang was equal in rank to an Imperial Duke, Conju was really only guessing. 

They were all trying to work it out from hints dropped by the Emperor. It was a shame that none of them could just *ask*. 

He finished his sandwich, and saw Sayo Mdang finish his lunch at the same time. They both washed their hands at the mirrored sinks, and then Conju carefully stepped over the line.

“What first?” asked Sayo Mdang, smiling slightly.

“Let us begin with matters sartorial,” said Conju, primly.

Sayo Mdang shrugged, but led Conju up a wide staircase, and down a corridor. “I live in my uniform,” he said, somewhat defensively, as he opened a door marked with a large yellow primrose, “I do have a court version of it.”

Conju rolled his eyes. Did Lord Igel seriously expect Sayo Mdang to be sworn in as the Hands of the Emperor in an _Imperial Bureaucratic Service uniform_? Surely that had been supplied earlier. Once again, they were struggling with the problem of how to present Sayo Mdang correctly.

Sayo Mdang opened his wardrobe, and Conju looked at the small collection in horror. Three regular uniforms, one court uniform, a sleep shirt, and a dressing gown. Oh, and some strange confection of silk leaves that had to be a fancy-dress costume.

“This is all? No cosmetics, no jewels?” he asked, stunned.

“No,” said Sayo Mdang, shutting the wardrobe, “That's what I was given. All of my old things were taken away. I had to send anything I valued back to my mother.”

Conju stamped down hard on his emotions. He needed to be clear-headed, and not ashamed of how his charge was being treated. The staff in the Tower were surely doing their best, but constructing a whole new wardrobe took time, and if it had to be of purified fabrics that would add even more.

“I'll ask… someone… about that,” said Conju, turning away from the depressing wardrobe.

“Lord Igel?” asked Sayo Mdang, curiously.

“Not if I can avoid it,” replied Conju, with an artful raised eyebrow. “Do you know when you're being sworn in as Hands of the Emperor?”

“No,” said Sayo Mdang, “I haven't been told when, or what it involves.”

Conju heard footsteps, outside in the hallway. Spears stamped. Both men spun around to face the door. As it opened, they both fell down into the obeisance. Conju was chagrined to notice he was a beat behind Sayo Mdang.

And then, the Lord Emperor stepped into the room.
