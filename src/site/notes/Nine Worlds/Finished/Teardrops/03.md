That night, as you returned from court, Sayo Mdang's desk was empty. You released a small knot of tension you hadn't known you were holding. Some compromise had been found.

You walked through the study, and the breakfast room. As the guards opened the door to the hallway, you spotted a figure near the other end. It was not so unusual to see your attendants moving between rooms in the imperial apartments. They often took time in the quiet of the night to make repairs.

But as the figure fell into the obeisance, you noticed they were wearing red and black. Had Sayo Mdang got lost, and ended up in the public rooms? 

You hadn't explicitly asked Lord Igel to give him a tour. To explain which rooms were public, and which he may freely wander of a nighttime. Why was it so hard to find staff that were competent and not painfully irritating? You seemed to only be able to manage it with the guards.

You turned to one of the guards, Roald. “Help Sayo Mdang to return to his room, and explain the layout of the Tower to him.”

Guard Roald saluted, and walked down the hall to the prostrate form of Sayo Mdang. You entered the door to your dressing room.

The next morning, you read your reports. The author of the summaries had changed, perhaps your secretary was delegating that task as well. If they were, then Sayo Mdang's hand was much neater.

At the bottom of the stack was a report on crop yields across Ysthar. There was a noted discrepancy with the forecast, and you felt obliged to check the original. It should have been in your private library for quick reference.

Since the Emperor cannot amble, you strode to the library, flanked by your guards. The left one opened the door.

A figure turned around, saw your guards, and fell to the floor. Your first flash of thought was that someone had come to refresh the papers and ink. But then your mind caught up, and registered the red and black robes. 

You gave the signal to rise, and entered the cramped room. “Good morning, Sayo Mdang,” you said, wondering what he had needed in the little library. 

“Good morning, my lord,” he replied. Once again, he looked directly into your eyes.  

You did not flinch. You turned quickly to the shelves, looking for the recent records for Ysthar. Last year's report was there, but this year's forecast was missing. You spun back, to see if it was on the desk.

It was not. It was in Sayo Mdang's hands. 

“The Imperial Archives also have a copy of any document or book in here,” you said, trying to keep any hint of approbation out of your voice. Of course, this library was in the Tower, and the Imperial Archives were a good twenty-minute walk away. No doubt, he simply wanted to reference the closest copy to hand.

Sayo Mdang blushed. He put the document down on the desk, and stepped back to make as much of a bow as space permitted. Any apology to the Emperor should have been made with a full obeisance. You didn't really care, except that if other people saw the mistake, they would often decide to take offence on your behalf. 

“My apologies, my lord,” he said, a small tremor in his voice, “I thought, that since this was just down the hall… I will request from the Imperial Archives in future instead, my lord.”

“You may reference any text in here, Sayo Mdang,” you said, perfectly serene, “Only do not remove them from the room.”

You picked up the document from the table, and flipped to the relevant section. The number in the report was correct, which was irritating. That meant the forecasters would be tittering and nervous trying to work out what had changed in the last six months to make their formulas wrong.

Finished, you shelved the document, and swept back out of the library. Sayo Mdang left quietly behind you.

It wasn't until you had entered your official study that you realised that Sayo Mdang had said “down the hall” and not “in the Tower” or “up the stairs”. 

You stopped near the mechanical bird, gazing out the fretwork of the windows into the tiny shapes of grey and blue. A distant flock of sparrows swirled, making amorphous shapes in the early morning sky.

You were sure you had said to Lord Igel that Sayo Mdang should be given space appropriate to an undersecretary. Was the tower bursting with staff, such that Sayo Mdang would have had to share a room on the levels below? You had explicitly said he was to be granted his own room.

If, *if*, Lord Igel had given him a room in the Imperial Apartments proper, well. It was done. You could not move him out, without marking him as disfavoured. To keep him there would perhaps be just as bad. Word would get out soon, if it hadn't already begun to circulate.

They would think you had finally accepted someone.

Oh, sometimes your household staff might temporarily sleep in the Imperial Apartments. If you were ill. If they had some reason to need to be there both late into the night and for the dawn, particularly in summer.

Any member of your family come to attend court would stay in their own apartments. Not that they often did come, but they could stay in comfort and be less directly in the public eye.

You shied away from thinking about how Emperor Eritanyr had used the Imperial Apartments. Empress Anyoë, that was a less troubling time. She had separate suites for herself, her consort, and her concubine. There had been further rooms for other concubines under Lodyr, but those had given way to Anyoë's magnificent baths.

Well, you didn't know for certain where Sayo Mdang had been put. Perhaps he had said “down the hallway” simply as a shorthand, or because it was down the hallway from his desk. You did not need to jump to assumptions. Perhaps Lord Igel had understood and followed through on your instructions for once.

You turned your mind to the business of the day. Your secretary was very nearly late. Perhaps it was time to ask the Master of Offices *again* if it was possible to supply someone competent.

---

At lunch, as you pushed the rice around your plate, you contemplated the fact that it simply wasn't possible for you to wander around your own apartments. You had a schedule, which you were expected to keep. If you needed something, an attendant would fetch and carry it for you. If you went to the toilet or the bathroom, there were expected routes. You might request to dine in one of three rooms, or read in one of four rooms, or receive special guests in any of those.

There were rooms you hadn't visited since your very first tour of the Imperial Apartments in the days before your coronation. There might even been rooms you hadn't seen then, some service rooms blended in perfectly with the panelling.

It would not, in conclusion, be possible for you to poke around until you found where Lord Igel had put Sayo Mdang. And if it wasn't in the Imperial Apartments proper, if he was on the floors below, then of course there was no way for you to venture to those locations.

You would need to either ask your guards, or Sayo Mdang himself. It was probably best to start with the guards, as you had already thoroughly embarrassed Sayo Mdang today.

And so, you waited until Roald came back on duty. Well, you gave him twenty minutes to relax into his work day first. Partially because you were being dressed for evening court at the time, partially because it was surely impolite to ask someone a question as soon as they arrived.

So, as you swept through the hallway, you asked, “Guard Roald, how was Sayo Mdang? Did you explain how the Tower works?”

Roald saluted. “Yes, my lord. I gave him a short tour and then took him back to his rooms.”

Rooms. Plural. You tried not to think too hard about that.

“And had he been supplied with the necessities?”

“Yes, my lord. He was most thankful for how helpful and kind you had been, my lord. I think he was a little overawed by the Primrose Rooms.”

You nodded, but made no further comment. They might have just visited the Primrose Rooms. You remembered vaguely that it was on the floor above the imperial bedroom, named for a large ornamental glass light fixture (yellow, naturally). Next to the drawing room with the very uncomfortable chairs, where you took guests who were likely to ramble, in an effort to encourage them to stop.

As you walked out of the official study, you noticed Sayo Mdang at his desk still. Well, it wasn't quite so unreasonable to be working now, as it was after midnight. He made his obeisance correctly, but you did not stop. You told yourself that this was because your schedule had no time to spare.

It was not at all because you were worried about that disconcerting gaze.
