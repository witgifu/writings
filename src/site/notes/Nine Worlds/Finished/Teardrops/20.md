Kip sat at his desk in the official study, taking notes. He suspected many of the nobles didn't even notice he was there. That was fine with him, he could always check the spellings of names later. Shorthand captured the sounds efficiently. He didn't even need to take a word-perfect record, so that cut out a lot of it. The lords did tend towards vapid fawning.

Shortly after the bells rang for three (petitions inevitably ran over time), the guards stamped their spears, and announced Lord Conju. The Emperor admitted him, but his eyes did flick to meet Kip's. Kip had no idea what the Emperor saw there.

Lord Conju entered, dressed beautifully. Not ostentatiously, as some minor lords did, but elegantly. He performed his obeisance at the exact correct distance, his eyes carefully watching the Emperor's hands. Kip admired his poise.

He could also believe that Lord Conju had spent at least an hour selecting the outfit. Kip was sure it contained many subtle messages that he couldn't read. He would have to ask Conju to explain it later.

“Rise, Lord Conju. What brings you before us today?” said the Emperor. It was the exact tone, words, and gesture, that the Glorious One had used for every petitioner since Kip had been secretary. The Emperor was carefully looking at Conju's hands, as Lord Conju looked at his.

“Glorious One, I have been extraordinarily honoured by your condescension in granting me a position with Sayo Mdang,” said Lord Conju, adding that same gesture he had used before with the Emperor. 

“I have consulted with Lord Igel, the Master of Etiquette, and the Master of Ceremonies. I offer my most sincere apologies for my ignorance, Illustrious Lord, but I have been unable to determine what rank and address should be given to Sayo Mdang upon his appointment as Hands of the Emperor. All the previous appointees I can find record of were princes or imperial dukes at their appointment, and retained those titles and addresses.”

Lord Conju made a bow of second degree apology, with another gesture. Kip wondered if it would actually be possible to hold an entire conversation in court gestures. He realised he should probably learn them all, at least so he could take more accurate notes.

“Is this purely a matter of etiquette, then, Lord Conju?” asked the Emperor, his voice serene, his posture perfect.

“It is also a question of perception and honour, Glorious One,” answered Lord Conju.

“And what did the Master of Etiquette have to say?” asked the Emperor, although his eyes were now on Kip. As soon as Kip noticed the Emperor's eyes move, he looked down at his desk and his notes.

“The Master of Etiquette has planned a ceremony that combines the oaths of a concubine and those of the Hands of the Emperor. He informed me Sayo Mdang's rank will be equal to a master craftsman and his address will remain the same.”

Kip was sure his face and ears were completely red. He hoped the Emperor had looked away, but Kip didn't dare look up. He did not want the Emperor to think he had shamed Kip. A master craftsman was an honourable rank, many of his uncles and aunts would hold that rank. It was the oaths he was blushing about. 

“And Lord Igel?” asked the Emperor.

Kip thought the sound of his pen on the paper was loud enough to be a storm blowing. His heart rate had jumped significantly. Anxious, he wondered if perhaps he had put Lord Conju in danger, asking him to make this petition. It had not occurred to him before (although surely Lord Conju had realised, why hadn't he said anything?) that this could put Conju in the position of being against some of the most powerful people in court.

“Lord Igel, Glorious One, has kindly granted me access to the full catalogue of those jewels in the treasury which were collected under Emperor Eritanyr to adorn his chosen. He also informed me that Sayo Mdang did not require any staff of his own.”

Kip couldn't bear it any more, and looked up at first Conju, and then the Emperor. They both looked perfectly poised, elegant gentlemen of the court. But in the Emperor's eyes was some fire, perhaps anger. Kip's stomach twisted.

He wanted to throw himself out from behind his desk, and onto the floor. He wanted to beg the Emperor's forgiveness, and confess he had asked Lord Conju to do this for him. Would Conju suffer for Kip's temerity? Had he already badly misstepped in this dangerous world?

But the Emperor had said, so clearly, that all he wanted of Kip was a secretary. His Radiancy had said this presentation was for Kip's protection. Did that protection extend even to the Emperor's own staff? His own Groom?

There was a painful silence, as Kip and Lord Conju waited for the Emperor's verdict.

“Sayo Mdang, prepare a memo and pronouncement,” said the Emperor, and began pacing. “The position of The Hands of the Emperor shall be of rank equal to an Imperial Duke, with the address of 'Lord Cliopher, the Hands' or 'Lady Felicia, the Hands' et cetera. It is not heritable by children natural or adopted, and is an appointment for life unless otherwise rescinded et cetera. The usual opening and conclusion for the pronouncement, which should go out in three days time. Send the memo to the Master of Ceremonies and Master of Etiquette today.”

He whirled to face Lord Conju again, and paused. Then he resumed pacing.

“Sayo Mdang, a memo to Lord Igel. The Cavalier Conju enazo Argellian an Vilius is authorised by Us to appoint in consultation with Sayo Mdang those persons they deem necessary for the maintenance of Sayo Mdang in a manner appropriate to his rank and position. Furthermore, the Cavalier is to have access to the jewels, accessories, adornments, and other items, stored in our treasury and designated for either Imperial Concubines or Imperial Consorts, on behalf of Sayo Mdang, until such time as others are appointed for such duties. Usual opening and closing. Have a page send it to Lord Igel.”

The Emperor stopped, and once again faced Lord Conju.

Lord Conju sank into a deep bow of thanks. With what Kip was reasonably sure was a gesture for a debt owed, presumably the extra flourish was to indicate a large debt.

“There is no debt owed, Lord Conju,” said the Emperor, confirming Kip's interpretation, “Thank you for raising this issue with us. We will need to be more clear in our instructions in future, to prevent such misunderstandings. If you have any similar concerns, please do not hesitate to raise them immediately.”

Lord Conju took this as his dismissal, and said “Thank you again, Glorious One,” as he folded into his obeisance. He left.

Kip began carefully writing the memos out in his neat, but not formal, longhand script. The pronouncement he would write out this evening in chancery hand, and send for copying. He liked to get as many of the memos written out as possible while waiting between petitions, or waiting for petitioners to get to the point.

On this occasion, it also meant he had an excuse to keep his eyes firmly on his desk, and not on the Sun-on-Earth. His mind was boiling with emotions. Relief, that Lord Conju had not got in trouble. Awe, for the generosity and kindness of the Emperor. Frustration, that safety meant titles and jewels. Worry, that the person closest to the Emperor, his Groom, so clearly knew him so little. 

The guards announced the next petitioner. Kip shuffled his papers around his desk, to have his copy of the notes he had made for the Emperor to hand for reference.

The doors did not open.

“Sayo Mdang,” said the Emperor. 

Kip pulled forward an empty sheet, assuming the Glorious One had something else he had thought of to dictate. He held his pen ready.

“Sayo Mdang,” said the Emperor, again.

Kip looked up. The golden eyes were looking directly into his. There was a hint of dampness.

“Sayo Mdang, if you are ever in need of anything, have any desire or whim, please tell us. We offer no censure, we had not invited, and so you could not ask. We wish you to have every comfort and joy.”

Kip swallowed. “Thank you, Glorious One,” he said, very quietly. 

He watched the Emperor's elegant long fingers brush across his sandalwood desk. Then they flicked out, in the gesture to admit the person at the door.
