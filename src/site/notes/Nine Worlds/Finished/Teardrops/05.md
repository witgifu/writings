The next morning, you paced around your office. “Next, we need to revise yesterday's letter to General Halioren,” you said, turning towards your secretary's desk.

“Of course, Glorious One,” he replied smoothly, and began shuffling papers.

“Begin by reading it back,” you said as you turned again.

The paper shuffling sounds continued. You completed another side of the triangle, and he still had not begun reading.

“I apologise most profusely, Glorious One. I will need to speak to Sayo Mdang, that letter is not among the notes he prepared.” You detected a very slight trembling in his voice.

A flick of your hand, and the guards opened the door to the room beyond. You were facing the wrong direction, but you could hear the tone of the whispers. 

Your secretary was biting, angry. Sayo Mdang was apologetic. There was a small rustling of papers.

You turned in time to see your secretary slap Sayo Mdang across the face. Sayo Mdang looked down at his desk, his face reddening from the slap and from embarrassment. He muttered another apology.

“Guards, escort Our secretary out, and inform the Master of Offices We will require someone else from tomorrow” you said, holding your serenity only with carefully controlled breathing.

Behind your back you knew there was assault, abuse, even the odd assassination. To do it in front of you, in the Imperial Apartments, was outrageous. Surely even the jumped up lording of a secretary knew that.

You did not pause in your pacing. “Sayo Mdang, move to the desk here so We may continue.”

A third, brief, rustling of papers. A scrape of a chair, as Sayo Mdang took the new seat. 

“To General Prince Benneret Halioren, commander of the Fourth Division of the Seventh Army, greetings…”

You did not mention that Sayo Mdang should have performed his obeisance on entering the official study. Or that he should not have commenced reading until after you had instructed again. He was efficient, and you decided to let this small treason sooth your irritation.

The morning was immensely productive. True, Sayo Mdang has to ask you to clarify several place names on Ysthar, but they are those where the spelling is most unlike the pronunciation. And it is not as if any of your previous secretaries would have known any place names from Zunidh. Despite these small interruptions (he interrupts you! how magnificent), together you managed to get through more work than you would have in two days with any of your recent secretaries.

Your desire for a competent secretary warred with your desire to keep him as far away from your person as you could.

You decided to wait and see who the Master of Offices sent tomorrow. Perhaps, against all odds, they would also be competent, and you would have two competent people working for you.

The productive morning gave you a fizz of energy into the afternoon. That was good, because you had a series of awful rituals with the Ouranatha, that ran into the evening. These particular rituals, performed at the stone that was the Heart of the Empire, were exacting. They made you exceedingly sore, tired, and nauseous. 

It wasn't anything *you* did, seated on the small blackwood throne. Holding your hands out, holding them up, resting them down. Simply being near the stone for so long always made you a bit nauseous. Like all the rituals of the Ouranatha, binding the Empire together into your flesh, these scraped your soul and your mind. Compound that for hours, and of course it was bad.

You endured, because what else could you do?

The only good thing about these rituals was that they always fell on days when it was inauspicious to hold formal court. And so, when you were done, you could float in your bath for an extra quarter bell.

You lay there, cocooned in the warmth, balanced, almost weightless. Enjoying the slight tickle of the edge of the water on your skin. Eyes closed, you tried not to think of anything at all. Not to be the Emperor, not to be the lord of anything at all. Not even to be me. Just to be.

The quarter bell rang, and you reluctantly stood up. Exited the bath, via your usual route, into the dressing rooms. Your attendants were silent and precise as they dried you, anointed you, and wrapped you in a pure white dressing gown. It was covered in tiny embroidered gold bees.

They finished, and stepped away, looking down. You stepped off the plinth, and walked (the Emperor could not shuffle) on your slippered feet to your favourite small drawing room. An error of design had meant that, sometimes, the tiniest sliver of direct moonlight would make it through the one window. You loved that reminder that not even the Palace of Stars could hide you entirely from the sky.

Your guards opened the door for you.

The room was lit by mage light, turned warm by the yellow fixtures. Two comfortable chairs in white, a chaise lounge in gold. A fireplace, filled with fresh flowers on this warm night. By the window, a small writing desk, set to face the room and the fire.

At the desk, Sayo Mdang. He rose from the chair, and sank into the obeisance. Internally, you sighed. Why was he here, now? Guard Roald had said Sayo Mdang had 'rooms', plural, and so surely he could be relaxing there.

Your temper shortened by the pain of the rituals, you let slip the question. “Why are you here, Sayo Mdang?”

He did not rise, as you had not signalled to do so. “My sincerest apologies, Glorious One. Lord Igel told me to stay here until the midnight bell, I assume there are some works required in my rooms.” He spoke into the floor, not even looking up. This was obviously etiquette he had been drilled in.

You waved him up. That seemed to be the answer to the question you had earlier; it was clear that Lord Igel was trying to play matchmaker. He would, therefore, have put Sayo Mdang in a suite in the Imperial Apartments. 

Lord Igel would have noticed that you had asked Sayo Mdang to act as secretary today. Apparently he had decided to take advantage of your weakened state, following the rituals, and push the relationship forward. Throw him into your arms.

You twisted your hands behind your back, where only your guards could see. What should you do next? What could you possibly do to extricate Sayo Mdang from this horrible mess you had put him in?

He was looking at you, his eyes beautiful amber pools. He did not have the court mask that the rest of your staff had perfected. You could see there confusion, and guilt, and underneath an unquenchable self-belief. Not the kind of Lord Igel, who believed he knew you well enough to anticipate your wishes, and so would act without asking. The sort of self-belief that declared that he was worthwhile, capable, true.

Things you no longer believed about yourself.

While you were standing there, the silence becoming increasingly awkward, an attendant arrived behind you. No doubt with some kind of bland and nourishing soup. They had all been told by your physician to try to coax you to eat.

You stepped into the room, automatically, to give the attendant space to enter. They arranged the gold rimmed bowl on the table, with a small white loaf of bread cut into five slices. There was an elegant gold-rimmed glass of watered wine as well.

They bowed, and left. The guards closed the door behind them. You sat on the chair nearest the soup, and considered it. You did not want to eat it (you wanted to scream).

You heard Sayo Mdang sit down again, somewhere to your right. Picking up a piece of bread, you began shredding it into tiny pieces.

If there was anyone, anyone at all, in the Palace, in the whole rotten Empire, who you could trust. You could perhaps have asked them to talk to Sayo Mdang for you. Your sister, who you did trust in almost every way, had actually said 'no' when you had given her the choice. If she was here, perhaps you could ask her, and she could ask him.

If you summoned her here, if it was known she had been talking to Sayo Mdang, no matter what he said or where he went, you would be trapping her. The pressure on both of you to marry would rise and rise. You could not subject her to that, not when you had promised to let her 'no' stand, to protect her (from yourself, from the terrible demands of the Empire on the bodies of its daughters).

Princess Anastasia, your great-aunt, was always willing to speak her mind. You did not need to summon her here, though, to know what she would say. She would say to take Sayo Mdang, and to marry Melissa while you were at it. She did not understand why you did not do as you pleased (as if either of those things would please you) or why you deliberately avoided your first duty: to provide an heir.

You tried a very small sip of the soup. Your insides roiled, bubbling, and you put it down. It was perhaps not entirely the fault of the soup.

Giving up on food, you took the glass of watered wine over to the chase lounge. The little table beside it had a book that you didn't recognise. *The Songs of Those Who Live Under the River of Stars*

You flipped it open, and saw that it was some kind of epic poetry. It had hand-tinted etched illustrations, even. Still not knowing what to do about Sayo Mdang, not knowing how to ask, you resorted to distraction. The songs were, indeed, beautiful. Even without the music.

The book was long, and you didn't finish it that night. At the eleventh bell, you put it down, your mind full of seafaring adventurers. “Good night, Sayo Mdang,” you said as you stood up.

Sayo Mdang looked up from his writing. “Good night, Glorious One,” he said, a clear joy in his eyes as he met yours.

You went to bed. As the curtains were closed on you, you hoped that an early night and a new day would provide clarity to your heart. In the end, you did fall asleep, but well after the midnight bell.
