The first unusual thing you noticed in the morning, was a full vial of sedative on your nightstand. When your attendants saw what you had looked at, it was quickly whipped out of sight. You couldn't remember any nightmares, but then you often didn't. Perhaps they had got the medicine ready and were about to wake you when the nightmare stopped. That was a nice thought.

You performed the dawn ritual, and ate your breakfast, and read your reports. Everything seemed smooth, your household seemed happy. Lord Igel said nothing, which you liked. Gossip could be interesting, but blather was intolerable.

When you stepped into your office, you noticed immediately that Sayo Mdang looked tired. You knew if you asked him how he was, he would almost certainly lie for your comfort and reassurance. You never found it reassuring, but everyone seemed to think you should.

Putting it to the back of your mind for a moment, you started with the work for the day. It wasn't until almost half way that you pieced it together. 

Sayo Mdang was making a fair copy of a letter for you to seal, since it needed to be sent immediately. As he did so, he hummed gently. His singing voice was familiar.

Of course. You had a nightmare. Your attendants had been unable to wake you to take the medication, they had sent for Sayo Mdang. He had… sung to you. You couldn't remember any of the nightmare, but you could remember a sweet voice singing in a language you didn't understand. Somehow, you had known it was calling you.

Sayo Mdang had ruined his own night's sleep, for you. And he had found a way to help without the sedative. It was unbelievable. Why would he do that for you? Especially after what you had done last time. You wouldn't have blamed him if he had pried open your jaw, tipped the medication down your throat, and stomped back to bed. You had given him permission to do that.

But he hadn't wanted to. You remembered what he asked, if the medication helped. He seemed disturbed that your attendants were drugging you. Well, perhaps it was disturbing, objectively. You had learnt that, untouchable you might be, but your body was not your own. It was bound to the Empire, it worked for the Empire, it did what the Empire needed.

Cliopher sayo Mdang did not agree. It was unbelievable. What an extraordinary gift.

You resolved to see if there was any possible way you could repay him. And convince him the medication was fine, really. He deserved sleep.

Sayo Mdang finished the letter, you sealed it, and it was whisked off to a page. You watched Sayo Mdang more closely for the rest of the afternoon. He did seem to genuinely enjoy the work, offering to research alternatives and do further investigations where information was lacking. There was a very keen mind behind those beautiful eyes.

Kip was eating lunch in the kitchen when Lord Conju found him. 

“I've talked to Lord Igel, and the Master of Ceremonies. Let's have a chat about how this is going to work, Sayo Mdang,” said Lord Conju. 

“I have to be back for the petitions in half a bell, Lord Conju” said Kip around hasty mouthfuls.

“Yes, yes, Sayo Mdang, it won't take that long, let's just go up to your rooms,” said Conju brusquely. He grabbed Kip's elbow and steered him (still holding a bowl and eating sticks) out of the kitchen and up the stairs.

“What's wrong?” asked Kip, not able to eat and walk while being pulled along.

“Nothing is wrong, dear, just important,” said Lord Conju. He'd never called Kip 'dear' before. Something clearly had the usually composed and elegant man flustered. Kip decided to wait until they had reached his rooms to pry.

Back in his rooms, Kip took a chair and resumed shovelling food into his mouth. Lord Conju carefully closed the door behind them both.

His mouth too full to talk, Kip gave Lord Conju a querying look instead.

“Lord Igel... does not share my assessment,” said Lord Conju, arranging his robes as he sat.

Kip repeated the querying look, and added a shrug.

“He claims it will be sufficient for you to borrow Tower staff, and you don't need a valet. When I asked about jewels, he gave me the catalogue of the treasury holdings for concubines,” said Lord Conju, looking irritated.

Kip swallowed. “I told you. Also, I don't need jewels.”

Lord Conju sniffed. “You certainly don't need *those* jewels, at least not in court. And we don't have time to get them remade into something more appropriate.”

“I'm curious now, what exactly are they?” asked Kip, trying to imagine what weird fancy dress might be kept in the treasury.

“Most of them were commissioned by Emperor Eritanyr,” said Lord Conju.

“Oh,” said Kip. His dreams of tiger costumes replaced with the descriptions Baru Tovo had given him of Eritanyr's princely court. No, he did not want to be wearing *that* in court.

“Exactly,” said Lord Conju, “Lord Igel knows the Glorious One's wishes best, so this must be what his Radiancy wants. It does rather change my plans, but will have to work with it.”

“I'm not sure that he does, Lord Conju,” said Kip, cautiously.

“Pardon?” replied Lord Conju, who had clearly begun making mental plans.

“I don't think Lord Igel know what the Glorious One wants, or if he does, he doesn't care much,” said Kip, wiggling awkwardly in his chair. This was something he'd been piecing together.

Lord Conju gave Kip a most incredulous look. “And how would you know that?”

“The only breakfast the Glorious One always eats all of is flatbread and marmalade,” said Kip, “and Lord Igel hardly ever orders it. He's repeatedly ordered fried rice, and the Glorious One just picks out the peas.” Kip quite enjoyed the fried rice, but he could understand how the salty dish might not sit well on a delicate stomach. Many of his family refused to eat anything but fresh fruit and coffee before noon.

Lord Conju stared at Kip in silence for a while. “He's been Groom of the Chamber for years, nobody else has lasted as long. He probably just hasn't noticed—”

“Isn't it his job to notice?” said Kip, slightly sad, “Just because he's the best so far doesn't mean he's good. And… there's been other things… worse things.”

“What kind of things?” asked Lord Conju.

Kip just shook his head. He felt bad enough that Lord Igel didn't seem to care about the Emperor's terrible nightmares. Perhaps it was common gossip. But Kip did not feel right telling Lord Conju about the medication.

“So you don't think I should trust Lord Igel's opinion. If he's wrong, that puts you in an even worse position. Dressing you up to present to the court looking like one of Eritanyr's concubines, or like a poor secretary, only to have the Glorious One blame you,” he said, looking stressed. Belatedly, he added, “And me.”

“You could petition the Glorious One,” said Kip, moving to his desk and thumbing through a stack of papers.

Lord Conju sat up perfectly straight and poised. “It would take weeks to get a hearing, and I'm in no position to be bothering the Lord Emperor. I'll go through the catalogue and see if there are any sets where we can take one piece and somehow make a cohesive whole.”

“I can fit you in at three,” said Kip, trying to suppress a grin, “The Earl hasn't filed his paperwork so he won't be attending. And any noble has the right to petition the Emperor.” Kip smiled at the schedule, “Provided they fill in the correct paperwork.”

“I haven't filed the paperwork. I don't even know what it is,” said Conju, his voice controlled and bland.

“If you don't have any other issues to raise, I can file it for you, Lord Conju. It's mostly so I have enough information to prepare a brief on the issues for the Glorious One,” said Kip. He was trying desperately not to enjoy how much this was discombobulating, Lord Conju. “I, of course, as a commoner, am unable to petition the Sun-on-Earth directly.”

Lord Conju closed his eyes, and took a long, slow breath. “Then please put me down for three, Sayo Mdang. I have no other topics to bring before the Glorious One.” He stood up, and strode to the door. “I will go dress.”

“Don't forget to eat lunch,” said Kip, “We don't want you fainting.”

Lord Conju gave Kip a glare that could have curdled milk. He didn't reply, but he did pull the door shut rather loudly behind him.
