After the last petition, you went directly into the private study. You couldn't even look Sayo Mdang in the eye as you did. Soon to be _Lord Cliopher, the Hands_. You were running away.

You closed the door behind you, and I slid down to the floor. My silk robes bunched up against the perfectly polished wood. Hands pressed against the cold marble floor. I buried my face in my knees. 

You had been suspicious of Lord Conju, and here he had come to petition you, on behalf of Sayo Mdang. You had trusted Lord Igel, why? When Sayo Mdang had already shown Igel's treachery? Why had you listened to Lord Igel's assurances?

Were you just so used to everyone being untrustworthy that you had become numb? That you accepted their inevitable betrayal, and tried to get what you could out of them until then? You had resolved not to play politics but to do justice, and you had failed.

I couldn't even apologise, to Lord Conju, or Sayo Mdang. 

I had no hair to tear out. Even the tiniest injury would be tutted over by my attendants, and seen by Lord Igel as a sign of weakness. I could cry silently. I could break things, tear paper, but none of *them* deserved the punishment.

The Emperor could never be wrong.

Lord Conju was from a well-respected family. He was not without protection, not without friends. And now, it seemed, neither was Sayo Mdang. However inadvertently, you had managed to find him a scrap of protection. From yourself, from me, from Lord Igel.

Igel was going to have to go. You had given him enough chances, had excused enough bad behaviour. If you dismissed him now, however, you had nobody to replace him. That meant plunging your hand into the viper's nest and seeing if you would get bit by what you plucked out.

You needed a plan. I wished I had some way of asking Sayo Mdang and Lord Conju for help. I was, as always, lonely, and never alone.

Perhaps, I could… oh, my stomach clenched before I even finished the though. 

You had tried so very hard to be, not the best Emperor (a depressingly low standard of late), but the best the Emperor could be. You had tried to bend the generals towards peace, the Ouranatha towards tolerance, the Service towards honesty, the laws towards justice. Ten years, you had tried. You had tried.

Would I destroy all that, for a chance to talk to Sayo Mdang?

You could pretend to seek an assignation with Sayo Mdang, in here. Under that pretext it wouldn't be possible to talk, (ears would be listening) but you could, perhaps, pass notes.

Surely everyone had waited, when you took the throne, to see what kind of evil lay in your heart. They said, surely, behind your back, that you would show your true colours soon. Slothful like Anyoë, greedy like Eritanyr, cruel beyond comparison like Shallyr.

In one suggestion, one gesture, you could undo all of that. You would be Emperor Lust, and those who had known how to manage Eritanyr would look at your hands and smile. There would be more candidates purified, in the hope of winning your favour. I shook with fear.

It was possible that the damage was already done. He had been in your bed twice. Was that enough? Had I already destroyed everything I had worked so hard for? 

If the reactions of Lord Igel, the Master of Etiquette, and the Master of Ceremonies were any reflection, then it was too late. You were known to have taken a concubine. To have assaulted a commoner, one of your own staff no less. And then to see no shame in that, but be preparing to parade it before nine worlds. As your justly taken tithe.

I shook.

I so wished that I could trust my household, my guards, not to gossip. I did not know if I could trust them, so I trusted no one. 

So my thoughts circled, unable to break out of the loop. The panic slowly rising. And with that, always the danger that my magic might try to break out, and kill me.

Count seven to breath in, count seven to breath out.

I could see the desk, a sliver of daylight through the window, a book of poetry from Voonra, a length of crimson velvet, a bottle of ink.

In, out.

I could feel the cold marble floor, the worn patch of upholstery of the chair next to me under my fingers, the smooth wood of the door behind my head, the bunching of my robes against my back.

In, out.

I could hear the guards shuffling outside the door, my own breathing, the slither of my sandals as they slid along the floor.

In, out.

I could smell the rose perfume they had doused me in today, and some kind of nutty oil in the cosmetics.

In, out.

I could taste the memory of tea in my mouth. Astringent and herbal.

In, out.

My allotted time was up. I stood up, you opened the door. Sayo Mdang had left. You went to bathe and be dressed for evening court.

After court, you lay in bed, unable to sleep. This was not unusual. The lights and sounds of court, followed by the stress of being undressed. You often lay there, in your own glow, ruminating over the events of the day.

After exhausting all your usual tricks to sleep, you finally reached out for a book. It was the book you had taken from Sayo Mdang. Was it self-justifying, to think it was better to steal a book and read it, than steal it and let it lie unread? You decided to read it, and perhaps then to keep it somewhere Sayo Mdang could read it as well, if he wished.

The rhythm of the poetry, the back-and-forth of the challenges, was soothing. These were surely tales of the people of the Wide Seas. Were they the songs of Sayo Mdang's people? You wondered if he had known these as a child, had imagined himself in them, as I had in the poetry I had loved.

About half-way through the book, you reached a story you recognised. This version named the Seafarer King as Elonoa'a. Not a king, but a paramount chief. It was engrossing, to read this story that you knew, but didn't know. And yes, your ancestor was once again stolen by the Sun (that tale that kept you in the shade). But this time, Elonoa'a sailed to the Devine Lands in search of him.

If the Sun stole you, nobody would come. Your heart ached for a friend. Once, you had nine. You would give almost anything for just one. Someone to care if I was gone. Not the Emperor, of course many people would care if the Emperor vanished. But me.

You couldn't face reading anymore. Eyes itching from tears, you put the book away. Curling up, a ball of light in a bed that could have been as large as a boat.

You had dreams of the islands Elonoa'a knew. Dreams of a friend, on a small boat, in a large ocean. In your dreams, I was free.

After the breakdown in the private study, it was probably unsurprising that those sweet dreams turned to nightmares.
