The Master of Offices sent a new secretary the next day. The poor young lordling practically tripped into the obeisance. You told him to rise, trying to project calm, but by the time he got to the desk he was pale and sweating. He shook so badly he couldn't keep up with your dictation.

You decided to take pity on him at the end of the first letter. “When you have finished that, please swap with Sayo Mdang,” you said, as you turned in your pacing.

Relief was clear across his face. He didn't say anything at all, but abandoned his notes to flee to the desk in the antechamber.

Sayo Mdang entered, performed the obeisance as he had been trained. He rose when I gave the sign, and sat at the desk. Once again, you had an efficient morning of work, with a competent secretary.

After lunch, you heard petitions from various nobles. Sayo Mdang returned to his desk to be the recording secretary. As you listened to the complaints of one Duke against another (of course very politely worded), you looked across at Sayo Mdang. His eyes sparkled, as his pen sang across the page.

You bathed, and were dressed, those eyes and that small smile stuck in your mind. He was, as far as you could tell, genuinely enjoying the work. You found this slightly baffling, you couldn't understand why anyone would want to listen to overstuffed lords complain that their neighbours were rude to them at dinner. But apparently Sayo Mdang did. Maybe he could see the humour in their self-importance.

It was a court dinner, and so you ate, and watched a hundred other people eat, and they watched you. You wondered if Sayo Mdang was eating in the Tower refectory, and what was being served there tonight. Surely not lemony chicken covered in gold leaf (what was even the point? did people enjoy the glitter at the other end?)

Your courtiers danced, and you watched their swirls and eddies. Across the seas, islands, and continents of five world. You wondered if any of them followed the paths of those adventurers, Those Who Live Under the River of Stars. You watched those dancing on Zunidh, and as you saw flashes of land and sea, between arms and robes, you named the places you knew.

---

As you entered your dressing room, you asked if someone could bring the book of poetry for you to read that night. You stepped up, and stood on the plinth. The marble base on which they painted and unpainted the idol of their Emperor.

The fear of touching someone accidentally again had dulled over the years, but the chances were highest when they were so close. You closed your eyes, and focused all your thoughts on staying absolutely and perfectly still. Counting your breaths, ignoring any sound, any feeling on your body.

It was never an exercise conductive to sleep.

When you entered your bedroom, Sayo Mdang was there. For a moment you had a terrified jolt that the nightmare had happened, and they would force him…

But no. He was dressed in his uniform. He bowed low to you, a book in his hands. *The* book, you noticed. He placed it on your nightstand, and stood back, waiting to be dismissed.

“Is this your book, Sayo Mdang?” you asked, suppressing how tired you felt. How much you did not want to be dealing with this problem. How scared you had just been.

He bowed again. “It was, Glorious One. But I know every word by heart, so if it pleases your Radiancy, I would be most glad for you to have it.”

There could be nothing that you could see and desire that could be denied to you. No fine jewel, no masterful artwork, no mortal body. No book, however treasured by the current owner. You made the gesture to accept a tithe, your heart once again aching.

You dismissed them all, and got into bed. You left the book on the nightstand. You *had* been looking forward to reading it, but that had been tainted now. You had stolen the book. You, who had a copy of every book published in the Empire, more than any library or bookstore. You had stolen from a man who surely could have only brought a few books with him on the long journey across the Wide Seas and then to another world.

You were a monster. You cried, silently. Your tears sparkled in your own glow.

---

The next morning you were determined to send Sayo Mdang back to his home in the Wide Seas. It did not matter how much you enjoyed his competence as a secretary. It did not matter how much you enjoyed his warm eyes and gentle smiles.

Your happiness would be temporary, until you hurt him again. And you would, the Empire would. Perhaps, if you sent him away, you could protect him from yourself.

You entered your official study at the usual time, to find Sayo Mdang already at the desk. He stood, and made his obeisance smoothly. You gave the sign to rise, and he smiled as he resumed his seat.

You would miss that smile.

“Sayo Mdang, I will be sending you back to Zunidh,” I said, unable to look at him.

“My lord?” he asked, confused.

The Emperor could not apologise, because the Emperor could do no wrong. But Sayo Mdang deserved an apology.

“The way you have been treated here has been abominable. Intolerable. We will send you with compensation and appoint you to a position in the Ministry of Tithes. We know this cannot make up for all the indignities you have suffered here, but we hope it offers some solace and comfort.”

Sayo Mdang slid out of his chair, and prostrated himself on the floor.

“My lord, please, I apologise unreservedly if I have given any offence, or failed you in any way. But, Glorious One, I beg you, do not send me away.”

I looked at him out of the corner of my eye. He was shaking. He thought he was in disgrace.

“Sayo Mdang, you have done nothing wrong. You have been our most competent secretary, and been courteous in all your actions. We are sending you away for your own protection.” 

His shaking reduced slightly. “My lord, it has been the greatest honour of my life to serve you,” he looked up at you, and saw that you still were not looking at him. “If there is anything at all I could do to persuade you to let me stay, my lord, I would do it gladly. Truly these past weeks have been a joy and a wonder, Glorious One.”

You had to face him. It did you no credit to hide your face from the one man in the palace for whom it was safe to look. You turned, robes flaring gently around you. The elegant rustle of silk felt like a gong in the tense silence.

You were intensely aware of your guards listening, and no doubt others with ears against the doors. If you had your magic, you could have raised the Wall of Silence, and had some privacy for your questions and assurances. Instead, everything you wanted to say to Sayo Mdang would be passed on to all your household, and probably the entire Palace.

The only method of communication you had, that nobody else could see or hear, was your eyes. You looked into his, brimming with tears. You felt your own respond, and blinked back any dampness. You felt like you were swimming in his eyes, trapped by their plea.

It should be a fine thing, a young man who wanted to join the government. It should be a privilege for the government of Astandalas to benefit from the knowledge of *all* its peoples. But all these people in this terrible place had taken this young man, and seen him as nothing more than an exotic body to tempt their idol, their puppet, into compliance.

And still he wanted to stay! What life could he make here, as the two of you attempted to dance around the court's assumptions? If in every hall, in every room where you were not there for his protection, he would be subject to snide remarks? Could they take him seriously enough as a part of your government for him to achieve his dreams any more?

It would be so cruel, however, for you to force him to give those dreams up. Another tithe, another demand. When you had told him you would send him away, he had not thanked you for your generosity. A courter would have at least done that, even if they felt obliged to protest at being sent away.

Those eyes, those eyes, begging you to let him stay. Begging me, for I was sure now that he had seen past the magnificence and glory, and seen something of the man hidden underneath.

Your heart, unlike that of the Empire, was not made of stone.

“Very well, Sayo Mdang, you may stay. But you have my word to release you back to your homeland whenever you ask, as Prefect of Tithes for the Wide Seas.” You breathed, counting slowly, and gave the gesture again to rise.

Sayo Mdang resumed his seat. “My most humble and heartfelt thanks, Glorious and Illustrious One.”

We both took a few moments to compose ourselves. Then you settled into the mask of serenity once more, and resumed your usual pacing route. 

“Has there been any update from Agriculture on Ysthar?” you asked, picking a topic almost at random.

“Yes, my lord,” said Sayo Mdang, and so flowed on the rest of your morning, as if the same as yesterday. As if you had not almost beard your heart to Sayo Mdang. Your secretary.
