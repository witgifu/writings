Robin looked over at Raphael, curled improbably against a box. It didn't look like a natural way for a human body to bend.

“Hey,” he whispered, “You awake?” 

Robin tried to reach out his feet to poke the man, but they didn't quite reach. He grumbled at the chains. Rope he could have done something about, but metal didn't like him. It was unfair, because most things in this cupboard were metal or plastic.

Eventually he found a forgotten paper straw in a bucket. It took some convincing, but he eventually convinced it to wiggle over to Raphael and jab him in the lips. Robin tried not to think about if the straw had been used.

Raphael groaned, and writhed into a position more commonly associated with living humans.

“In a cupboard, chained to the shelves, no I don't know how to pick locks,” said Robin, watching his friend intently.

“Ugh?” said Raphael.

For a moment Robin worried he was more hurt than he looked, but then Raphael blinked open his eyes and said, “Ow. I'm too old to sleep like that.”

“I think technically you were passed out, not asleep,” said Robin, in a falsely cherry voice.

“Magic?” asked Raphael, looking around at the cupboard.

“No, they punched you,” said Robin, with a smile. “I haven't seen a punch that good since they closed the East London bear pits. And that was a bear. You were hit by a human.”

Raphael nodded absently, and their chains did a strange wiggle, and suddenly weren't holding them.

“Before you go walking through the ship and locking everyone else in cupboards, we're still in the middle of the Indian Ocean. And neither of us knows how to sail,” said Robin, aware that sometimes his friend acted first.

“I know how to sail,” said Raphael, slightly defensively, “But this doesn't look like a yacht.”

“It's some kind of fishing vessel,” said Robin, “Although the machine gun turrets are likely to be an after-market addition.”

“Ah,” said Raphael, “And I probably don't want 'James Inelu Captures Pirates!' headlines.”

When the pirate captain realised he had _the_ James Inelu, he doubled the ransom.

“How do you think I would get that much cash to a boat in the Indian Ocean?” said Raphael, fully in the persona of the film star.

It wasn't the first time Raphael had been captured by pirates, Robin was reasonably sure. But it might have been the first time since the invention of the wire transfer.

Robin, who had claimed to be Mr. Inelu's translator, repeated the question in Arabic. The pirates claimed they didn't speak English, and Robin thought it would be suspicious if he spoke Somali. The Italian pidgin they'd also offered could have worked if he'd responded in modern Italian but doing that would have given him a headache. So, like centuries of people in these waters, they'd settled on Arabic as a language they could all speak well enough to get by with.

“There's a Western Union office next to my brother's house,” said the captain, “That'll work nicely.”

For appearances’ sake, Robin repeated the answer.

“The banks will lock my accounts when they see the money is going to Somalia,” said James, “The UK has a policy of not paying ransoms.”

Robin was relieved to know that Raphael did have some awareness of how modern financial systems worked. Perhaps his insistence on never having a credit card made more sense now.

“The Americans don't care,” snapped the captain, “A fancy film star like you has offshore accounts.”

Now Robin was getting worried. Weren't pirates mostly fishermen and ex-mercs? How many rich businessmen had they taken hostage that they even knew what 'offshore accounts' were?

Something of his surprise must have shown, because the captain addressed him directly. “A rapper from Mogadishu made a song about the Panama Papers,” he said, tersely.

“I do have an account in LA,” said James, “But I'd have to call my lawyer, and I don't think it has much. Possibly only enough to get him to answer the phone.”

“Not my problem,” said the captain, tired of negotiating.

Robin wasn't quite sure why Raphael seemed so sure it was a bad idea to throw all the pirates overboard by magic. Sure, it was hardly the proper legal procedure, but they were _pirates_. 'Live by the sword, die by the sword', or in this case the rocket-propelled grenade launcher. There weren't even that many of them, as it turned out, only a dozen.

Raphael was making Robin translate a set of stupidly detailed instructions to get the pirate captain to contact his lawyer in LA. He claimed this was to help them avoid detection, and to verify with his lawyer that the instructions were genuine. Robin was reasonably sure that Raphael was stalling for time, but couldn't figure out why, when the pirates were becoming increasingly twitchy.

Then he saw the white dot on the horizon rapidly resolve into two boats moving at a fantastic speed. Before Robin even had time to do anything more than take a deep breath, they had fired and taken out both the front and rear gun platforms.

“Oh good,” said Raphael, “They got my message.”

The Indian Navy were _very_ polite, when they boarded. At first, Robin thought this was because they recognised Raphael as James Inelu, but after the third time they had been asked to explain what had happened, he started to get suspicious.

It was the Lieutenant who finally explained. “There's nothing better than rescuing English toffs,” she said, reviewing something at the computer, “It's so satisfying.”

Robin looked at Raphael, who mirrored his look of confusion. Robin had, he supposed, been speaking fairly posh, but only because 'BBC London' tended to be better understood than his preferred 'Broad Yorkshire'. Raphael had been using James' 'Mid-Atlantic' accent.

“You're obviously not Americans, because you didn't immediately demand to see your Ambassador,” said the Lieutenant, “Anyway, the fun bit is getting to call up your navy and telling them to come collect you. They used to patrol the whole of the Indian Ocean, but I guess you finally figured out you'd actually lost all the colonies.”

All the Indian sailors were whistling or humming 'Rule Britannia' as Robin and Raphael climbed into the little inflatable boat, behind the two very stern looking members of the British Navy. Who must have forgotten their sunscreen, because they couldn't possibly be blushing.
