The office manager gave Kip a look that told him she thought he shouldn't be here.

“Excuse me, Saya,” he said, “I need to book passage to Gorjo City in the Vangaveye-ve.”

“For who?” she said, clearly deciding from his accent that he was a servant of some more important person.

Cliopher moved closer to the desk, “For myself, Saya, Cliopher Mdang.”

She sighed, “If you head down to the port—”

“I am aware, Saya,” he said, interrupting her, “I understand that passengers travel on merchant ships, and that berths on postal ships are reserved for officials travelling in the course of their duties.”

He leaned forward, “I am _also_ aware that at other times they are sold to those willing to spend the money to keep the crew quiet.”

She looked at him more closely, and then her eyes flicked to the charter on the wall. “I do not know where you have heard such slander, Sayo Mdang,” she said, “But of course _you_ would be travelling on official business, sir, so there would be no problem at all.”

“I am not,” said Cliopher, “Travelling on official business.”

“Oh,” she said, a little tension now creeping into her voice, “But sir, surely… for you…?”

Cliopher sighed, and withdrew a letter from his writing kit. She picked it up, and flipped it open. He had refused to sign it himself, or even write it, arguing it was an abuse of his position. His Radiancy had arranged for Kiri to write it, and had sealed it himself, before presenting it to Cliopher as a fait accompli.

It fell from the office manager's shaking fingers onto the desk. “I am of course happy to do whatever the Glorious One commands, sir,” she said, “If you would kindly make your way to the port, I will have the captain meet you and depart within the day.”

She slid the letter back to Cliopher, as if it might suddenly burst into flames.

“I hope you will treat the auditors with all respect when they arrive this afternoon,” said Cliopher, and taking the letter, turned around without waiting for a reply.
