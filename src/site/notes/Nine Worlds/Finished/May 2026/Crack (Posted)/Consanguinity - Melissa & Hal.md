There was a knock at the door. It was strange, nobody ever knocked. What was the point, when it was locked from the outside? Only the priest-wizards and servants came, the second cowed by the first. The Glorious One seemed afraid to execute her, even though he could have simply starved her in this corner of the Palace of Stars and nobody would have known.

The knock came again, and Melissa realised they were actually going to wait for a response. "Come in," she called out, her voice a little horse from disuse.

Two priest-wizards opened the door, but behind them wasn't the Lord Emperor, come to gloat. Nor was it one of the ritual executioners, robed in white. It was Thalassior Barred, Lord Magus of Ysthar.

Melissa scrambled to her feet, and bowed low. She respected the man's political and magical skills. Even when the entire court had turned against her, after five years without not just an heir, but a single pregnancy, he had not. Nor had he dared defend her, but even to step aside and not denounce her, had been an enormous risk.

"To what do I owe this honour, my lord?" she asked. Kept away from any information, she had no idea what purpose he might have. Was he here to steal her away? Would she be a hostage in a civil war?

Thalassior, however, prostrated himself at her feet. "Glorious One," he said to the floor, "Emperor Shallyr is dead."

A tiny spark of hope kindled in her heart. She wasn't free, she had known since she was a small child that she would live and die in the service of the Empire. But perhaps, there had been some justice, that she should outlive her tormentor.

Three days later, Melissa sat on a golden chair, in the Lilly Room. What staff remained in the Tower had done their best to make her presentable. Magic to soften and even out scars. Cosmetics to cover wrinkles and mask sleep-deprived eyes. Dye for her hair, paint for her nails, and everywhere gems sparkled.

Spears stamped, and she gestured to admit the guests.

"His Highness, General Prince Benneret Halioren; His Grace the Honourable Halioren Lord Leaveringham," said the guard on the left. All the guards had changed since Melissa had last been in the Tower. She needed to learn their names.

In came the General, in his full dress uniform, followed by his great-nephew, who looked rather more nervous. The General saluted, and his young namesake made a very well-practiced obeisance.

Melissa gestured for the young duke to rise, keeping her gaze firmly behind them both. As yet uncrowned, she couldn't blind them. But it was important to practice. The coronation was only days away. It was amazing how quickly it could be arranged, given how long it had taken to plan her wedding.

"Your highness," said Melissa, choosing the political title carefully, "Your grace, to what do we owe this pleasure?"

A life of training, and a decade chained to a tyrant, had taught her absolute control. So she made no visible reaction, when General Ben, one of Astandala's heroes, dropped into the full formal prostration before her. As did the young duke.

"Glorious One, I have come as a representative of our family, to beg for your mercy," he said, and Melissa could hear only the voice of one who had defended his men and stayed to the end at Loe.

"The Kings of Rhondé have ever been our loyal subjects," she said, carefully, "We know of no reason to doubt you, Prince Benneret, Duke Halioren."

"In the reign of your uncle, Glorious One, there was a disagreement between the Emperor Eritanyr and his younger brother," said Prince Benneret.

Melissa knew the cause of their dispute, although perhaps nobody else alive did. Her mother had told her, all those years ago. When she had first learnt she had an older brother. Someday, perhaps, she would look for what had happened to him.

"The Emperor's brother left, many thought he had left the Empire entirely. Emperor Eritanyr forbade anyone to talk of him, Glorious One," continued the prince, his voice perfectly court-formal, "He went to Rhondé, to the court of my mother. He had a daughter, who married my nephew. This is their son, most merciful and illustrious lady."

"Our cousin's son," said Melissa, carefully, "We see no reason to uphold Eritanyr's decrees about his brother. There is nothing to forgive. Please rise, both of you."

They both did so, with a fluidity that spoke of long training for court. "My eternal thanks, Glorious One, for your generosity towards our family," said Prince Benneret, "I had only thought to plead for the lives of the people of Rhondé, and did not imagine that you could be so merciful."

This, Melissa knew, was not at all true. They had protected their secret through most of Eritanyr's rule and all of Shallyr's. Yet, there must be some reason to admit to the truth now. Some gain they could see, or some risk that had grown.

With a twist in her heart, she saw it. Eritanyr had managed the Imperial Family tree, carefully matching members to best advantage himself. His son had pruned it ruthlessly. Now, the King of Rhondé had seen the chance to cash in his mother's bet. How had he moved so quickly? Or was this Prince Benneret's initiative?

"Is your mother still with us, cousin?" asked Melissa, turning to young Halioren. Whatever the machinations of his family, she thought the boy was probably not the instigator.

"She is, Glorious One," he said, his eyes carefully on her hands, "Her father, my grandfather, died before I was born."

"And how old are you, cousin?" said Melissa, once again granting him the familial term. Knowing that inside, Prince Benneret must be thrilled.

"It is my ninth birthday today, Glorious One," said Halioren.

Melissa doubted it was, but it might be near enough as to make no difference. Old enough to be presented at court. Not, thankfully, old enough to be betrothed or married. He would have to wait until he was sixteen for the betrothal, and then a year more to be made Consort.

"We welcome you to our court, cousin," said Melissa. 

The same formal words that Shallyr had spoken to her when she was nine, and he twenty-nine. She felt sick, dizzy, disgusted. Would she do to this boy what Shallyr had done to her? No. She would not. Perhaps she couldn't give him his freedom to choose, to love. But she would give him whatever she could. 

"My eternal thanks, Glorious One, for your extraordinary kindness to me and my family," said Halioren.

It was not, Melissa knew, a kindness. Sending him home, denying the connection, would have been the kindest thing she could have done for him. But Prince Benneret had come and offered the support of the Kind of Rondé, the army, and his own, not insubstantial, political weight. It was an offer she could not afford to refuse.

Melissa didn't know if she was barren, or if the problem had been with Shallyr. The best healers in the Empire had declared that there were no issues with either, and so the priest-wizards had decided it had been the choice of the gods. They had lied, of course. In another ten years, however, she might well be beyond having children.

"Cousin Halioren. We shall grant that you be known also as the Grand Duke of Damara on Zunidh, and all the rights and property associated with that," said Melissa. It was her first serious appointment, having not yet been satisfied with any of the candidates for the high posts of her household. Apart from demanding the Tower be returned to the styles of Empress Anyoë, she had as yet made no waves.

She couldn't tell if Prince Benneret was surprised by this declaration, but she did manage to catch the young Halioren's astonishment. 

"If your mother, our cousin, would like to attend court, she would be most welcome. But we understand that she may prefer a quiet life, and would not wish to disturb her if that is the case," said Melissa, "We look forward to seeing you both again."

The General and the boy accepted this as their dismissal.

Melissa did not cry.
