Cliopher had never written a letter to his Radiancy. He’d written many, many letters *from* his Radiancy, and read many letters addressed to him. He’d also written, edited, reviewed, or summarised, any number of reports for him. Somehow, writing a note to his lord, seemed awfuly forward.

On any other day, he would have simply included the draft report with the morning reports, and added a cover message pointing out the unusual difference. But Cliopher hadn’t collated the morning reports today.

His other approach would have been to try and raise it in conversation. That required more skill, as it was his Radiancy’s role to set the topic of discussion. Cliopher had worked on bringing his Radiancy around to a topic that Cliopher felt they needed to cover. That was also not going to be possible today.

Cliopher also wanted to restate some of what he had said the night before. He didn’t want his lord to think that Cliopher had any change of heart in the light of day. He also wanted to apologise again for not realising what was happening. Topics also probably best approached in person. 

But Cliopher was not going to disobey his lord’s direct order to stay in bed.

He sat long enough trying to work out how to address the note, that the ink dried on his pen. Sighing, he cleaned it off on the rag, and waited until he was sure to dip it again.

There was no correct etiquette for sending a note to his lord, because he wasn’t supposed to do it at all. If his Radiancy had sent Cliopher a message asking after his well-being, the correct response would be a grovelling letter apologising for the inconvenience he was causing (the exact kind of letter his lord would find tiresome).

However, he could hardly write to his Radiancy as he would Vinyë or Basil. It would not be right (even if in his heart he wished it could be, wished he could wipe away the terror he had seen on his lord’s face with anecdotes and jokes).

He made an attempt anyway.

> I humbly beseech your Radiancy, to pardon your ~~servant~~ secretary to be so bold in sending to know how my lord does and is, being the thing I most ~~pray~~ hope for, his happiness. For myself, I am feeling much ~~better~~ recovered and am thankful for your forbearance. I will continue as you have kindly instructed, and hope to return to you shortly.
> 
> I therefore ~~plead~~ repeat again what I have said before, that I would offer you ~~myself~~ any aid I may, and that it is my greatest desire always to serve you and ~~your~~ ~~our~~ the people of Zunidh. This despite my many recent failings, for which I can only plead your forgiveness, most gracious lord. I dearly hope ~~your~~ the holy priests in the Ouranatha can be ~~taught~~ persuaded the same, even though the most recent audit would suggest they have been even more ~~wasteful~~ profligate with their most precious stores, ~~wasting~~ those things which may no longer be replaced. 
> 
> Ever your devoted Cliopher Mdang

Cliopher read it back and grimaced. It was both too formal to be a letter from him, and too informal to be sent to the Lord of Rising Stars. He put it aside, and finished the letter to his mother.

After some more history, and then lunch, Cliopher wrote out a fair copy of the note. He hadn’t had any better ideas. He folded in half, but didn’t seal it. He didn’t want Conju to think he was trying to sneak in work. If Conju read it there was nothing incriminating. 

Except that he had written at all. Cliopher hoped Conju wouldn’t be too offended by this breach of etiquette, and would be willing to deliver it.

He put the finished letter and note to one side. He was sore again, and could feel his mind fogging up as well. Ruefully, he decided he could probably benefit from a nap. He snuggled back under the covers. With his mind full of his writing, he fell into a dream of trying to introduce his lord to his mother, at a cheese shop in Astandalas.

---

When Cliopher woke, everything had been tidied. His writing kit closed up and tucked away. A proper bookmark instead of the edge of the sheet marked his place in the history. And the letter and note were gone.

He sat up, pulled over his writing kit again, and started working on a letter to Basil.

Cliopher dithered over telling Basil about the purification. He always had to be careful, since he had no idea where these letters ended up, or whose hands they might pass through. He decided to be honest about feelings rather than sharing the facts.

> I made a mistake of omission recently, and I’m afraid it might cost me dearly. I hope there is something I can do to convince my lord that I had the best intentions, and I am willing to weather what may come. But it may be that the public good and justice are best served by my dismissal. Or that, and I can hardly bear to think it, he no longer trusts me.

He took a break for a deep breath and to make sure he didn’t drip tears onto the page. It was true, when he let himself think about it, he still was afraid. 

> If that does happen, I will try to get to Alinore to visit you. I am not sure I could face the journey home only to tell my mother of my disgrace.
>
> I am staying at the moment with my friend Conju while I convalesce from what I am sure is nothing more than one too many late nights or skipped lunches. Nevertheless, my lord has ordered me to bed, and so I am writing to you from there. I hope it is only a couple of days enforced idleness or I might go quite spare.

While Cliopher was trying to decide whether to compare Conju’s care to his mother or his sister, the man in question arrived.

“I am writing a letter to my cousin Basil,” declared Cliopher, “and I have read some Alinorel history, and I have even had a nap.”

Conju smiled slightly, “I suppose I should check your books to make sure you haven’t squirrelled away a report in it.”

“They’re your books Conju,” said Cliopher, grinning back, “So any reports hidden in them would have to have been planted.”

“I know you adore forward planning, but I don’t think even you could have predicted this, so you are probably correct.”

Cliopher nibbled at his lower lip. “Did you, uh, get a chance to—?”

Conju gave Cliopher one of his most withering looks. “A personal note to the Glorious One, yes, I consented to your treasonous lack of tact, and gave it to him.”

“And?” asked Cliopher, nervously grasping at the sheet.

”He read it, and put it in the pocket of his court robes,” replied Conju, with a sniff, “It almost ruined the outline, that pocket is supposed to be just for attaching a jewelled pin closure, it’s not ment to hold anything.”

”Sorry Conju, thank you Conju,” said Cliopher.

“Don’t patronise me, dear, I’m much better at it than you,” replied Conju acerbically, “I’m off to dress for court, I expect you to be asleep when I get back.”

“Yes Mother Conju,” said Cliopher, in a sing-song voice.

Conju huffed, and left the room. “Eat all your dinner!” he called back as he walked down the hallway.

Cliopher didn’t retort. His Radiancy had read the note. He’d wanted to keep it with him, through court. Why? 

Did he not want anyone else to read it, and didn’t have anywhere else to put it? But he was a powerful mage, surely he could have easily smudged it. Or burnt it, or turned it to powder, or even vanished it (Cliopher was not actually sure if mages could do that). He could have thrown it into his private study on the way past. He needn’t have kept it.

Did he want to keep it as evidence? Oh no. Cliopher had overstepped. Compounding this new error on top of yesterday might be too much. His lord would quietly send Cliopher away, like he had threatened to last night. That was surely it. He was planning to give it to the guard captain.

Cliopher could barely eat anything at dinner, despite Conju’s admonition. He pushed the food around the plate and tried to think what he could do to repair things. He would need to apologise in person, but he was ordered to stay in bed. He would have to wait until he was summoned. It was going to be an excruciating wait.

After dinner the manservant led him to a bath. Cliopher scrubbed thoroughly, as if he could rub off the shame of his offences against the Sun on Earth. 

Clean, back in his usual sleepwear (and not the underwear the Ournatha had given him), Cliopher returned to the bed. He tried to distract himself with the history book again, but found himself reading and re-reading the same paragraph.

Eventually he pulled out his writing kit again, and continued his letter to Basil.

---

Cliopher finished the letter to Basil, and a response to Louya's latest ramble. After that he picked Ghilly, which was a much more pleasant time. At this rate he might actually write everyone a good long letter.

He was still writing when Conju returned. 

"Did you hear the midnight bell?" Conju asked, pointedly.

"Hmm? Oh, yes, sorry Conju, I just wanted to finish this," replied Cliopher, distracted.

Conju gave Cliopher a piercing stare. "Put that all away now, or I won't give you this," said Conju, holding up a folded slip of paper.

Cliopher finally looked up, confused. "Is that—," he asked, a tremor in his voice. He gripped the sheets to stop himself reaching out and grabbing.

Conju gave Cliopher a significant look. "His Radiancy spent a full half hour after court finished writing this, and was late to bed."

Cliopher bit his lip. They both knew that his Radiancy never got enough sleep. Conju did everything in his power to see that his Radiancy got what he could by getting to bed on time. Conju had the post-court routine down to a speed art. He would not forgive Cliopher for being the cause of keeping his Radiancy awake.

“I’m sorry Conju,” said Cliopher with genuine contrition, “I will read it now and if a reply is needed I will make sure it is ready to include in the usual morning documents.”

Conju sniffed haughtily. “I should think so. I’ll have a page sent down at dawn to clear your outbox.”

“Thank you, Conju, for everything,” said Cliopher, and took the slip of paper from between Conju’s elegant fingers. He managed to resist reading it until Conju had wished him goodnight and left the room.

With trembling hands, Cliopher unfolded the note.

> Cliopher,
> You have done *nothing* wrong, and have *nothing* to apologise for. I only want to make sure that, having been denied your *right* to choose previously, you are given every chance to make a *free* and *fair* choice from now on. We give you our word to respect your choice, and will do all We can to support you.
>
> We are glad to hear you are feeling better. Please take *all* the time you need to recover. We know such things can be very draining.
>
> Do not worry about anyone else. We will cut out the rot of corruption. 
>
> — 𝓐 

Cliopher held the note to his chest. His lord was not angry. Tears of relief spilled down his face.

He should write back. As an indication of how tired he was, all he could think was a wordless fog of thanks. It wouldn’t be appropriate to just fill a page with “thank you” repeated over and over again, even if he was feeling very tired. Gathering his remaining energy, he wrote out a more coherent reply.

> I could not be more plentiful in giving thanks for the extraordinary kindness received from your Radiancy. Truly, I was full of sorrow at the thought of departing from your Radiancy’s wonderful presence. Always it is your good opinion that matters to me most, and I was full of shame at the thought that I might have caused you any distress. 
>
> Ever your devoted Cliopher Mdang

He stacked all the finished letters on the dresser, and packed up his writing kit. Then finally, his head starting to ache a little, he drifted off to sleep. Still holding the note from his Radiancy.
