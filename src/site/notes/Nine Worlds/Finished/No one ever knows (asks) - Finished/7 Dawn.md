Ludvic showed Cliopher a route he'd had no idea about. A wall panel that looked like all the others. Ludvic pushed it with his free hand, and it bounced open to reveal a staircase. 

Of course there were other routes through the Imperial Appartments, Cliopher realised. You couldn’t go hauling bags of onions or dirty laundry through the ceremonial entrance and the offical study. Maybe in the days of Astandalas they had done it all by magic. Probably not.

The stairs came out into corridor, and then there were more stairs, and more corridors. Cliopher focused on walking, and holding onto Ludvic. There was only magelight, but Cliopher was too tired to notice if that was because it was nighttime or there were no windows.

Eventually they came out into a broad public hallway, that Cliopher recognised. He could see the door to Conju’s apartments. He tried to stand taller, lean less obviously on Ludvic. The hall was empty now, but a page or a courtier could rush through at any moment.

They walked casually over to Conju’s door. Ludvic knocked. The door was opened so quickly there had to have been someone waiting on the other side. Conju’s major-domo bowed them in, and shut the door behind them.

Cliopher took a deep breath. Nobody had seen them, or if anyone had caught a glimpse it was just of Conju’s two friends arriving for drinks, as they sometimes did. Or had, until all Cliopher’s spare time had been taken up by—. He grimaced, and abandoned the thought.

Ludvic and Cliopher were shown not to Conju’s reception room, or the more private parlour they often visited, where Conju had is magnificent displays. They were shown to a guest bedroom, that Cliopher hadn’t noticed.

Of course the an Vilius apartment would have more bedrooms. Before the Fall surely more of his family had lived here, or at least visited regularly. Cliopher wondered how empty it had felt, immediately after the Fall. Somehow knowing someone would return to a room made it feel less empty. So very many rooms in the palace still had not been used since the Fall.

The room was minimally decorated, by Conju’s standards. A cupboard, a comfortable chair, a dresser, and a sumptuous bed. Ludvic helped Cliopher over to the bed, and he collapsed onto it. There was a window, which showed it was indeed night proper, although from the bed Cliopher could only see a piece of black sky.

Despite Cliopher’s protests that he could do it himself (he couldn’t), a manservant and Ludvic helped him out of his outer robes. Thankfully he had already had a bath. He was efficiently tucked into bed.

Cliopher relaxed, finally. The bed was very comfortable. He’d been ordered into it, and the ache in his body was finally starting to fade.

“Sleep well, Cliopher,” said Ludvic, as he closed the door.

For several hours, Cliopher did indeed have a dreamless sleep.

---

Cliopher woke, suddenly, from a dream of drowning. It was a mix of his father's death, pearl diving, and the Fall. The fear filtered through as he noticed he wasn't in his own bed or bedroom. Nor was he in any room he recognised.

He tried to calm his breathing. Slowly, deeply.

There was a knock at the door. "Come in," he called out.

A manservant came in, with a breakfast tray. It even had a little vase with a single hibiscus. "Good morning, sir," he said.

Cliopher racked his brain to identify the face. It eventually clicked, he was one of Conju's staff. Cliopher smiled, and returned the greeting.

As the man bustled around, settling the tray, Cliopher's memory caught him up. He was in Conju's apartment. Because Conju didn't trust him to look after himself. Because his Radiancy had ordered three days of bed rest. On the physician's recommendation. Because the Ouranatha had—

Ah. A little shiver went through him, as he pulled himself up to sit. Not at the memory of what the Ouranatha had done, but at the memory of his Radiancy when he realised what had happened. The shattering of the serenity. Not the usual glimpse of wry humour Cliopher like to try and elicit from his lord, but terror.

"Is there anything I can get you sir? Are you cold, I could fetch a bed jacket?" the manservant asked, having seen Cliopher's reaction.

"No, thank you, I'm fine," said Cliopher, "Just remembering a dream. This breakfast looks delicious!"

The tray did indeed look impressive. Cliopher often just grabbed a sweet bun and a coffee of a morning. Here was a selection of small items; a bun, a cup of soup, a grilled sardine, and some artfully arranged sliced fruits. Plus a generous cup of coffee.

The delecate white porcelain was painted beautifully with landscapes. Cliopher suspected that if he looked closely there would be snakes in the trees. But instead he tucked in to the soup.

The manservent left, quietly, while Cliopher was occupied with the soup. His aches had returned. But he noticed the bottle of pills from the previous day was also on the tray, and he took another. The savoury soup broth had a slight seaweed flavour. Cliopher made his way through the dishes. 

The bun was excellent, soft and sweet. The fruit was middling, which was always to be expected in the Palace of Stars. Cliopher wondered if it was because it often had to come so far, or if because the Emperor couldn't eat fresh fruit they didn't bother bringing the very best.

When the manservant came to clear away the tray, Cliopher asked him politely if he might have his writing kit. The manservant gave Cliopher a wary look, "I will need to ask my lord, sir."

Cliopher sighed. He couldn't possibly _sleep_ for three days. He couldn't play his oboe in bed (well, he kind of could, but the posture was not good). He couldn't walk in the gardens in bed. He didn't embroider like Conju, he couldn't whittle like Ludvic (although for a moment he did enjoy imaging Conju's face if he came in to find the bed covered in tiny wood shavings).

"Would sir like to read something? Novels, poetry, history?" offered the manservant, in a conciliatory manner.

Cliopher agreed that a history would be fine, and the manservant went and retrieved a selection. Two of them were pre-Fall, and two more recent. Cliopher picked the pre-Fall Alionrel history.

When the next hour bell rang three, Cliopher realised that he must have slept unusually late. He supposed that maybe four weeks of daily purification _had_ been harder on his body than his usually routines. Then he brushed the thought aside, and resumed disagreeing with the author of the book about the importance of centralised banking.

---

At the next bell, Conju came to visit. Immediately on entering, he held up a hand to forestall Cliopher's questions. 

"His Radiancy appeared perfectly fine this morning, and is reviewing his correspondance with sayu Kalikiri. He will spend the afternoon on magical workings, and the evening at court. Sayu Kalikiri told me she is relieved you have been ordered to bed, as she hadn't been able to convince you to take time off."

He paused, to give Cliopher an unsympathetic look.

Wanting to convince Conju he could be trusted, Cliopher attempted to bow an apology sitting in bed. It didn't really work, he wasn't sitting up enough, but Conju at least got the idea that Cliopher felt some kind of contrition.

"Could I have my writing kit please? I'm afraid it's in my room..." asked Cliopher tentatively. 

Conju held up the item in question.

"I want it to be very clear that you are to do _no work_," said Conju, as Cliopher reached out, "Personal letters only, yes?" He pulled the box back slightly, and gave Cliopher a serious look.

"Yes, Conju," said Cliopher, dropping his hands, "but if I don't write to my mother soon she's going to make Vinyë send a worried letter and it'll all cascade."

Conju nodded and passed Cliopher the box, "We must not worry Eidora. I will come back and check on you later, alright?"

"Before court?" asked Cliopher, wondering if Conju would be in the mood for gossip collecting or gossip sharing tonight.

Conju smiled, and said, "Audits must be a surprise to be effective." Then he whirled out the door in a swirl of aqua and white silk.

Cliopher did feel a little miffed to have his own words quoted back at him. He had often tried to explain to Conju about the importance of audits and the best way to run them. Conju had tolerated this, as something Cliopher enjoyed, and Conju could put up with. Cliopher now had evidence that Conju had indeed listened!

Cliopher opened his beloved writing kit. He hummed as he pulled out his nearly-finished letter to his mother, and his favourite pen. But Conju’s words had him thinking about audits, not gossip to send to his mother. And there was, tucked in the back, the draft report from the most recent audit of the Ouranatha’s supplies.

Since the incident with the salt, his Radiancy had asked the Ouranatha for an inventory of their stores. When that had arrived late, and very clearly incomplete, Cliopher had instigated an audit. It wasn’t *officially* an audit, as Cliopher didn’t have the power to do that, it was only seconding people to help them complete the inventory. And since then they had worked together regularly, to keep the inventory up-to-date, and help with requests to the Department of Trade.

His fingers itched to pull out the report. He was sure it would be both amusing (how much weight of ruby dust *was* reasonable to use in a year? what about yellow rose petals?) and infuriating (nails and medical supplies they’d been hoarding since before the Fall, still untouched). 

It was just a couple of sheets, the difference between last time and this time, from most-difficult-to-source, to least. Those rose petals, for example, were near the top because they had to be imported from Ysthar under magical containment to stop them wilting. Sawdust was near the bottom, as a waste product that could be acquired in Solaara.

Cliopher pulled open the section the report was in, and leafed through the pages there without pulling them out. If anyone came in, he could say he was looking for his mother’s last letter to make sure he’d answered all here questions. He found the first page, and read the first item.

> Imperial Incense — less 18 boxes (no longer obtainable)

He stopped. Last year they had gone through a single box. Cliopher knew his Radiancy had thought that was profligate, and had requested they only use it when no other substitute could possibly be used. His Radiancy had said that should mean never, the Ouranatha insisted that it was required for all major rituals, they had compromised on just a few major public rituals.

*Eighteen boxes*. What could they possibly—Oh. *Oh*. 

He remembered the smell. He’d assumed they’d been using a mix of types of incense. He’d recognised myrrh and sandalwood, but it hadn’t clicked. They’d been using Imperial incense for every purification ritual.

His Radiancy was going to be furious.

Cliopher’s mind started working. His Radiancy needed leverage against the Ouranatha, a way to punish them for purifying Cliopher without his lord’s request, but without admitting he knew what they’d done. He *could* require them to hand over all their irreplaceable stores, so they could be “better managed”. Forcing them to apply whenever they wanted to use Imperial incense, or Noirel honey, or the bones of extinct animals.

Cliopher put aside the letter to his mother, and started on a note to his Radiancy instead.
