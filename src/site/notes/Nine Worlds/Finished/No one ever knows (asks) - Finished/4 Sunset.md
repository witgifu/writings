There were so many stairs. Cliopher thought he was fit, thought he was used to the long walks and many stairs that made up life in the Palace of Stars. But these few flights (seven? maybe? He was good with numbers, why had he lost count) were excruciating.

As he panted at the top of one flight, one of the guards patrolling the hall noticed. “Sayo Mdang, are you alright?”

Cliopher stared as the familiar uniform swam before his eyes. He wanted to reply in the affirmative, but he was worried if he  nodded he might throw up. “I have been summoned…” he said, weakly.

Thankfully the guard neither insisted he go to his room or to see a doctor. Some of his friends might have, but the guard understood that a summons could only be refused if you were out cold. Cliopher was walking (sort of), so he had to go.

The guard sent his patrol partner off after a page. Then he turned back to Cliopher. “Would you allow me to help you, sayo Mdang?” he asked kindly.

Cliopher took one stumbling step forward. The guard caught him, before he fell into a bust on a plinth. “I am Pikabe, sir, let us get you up to the Glorious One in one piece,” he said, gripping Cliopher firmly but gently around the back.

They weren’t going in the right direction. Or at least, it didn’t feel right to Cliopher’s aching head. He didn’t have the energy to try and explain. Pikabe said he would take him to his Radiancy, he was a guard, Cliopher would just have to trust him.

Indeed, Pikabe was as good as his word, and led Cliopher right to the doors of the Imperial Apartments. Cliopher caught a glimpse of two very worried looking pages, before turning to face the guards there.

The guards were better at hiding their reactions to seeing Cliopher dazed and dressed oddly. Pikabe said to them, “Cliopher sayo Mdang, Hands of the Emperor.” They stamped their spears, and the doors began opening. Pikabe added, “Could someone take him in? I’m not sure he can walk on his own.”

There was a short whispered discussion between the two door guards and Pikabe. The result was that Pikabe continued to support Cliopher as he stumbled in. Doors, spears, doors. Cliopher was beginning to wonder if he was caught in one of those annoying dreams where you walk forever and never reach your goal.

They turned through the study, and to the ivory doors of the inner apartments. Cliopher swallowed. He had been summoned. Even if he desperately wanted to collapse and try again the next day, it didn’t matter.

Those doors opened too. The guards must have been signalling to Pikabe, because without a word he turned confidently down a hallway, and through another room. Cliopher was in no state to notice anything about the decoration except that it was black, white, and gold.

A final set of doors, a final challenge, a final thump of spears. A pause, while the guards passed word through the door. A whispered conversation. Cliopher didn’t catch much of it, except for Pikabe saying “Sayo Mdang said he had been summoned, sir.”

“Yes,” said Cliopher vaguely in response.

Eventually they announced him, and let him in. Cliopher took a few steps forward, out of Pikabe’s support and through the doors. He heard the doors snap closed behind him.

The room swam in his vision. But he could see his lord, a dark smudged shadow, rising up to stand. Cliopher sank into the obeisance. It was not as smooth and practiced as it should have been after so many years (decades?). But he did not disgrace himself by falling down.

There was a long pause. Cliopher imagined that his lord had probably given him the signal to rise, but he couldn’t see it. In any case, having got down to the floor (a lovely soft silk carpet) he wasn’t sure he could get up again. Not without help at least.

“Ludvic, fetch Our doctor and Conju, and help Cliopher to a chair.”

His Radiancy’s voice had the serene tone that Cliopher had learn to associate with contained fury. He pressed himself a little further into the carpet. Had he not been summoned? He struggled to remember the exact words of the acolyte. Maybe in his confusion he had mistook an instruction to return to his lord as meaning immediately when they meant tomorrow.

There were more whispers behind him. Then two strong arms lifted Cliopher and carried him to a divan. Cliopher sank back into the cushions in relief.

Someone else entered the room, and Cliopher heard Conju’s elegant voice. “My lord?” he asked. Although Cliopher couldn’t see him, he could hear confusion in Conju’s voice. Cliopher imagined Conju looking from his Radiancy, full serenity, to Cliopher, collapsed on the divan.

“The Ouranatha have done something to Cliopher, and then sent him to me in this state,” said his Radiancy.

Conju stepped lightly over to Cliopher. “What happened, Cliopher?” he said softly, resting the back of his hand on Cliopher’s forehead. 

“It, it was just a ritual, my lord” said Cliopher. Somehow he still remembered to answer to his Radiancy and not Conju. “Did they not tell you, my lord? I thought…”

It was hard to make words into sentences. Cliopher tried again. “They said, I thought they said, my lord, you needed to finish it?” Yes, that was what he needed to say. And now he had said it. He closed his eyes against the light.

This time it was his Radiancy's sandals that shushed against the carpet, approaching Cliopher. He tried to sit up, it wasn't done to lounge when the Emperor was standing, but Conju put a hand to his shoulder and pushed him back into the cushions.

"If that is the case, Cliopher, I will need to examine you magically," said his Radiancy, softly.

"Of course, my lord, anything," said Cliopher, his eyes fluttering open. The Sun-on-Earth was bending over him, serene mask in place. Cliopher's eyes wouldn't focus enough to see if his Radiancy's eyes were full of magic. But he trusted his Radiancy entirely.

Cliopher couldn't feel magic, couldn't see it, couldn't hear it. Well, he could see the outward parts of the Ouranatha's ways of working. But his lord's magic was of a different level. Sometimes a word, or a gesture, but sometimes nothing more than a rising light in his eyes.

"This feels complete," said his Radiancy, "but I would have to enter a full trance to be sure, and to be sure of what it is. I would like the Domina's assurance that there is nothing wrong physically first."

"Thank you, my lord," croaked Cliopher.

"I'll get you some water, dear," said Conju, in the officious voice Cliopher knew he used to mask real concern.

Conju had to help Cliopher sit up a bit to drink, but the cool water felt so refreshing and clean in his mouth. He hadn't realised how much the smoke had got into his throat.

"What do you remember of the ritual?" asked his Radiancy, "That might at least tell me what kind of thing they've done to you."

Cliopher took another sip of water. "There was a bath, my lord, and then I put on these clothes," he gestured at the undyed robes, "then they took me to a temple room, with the, the Sun in it." Cliopher had to pause for breath. The water was helping clear his mind as well, but it was still hard. "There were five of them," he added, "they stood around me and did... ritual things. I remember the smell of rosemary, and mint. Water, fire, chanting. I'm afraid I don't remember any details, my lord, they all blur together after a while."

"Blur together, Cliopher?" asked his lord, voice carefully even.

Cliopher drained the glass, and handed it back to Conju. "Yes my lord, I'm afraid I've lost count how many rituals there have been."

"It started about six months ago, didn't it?" asked Conju, "Twice a week?" Conju turned to refill the glass from an ewer on a table by the wall.

"Nine months ago. And it was four times a week, last month," appended Cliopher, "and this month, every day."

Conju tutted. "No wonder you're so tired Cliopher, I'm sure you haven't taken a single day off in all that time either."

Cliopher saw his Radiancy's hand clench briefly before he forced them to relax. He had worked out what the spell was, and behind the calm facade Cliopher could tell his lord was furious.

"My lord?" asked Cliopher. He did not mind what had happened to him, he would recover in a day or so. But it clearly distressed his lord, and that did worry him.

His Radiancy took three carefully counted breaths. "You have been purified," he said, finally.

Conju dropped the full glass of water into Cliopher's lap. "For nine months?" he hissed, his voice going high.
