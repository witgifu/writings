Two days after the court session where he was appointed Hands of the Emperor, Cliopher woke up to a loud knock on his door. He rolled out of bed, and pulled on yesterday's crumpled robes.

"Coming!" he yelled out, as the knocking came again.

Being woken up in the middle of the night had lost the fear it had once held. Before the Fall, he had been (justifiably) afraid a late-night visit would be for his execution. Or torture, and then execution. Or execution by torture.

Those fears hadn't been entirely washed away by the Fall. The night after he had first looked on his Radiancy's eyes, he hadn't slept at all, waiting for the knock. He was sure the Emperor, or at least the Ouranatha, would have ended him for breaking one of the great taboos. His only comfort through those hours had been that at least the torture chambers weren't  in use anymore. The Emperor favoured clean beheadings, they said.

But nobody had come for him that night. In the months (years?) between, he had been woken in the middle of the night. Emergencies of government don't only happen in reasonable working hours. So he had been woken for everything from those things he could do nothing about (an elderly courtier dying) to those he could (organising emergency supplies after a fire). Eventually he'd got through to the pages which things he should be woken up for, and which could wait.

So, this must be something important.

He opened the door, soft light from his room falling on the silver faces beyond the door. Not faces, masks.

Cliopher took an unconscious step backwards. Perhaps his old fears had not gone, and had not been so unreasonable. And yet, he couldn't see any guards. He took a careful breath, and prepared his best court voice.

"My lords," he said, and bowed. He wanted to demand why they had woken him. But, above everyone else, the Ouranatha relished etiquette. They outranked him, so it was technically their prerogative to interrupt his sleep and not offer reasoning. Although if this proved to be a regular occurrence he might slip a memo to his Radiancy about their actions.

But, perhaps they had a good reason.

The central mask, shaped somewhat like an owl, inclined their head. "We require the Hands of the Emperor for a ritual," they said, in careful neutral tones and a perfect court accent.

Cliopher wanted to groan. Their stupid little rituals. He was sure most of them were empty. Perhaps his Radiancy's recent magical workings had enabled some of them to have some effect, but surely they could do the same without him. What did they want him for, taking notes?

They had asked for the Hands of the Emperor though, not Cliopher. He suddenly realised that this probably meant that until Cliopher's recent appointment they had been waking *his Radiancy* for these silly things. Which surely, they could have done with a little devotional portrait like the old aristos did. Well, if Cliopher could enable his Radiancy to sleep through the night, that was worth a little discomfort on his part.

Cliopher inclined his head in the same way as the priest-wizard had. "Of course, my lords," he said, not letting any of his irritation show. They could have sent him a note during the day about this, and he could have gone to bed early to make up the time. Such courtesies, however, where beyond the priest-wizards. Or they didn't feel they should be extended to a mere commoner secretary.

The central wizard stepped into Cliopher's room, surveying it. He was followed by an acolyte in black, carrying a bucket of supplies.

Cliopher was surprised. He had expected to be taken to one of the various consecrated rooms belonging to the Ouranatha. But of course, if they'd wanted him there they would have just sent a page or acolyte to summon him, not all come themselves.

Two of the priest-wizards took up posts outside the door like guards. They pulled the door shut.

Turning, still feeling very much off-balance, Cliopher watched the acolyte push his chair and rug into the corner. Then the young woman started chalking out patterns onto his floor.

When she had finished, she pointed to a spot in the centre of the design. "If you could stand here please sayo Mdang," she said softly.

As instructed, Cliopher stepped into the space in the centre. He was careful not to disturb any of the markings as he did so. He didn't want to get her in trouble, or have this take any longer than it absolutely had to.

There were smokes, bells, powders, and words that Cliopher didn't understand. A strong smell of rosemary, and a soft chanting from the acolyte. Cliopher stood there, carefully schooling his face to be neutral. He hoped none of his neighbours were woken up by the performances.

The bells had stopped for the night, but Cliopher thought it took them about half a bell to do the working. He inwardly cringed at the waste of resources, of precious oils and essences carried across worlds for this nonsense. He comforted himself knowing that the Ouranatha were surely using the cheapest version they thought they could get away with, because it was just him and not his Radiancy.

When they stopped, the priest-wizard used their foot to smudge the edge of the chalk. Then they walked over the design and out the door without another word.

Cliopher let out a breath he hadn't realised he was still holding.

The acolyte gave him a tentative smile, "Sorry about the mess sayo Mdang, it shouldn't need any magical cleanup, whatever you usually do will be fine."

Cliopher did not say that he had no idea how to get rosemary oil out of floorboards. He just nodded, and stepped carefully out of the remains of the design. He didn't really want to sleep in the same room as it, even though the wizard had scuffed it quite a bit walking through it. He'd find a rag, maybe in his writing kit, and at least make it less obvious to the cleaning staff that someone had been doing a magical working in his room.

The acolyte gathered everything back into her bucket. As she reached for the door handle, she turned back to Cliopher. "I will get you a schedule tomorrow, sayo Mdang, future rituals will be in our offices," she said, and left without waiting for a reply.

Suddenly incredibly tired, Cliopher sat down on his bed. It might have been some component of the ritual, or maybe it was just his body reacting normally to the late hour. He sat there for a few minutes, trying to gather his thoughts.

He didn't manage to make any effort towards cleaning the floor that night. He did manage to remove his robes and put them outside the chalk area. Then he crawled back into bed, and fell into a dreamless sleep.
