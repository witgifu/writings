Cliopher did indeed receive a schedule the next day, as he sat in his office. It seemed he would be required to attend twice a week for the next nine months. Presumably after that a new schedule would be sent to him.

He sighed, looking over the dates and times. Most of them were during usual court sessions, late afternoon and evening. None of them clashed with any of his regular meetings or morning sessions with his Radiancy. That was an unexpected boon, although it probably meant that the Ouranatha were just trying to keep him out of the public eye.

Well, that was fine with Cliopher. He didn't much like court, unless Conju was there to gossip with. It was necessary to attend, sometimes, but the game of manners was painful to Cliopher. If he had a good excuse to miss most sessions, he wouldn't complain.

He dutifully copied the appointments into his diary, and didn't think too much more about it. It was good to have lots of notice.

---

The sessions themselves were all much the same, as far as Cliopher could tell. Smokes, powders, oils. Smells and bells and chanting. Sometimes two priest-wizards, but mostly just one. Always the acolyte to set everything up and put it away.

Some sessions began a little earlier, with the last bell of the afternoon. For some reason Cliopher didn't understand, those sessions required him to have a bath first. But the bathroom the acolyte showed him was private and spotless. It was hard to complain about being told to spend at least thirty minutes in the warm scented water.

All of the rituals did leave him very tired, and aching a bit in a non-specific way. This was of a piece with what he had seen of His Radiancy after rituals with the Ouranatha (and sometimes ritual-free budget sessions). Since they were usually the last thing in his diary for the day, that wasn't really a problem. Cliopher could use the walk back to his room to stretch is aches out, and then fall into bed.

It was Conju who noticed first. Cliopher was visiting him for a late drink and gossip, when Conju raised it.

"I haven't seen you at court in two months! What audit has got you so busy?" he asked, sniffing the port decanter to check it was still fine.

"Oh, it's not an audit that's kept me away," said Cliopher, sighing and easing himself onto one of Conju's comfortable chairs, "it's the Ouranatha, they've got me doing 'hands of the emperor' rituals at least twice a week."

Conju decided the port was still acceptable, and poured two glasses. "Oh? I assume they quoted some ancient tradition at you, and Himself grimaced and said you better do it."

Cliopher snorted, in a very un-courtly way. "As if they would explain themselves to me," he said dryly, "I had assumed they were rituals his Radiancy had been doing that they thought they could get someone more biddable to do."

Cupping the glasses to warm the drink, Conju took a seat opposite Cliopher. "I haven't noticed a drop in the rituals they drag Himself into," he said, cautiously, "but he could be spending the time on other magical workings outside the Tower."

Cliopher raised one eyebrow, and took the glass Conju proffered. "Well then, I don't know what they're up to, unless they're just trying to keep me away from court." He sipped the port, which was smooth and rich.

"You spend more time with his Radiancy than anyone, except maybe me," said Conju, taking the matter seriously, "perhaps there's some court faction they're trying to keep you away from? Now that they've realised you're here to stay."

Taking a moment to consider, Cliopher swirled his drink gently. There wasn't an obvious faction. It would have to be among the lesser nobles, rather than the princes who Cliopher saw plenty of in various committee meetings. Maybe a group of academics? He hadn't noticed any, but then since he wasn't at court, he wouldn't.

"I can't think who it would be," he said eventually, "I'll have to rely on you to keep an eye out for anything interesting at court in my abscence. Without a concrete reason to refuse the Ouranatha, it's a bit tricky to get his Radiancy to try and put them off."

Conju smiled, "Of course, of course," he said, his voice warm, "You know I love to tell you all the best news anyway."

Cliopher nodded, smiling. And Conju began telling him all about the latest argument between the hangers-on around the Jilkanese princes.

---

It took Ludvic six months more to say anything. Cliopher suspected he had known from the first day. Ludvic was very observant, he just kept most of his observations to himself. Until he caught Cliopher falling asleep on a garden bench.

"Sayo Mdang," Ludvic's deep voice came creeping into Cliophers nascent dream.

"Mmm?" said Cliopher, looking up at Ludvic, his head resting against the lampost.

"That doesn't look very comfortable, sayo Mdang," Ludvic continued, his face betraying neither concern nor scorn.

"It's not," replied Cliopher, forthright, "it's just convenient. I have to be at the Oranatha offices in half a bell, so I don't have time to go back to my room."

"Don't you have an office?" asked Ludvic, and sat down next to Cliopher.

"Mmm, yes," said Cliopher, closing his eyes again, "but lots of people go looking for me there. I wanted to read this report without interruption."

"Do the Ouranatha take up a lot of your time?" Ludvic probed, but kept his eyes firmly on the garden view from the bench. It was lovely, in a wild sort of way.

"Increasingly," said Cliopher, and winced, "at first it was just twice a week, but now it's four. Next month it's going to be every day."

Ludvic didn't ask for clarification. He just nodded, and waited.

"But after next month, they say it will all be done," added Cliopher, "although I'm not sure if that means we go back to twice a week, or what."

Now Ludvid did look at Cliopher. Cliopher's face was a little grey, his hands a little shaky. The only reference point Ludvic could think of was when his Radiancy had spent too long in a trance.

"Are you ill?" he asked Cliopher, a little worry creeping into his voice.

Cliopher shook his head, and then grimaced. "No," he said, "it's just the stupid rituals. They make me so tired. Plus Education are dragging their feet on the budget."

"If the Ouranatha are taking up so much of your time, maybe you should let His Radiancy know?" asked Ludvic, trying to subtly hint that this wasn't normal.

Cliopher waved away Ludvic's concerns, with an ink-stained hand. "Six week to go," he said, "I can make it six weeks. We managed to hide the bonebreak fever, I can cope with being a little more tired than usual."

Ludvic didn't look convinced. However, it seemed that Cliopher wasn't likely to be convinced. "As you wish," he said, his voice carrying a little bit of approbation.

Cliopher lent back against the lampost, and lifted up the report. He carefully ignored Ludvic's tone.

Ludvic stood, and strode off without further comment.
