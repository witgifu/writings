His Radiancy stepped backwards, and backward, until he was in the furthest point in the room from Cliopher. Counting breaths the whole time.

"My lord?" asked Cliopher again, not understanding what about this had distressed both his lord and Conju. Many things and people were purified in service to his Radiancy, surely then this was normal?

"As soon as the Domina has assured us there are no other effects, sayo Mdang, We will have you taken care of in Solaara. We will see that you are granted an estate, anywhere you would like, or if you would prefer to leave Zunidh, We can arrange that," said his Radiancy, not looking at Cliopher but at the a large yellow vase standing near the door.

"My lord!" said Cliopher, and pushed the mostly-empty glass back into Conju's hand. Cliopher rolled himself off the divan, and prostrated himself completely on the floor.

"I apologise unreservedly, Glorious One, for not informing you of my and the Ouranatha's actions sooner," he said, not raising a finger-width from the floor. "I in no way wished to cause you distress, and I am full ashamed, Illustrious One, for having done so."

Cliopher heard the slightest hitch in his lord's carefully measured breaths.

"I will submit myself unreservedly to whatever punishment you see fit, my lord, but if I have ever pleased you, I beg of you, my lord, do not send me away."

Cliopher's arms and shoulders shook. Holding back tears, trying to hold in fear, exhaustion. Would he be executed, for daring to ask to stay, when his lord clearly wanted him as far away as possible? At least he had been able to bring his lord a little peace, with the Littleridge Treaty. A little peace, a little hope.

Another hitch in the carefully measured breathing. Another long wait. His Radiancy said nothing, to dismiss him or to relent. 

Finally, he said, "Sayo Mdang, We are not punishing you. You have done nothing wrong. Our priests have taken great liberty with your person, body, mind, and soul. We would offer you what restitution We can."

Fear was replaced by confusion, and Cliopher raised himself up a fraction so he could just see his Radiancy's hands. They were clenched in his robes, and twisting the delicate silks. 

"My lord, I am only a little over-tired—" said Cliopher, trying to assuage his lord's fears, but his Radiancy interrupted him.

"Even if that is all the physical ramifications, the Domina will have to confirm, you have been bound magically, without your consent, to a role that—" his Radiancy took another counted set of breaths.

Cliopher had never seen his lord so decomposed. He had seen his lord build the serenity like armour in front of princes and ministers and the priest-wizards. He had thought his lord unflappable.

"The role of Hands of the Emperor requires no purification," his Radiancy continued, "The Ouranatha subjected you to the purification required for a role that has often be co-occurrent, but unrelated."

Finally, Cliopher could see the shape of what his lord was trying, struggling, to say. Etiquette dictated that neither Cliopher or Conju should attempt to fill the gap in his Radiancy's words. But since Cliopher could see it likely that his career was to be cut short anyway, he decided he dared.

"My lord," he said, and looked up into his Radiancy's face, which was stricken, "I understand you have never desired a consort or a concubine, and I am deeply sorry for your distress at having one forced on you by the actions of the Ouranatha. If I can best serve you by taking myself away as far as possible, my lord, I will gladly do so. But if you have no desire for a consort or a concubine, my lord, would you still have a place for a secretary?"

With the last question, Cliopher let his face fall to the floor again. He had thought he was brave, when he had dared to look his Radiancy in the eye. But he was terrified. Unmoored. His ke'e lost, his ke'ea covered by cloud. He wished with all his heart that things could go back to how they had been this morning, but he could see only the fog of his grief for his lord.

Four carefully counted breaths from the Lord of Rising Stars. "Cliopher," he said, "the purification cannot be undone."

"I understand, my lord. Are there any... other requirements? Would you come to any harm if we did not... act on it?" now it was Cliopher's turn to struggle to talk about the roles the Ouranatha had tried to force on them.

"No," said his Radiancy, firmly, "it would be perfectly safe for you to leave immediately."

This was not what Cliopher wanted to hear his lord say, but it did contain the information he had asked for. 

Part of Cliopher felt the need to beg for mercy as he had watched those who came before the Throne of Judgement do. But his lord had said Cliopher had done nothing wrong. He said he wanted to make restitution. As if his lord had any control over this either.

Cliopher rose to his knees. Once again, he looked into his Radiancy's eyes, that were full of pain. He wanted desperately to reach out, to grip those beautiful hands in consolation. To offer support, to show that his Radiancy was not alone, not at fault, that Cliopher truly wanted only his happiness.

Then it hit him. He had been purified. He could—

Cliopher stood up. He held out his hands just in front of him, loosely. Slowly, slowly, shaking, he stepped towards his lord. Giving him space to turn, to leave, to order Cliopher to stop. His Radiancy stood, transfixed.

When Cliopher stepped past the proscribed distance, he heard Conju make a small noise. Cliopher ignored it. He had eyes only for his lord. Only his lord’s opinion mattered. Cliopher’s aching, exhausted body didn’t matter.

Cliopher stopped at the distance of close friends. Where he would grasp arms in the Astandalan greeting, touching heads in the Vangaveye-ve. He brushed the tips of his left hand across the back of his lords right hand.

His Radiancy did not move or make a sound, but the hand Cliopher touched relaxed ever so slightly.

“Then, it would be safe for me to stay,” said Cliopher, softly, gently, as to a frightened child. Oh, he could see the pain and torment in his lord’s eyes, and he wanted to hug him and tell him everything was fine, would be fine. All he dared was this tiny touch.

“And if you ever want to hold someone’s hand, my lord, I will be here.”

Cliopher moved his hand away slightly, but did not step back. Giving his lord enough space to choose, but also to make clear what Cliopher wanted. He hoped desperately his Radiancy understood that this was his choice, that Cliopher did feel free to leave, but that he did not want to.

The guards were right there, if his Radiancy wanted Cliopher removed. If his lord said, clearly, that he wanted Cliopher to go, he would leave. Cliopher hoped. 

His stomach clenched and his heart beat faster, both of which had nothing to do with his exhaustion. He was taking a risk, diving deeper than he ever had dared to dream. He slowed his breathing too, to match his Radiancy. They were both overwhelmed.

His Radiancy breathed. Four, five, six, seven.

“Please,” whispered his Radiancy, his voice so full of fear, “Please don’t leave me.” His right hand unclenched from his robes, and brushed for the tiniest instant against Cliopher’s hand. An echo of Cliopher’s gesture.

“I will stay,” said Cliopher, “Nobody else needs to know what happened.”

His Radiancy nodded, once. He breathed, two, three, four. The Serenity returned to his face, his hands unclenched. Cliopher could still see his eyes were swimming with emotion.

Before Cliopher, or Conju, or his Radiancy, had to face the question of what to do next, the guards thumped their spears.

Cliopher took a step backwards, with a turn to face the door. 

“Domina Audrey,” announced Ludvic, and the physician stepped into the room. Cliopher could see her do a rapid assessment of the room, the three of them standing and nobody obviously wounded or in immediate danger. She performed her obeisance.

“Sayo Mdang has had a reaction to a ritual with the Ourunatha. We have confirmed there is nothing wrong magically, but We are concerned it my have had a physiological impact,” said his Radiancy, as he gave the gesture to rise.

Cliopher’s body chose that moment to decide that the danger that had powered him up and across the room was over. As his knees gave way, Conju and Domina Audrey both scrambled forward to catch him.
