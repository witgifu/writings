With Pioné gone, it would be easiest to eat in a refectory. So close to the Tower, they could use the one frequented by the Tower staff. Much reduced at the moment, given the sleeping Emperor had little use for bath attendants and assistant dressers.

They arrived to find a sleepy crowd. The food available was clearly designed for those who were expecting a long day; lots of coffee and sweet buns. Conju took a tray, and selected some fruit and a few steamed buns. He watched Aurelia take one of each of the Alinoral pastries, and refrained from commenting on the quantity of food.

He chose a table a little out of the general flow.

"Cousin," he said, and pulled out a chair for Aurelia. Her face betrayed a snicker, but she didn't make a noise. He would need to teach her how to control her emotional displays, it might have been acceptable in Alinor, but it was uncouth at court. If such things still mattered.

He took the chair opposite her. Conju watched her. She ate neatly, with adequate table manners. She looked around the room avidly, observing everyone who came in, what they ate, and who they talked to.

“I promised you a visit to a library, I believe?” he said when he had finished his food, and was sipping on a glass of cold mint water.

Aurelia immediately abandoned attempting to eavesdrop on the two medical staff flirting furiously two tables away. “Yes you did,” she said brightly, “Can we go today? What kind of library? I mean, can you borrow books or is it a reference library? Is it open every day? Can anyone visit or do you need a pass?”

Conju was surprised by the volume of questions. “We will go today. I believe it is a reference library, it used to be open every day, and you used to need a letter of introduction. I don't know if that still holds.”

“What kinds of books does it have? Is it far? Will it be busy?”

Conju instinctively made the court gesture to ask the other person to slow down.

“I'm being annoying, aren't I?” said Aurelia, making a surprisingly good guess, and taking a bite of her second-to-last pastry.

Conju chose not to dignify that last question with a response. “The main Palace library has an extensive collection. Including a very beautiful embroidery of the Imperial family tree on one of the front walls.”

Aurelia nodded, “Let's go now, then.” She looked at the last pastry, and clearly giving up on the table manners she had been carefully using before, picked it up and ate it in three quick bites.

At this time of day, the centre of the palace was illuminated by the sun. It felt a world away from the night journey Conju had made only a few days ago. Light filled all the spaces, and people walked around chatting and looking busy.

The main library was on the same level as the throne room, although a little more tucked away. The door was closed but unlocked when they arrived, and Conju pushed it open, bowing Aurelia in.

Aurelia rolled her eyes at Conju, and stalked past him. But as soon as she was inside, she had eyes only for the room.

It was, Conju had to admit, impressive. Of course, it should be, in the Palace of Stars. It should be the most superlative library in the Empire. Magnificent columns rose up into a fluted ceiling. The centre of the room was taken up by elegantly simple mahogany shelves. The edges had niches for study, each with their own useless mage light. A balcony ran around the outside, with two elegant curving staircases leading up to it.

There was, as far as Conju could see, only one person there. At the front desk, an elegant young man was making notes. On one side, a pile of dispatch boxes, on the other a stack of books.

Behind the young man, was the enormous wall handing of the Imperial family tree. Appropriately, the very top was simply a giant Sun-in-Glory, representing their ancestral claim to be descendants of the *actual* Sun. Somewhere around the level of the desk was Artorin Damara.

Aurelia stood in front of the desk, quivering slightly. “Excuse me,” she said to the young man, “Can I…?”

The man looked up from his notes. “What?” he said, looking mildly annoyed at having been interrupted, but softening when he saw Aurelia's obvious excitement. “Oh, yes, just don't take anything out. There's a spare lamp over here if you need it.” He waved to the table beside him, where there were three lamps, one lit even now.

Conju prevaricated between introducing himself or going directly to the embroidery. Eventually his sense of duty defeated his desire to flirt, and he walked around the desk to look over the tree.

Trees were very familiar to Conju, as his family estate was renowned for their beautiful timber. His family crest even included an oak branch. The Imperial family less resembled a tree, and more an elaborate piece of knot-work. He needed someone born in the first two decades of Eritanyr's reign. Probably male, as the Imperial family had been rather short on female members of late.

He wandered over the expanse, collecting possible candidates. There were three possible fifth-degree cousins. He made a mental note of their names, before sauntering over to the 'modern history' section to see what they had gotten up to.

Fifteen minutes later, he had chosen a candidate. Zhanyr, born in the tenth year of Emperor Eritanyr, died just after Artorin took the throne. Largely unremarkable, only achievement of note was that he had published a monograph on anthropology through Tara. Given the very short biography with scant details, Conju wondered if he'd somehow run afoul of Ertitanyr. It wasn't as if it had been hard to do.

That book gave Zhanyr a link to Conju's family as well. Conju's second cousin had taught anthropology, and so possibly could have introduced Zhanry to her daughter Lavina. And Lavina had died at about the same time as Zhanry. They could have left behind a small daughter. 

It would also explain why Aurelia knew nothing of court life, or her parents. It could almost even be true, he thought. Except that he'd never heard of Lavina having a spouse or even a lover. He closed the book with a slam, sending a small puff of dust into the air. Well, it wasn't like many people would have use for these texts at the moment.

The story planned, Conju turned to the librarian, to see if he was as charming as he was cute.

Their days developed a pattern, although it was hard to identify the weeks. Breakfast together, and then Conju would escort Aurelia to the library. He would flirt with the librarian, and gossip with anyone else who came by. He enjoyed re-shelving books, returning order to the space. 

Over and after lunch together he would grill Aurelia on etiquette. She was a quick learner, even if she often complained that the rules made no sense. They would have long circular discussions about if court gestures constituted a language of their own.

After dinner, they would go through the things in Lady Nerisse's room. Conju throwing out dubious patent medicines, Aurelia attempting to make the most wild outfits. Both of them wondering over a miniature portrait of a little boy, that Conju didn't recognise.

It was, thought Conju, surprisingly pleasant.

Conju heard the sound of footsteps outside his bedroom door. Trying to think quickly, he slid off the bed and onto the floor. He re-corked the bottle, and shoved it under the bed skirt.

The door opened, and Aurelia appeared. “Where have you been?” she said, exasperated, “I had lunch alone, and dinner.” Then she caught on to his rumpled appearance, and that he was sitting on the floor.

“I met a friend,” he said, slightly defiantly, “I'm sorry I missed you, we got caught up, and I didn't notice the time.”

Aurelia sniffed. “Was your friend a bottle of that awful wine people make out of the wild fruits? Because you reek of it.”

Conju pulled himself up off the floor. “I don't know—” he began, but Aurelia cut him off with a huff. 

She strode up right next to him, and then crouched down. Still looking him in the eye, she felt around under the bed with one hand. Conju heard her hand knock against the bottle, and it rocked slightly on the hardwood floor. Triumphant, she pulled it out from under the bed.

“Is this the friend you've been with all day?” she asked, sourly. “You leave me to practice bows in the mirror, and run off to get drunk. How long did your responsible sobriety last? A month? Two?”

“I didn't...” began Conju, but then reconsidered. He had met one of his old set. He had flirted, the other man had suggested meeting a third. Both of them had been drinking, were drinking. They had teased Conju until he also had a drink. That was all it had taken to break his resolve.

Conju didn't meet Aurelia's eyes, but made a first-degree bow of apology. When he straightened, Aurelia was giving him a curious look.

“Do you mean that?” she asked, and he thought he heard a note of fear behind her sarcastic tone.

It hit him then what this would look like to her. He had impressed on her the danger she was in, and how he would try to protect her. And now, here was her so-called-protector, abandoning her for hours while he sought his own pleasure and oblivion.

What was worse, was that she was right. He had completely forgotten his duty, forgotten her. The shame bit at him harder than her words.

He made a third-degree bow of apology, concentrating hard to make sure it didn't wobble. “I apologise unreservedly for my behaviour, Lady Aurelia, and for breaking my word. I will endeavour to find you a more diligent guardian.”

Aurelia snorted. “I don't want a 'more diligent guardian', Conju,” she said, her tone sharp, “I want *you* to keep your appointments. I only know about three people here, and the nice Imperial Guard at dinner didn't even tell me his name.”

_Shit_ thought Conju, and steadied himself on the bed. 'Nice Imperial Guard' was not a phrase he wanted to hear from a fourteen-year-old left to herself. It could be purely innocent, the guard making sure nobody else bothered her. The Imperial Guard were the ones keeping order (such as it was). However, if one of them had taken an interest, perhaps the other guards would let him… he could feel a headache building, as anxiety warred with the alcohol.

Aurelia looked the bottle over. “This really does stink, you know. There's fortified wine under the kitchen sink that smells better.”

Conju took a deep breath. “If you would be so kind, Lady Aurelia, would you please put all the alcohol you can find in this apartment outside the front door? And then bolt it shut from the inside.” He looked at her, and she nodded firmly. “I am likely to be sick most of the night,” he added, a note of chagrin in his voice, “Tomorrow I will do better.”

“Good night then,” said Aurelia, and left with the bottle.

Conju stumbled around the bed, to the bathroom. Hoping for a headache powder he could take and keep down.
