The only lights in the Palace of Stars were oil lamps. Carefully tended, for if yours went out you would need to find another light or fire to restore it. The Ouranatha had candles, of course, but they wouldn't share.

Or at least, they wouldn't share with someone like the Cavalier Conju enazo Argellian an Vilius. Who considered the Oranatha at best a waste of time and resources. Resources like candles, and wine. Even before, he’d disliked them as the center of all schooled magic that had driven away his friend. Since, they’d gone in for even more pomp and ceremony, in those ghastly robes and ridiculous masks. Conju could tell they were sneering at him and his friends, even in the masks.

So Conju walked carefully up the Ystharian Wing, making sure his oil lamp was steady. This was harder than it might have been, because he was quite drunk. There hadn't been any drugs today, as they were a bit harder to acquire, and because he knew he had to make this journey. The drugs did make remembering things hard; on the whole a welcome relief. But a lack of memory made keeping appointments, and navigation in the palace, tricky.

It was probably the second hour of the evening, although Conju hoped he wouldn't be in the hallway when the next hour bell rang. The hour bells were louder in the hallways, than holed up in the various rooms he spent most of his days in. Somehow the bells were louder when he was drunk, louder than the few times he had been more sober.

He had promised to visit his great-aunt, Lady Nerisse, who his favourite sister had been named after. His great-aunt was, as far as he knew, his only relative left. Certainly the only relative in the Palace, and venturing outside was decidedly unwise these days. Conju and Lady Nerisse did not like each other. But both of them had held on to the idea that family was important, and so Conju visited.

Since that fateful Silverheart, Lady Nerisse had caught every sniffle and sickness that had gone around the Palace. Whenever she was well, she sauntered around like nothing had changed. Her set of aged nobles would dress up, flit through the hallways to each others apartments, and take tea and nibble cakes. And if every gown was remade, and the tea cut with mint, they just claimed that was the fashion. There were no court entertainments, with no Emperor to hold court. But they would gossip and plan like the next one was surely only days away.

Whenever she was sick, Lady Nerisse holed up in her apartments with her lady’s maid. Pioné was the only member of her staff left. Lady Nerisse had always been hale before, this behaviour seemed to be the one change in her life, her one concession to the ongoing disaster. It was if she swung from claiming nothing had changed, to sudden awareness of her own mortality.

Conju didn't understand how she could pretend nothing had changed, as he walked along a part of a hallway illuminated only by his lamp. The views out the windows were no longer of Astandalas the Golden, a great many people were missing, and there were many parts of the Palace in ruin. He stumbled slightly on a crack in the floor, but the lamp held steady.

He'd promised to visit Lady Nerisse today, because she was once again sick. A note had been passed from Pioné through to the valet of one of his regular acquaintances. Friends, perhaps. Not like the friends he’d had at home, but a collection of those young people in a similar situation to him. A loose circle of young nobles who drank, took drugs, and generally debauched themselves. It was what passed for fun, and if some of them had gone from wonderfully well-built to a little gaunt, well, that was to be expected. Conju tried not to look at his reflection, he didn’t want to know if he looked the same.

Conju passed increasing numbers of people as he neared the centre. Some clearly working late, carrying baskets or stacks of documents. In pairs or threes, one lamp between them. He didn’t find the idea of working yourself to exhaustion any better than drinking. What did the work achieve? Did they imagine that with enough reports they could put back all that had fallen down?

On the other hand, were those slumped in a corner. Their lamps guttering, as they gazed with glazed eyes, left behind. Conju’s lamp illuminated one young woman, her light gone out, left by her friends to sleep off whatever she had taken. He hoped to sleep. The lamplight made it hard to see if she was breathing. He almost stopped to check, but… the guards would find her soon. He was in no condition to help anyone, not even himself.

The halls still felt empty, compared with before. 

Eventually he wound his way up to the apartment of his great-aunt. For some reason he had never bothered finding out, she was in the bottom of the Collian Wing, but near the Tower. Thankfully, her rooms were close enough to the Tower that they hadn't been damaged or cursed. She had lived there since the first time he had visited the Palace, and her apartments had looked like she had lived in them for many decades even then.

He knocked on the door, and it was opened by Pioné. Usually, she made some remark about Conju's tardiness, or his appearance. She was the kind of servant who thought herself even more proper than her employer. This time she did not comment, but refused to look at him directly. She did bow. Conju couldn't decide if it was worse or better, and stepped past her. He put his lamp down on the hall table.

He made his own way down the hall to Lady Nerisse's bedroom, where he knew she would be lying in state. She liked to put on a show for him, when she was sick. He didn't see the point, but he never said anything directly. She wouldn't listen, would rant at him for his dissolute ways, and he would just get irritated.

But before he could reach the door, Pioné slipped ahead of him and blocked his progress.

“My lady is truly very ill, my lord,” she whispered, “I am worried… she may not recover…”

Conju hid his surprise, as best he could. His old self would have been disgusted by his lack of manners and control. He tried to excuse himself, because it was harder to care now, harder to practice, harder generally. Those kinds of excuses were also once something he despised.

Pioné didn’t notice, or at least pretended not to notice, focused instead on the door. She typically took Lady Nerisse's ailments very seriously, plying her with whatever remedies she could find in the disarrayed Palace stores. But she was also frequently insistent that her ladyship would be feeling better very soon.

“Thank you, Pioné,” said Conju, wondering if that would mean he would avoid a lecture, and immediately feeling guilty, “I will keep that in mind.”

Pioné opened the door to Lady Nerisse's bedroom. A wave of humid air flowed out. Pioné hurried Conju in, snapping the door shut behind him. The room was stifling.

The fire was lit, and between that and one oil lamp beside the great bed, there was just enough light to see. On a tripod over the fire was a small pot of something boiling. The herbal scent of the air suggested to Conju it was supposed to help with fever. He could distinguish yarrow, sage, and feverfew, but he wasn't quite sure if there was something else as well.

Lady Nerisse was not sitting up on a grand pile of cushions, draped in a lace bed-jacket, as she had been last time she was ill. She was lying on top of her blankets, in nothing but the thinnest linen shift, drenched in sweat. Her skin, usually the same warm tone as Conju's, looked pale, but splotched with red.

Conju knew almost nothing about medicine, but this looked bad, and possibly contagious. He kept his distance. “Good evening, Aunt,” he said, trying to soften his voice, and bowing.

“Conju?” came Lady Nerisse's voice from the bed, weedy and weak.

“Yes, aunt, I am here,” he said, but did not walk closer. In addition to being cautious of catching whatever she had, he was also aware that his great-aunt was very nearly naked. It was not a state he had expected, or wanted, to see one of his most imposing older relatives.

“Conju, I'm dying—” she began, but had to stop to breathe heavily.

Conju wanted to deny it, he wanted to tell her she was being as ridiculous as always. He had thought, perhaps those terrible weeks or months after Silverheart had inured him to death and dying. But looking at her face, hollow cheeks, and thin cracked lips, he felt sick. Should he have come sooner? But what could he have done? 

She believed he was useless. He had snapped at her before, and denied it. But in the cool strange hours before dawn, mouth tasting of sour wine, he had agreed with her.

“—and when I do, you will have to look after Aurelia,” she said, bringing his wandering mind back to where he was.

“Who is Aurelia, aunt?” asked Conju, thinking maybe this was some figment of his great-aunt's fevered imagination. He was also desperately hoping it wasn't a pet. He didn't know what he'd do with a lapdog or a songbird or whatever fashionable toy Lady Nerisse had collected.

“She is my ward,” said Lady Nerisse, struggling again to breathe. She flopped a hand in Pioné's direction.

“Aurelia saya Anyra came to my lady shortly before Silverheart,” whispered Pioné, “She was the ward of Professor Chilperic, who had to travel and asked my lady to take her in for a few weeks, and perhaps introduce her at court.”

*And was trapped here with you*, Conju filled in mentally. It was hard to tell if the survivors in the Palace had fared better than those out at Silverheart parties in Astandalas the Golden. They had heard nothing, in the week or months it had been. (Years, if you believed the Ouranatha; Conju did not). Had those who walked into the gardens and never came back found their way into the city? Into the countryside?

Was Professor Chilperic frantically writing letters to Saya Anyra? Unwanted came the hope he tried to suppress, to ignore, that somewhere one of his siblings or his parents was doing the same. Trying to find him, trying to find all of them.

He wanted to ask why he hadn't been introduced to Saya Anyra before. One of his greatest attributes as a young courtier had been that he was 'safe' around precious daughters. Indeed, his position had been as an escort to one such young heiress. Before Silverheart, Conju had little to do with Lady Nerisse, as they had mostly moved in different circles. But at court, perhaps, they could have been introduced.

Of course after Silverheart was different. Lady Nerisse had found him first. He and his friends had been working their way through rooms near the Collian ambassador (they had found good drugs there, and had hoped to find more nearby). They were camped out in the ambassador’s rooms, when four of them, including Conju, had gone out looking. All drunk, half-naked, and two of them on the drugs that made you feel invincible. They had accidentally sauntered right into a tea, and his great-aunt’s line of sight.

Conju had pretended to laugh off Lady Nerisse’s admonitions, her embarrassment at seeing him so. Lord Biroh swiped a bottle right off the table. When Lady Nerisse asked Conju cooly if he would give it back, Conju had just given Lord Biroh a sloppy kiss, and slid a hand up the back of the other man’s tunic.

So he couldn’t exactly blame Lady Nerisse. His great-aunt would not want her young ward mixing with his set. Even if maybe the young lady in question might have enjoyed it. 

If she was a *young lady*.

“How old is she?” he asked, suddenly realising she could even be an infant. He felt a wave of horror at the idea of even holding such a squirming, smelly thing.

“She was fourteen at Silverheart,” said Pioné. With time since then behaving so erratically, it was hard to know how old anyone was now. She probably still looked fourteen.

“Conju!” said Lady Nerisse, her voice cracking. She tried to lift herself up to look at him, but collapsed back immediately.

Conju made a bow of third-degree apology, which was only slightly wobbly. “Please do not fret, Lady Nerisse,” he said, “I am sure you will return to health soon.”

Lady Nerisse waved at him again. “Boy! I want your word!”

He knew what she meant, what she was trying to get him to do. But he didn’t see *why* it had to be him, and he didn’t feel inclined to admit it. Instead, he pretended to misunderstand her. “Dearest aunt, I am no physician, I spoke only knowing your love of life.”

A very unladylike snort came from the bed, “Promise me you will look after Aurelia, Conju.”

“I know how you disapprove of me, Lady Nerisse, surely there is someone else more suitable—”

“—Conju!” Lady Nerisse interjected, gasping, “I could not impose on my friends, most of whom are as sick as I. Somewhere in you, Conju, is the son your good mother raised. Promise me!”

That was a low blow, even from Lady Nerisse. It hit hard, and sharp, not at all blunted by the alcohol. Conju did not want to think what his mother would say to see him now. He had written to her just before Silverheart, with promises of all the things they would do together when he got home. He couldn’t even bear to look at his little portrait of her.

She had taught him to distill, to infuse. What would she say of the wine he and his friends made from the half-rotten strange fruits that fell on the garden paths? Of his days filled with sleep and his nights with debauchery?

He bit back the sharp retort that had been forming in his mind. For his mother. Who would have put the honour and reputation of her family above her own comfort. Who had taught him what it meant to be one of the Upper Ten Thousand. “You have my word, I will look after Saya Anyra.”

Lady Nerisse waved at Pioné again. The maid turned to Conju, and hurried him out of the room. His great-aunt had got all she wanted. Back in the blessedly cool hallway, Pioné led Conju not back to the front door (as he had expected), but to his great-aunt's sitting room. 

Conju was already regretting his promise. Damn his great-aunt for knowing exactly how to push him to get what she wanted.

In the sitting room, someone was sprawled across the settee. She rose awkwardly when she saw a figure behind Pioné coming into the room. Her skin was nearly entirely black, a true Shaian colouring, and her hair was tightly curled. Her eyes were dark, but glinted in the dim light that shone from the lamp on the mantlepiece, and the one Pioné held. Her nose looked like it had been copied from any portrait of an Imperial family member he had ever seen, down to the smallest, oldest copper coin. Conju wondered that she was only *saya*, and did not even have a courtesy title.

“My lord,” said Pioné, clearly uncomfortable performing this introduction, “this is Aurelia saya Anyra. Saya Anyra, the Cavalier Conju enazo Argellian an Vilius.”

Conju bowed, Aurelia bowed, and the two of them sat down. Pioné, obviously uncertain if Aurelia should be left alone with a degenerate, hovered awkwardly by the door.

Conju decided he had had enough of the maid, and said, “Pioné, could you fetch some refreshments?”

Pioné bobbed an acknowledgement, and bustled off.

As soon as she left, Aurelia gave Conju a piercing look. It made his eyes itch, and he could feel a headache building.

“You're drunk,” she said, without formality.

He raised one eyebrow, and lent back in his chair. He did not want to fence with a fourteen-year-old girl. Particularly not one who was clearly an incipient, or even a trained, mage. He could feel the tingle of the power around her, as he had seen it in her eyes.

“Why did Lady Nerisse hand me off to *you*? I can look after myself.” Aurelia continued.

“Lady Nerisse an Vilius is my great-aunt, Saya Anyra. Given the circumstances we are all living under,” he waved vaguely around the room, “she claims I am the best choice. I have been a companion to young ladies of quality before.”

“So you'll take me back to Tara?” said Aurelia, hopefully.

It took all of Conju's court training to resist a bark of laughter. He managed to turn the snort that did escape into a sniff. “We cannot even safely venture into the gardens, Saya Anyra,” he said coolly, “but if it becomes possible to travel to the portal to Alinor, of course I would escort you back.”

Aurelia slumped back down, lying on the settee and staring up at the ceiling. Conju sympathised. He had no idea, none of them did, if any of the other worlds even still existed. And if they did, what had happened to them? Should you hope your family survived? Or that they had died quickly, rather than starving in cities that were unable to cope, disconnected from the Empire?

It was part of the reason he drank.

They sat there in silence. Aurelia stared at the ceiling. Conju looked around the room, trying to see if there was a bottle of spirits.

Pioné returned with mint tea and ginger biscuits. No wine, no port, not even proper tea. Conju kept his sigh to himself. 

By the strictest etiquette, Aurelia should have poured the tea. But she seemed unaware of this, and Conju was slightly worried about chipping his great-aunt's tea set. So he waved for Pioné to serve it.

Aurelia didn't sit up, or acknowledge the tea or Pioné at all.

Conju sipped, and nibbled on a biscuit. 

He had assumed he would never have children. Since he wasn't heir to his father or mother, he hadn't had to marry to have children. And so he had been free to love for love's sake alone. His siblings might have had children, and he had vaguely imagined he might introduce one of them to court.

And now, he thought, looking at the flecks of mint in the bottom of his cup, it was very likely that he would be looking after a teenage girl. In the absolute chaos that was the Palace of Stars now. A girl who probably already disliked him, and had spent… however long it had been… stuck hiding with his great-aunt. Who was possibly the only person who had cared about Aurelia, and who was dying.

He was going to have to sober up. And then—no, he wouldn't think about more logistics yet. Sober was going to be hard enough. He was going to have to start right now, because who knew how long Lady Nerisse would last.

Fuck.

Conju ate another biscuit, and tried not to think about how much he wanted a drink. He did briefly consider trying to fudge his promise to his great-aunt. He could maybe see if he could find someone more suitable (sober) to take her on. But the thought left a sour taste in his mouth. He did believe his word still mattered, even if given grudgingly. 

Passing her on, like so much baggage, was also part of it. Also, in some strange way he couldn't quite put a finger on, he felt like he owed her as family. It was a tentative family, she was only his great-aunt's ward by accident. But alliances between noble families hung on much thinner tendrils than fourth-degree adoptions. 

If he was to do his duty, keep his promise, he would need to be sober. Eat regular meals. Not wake up with two other men he only barely recognised, in a bed that belonged to none of them. Do something useful with himself.

If he went back to his rooms in the Ystharian Wing now, he wasn't sure his tentative resolve to be sober would last the night. He had a personal stash of spirits and wine there. He even had alcohol in his perfume making supplies, although it was so strong it would almost certainly cause permanent damage.

“Is there a guest bedroom I could stay in here?” Conju asked. He'd only ever seen this sitting room and his great-aunt's bedroom. This close to the Tower there should be more rooms, but for all he knew his great-aunt had filled all of them with treasured collections of ceramic figurines.

“The Cherry Room is available, my lord,” said Pioné, who had gone back to hovering by the door. Her tone was not entirely disapproving.

He needed to go back to his rooms and get clean clothes, and he wanted his own toiletries, and his favourite dressing gown. Could he trust himself for that much time? Maybe if he had someone to watch him.

“Then I should stay, until Lady Nerisse gets better,” said Conju, “My rooms are too far away, if something should happen unexpectedly.”

“Very well, my lord,” said Pioné.

“I will need to collect a few things,” he said, and paused, “Saya Anyra, would you be willing to escort me? I'm afraid I haven't been able to replace my valet, and will have to carry everything myself.”

Aurelia finally looked over at Conju. “Can I say no?” she asked, sharply.

“Of course, Saya Anyra, I would not wish to impose,” he replied, court-smooth. He hoped she would come, he was much less likely to grab a bottle of something with her eyes on him. But he didn't want to start their relationship by demanding.

She gave him a sceptical look, but then smiled. “Okay,” she said, “I've always wanted to see what a hedonist's rooms are like.” She jumped up, and shoved a biscuit into her mouth whole.

Internally, Conju groaned. Externally, his face was as placid as if she had simply asked to see the architecture of the Ystharian Wing. He stood, bowed to her, and indicated towards the door.

“I'll prepare the Cherry Room, my lord,” said Pioné, and left.
