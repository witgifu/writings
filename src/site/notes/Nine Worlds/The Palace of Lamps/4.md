They were having breakfast in the refectory the next morning. Conju was feeling very much the worse for wear, and wondering where he might restock his supply of headache powders.

Without any preamble, Aurelia said, “Cousin, you must find a job.”

“What.” said Conju, far more tersely than was polite.

“A job, Lord Conju, I'm sure you're familiar with the general idea, even if you find the practice distasteful. Something *useful* to do with your time. With other people, also doing useful things.”

Conju gave Aurelia a glare that _would_ have been withering, if halfway through it hadn't transformed into a wince of pain. He sipped at his tea.

Aurelia persisted, “*Idle hands fall quickest into sloth*, as said by that awful book, *Lessons to Young Women*, that Lady Nerisse made me read.”

Contemplating a steamed bun, Conju retorted, “Escorting a young lady around court _was_ my last appointment.” He decided to brave a bite.

There was an awkward moment. “Well,” said Aurelia, “there's not exactly a lot of balls and tea parties at the moment.” She took a bite of her pastry.

Conju had hoped that might be the end of the topic, but he was wrong. 

“What other skills do you have?”

He took another bite of his bun. “I can sew and embroider, do simple accounts, ride… all the usual things,” he said with a wave of his hand.

Aurelia licked a smudge of jam off her finger. “I can do none of those things, but I assume that if you claim they are *usual* then many other people here can. What makes you different?”

Conju thought back to things his family had laughed at, or asked him to do particularly. “I can make a good enfleurage, and I am fastidiously tidy.”

“Everyone appreciates someone who is tidy.”

“Not,” said Conju, cringing internally at the memory, “If they yell at you for disturbing their sorting system.”

Aurelia are the last bite of her pastry. “Hmm. Let's see if we can find who is managing the stores, there's probably something you can do there.”

Conju sighed, seeing that she wasn't going to give up. 

Aurelia grinned, “That's the spirit!”

---

Conju had inquired discretely, Aurelia enthusiastically, about who was in charge of organising supplies. They were directed to the Duke of Blix, who they found in the bowels of the Palace.

The room seemed to be something of a record office. Tallies were written on slates propped around the room, and even on the walls. The scatter of desks were half-occupied by members of the Service comparing ledgers. At the front, the Duke was standing at the front with a low-ranked member of the Ouranatha.

“If you can send me weekly lists of what you need, I'll see what I can do,” the Duke said, and waved off the initiate.

The Duke saw Conju and Aurelia in the doorway. “Well?” he said briskly. 

Conju bowed to the correct degree, and Aurelia copied him only half a beat behind. “Your grace,” he said, “I was wondering if I might be able to help?”

The Duke did not return the courtesy. “Any particular skills?” he asked, giving them both an assessing look.

“Organising and sorting, your grace,” said Conju, feeling very self-conscious.

“One for you, Nina,” said the Duke, looking at someone in Service robes, and turned back to his pile of documents.

Nina waved Conju and Aurelia over. “I'm Lady Nina of Kiluzs, I manage the stores inventory.”

Internally, Conju cringed. This would be the first test of Aurelia's story. Kiluzs was not far from his family estates. Outwardly, he was perfectly polite. 

He bowed, even though that didn't seem to be the done thing down here. It was the *correct* thing, and he didn't have to leave off the formalities just because others chose to. “I am the Cavalier Conju an Vilius, and this is my cousin Lady Aurelia of Finchely.”

Nina nodded, and pointed at the slate board closest to her. “These are the things we're most looking for at the moment, if you find them come right back here and report. You'll be doing inventory. Paper, pens, and ink are in the second storeroom in the first basement. Start there.”

Conju accepted the dismissal, and bowed. Then he looked at the board. It was an odd assortment of items. Some obvious, like “foodstuffs”; some less so, like “ammonia”. He made a mental note of it, although felt it unlikely anything would turn up in what must be a stationery supply room.

He had a position again. As they left the record office, Conju turned to Aurelia. “Would you prefer to stay in the Library?” he asked, wondering how interested she would be.

“No, I want to help,” she replied, and shook her head, “I'll be your scribe.”

Conju refrained from rolling his eyes. “I've seen your handwriting, I'm not handing over anything you've written.”

“I can write neatly, *cousin*,” she replied, with a slightly sarcastic tone, “I just don't bother for my own notes. I will make yours beautiful, just watch.”

Sighing, Conju collected a wax tablet for note-taking, and handed it to Aurelia. She pouted. “I'm not a child who can't be trusted with an open ink bottle.”

“I know,” said Conju, “But this is easy to erase, and you can write a clean copy once we've actually organised everything.”

Arriving at the storeroom in question, it looked like both an earthquake and a raid had attacked it. Well, that was not entirely inaccurate. Conju looked it over, trying to decide where to start.

“This is worse than I was expecting,” he said, “We will have to begin with making space so we can sort.”

By lunchtime, they had managed to clear out the collapsed shelving unit, and do an initial rough sort of the supplies. Conju didn't want to admit it, but even though he was smeared with plaster dust, it did feel good to be doing something.

It took them days, possibly a week, to sort through that first storeroom. But in the end they took their inventory back to Lady Nina. She checked it over, with a small smile.

“I _do_ hope we have a use for the proclamation ink someday soon,” she said, with a sigh.

Conju didn't ask what the Emperor would be making a proclamation about, if he did wake up. Banning the plants from invading the Palace? Posting a reward for the first person to contact anyone outside? He tried to keep away from the more maudlin thoughts.

“Is there any particular order you wish us to continue in, Lady Nina?” asked Conju, diffidently. A couple of things had been crossed off the list of urgent supplies, and 'salt' had been underlined.

“At this point, Lord Conju, it would be best to be systematic,” she said, handing his papers to someone else. “Just take the next door you find.”

Together they worked their way through the store rooms. After the first one, they often found themselves needing to trek to the library to work out what an item was. Conju actually found himself enjoying it.

Aurelia told him stories about her life as they worked. How she had been found outside Tara, what strange educational experiments the professors had tried. The things she had studied about magic, natural history, poetry, whatever someone was interested in and wanted to teach her.

Conju didn’t share much about his own family. Were they on Ysthar still? Was Ysthar still there? Thinking about it made him want to drink, and he needed *not* to do that. Instead, he traded her gossip from his time in the Palace before the Fall.

Aurelia teased him into talking to more people. One of the guards, who was built like an entire army in one man. Conju was initially cautious, worried about his intentions.

“We found a rock collection today,” said Aurelia to Guard Omo, as she made quick work of the curry. “Apparently that's good, because the Ouranatha will trade it to the Duke for salt. They're low on all sorts of crystals.”

Guard Omo nodded, eating much slower.

“There are a lot of scientific instruments in this storeroom, so it's going to take us *ages*, but at least it's interesting,” said Aurelia.

“What is your position, Guard Omo?” asked Conju, keeping to a warm tone that was entirely artificial.

“I guard the Glorious One,” he said, softly.

“You watch him sleep?” asked Aurelia, cradling a cup of weak coffee, and suddenly curious, “Why?”

Guard Omo considered the question, before answering, “To make sure nobody comes too close, and to be there for him when he wakes.”

Aurelia had finished her dinner. “You think he will wake up? What's he like?”

“He's sleeping,” said Guard Omo.

“Perhaps he just needs a very loud alarm clock,” said Aurelia, with a smile, “Or a good shake.”

Conju's eyes went wide. “I would not try, cousin. To touch the Emperor is death.”

Aurelia looked between Guard Omo and Conju, “Surely nobody would enforce that *now*?”

“It's a magical taboo,” said Conju, carefully. It was one of the few things his father had told him about Emperor Eritanyr, and how he was different from the Prince he had known. “Any contact, even accidental, and the person immediately burns to death.” He didn't add that Eritanyr had used it as a method of highly personal execution. The old Emperor had died around the time Aurelia was born, and the Empire was now dead as well.

Aurelia looked horrified, Guard Omo obviously knew this already.

“I can see why he needs to be guarded then,” said Aurelia, softly, “That would be...” She shuddered, not finding the appropriate word.

“But people go and visit him?” she asked, “Like a… shrine? Why?”

Guard Omo nodded. “It gives them hope, to see him breathing.”

“Can we go, cousin?” said Aurelia, turning to Conju.

Conju could see a reason not to, so the next afternoon, they went to visit the sleeping Emperor.

The candles were dim, which added to the sombre mood. Conju was mildly offended that the Ouranatha thought the sleeping Emperor needed candles, but refused to give any to the Duke of Blix. Yet, he walked up to the line marked for that purpose, and made his obeisance to the Emperor who acknowledged nobody now.

As he and Aurelia stood up, the Emperor unable to signal anyone to rise, two of the priest-wizards entered. His heart beating rather fast, Conju turned Aurelia away and moved them to the side. She gave him an annoyed look, ready to shake his hand off. But something of his anxiety must have slipped through his court mask, because she relented.

Both of them stood at the edge and watched the four guards and one sleeping Emperor, seemly as frozen as a painting. The priests approached with incense and chanting, doing some ritual, possibly to try again to wake him. Slowly, Conju edged Aurelia both out of the room.

“I thought the priest-wizards did magic?” asked Aurelia, as they headed down to the store rooms to continue their inventory.

“Yes, of course,” said Conju automatically, “The Ouranatha are charged with the maintenance of Schooled Magic throughout the Empire.”

“I didn't sense any magic,” said Aurelia, matter-of-factly, “Not a drop.”

“I am sure they were doing what they believe is necessary,” said Conju, keeping an eye out for anyone listening. “They are the experts.”

“Can't they tell it's not working? Why waste—,” said Aurelia.

Conju interrupted, “Lady Aurelia, please,” he said in his most formal tones, “Let us focus on what *we* can do.”

She rolled her eyes, and sighed, but accepted the change of topic. After all, they were learning a surprising amount about the study of optics. Aurelia's enthusiasm was contagious enough that even Conju was willing to admit he could see how these devices might be useful.

They drifted apart in the library, Conju continuing to look through sales catalogues and periodicals while Aurelia went back to check in the textbooks. He had become accustomed to Aurelia finding him for dinner, so when his own stomach reminded him instead, he was surprised.

She wasn't in the textbooks, or in the general optics books. He walked up the isle to the cross passage, wondering if perhaps she got distracted by a title elsewhere. His heart rate rose and steps quickened as he spotted a book on the floor.

It was lying open, spine up, no way to treat a book. 'Imperial Magics and their Origins', said the gold foil letters on the spine. Picking it up and turning it over, Conju saw there was a tear through three pages, as if someone had been holding it and…

Heedless of the further abuse, he dropped the book and ran to the front desk. No librarian. No spare lamps.

Gripping the edge of the counter, he tried to force himself to calm down. She might have suddenly needed the toilet, or gone to dinner without him. She could have asked the librarian for help finding something and both disappeared into the stacks. Panicking would not help.

Stepping around to the other side of the counter, Conju found paper, ink, and pen. Quickly, he wrote a note telling Aurelia he had gone to dinner, and to meet him there. Leaving in plain view, he left the library and did his best to walk calmly to the refectory.

Dinner was out of the question. Wine was *not* a good idea. Conju sat in a corner where he had a clear view of the doors, holding very tightly to a cup of herbal tea. Trying to pretend that he was at least vaguely interested in the gossip swirling around him.

“You are waiting for your cousin,” said Guard Omo.

Conju was startled out of his circling thoughts. “Yes,” he said, without thinking.

“Where did you last see her?” asked the guard, sipping a cup of coffee.

“The library,” said Conju, “I couldn't find her, I left a note. I thought maybe she had gone to dinner early.”

They sat there in silence for a while. “I know some people go missing,” said Conju, “But Aurelia isn't like that.”

Guard Omo nodded, but didn't say anything.

“Guard Omo, would you be able to wait here, in case she does come? I just want to check she hasn't returned to our apartment,” said Conju. He didn't like having to ask a favour of a near-stranger, but he couldn't be in two places at once.

To Conju's relief, Guard Omo said, “I will tell her to wait here if I see her.”

Abandoning his cold tisane, Conju said “Thank you,” and bustled out of the refectory. He did his best to look like he was moving _urgently_ but not panicking. It would be good to not attract the attention of anyone else. The light of his lamp moving along the walls reminded him too much of when Lady Nerisse had been ill. There had been nothing he could do for her, nothing he could do for the many who had died. He didn't want to have failed Aurelia.

As it should be, the door to the apartment was locked. Conju opened it, and stepped inside, bringing in the light. “Good evening?” he called out, as he snapped the door shut behind him. 

There was a quiet groan from further in.

Gripping his lamp tightly, Conju rushed through the now-familiar rooms. He should probably go carefully, watching out for danger. But what was the point? He was no fighter, he needed to get to Aurelia, if that was her.

To his relief, he found her in her bedroom. “No light, please,” she whispered.

Confused, Conju put his lamp down in the hallway. Then he walked over to her bed. “Aurelia, what's wrong? What happened?” he said.

“I think… you remember… the coffee?” said Aurelia. 

Conju couldn't see her expression, but saw her dark hands gripping the blankets. He reached out one of his own and placed it on hers. “You just had a tiny amount,” he said, “And that was hours ago, at lunch.”

“It… something… caught up with me. I got the shakes… I have a headache… sorry if I bothered you…” she said.

Both worried and relieved, Conju squeezed her hand. She looked down in surprise. Embarrassed, Conju pulled his hand away.

“I'll get you a headache powder, and some water and food should help,” he said, to cover his feelings.

“Thank you,” said Aurelia, “Were you worried?”

Conju turned back in the doorway. “Of course I was worried,” he said, trying to keep his voice light, but failing and hearing it crack slightly, “But we can talk about it when you're better.”
