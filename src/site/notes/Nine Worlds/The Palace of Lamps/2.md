Conju led Aurelia down the halls, back towards his rooms. He was glad he had drunk so much mint tea, as he was pretty sure otherwise he'd have a splitting headache, rather than just a niggling one.

“What do you do?” said Aurelia, as they turned into the Ystharian wing.

“I have made something of an exploration into the art of perfume design,” replied Conju, carefully keeping the bitter tone at bay. What a question to ask a nobleman!

“You're a perfumer? For a fashion house, or an independent perfumerie?” continued Aurelia, as the crowd thinned.

“It is not a *profession*,” said Conju, letting out a drop of the exasperation he was feeling into his voice, “I do it as an *art*.”

Aurelia did not take the hint. “So, what do you do as a *profession* then? Are you with one of the ministries? A scholar?”

Conju did not stop, and he glared at a chipped vase rather than Aurelia. He had a headache, he wanted a drink, and this girl had *no manners*. What was he supposed to answer to a question like that?

“I suppose, before Silverheart, I was a courtier,” he said, completely deadpan.

“And now?” Aurelia persisted.

“I suppose I am, as you said, a hedonist,” he spat out.

They reached the door to Conju's rooms. He unlocked them, and bowed Aurelia inside. It was, at least, tidy. The Palace staff were under-resourced, and Conju under various influences. Both visited rarely, and did only a minimal job. That meant there was a slight air of mustiness and a trace of dust.

Conju handed Aurelia a small brass key. “If you would be so kind, Saya Anyra, the oak cabinet with the fretwork front contains a number of bottles. If you would put them outside the front door, I will collect my things.”

He turned away, so he did not have to watch. Some bottles in there had been gifts from friends and family, treasures he hadn't been able to face opening. As if doing so would be to admit that the givers were actually gone.

In his dressing room, he carefully packed. Clothes, jewels, lotions. Mourning clothes. He cared about how he looked, how others saw him. He skittered around thoughts of what people saw in him now. If they did at all.

Conju opened his small horde of medicines. Anything narcotic had long since been consumed, but he still had a few others. Twisting the bottles to check their contents, he selected the valerian extract, and the bromine headache powder.

When he returned to the front room, Aurelia had draped herself across his divan. She threw him the key. “All done,” she said, as he fumbled the catch and had to retrieve the key from the floor.

“My thanks,” said Conju, and made the court gesture indicating a small debt owed. Aurelia probably didn't know what it meant, he realised. He was going to have to teach her. He would have to introduce her—not to court, there wasn't really a *court* such as there had been—but to important people. Those people who would be looking into reaching out to other cities (if they existed) and other worlds (if they could be reached).

A thought for later. For now, he bowed her out of his rooms. Then he locked the door, with one last look at the bottles arrayed outside. They were tidily organised, like a choir, and he could imagine them singing to him, to take one of them with him. He wrenched his eyes away, and began to walk firmly down the corridor.

“If you're giving up the hedonism,” said Aurelia, as Conju resisted cutting her off, “what will you do now?”

“I am probably going to be violently ill for the next few days,” he said, bitterly, unsure of how much to explain, “and then it will depend on Lady Nerisse's health.”

Adjusting his grip on his bags, he gave Aurelia a more appraising look. “What have you been doing, Saya Anyra? Have you made some friends?”

“This is the first time I have left Lady Nerisse's apartment since Silverheart. She did not consider it safe,” replied Aurelia, and Conju could hear the bitterness clearly in her voice. “I have been working my way through her book collection, but it is very dull.”

“I believe my great aunt is considered a minor scholar of early Collian ceramic goddess figurines,” said Conju, neutrally. Since it was a safe topic of conversation with Lady Nerisse, he had actually heard her talk about it at length.

“Yes,” said Aurelia, and sighed dramatically, “And she has a lot of terribly morbid poetry, and some truly boring histories of Ysthar.”

“Well, Saya Anyra, I might be able to introduce you to some of the Palace libraries,” he offered, as an olive branch, “Perhaps next week?”

“Thank you, Lord Conju,” she said, in imitation of his mannerisms, which he found irritating, “that would be delightful.”

They walked on for a few minutes in silence. Finally, Aurelia asked, “Do they have anything on magic? On wild magic?”

“The Imperial Library has a copy of every book ever printed in the Empire. I would imagine they have books on both schooled and wild magic. Have you studied magic, Saya Anyra?”

“Yes,” she said, quietly, “but since Silverheart, everything has felt wrong.”

Conju considered her answer. The Ouranatha, he was reasonably sure, had little to no magic left. Why else would they be hoarding candles, when surely they could produce or repair mage lights? Some of the old spells still held, particularly the preservation spells on the storage rooms.

Her mention of wild magic was also bringing up memories. Long suppressed memories of old friends. It hurt to think about.

But Aurelia had said *wrong*, not broken. He put the thought of magic aside for later. He would deal with that when he was sober.

---

Upon reflection later, Conju struggled to imagine what torments the Cirithians thought occurred in their many hells that he hadn't experienced over the next three days. Despite (sparingly) dosing himself with what medicines he had to hand, it was the worst thing he had done to himself.

Headaches and anxiety turned to sweating and vomiting. He couldn't sleep, but he was exhausted, and his heart was beating far too fast. He asked Pioné for only the blandest foods, and lemon water, but he could barely keep a morsel down.

After two nights awake, and two days in bed, on the afternoon of the third day he finally managed to fall asleep. Only to wake up at the next bell from nightmares of the days (hours?) after Silverheart. He rolled out of bed to throw up into a bowl on the floor.

But that was the last time he threw up. Slowly the nausea dissipated, and his heart started behaving itself. That night he slept, fitfully and sweaty, but he slept.

The next morning, his head still felt like it was exploding out of his skull. But he washed and dressed, if a little slower than usual. His choice of depilatory cream over razors meant he didn't have to worry about the slight tremor in his hands.

He stepped out into the hallway, to find out what had happened while he was ill.

---

He found Aurelia in the dining room, with a tin of biscuits. She looked up at him, and winced. He had hoped he didn’t look quite *that* bad.

“Lady Nerisse died yesterday afternoon,” she said, and offered him the biscuit tin.

He made his way to the sideboard, where there was a half-empty pitcher of water, and a dubiously clean glass. He filled the glass, and sat down opposite Aurelia.

“Pioné?” he asked, his throat sore from so much stomach acid.

“Fetched some people to take the body away, and left with them,” she said, with a shrug, “I haven't seen her since.”

Conju took one of the biscuits and nibbled on it. It was ginger again. Now that he thought about it, Pioné hadn't brought him anything to eat or drink since lunch yesterday. 

At least the water was relatively fresh, and soothed his throat. He was still feeling moderately unwell. No, he did not feel fit to take charge of anything, or anyone. But here he was, and here Aurelia was. She hadn't run off; if she had, he wouldn't have blamed her.

Thoughts slowly gathered in Conju's aching head. He would need to take Aurelia out into the Palace. Introduce her. Find her some friends. Look for someone outside the Ouranatha who had some idea what had happened to the magic. Teach her some rudiments of court manners.

“Saya Anyra, I have no wish to diminish your relationship with Professor Chilperic. I promise I will attempt to return you to him as soon as it is safe to do so. However,” he paused, knowing that Aurelia already held him in very low esteem, “Given you may be with me for some time, it might be easier if I were to introduce you as my cousin, Lady Aurelia.”

Aurelia sat up straight, looking livid. “I'm not a lady, and I'm not your cousin. No.”

Conju wished he hadn't brought it up. But he was worried about her.

Not because she was an unattached girl in a Palace in chaos; orphaned children had been adopted all over. Not because she was a young woman in a Palace full of debauchery; one of the few things the Imperial Guard could do reliably was ensure that at least everyone involved were adults.

It was because, he realised, she looked uncannily like the Grand Duchess Melissa. It was the nose, yes, but the chin, the forehead, even her hair. Conju had only seen the Duchess in person once, she was mostly retired. But he had seen her portraits many times. Aurelia was, of course, far too young to be mistaken for the Duchess of Damara. But Conju could imagine the whispers.

If the Emperor ever woke, she would make the perfect bride. If he didn't, well, he was only asleep. Not dead. Perhaps the Ouranatha would work out a way to marry them anyway. Conju wouldn't put it past the Ouranatha to modify the family records to add in Aurelia, or to try to get an heir out of a sleeping Emperor.

Then there was the fact that she was a mage, and a mage with maybe more than zero powers. The Ouranatha would love to have her under their control. Possibly they would be just as happy to eliminate her, if they thought she was showing them up in any way.

He did *not* want to tell Aurelia all of this, or really any of this. But keeping her in the dark could be just as dangerous.

“Do you know who your parents were, Saya Anyra?” he asked, trying to add some sympathy into his voice.

She choked a little on her biscuit, and snatched his water. After recovering herself, she replied hesitantly, “I was a foundling, taken in by Professor Ben Chilperic in the first year of Emperor Artorin's reign. All I had with me was a slip of paper with my name on it. I was told I look much like Lady Jessamine, but she is from a long line of only children.”

“My dear, you look exactly like one of the portraits of the Grand Duchess Melissa Damara. The one for her presentation to the court of Emperor Eritanyr. She was sixteen.”

Aurelia looked sceptical. “Surely the Imperial family does not *abandon* their children at the door of Tara! If Emperor Eritanyr or Princess Lamissa had more children, they would have been carefully looked after,” said Aurelia, her voice shaking.

“True,” replied Conju, in calm, even tones, “but their younger brother left court. He could have had children, or grandchildren, with anyone in the nine worlds. He might not even have known if he did. Even if you aren't, Saya Anyra, it might not matter. Because you look like you could be…” he put down his half-eaten biscuit, and dabbed his fingers on a napkin before continuing. “There are people here, even now, who will want to take advantage of that.”

“And you think I will be safer if you can pass me off as your cousin? We don't exactly look like cousins!” She looked no more convinced than before.

Conju considered his own golden fingers, and the dark shapes of his aristocratic tattoos. And her near-black skin, and fine long fingers. “My father was a confidant of Prince Eritanyr, my grandmother of Empress Anyoë. My family moved in circles where someone *might* have been granted permission to marry a *very* minor member of the Imperial family. Dressed the part, Saya Anyra, you would pass.”

“Too minor to take advantage of?” she asked, uncertain.

“Exactly,” he said, more firmly, “I will need to sit down with an imperial family tree, and find a likely candidate, if anyone does start asking detailed questions.”

Aurelia sighed deeply, and started crumbling a biscuit into dust. “But what if I am actually Emperor Artorin's cousin, or second cousin, or whatever.”

Conju could see the pain there. He had always known every member of his family, out to the third or even fourth degree. But now he had lost every one of them. She had never known her birth family, and had been snatched from the family that raised her. He understood the hope that there might be someone out there, even if that feeling was newer to him.

“If someday, when you are back at Tara, it is safe to know, I will help you find out,” he offered.

She nodded, “Alright.” She offered her hand to shake, over the table.

Conju shook it, a little dubious of the custom from Alinor.

“Then,” said Conju, trying to regain his old composure, “Shall we go to breakfast, Lady Aurelia?”

Aurelia rolled her eyes at him, “Whatever you like, Lord Conju.”
