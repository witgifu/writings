The paperwork, as always, is the simplest part of the process. A contract, two statutory declarations, and Cliopher looks up to see Melissa and his lord actually smiling as they discuss the merits of some poets that he has never heard of. Then he realises he has presumed that this is the kind of marriage they want. Zunidh provides a wide variety of choice.

At a lull in the discussion, he asks, “My lord, your grace, would you have a preference for which marriage rite you would like? There are many kinds of civil and religious traditions available.”

“I have enjoyed studying their diversity,” says his Radiancy with a smile, before sobering, “Whatever is simplest is probably best, unless you have a preference, Melissa?”

“No, my—Artorin. Although I would prefer if the Ouranatha were nor involved,” she replies.

“Quite,” adds his Radiancy, dryly.

Since Cliopher hasn't yet heard anything from Rhodin, he is also inclined not to involve the priest-wizards. Well, a civil wedding is just as valid, and rather easier to organise. Especially as Cliopher happens to have already prepared it.

“Since you both have substantial estates and wealth, I prepared a contract, my lord. As I am sure any lawyer would advise under the circumstances,” says Cliopher.

“Yes, I would,” says his Radiancy, and Cliopher is not *quite* sure if that is a joke.

“If you would both please examine it carefully,” Cliopher says, and passes the document over. As he expects, his lord does so, and Cliopher is relieved when there are no corrections. He is also happy to see that when his Radiancy passes it to the Grand Duchess, she reads it over with the same diligence.

“The statutory declaration is the same for both of you,” Cliopher adds, “If Conju is willing to act as an additional witness, we can complete them now, if you would like, my lord.” He passes over the two papers, where he has tentatively put their declaration of intention to wed dated as the first regnal year of Artorin Damara. 

“Are we claiming to have lost the 'intention to wed' paperwork during the Fall, or in the subsequent centuries?” asks his Radiancy, eyes on the paper.

“Any credible public record would be sufficient,” says Cliopher, slightly nervous, “I am sure there are many copies of pre-Fall periodicals in the Archives that would qualify.”

“Quite,” says the Grand Duchess, exactly matching his Radiancy's tone on the Ouranatha. They share a small smile.

“Let us adjourn to the study, then, where there are more pens,” says his Radiancy.

They do so, Conju using Cliopher's pens, and the Grand Duchess using his Radiancy's. Finally, they get to the oaths.

“I, Artorin Damara of Solaara, swear the contents of this declaration are true and correct,” says his Radiancy, in the formal tones he would use for reading a proclamation in the throne room.

“I, Melissa Damara of Damara, swear the contents of this declaration are true and correct,” says the Grand Duchess, in the cool voice Cliopher has heard her use in the Council of Princess.

As they sign and date, Cliopher suspects that both his lord and her grace are relieved that there are no 'until death part us' or promises of obedience or declarations of love. It is sad, however, that neither of them have had the chance for that kind of public statement with anyone. They could not afford the risk. Perhaps someday, after they quietly separate, and when neither of them rule anything, they can have such a relationship.

“Shall I hold onto these until the announcement is made, my lord?” asks Cliopher, bundling the paper together.

“Yes, that would probably be safest,” says his Radiancy, “If we appoint you as Lord Chancellor at the opening of court tomorrow,” (behind him, Conju is closing his eyes and biting his lip), “Do you think we could hold off on the announcement until Emperor Day itself?”

Conju's sudden indrawn breath is perhaps slightly louder than he intended.

“Yes, my Cavalier?” asks his Radiancy, “You think that unwise? Very well, what would you advise.”

“If you would be amenable, my lord, perhaps we could make the announcement a month before Emperor Day? So that everything can be arranged appropriately. As for Cliopher, he will need a new court costume for his investiture, my lord, and I am sure you would wish to be involved in the design,” says Conju, bowing deeply.

Cliopher shudders inside at the cost that will be involved in his court costume as _Lord Chancellor_. He will have to ensure there is a budget assigned to the position, that is his only hope for tempering his friend's desire for extravagance.

“Very well,” says his Radiancy, “And you may tell a few people in the Office of State as required to organise things, Cliopher.”

“Thank you, my lord,” says Cliopher, but he has another question first, “Would you be able to tell, that is, have you noticed, any change in the curse, my lord?”

His Radiancy blinks twice in surprise. “Ah,” he says, “Indeed there should be. As we are now…,” he takes a deep breath, and closes his eyes, “Yes, I can feel it is… calmer. Quiescent even.”

Cliopher remembers what he has said when one of his colleagues had gotten married, or his cousins. The celebrations, the congratulations. What can he say now?

He makes the deepest bow of thanks he knows. “My lord, your highness,” he says softly, “Thank you, on behalf of all of Zunidh, for your incredible sacrifice for us.”

“Thank *you*, Cliopher,” says his Radiancy, “You have muted this curse with the skill of Harbut Zalarin. I only wish we could do more for you, Melissa. Again, the women of our family are left with the larger burden.”

Left unspoken by all of them, is the uncertainty of what the child of a hundred generations of inbreeding will be. It is not the Empire, where stillbirth and infanticide were the silent fate of their unnamed siblings. Cliopher is sure the cruelty to the point of madness of their cousin and uncle is weighing on them as well.

Being made Lord Chancellor is far more work than Cliopher expected when he accepted. It seems that his lord and Conju have decided to gang up on him with unannounced clauses. He is starting to wish he had insisted on a written contract, when Conju informs him that not only has he already been moved to the apartment for the Lord Magus of Zunidh, but the Groom of the Chamber has organised a household for him.

Unsurprisingly, when Cliopher tells her about the appointment, Kiri assumes it is the 'special project' Cliopher had implied. Since this is not entirely untrue, he says, “There is more to it than me being made Lord Chancellor.”

“Of course there is, sir,” is Kiri's enigmatic reply.

Cliopher thinks for a moment, and decides Kiri will definitely need to know more, so she can help organise things prior to the official announcement in a month. “Come into my office,” he says, “And let me explain a bit.”

He closes the door behind them, and Kiri's eyebrows go up. “Who am I allowed to tell?” she asks.

“For the next month, the absolute minimum number of people. Ideally, nobody,” said Cliopher, absently sorting the papers on his desk. “Then we will make the public statement, and of course everyone will know and no doubt all hell will break loose.”

Kiri crosses her arms. “Are you going to audit the Ouranatha? What is happening?”

“In a month's time, we will be informing the people of his Radiancy's marriage,” says Cliopher, pulling open a drawer, looking for a box of document clips. “A month after that, on Emperor Day, will be the public celebrations.”

Stumbling backwards into a chair, Kiri asks, “So… it's already happened? When?”

“Yesterday,” says Cliopher, finally finding the box, which is empty, “Just a civil marriage by statutory declaration. He did not wish to involve the Imperial Cult.”

“Just… just like that? When… did he propose?” says Kiri, clearly struggling to breath.

“Legally, in the first year of his reign of course,” says Cliopher, tossing the empty box into the recycling.

“Sir?” says Kiri, and Cliopher looks up to see her in some distress. He fetches her a glass of water from the carafe on the side-table. She takes a sip and says, “But… I thought you joined the Service in something like the seventh year?”

Cliopher blinks. How, exactly, is that related? “Yes… I did,” he says slowly.

“And before that you were in the Vangaveye-ve? At university?” she says, now more calm.

“Yes, well, it took me five years after I graduated to actually _pass_ the exams,” he says, somewhat embarrassed. It's not something he particularly enjoys talking about at work, although he has made substantial reforms to the exams that he thinks would mean it would take him fewer attempts. More people from the hinterlands apply and are accepted than ever were during the Empire.

“I still don't understand how you met his Radiancy in the first year of his reign?” ask Kiri, with the focused confusion on her face the Cliopher associates with unbalanced budgets.

“I didn't,” says Cliopher. Then it hits him. “His Radiancy has wed the Grand Duchess, Melissa Damara,” he clarifies. How is it that _everyone_ seems to be assuming his lord would be marrying _him_? And it's not like it's just Rhodin, who does have some strange ideas at times, or people new to the Palace. Conju, the Grand Duchess, and now Kiri. Some of the most grounded and logical people he knows.

“Oh,” says Kiri, quietly. After a pause she adds another, stronger, “Oh!”

“His Radiancy is wishing to undertake several simultaneous projects,” says Cliopher, “As Lord Magus and as the head of the government of Zunidh. Doing those in addition to his current responsibilities will simply not be possible, but the legal structures of power limit his options. By making the Grand Duchess into Princess Consort, he will be able to devolve some of his powers to her.”

“And by making you Lord Chancellor, they are both able to delegate to you,” adds Kiri.

“Exactly,” says Cliopher. “It may not be a popular choice, but we are hoping to use any… disquiet… to fuel further reforms, which will be part of the projects.”

Kiri sits there for a while, and Cliopher knows her well enough to see she is thinking through all the implications. She has a far better grasp on public sentiment than Cliopher does, particularly in the upper classes and larger cities.

“Do you foresee it causing problems?” he asks, tentatively, “No doubt someone in Nijan will have a strong opinion, but in general…?”

“I can't pretend a lot of people aren't going to be confused by his Radiancy marrying the Grand Duchess,” says Kiri, slowly, “Those who would like to see him marry to make an heir will be upset that she is too old. Those who believe he will live forever won't understand why he can't wait for whatever he wants to do. If we can be more specific about the division of powers, that will probably help.”

“And that they are brother and sister…?” asks Cliopher. It is certainly the part that _he_ found most distressing.

Kiri shrugs, “If his Radiancy is clear he is not seeking an heir, most people probably won't even think about it.”

Cliopher decides he will never understand how the public view their Emperor.

For some reason he cannot quite fathom, Conju, his Radiancy, and the Grand Duchess, have all decided that Cliopher needs to be invited along on the newlywed's dates. It's not as if they need a chaperone, like the cosseted children of nobles. But yet they all claim to need his attendance. 

At the beginning, these are all quite logical. He is invited on a walk in the gardens so they can discuss what changes should be made to the Emperor Day celebrations. A dinner where they strategise over the announcement to the Council of Princes. When the first draft of the official public declaration is complete, they gather to edit it.

One morning when his Radiancy takes his usual break for tea, the Grand Duchess appears, and they all move to the balcony. This precipitates a tricky discussion about prenatal care. His Radiancy suggest his own doctor, the Grand Duchess would prefer someone who specialises in midwifery. Cliopher offers to collect a list of doctors in the Palace and Solaara who are appropriately qualified, and have Rhodin check it over.

Yet, there are many more where Cliopher is not sure why he is being included. He is invited to join them at the opera, where they have a lovely discussion of the music. His Radiancy judges a cake decorating contest, and asks the Grand Duchess and Cliopher to help.

Finally, he decides to raise the issue with the Grand Duchess. He brings it up in her office, in a private moment when her secretary has gone to fetch a book she needs.

“Your grace, I am not quite sure how to ask this,” he says, shuffling his papers to hide his anxiety, “And it's not that I don't enjoy spending time with you or his Radiancy, of course, but I am just a little confused. Are you or his Radiancy worried about gossip if I am not with you? But that would not explain the private events…”

“Lord Mdang,” she says, and Cliopher cannot stop himself from wincing.

“Please call me Cliopher, your grace,” he interjects.

“Very well, Cliopher, then you must call me Melissa,” says the Grand Duchess. Cliopher looks at her in pure shock. She smiles, and continues, “If Artorin can manage it, I am sure you will cope.”

“Yes, your—Melissa,” says Cliopher, trying to reassert his court face.

“We have had two discussions without you,” says Melissa, “They did not go so well. Both of us have been trained in how to be what we are expected to be; it is hard for us to turn that off. And we both enjoy your company.”

Cliopher is now blushing furiously. However, the secretary returns, and he feels he can't ask any more questions.

“Will you be part of the delegation to Ysthar, do you think, Cliopher?” asks Melissa, as the secretary passes her the book.

“Yes, I would assume so,” says Cliopher, “And probably Ser Rhodin, a few other guards, and some specialists. His Radiancy might invite the Mother of the Mountains, I am not sure.”

“Good,” says Melissa, “I will appreciate having you as a familiar face.”

“Would you not want any of your own household to come?” asks Cliopher, confused.

“No, I don't think that will be necessary,” says Melissa, “It is not as if I will have to dress for court, and I am sure you and your staff will be best placed for anything else.”

When Vinyë was in the last stages of her pregnancies, she was helped by their mother and a rotating schedule of aunts and cousins. Her husband and best friend both attended the births. In the early months afterwards, all the family chipped in to help make sure Vinyë could focus on recovering and didn't have to worry about anything else. 

Melissa is talking about going to another world, and she doesn't even want to take a friend. Or perhaps, realises Cliopher, doesn't have anyone she trusts enough. Except for his Radiancy, who cannot be there, and his Radiancy's inner household. Oh, how lonely she must be. No wonder she wants to know Cliopher better.

“Of course, we will do everything we can for you,” says Cliopher, with a small smile. To the mental list of things he needs to ask the Lord of Ysthar about, he adds doula services.

“Thank you,” she replies, returning the smile.
