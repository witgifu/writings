Rhodin clears a shortlist of Solaaran health care practitioners before the announcement, and arranges for the first to visit Melissa the day before. Cliopher politely inquires if she would like any support, assuming that his Radiancy might want to be assured of her good health.

“I will if you would like, Melissa,” says his Radiancy, “But it might be easier for the midwife if I do not, at least at first.”

“Yes,” says Melissa, with a nod, “Perhaps later, once we are sure of them. But would you be able to come, Cliopher?”

“Of course,” he answers, without consulting his schedule. The only thing on it more important would be a meeting with his Radiancy, who would move it. “Any time, Melissa.”

Cliopher wishes again that Melissa had any friend or family she trusted to be with her. But Cliopher can't imagine a worse idea than inviting Princess Anastasia. If *he* had been pregnant (what a thought) he would want Vinyë or Conju.

To Cliopher's surprise, the Damaran apartment includes a small sickroom. Melissa will formally move to the Imperial Apartments after the announcement, and as far as he knows she hasn't told any of her staff. Cliopher has left such arrangements to Conju, with relief.

The midwife appears, escorted by Rhodin in civvies. “This is Domina August Winsome,” says Rhodin, with a casual smile.

“Please,” says the Domina, “Just August is fine, I have a research doctorate, not a medical one.”

Melissa smiles, “A research doctorate in redefining risk criteria away from using advanced maternal age. And you are an academic at the university. If the philosophers and botanists can call themselves Domina, why not you?”

August looks surprised. “Yes, that is true, your grace,” she says, and then smiles back, “Although these days I do teaching and practice, not research.”

“Shall we get started, then?” says Melissa.

Rhodin salutes, and closes the door with himself outside.

August turns to Melissa, “You believe you're pregnant, your grace? When was your last period?”

“About six months ago, and then three months before that. The date of conception was two months ago, the fourteenth” says Melissa, calmly.

“Do you know how old you are?” asks August, gently. Cliopher notices she hasn't got out any of the usual forms, or even a notepad. Perhaps Rhodin already had a talk with her about the need extreme privacy.

“I'm afraid the Fall rather mixed up the counting. Possibly somewhere around a thousand, depending on who you ask. I would say late forties,” says Melissa.

“But you're certain about the pregnancy? What symptoms have you had?” asks August. 

Melissa replies, “Are you familiar with the test where one injects urine into a female toad?”

The midwife looks very happy. “Oh yes, that's quite reliable. Well done, your grace. You wouldn't believe the number of people who still try burning a ribbon soaked in urine. So, you are tentatively ten weeks along,” says August. “Would you mind if I feel your belly, your grace? I wouldn't expect any movement or heartbeat at this stage, but it's good to have a reference point.”

There's a bed, and Melissa lies down and re-arranges her robes. Cliopher quietly turns so he can't see. It doesn't take them long, before Cliopher hears the sound of fabric moving back, and he returns slowly back to face them.

“Excellent, now, since you know how to do the toad test, I'm guessing you've already made some dietary changes?” asks August.

Melissa nods, “I am not sure if my information is up-to-date, but plenty of cooked seafood, and legumes.”

“I wish all my pregnant people were as prepared as you, your grace! I'm not sure why I'm even here," says August, but when she sees Melissa's reaction quickly adds, "No, sorry, I'm just teasing.”

There is something a little fragile-looking about Melissa. Cliopher moves over to stand next to her, and squeezes her shoulder. He knows she is anxious, and has every reason to expect this to be one of the hardest experiences of her life.

“We will be travelling to Ysthar on a diplomatic mission about a month before the baby is due,” says Melissa, looking up at Cliopher in thanks, and then turning back to August. “We expect to return when they are about six months old, although it will only have been a month or so here.”

August looks between the two of them, brows lowered in thought. “You seem prepared enough that I will change my question. What are your plans for the birth on Ysthar?”

“The Lord of Ysthar has kindly agreed to help us organise to have the birth at a major hospital in his home city,” says Cliopher, “I believe it is called Saint Mary's, and it has a highly regarded birthing unit.”

“Would you want me to accompany you to Ysthar, your grace? I am not sure the doctors and midwives there will appreciate an… interloper,” says August, but her smile belays the intensity of the dismissal Cliopher suspects she expects.

“I am a little worried about making the journey without any medical assistance,” says Melissa, and Cliopher cringes inside that he hadn't even considered that. What if she went into labour before they made it to Ysthar? What if they were on a sky ship? “And perhaps it would be possible for you to provide some notes for them? I'm sure people on Ysthar find themselves giving birth away from their home.”

August nods, “That sounds like a very responsible plan, your grace. How long do you expect the journey from Solaara to take?”

“One day by sky ship to the City of Emeralds, and then two days by road to the gate,” says Cliopher, having travelled the route when the gate was first established. “If you were able to come, August, we would make sure you were able to use a sky ship for the return as well. And full pay as a member of Melissa's household for the week.”

“I'm sure the university administration will be thrilled to hear I want to take a week or more of leave,” says August, “They tried to lock me out of my office, you know, to make sure I took some. I got one of my students to pick the lock.”

They all laugh a little, and open calendars to plan out the rest of the appointments. Cliopher is relieved that this first candidate is a good fit. He was worried that even if Rhodin had cleared them, the Grand Duchess's rejection might lead to someone 'accidentally' leaking information.

The morning Council of Princess session is always going to be the most stressful. They have scheduled the public announcement for noon, which means Cliopher ploughs through the agenda items with, even for him, unusual vigour. Finally, at half a bell before noon, he stands again.

“My lord, the last item for today is your announcement,” he says, facing his Radiancy, but unable to stop his eyes darting around the room.

“Thank you, Cliopher,” says his Radiancy, an unusually informal address in Council. In all but the most curated of circumstances, he addresses Cliopher as 'Sayo Mdang', or now 'Lord Mdang', in public. This is one of the exceptions.

“Since the Littleridge Treaty, the foundation of our government has been ourself. However, in the centuries that have followed, our government has, by necessity, become larger and more complex. As peace and prosperity had spread through our world, our focus has naturally moved from immediate amelioration of the worst disasters, to questions of trade and taxation, preservation of the natural world, and the development of new technology. We are facing another such step change, and it is no longer possible, nor desirable, for that to rest on one person.”

Cliopher looks around the Princes. It seems to him that Prince Rufus is hoping for more power to fall on him personally, he is barely containing a grin. Most of the rest look confused. Oriana is hardly paying attention.

“Yet, we are bound by the Treaty, and the structures of the old Empire of Astandalas that it rests upon.” (Prince Rufus's grin falls) “We have no desire to reopen old wounds or cause any damage to our precious peace by renegotiating what has served us all so well.”

“We have, after due consideration, chosen for Grand Duchess Melissa Damara to be our Princess Consort. We shall continue as Lord Magus, and judge of the Court of Final Resort. To her, we devolve much of our work with the Ouranatha, and the legislative and administrative powers of the mundial government. Including as chair of this Council.”

There is a tense silence. Everyone wants to look at the Grand Duchess, now the Princess Consort. Most of them hold to their well-trained etiquette and keep their eyes on the Last Emperor. Princess Oriana, however, gives Melissa a look of supreme jealousy. Princess Anastasia looks like she has been replaced by a statue of herself.

His Radiancy stands, and so, of course, does everyone else. With a regal nod to Melissa, he sweeps out of the room, followed by his guards. Two more guards enter, dressed in the full panoply. Melissa walks around from her accustomed position to stand on the dais. Then she sits on what had, until moments ago, been his Radiancy's throne.

The silence is incredibly tense. Princess Anastasia's left hand, Cliopher can just see, is balled into a fist. Princess Oriana starts to open her mouth, and Cliopher rapidly interrupts her.

“My most sincere congratulations, your highness,” he says, with the bow to an Imperial Consort, “I have always admired your stewardship of Old Damara, and all your work with this Council. I look forward to working with you more closely.” As if the three of them had not spent every spare moment of the past month carefully working through how this would function.

“Thank you, Lord Mdang,” she says clearly, and gives the gesture for everyone to sit. “I expect there will be some questions.”

To nobody's surprise, Prince Rufus asks the first one. It is, Cliopher realises two or three minutes in, more of a comment than a question. After five more minutes, Princess Melissa actually cuts him off. “If you would like to talk more in depth, Prince Rufus, about this new arrangement, I suggest you make a personal appointment.”

Cliopher contains the grin he wants to make behind his mild court mask. Working with Melissa is going to be *fun*.

“Of course, your highness,” says Prince Rufus, slightly flustered, “Will his Radiancy continue to preside at court?”

“Artorin and I will share that honour,” she says, calmly. Ignoring the twitch in Princess Anastasia's face at anyone addressing the Emperor by his first name.

“So you finally married? And we are calling you 'your highness' now? I never knew why you didn't use the title before,” titters Oriana. Cliopher thinks that's a bit rich coming from a woman who has shown no interest in marriage *or* governance.

“Yes,” says Melissa, coolly. Cliopher appreciates how she has used one word to respond to the questions, *and* imply Oriana is an idiot for not knowing why Melissa might have preferred to rule as Grand Duchess. Oriana won't catch it, of course, but everyone else on the council does. Even her Jilkanese cousins.

“Do you have any plans to change this council, your highness?” asks the Prince of Western Dair.

“No,” answers Melissa, with a warmer smile, “I have appreciated the hard work of everyone here, and will continue to chair it much as Artorin did.”

Princess Anastasia flinches again, and Cliopher wonders how many times that will happen before she gets used to it, or injured herself. Everyone else relaxes noticeably.

“Let us adjourn for lunch then,” says Melissa, and stands. Everyone else does with her, and it suddenly hits Cliopher. Part of Princess Anastasia's irritation is surely that she is now the *lowest* ranked member of their family. Last of three doesn't feel significant to Cliopher, but he has never cared much for rank. His Radiancy's great-aunt sets rather more store by it.

The slightly early dismissal means Cliopher can return to the Offices of State in time for the public announcement. Much as they would for any major announcement, they have set up a large meeting room for representatives of the various significant unions, newspapers, and guilds. Cliopher nods to them as he enters the room, grabbing an egg tart.

“Excellent, your excellency,” says Kiri, with a smile (she has been using this line a lot) “Since you’re here, you can handle the conference.”

Cliopher gives her a small sarcastic smile, “Very well.”

He moves to the front of the room, and pulls the announcement out of his writing kit. “Everyone here?” he asks, and a rumble of agreement runs through the room. “Let’s get going then.”

He reads the document aloud, with one eye on the room. Those representatives who have some relationship with Old Damara seem curious. Everyone else looks slightly worried. As he continues, Cliopher is quietly pleased to see most of the worry dissolves.

“Any questions?” he asks.

Unsurprisingly, the Cisven Flyer responds first. “His Radiancy and her highness have no plans for an heir to the Imperial throne?”

“That would be rather counter-productive,” says Cliopher, “Since we have already established that the role is too large for one person.”

The reporter gives Cliopher a curious look, but since they do not ask the next question, he puts it out of his mind. And no further questions are asked about the personal relationships.

Most questions are simply if various processes of the government have or will change. None of them are immediately, all of them will be subject to the usual reviews and audits and may change as needed. The newspapers want to make sure they have the details of the changes to the Emperor Day plans exactly right, which Cliopher appreciates.

The Cisven Flyer also asks the last question, “Will this change your relationship with the Glorious One, your excellency?”

“I've been Lord Chancellor less than a month,” says Cliopher, “I think that's enough change for me for at least the next six. But no, I don't expect our relationship to change.” All the journalists scribble frantically. Cliopher hopes nobody seriously thought he was in trouble.

They wrap up, with a few other minor notices, and then Kiri corners Cliopher with a plate of rice paper rolls.

That evening, he goes into Solaara with Conju, Rhodin, and Ludvic. His friends seem to have something planned, but refuse to give him any details. Ludvic shows them to a small bar, down in the Levels where Cliopher is nearly certain Conju would never go. The area seems friendly enough, and Ludvic takes them to a booth at the back.

When the bead curtain drops, the familiar feeling of silence surrounds them.

“If you just wanted that, we could have gone to any number of places,” says Conju, tightly. Cliopher can tell he feels uncomfortable with the level of poverty around them. Perhaps Cliopher could bring the Council of Princes down here to convince them of the merits of the stipend plan he has. Probably not, he decides. They'll just suggest another Indrogan Estate. But this is working poverty, not destitution.

“Stop thinking about work,” says Rhodin, handing him a small glass of clear liquid.

“What's this?” asks Cliopher, noting that the others all obviously have beer bottles. It doesn't smell of anything in particular, but he doesn't think it's water.

“Ròs,” says Ludvic, and Cliopher vaguely recalls the drink is highly potent.

“And why are you trying to get me drunk?” he asks, curious, “You know I have work tomorrow. Are you planning a coup and trying to get me out of the way?”

“Don't be ridiculous,” says Conju, “We're only doing what you did for me when that awful thing with the florist happened.”

“The one who ran off with his business partner and ended up arrested in Cisven for assault and battery?” asks Cliopher. It was particularly horrible, as Conju first blamed the business partner for stealing his boyfriend, only to find the florist had been abusing the poor young man for years.

“Yes,” says Conju, darkly.

Cliopher looks at them all, thoroughly confused. Eventually he turns to Rhodin. “There hasn't been another Emerald Conspiracy, has there? I don't think I've seen anyone since Susan.”

Conju stares at him, and swaps his beer for Cliopher's ròs. 

“You'll be happy to hear I have no security concerns,” says Rhodin, sipping his beer. “Since you seem chipper about the whole situation. You haven't even seemed slightly resentful.”

Conju very carefully sips the ròs, “This is better than I expected.”

“I'm still confused,” says Cliopher, looking at each of them in turn. “Please could one of you spell it out? Or do I have to wait until Conju finishes the ròs to find out?”

After a long, awkward silence, Cliopher adds another, “Please?”

“You love his Radiancy,” says Ludvic, his face as inscrutable as ever.

“Of course,” says Cliopher, “We all do, we wouldn't have these jobs if we didn't.”

“I might,” says Rhodin, and Conju gives him a truly withering look, “Although probably not if it was Anastasia.”

They all share a quiet moment of horror at the idea of working for the iron Princess.

“That was a terrifying thought, Rhodin,” says Conju. “Yes, we all do love his Radiancy. But I have a boyfriend,” (Rhodin looks at him interested, and Conju kicks him under the table when he opens his mouth) “Rhodin has a rotating collection of… acquaintances. You *have* had lovers in the past, but it seemed that you had decided your love for his Radiancy came first.”

Cliopher blinks. “This is about how Conju and Kiri and Melissa all assumed his Radiancy would want to marry *me*, isn't it.”

Conju stares, and takes a large sip of the ròs. Rhodin grins, “I knew you'd work it out eventually.”

“Since when do you call her highness the Princess Consort by her first name?” asks Conju.

“Since she asked me to?” says Cliopher, blinking, “Surely you've heard me do so before? We've been together plenty.”

“Have you called his Radiancy by his first name?” asks Rhodin. Conju looks ill at the very idea.

“No, of course not,” says Cliopher, “He hasn't asked me to.” He is starting to wish he had the ròs, the beer is delicious, but it isn't helping with his anxiety.

Conju whimpers slightly, eyeing the ròs as if it might be at fault for Cliopher's behaviour. Then he drinks more anyway.

“So you don't think the Grand Duchess has stolen your soulmate?” asks Rhodin.

Cliopher looks between them all, anger starting to rise. Then he remembers that Melissa shared her story when none of them were there. Nor did any of them see how devastated his Radiancy was when he first told Cliopher. He's not even sure if they know who the father of the child is.

“No,” says Cliopher firmly, “They are both victims of terrible crimes. And anyway, surely all of you know… it would be vastly inappropriate… I would never consider myself…”

“We know, Cliopher,” says Conju, with a sigh, “Well, at least you know he loves you too, even if it has to be a romantic tragedy.”

“What? No, he… I… no?” says Cliopher, possibly one of the least coherent things he has said while sober.

Conju closes his eyes, and puts his head on the table.

“Yes,” says Ludvic, “He loves you.”

Cliopher swears softly. 

Rhodin starts to chortle, but before long he has made it to guffaws. “I suppose,” he says, trying to breathe, “That you and he are the only people who don't get to watch you two together.”

Having run out of anything to say, Cliopher just nudges the glass of ròs closer to Conju's hand. Then he takes a swig of his beer.

“Tomorrow,” says Conju, into the table, “I will send you the article from the Cisven Flyer about the Princess Consort and you and him with all the innuendo underlined. Maybe with sufficient practice you can learn to see it.”
