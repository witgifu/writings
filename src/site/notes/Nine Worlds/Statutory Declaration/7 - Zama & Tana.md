Kip Mdang,

Your uncle Lazo forwarded me your request, and I must admit I am intrigued. I know your mother, of course, but Eidora had always given the impression you were a minor secretary for a lord of the court. Well, I suppose you'd hardly be signing letters home to your mama as 'Lord Mdang', but maybe you should once just so we can see her face!

I now have to do the annoying thing old people do, and say that you probably don't remember me, but I remember you. Although you were perhaps only five or six at the time, just starting school, when I last saw you at the hundred-day-feast of one of your cousins. So you can probably be forgiven for not remembering, and I can be forgiven for being surprised that you have grown so much. (Or at least I hope you have!)

Let me be honest, Kip. I am not sure if I'm the right person for you to be sending off on an important mission to Ysthar. I've never been further than Jilkano. Yes, the Ela go farthest, but sometimes I wonder how far that is when so many Islanders don't even leave the Ring. Well, and perhaps I haven't set a good example, not leaving it myself since the Fall.

However, if nothing else I can come to your little pre-visit get-together and complain at you there. We've talked it over, Lazo and Tovo and I. Lazo says you visit by sky ship these days, and that it takes you three days to get here from Solaara. So I assume that there must be other ways faster than the nine months I guess it would take to sail. If you still want me to come and tell you off for not listening to your elders, (or putting your mango-covered hands in the sand), let me know.

Kawa Ela

Cliopher can't say how relieved he is to get the zamà's response. It's not the definite _yes_ he was hoping for, but perhaps if she attends the little conference he's trying to organise, someone there will convince her to go. Honestly, he'd settle for her finding a surprise wild mage among the people of Solaara. 

He replies promptly with a suggested itinerary (including optional direct return from Solaara to Gorjo City), and more details on who else will be attending. He's not exactly sure how old she is, possibly somewhere between Uncle Lazo and Buru Tovo's ages. So he suggests that if she'd like to bring one or two people with her, she's welcome to do so.

Thus, Cliopher finds himself waiting at the sky ship dock on a slightly windy afternoon. Following some negotiation with his household, he has only one footman and two guards waiting with him. They don't quite seem to understand that it is important for Cliopher to help, that not doing so would mean he was claiming to at least be equal to the zamà.

It is nice, he admits to himself, to admire the view, and the sky ship staff at their work. With brisk efficiency they are moored, and the rope bridge attached. Then, coming across the bridge are two people, one he knows very well and one who looks vaguely familiar. As they step off the bridge, Cliopher bows over his hands to both of them.

“Welcome, Buru Kawa, Buru Tovo,” says Cliopher, “Is there anything we can carry for you?”

Kawa smiles, and gestures to the sailor carrying her trunk. He looks rather like he wishes he wasn't carrying it on his own, which is impressive for a man who does so much physical labour. The footman hurries over to help. Cliopher can't imagine what she has brought that is so heavy.

“Thank you so much for coming so far,” says Cliopher, to both of them. He's not entirely sure why his great-uncle has come, he was imagining that Kawa might want to bring a younger friend or relative. Buru Tovo is even older than her, and a bit hard of hearing, with weak eyes.

“Eh?” says Buru Tovo loudly, “I can't understand you boy, speak properly.”

Perhaps he hasn't slept enough lately, or perhaps it's the shock of seeing his Buru Tovo in such an incongruous place as the Palace of Stars. Bowing over his hands again, Cliopher says, “Tē ke’e’vina-tē zēnava parahë’ala.” As he straightens, he blushes awkwardly. He has no idea if Kawa speaks Language. Now that he thinks about it, Tovo told him off in Shaian, so he probably just meant Cliopher's accent.

“Tō mo’ea-tō avivayë o rai’ivayë,” replies Kawa, and then adds, “But I'm a bit rusty, so if you don't mind Tovo I'll stick to Shaian.”

“You still remember it?” asks Buru Tovo, giving Cliopher a look up and down.

“Yes, Buru Tovo,” says Cliopher, trying to thicken his accent to his home one. It feels incongruous, dressed in the grand clothes that Conju (and his Radiancy) insisted fit his new station.

Together they go back to his apartment, mostly in silence. It's a very busy time of day in the Palace, with many of the Service finishing up work. This means that a lot of people stop to bow. Neither Tovo nor Kawa mention it, so Cliopher doesn't bring it up.

Kawa and Tovo both enjoyed the sky ship, it seems. They trade stories of making friends with the crew and learning how the ship works. Kawa said she found the way the ships moved between the lights 'particularly elegant', and it seems that Tovo also appreciated the views. Cliopher is not brave enough to ask which _particular_ views he enjoyed, he suspects if he does so he will hear far more about how much time Tovo spent looking at the sailors rather than the landscape.

There is always the chance of the sky ships being delayed a day or two by bad weather, and so Kip arranged for Kawa to arrive two days before the conference starts. He is honestly uncertain what they would like to do, but Kawa also knows about the spare time from the itinerary Cliopher sent earlier. So Cliopher politely waits to be told over dinner.

“Do you still dance, Kip?” asks Buru Tovo, happily devouring the curry they've been served.

Cliopher hangs his head. “Not as often as I should, Buru Tovo,” he says. He had plans to find space in his new apartment to practice, and time. But trying to handle the ramifications of the curse have rather eaten into what spare time he had. Still, he could have made time if he'd really wanted to.

Kawa elbows Tovo, “Are you always so hard on him?”

Buru Tovo snorts, “Just a question, Kawa. Just wondering if now he's a big fancy man if he still remembers us, is all.”

Cliopher shrinks down in his seat even further. Isn't that just what his mother and friends would ask, what they will ask, if they find out he's a lord now?

“Oi!” says Buru Tovo, as Kawa kicks him this time.

“_Obviously_ he remembers you, you great gull,” says Kawa, grinning, “And now he looks like he expects to be sent off to weed the garden.”

Cliopher blinks at Kawa, astonished. “It's alright,” he says, automatically, “The clothes come with the job, I didn't choose them. I don't even pick them out in the morning, I don't think people trust me to do it right.”

Uncle Tovo laughs at that, “No such problems in a proper grass skirt, eh?”

“No, Buru Tovo,” says Cliopher, “Perhaps you can try to convince my costumer to let me wear one.”

There's a glint in Buru Tovo's eye that suggests he might. Instead, he says, “I guess you'll be working tomorrow and the day after?”

“Yes,” says Cliopher, letting out a little sigh, because he _would_ like to spend more time with his great-uncle. “Tomorrow evening I have court, and then the day after is a Council of Princess session. I—”

He is interrupted by the appearance of Frenzel in his field of view, tilting his head as if asking permission to speak. “Yes, Frenzel?” he says, instead.

“A message has arrived from the Imperial Apartments, sir, I thought you might want it immediately,” he says, proffering a folded piece of paper.

Surprised, Cliopher opens it, expecting perhaps a reminder from Conju. It is a message from his Radiancy.

---

Cliopher,

Ser Rhodin has completed his work. We will need to act swiftly. If your guest has arrived, you are welcome to bring them. There may be some dramatics.

A

---

Never before has Cliopher received such a summons. When invited to spend the evening with his Radiancy and Melissa, typically one of the tower staff had been sent with a verbal invitation. If it is work-related, his Radiancy would just send a page, not a note.

“Ow,” says Kawa, “That was a powerful seal on that note. Sounded like the sea train coming in.”

“Seal?” says Cliopher, looking the note over. It didn't seem to have be closed in any way.

“Yes, so only you could open it,” replies Kawa, wincing slightly, “I assume that's from the Lord Magus?”

Cliopher nods. “We have been asked to attend while he…,” Cliopher is uncertain how much he should say.

“Does something magical? And urgent?” guesses Kawa, “Well, I'm finished with dinner. Coming Tovo?”

 Buru Tovo grins, “Wouldn't want to get in the way of you mages waving your hands importantly.”

Cliopher suddenly has an image of a priest-wizard, masked and silver-robed, pushing their way into his apartment, and finding Buru Tovo. Or his staff.

“Frenzel, please take everyone out and lock the apartment. Anywhere public will do, a refectory, a staff hall, out in Solaara,” says Cliopher, his mind racing, “Although somewhere you will have people who know you would be good.”

Both Frenzel's eyebrows go up, “Yes, sir.”

Buru Tovo winks at Frenzel, “Don't suppose you know the bar where those imperial guards hang around off duty?”

Cliopher careful chooses to believe his great-uncle, who is over ninety, would feel more comfortable surrounded by Zundidh's most elite warriors. And not that his respected Buru Tovo is hoping to see if any of them also oil up when off duty. 

He puts the note into his pocket, and offers his arm to Kawa. She laughs at him, but takes it. The guards join them at the door. Sometimes being escorted by guards is one of the most distasteful things he experiences as Lord Chancellor. Still, perhaps now it is not so bad.

They proceed to the Imperial Apartments, Cliopher smiling and nodding to various people he knows. No need to spark any early suspicions. He is simply escorting his guest. To encourage this view, he talks about the various architectural features as they pass them. He suspects his comments are somewhat erratic from the barely contained laughter on Kawa's face. His mind is full of one question.

*How far does the rot go?*

The guards at the Imperial Apartments don't challenge him, which he finds mildly disconcerting, but perhaps this is a special circumstance. They are announced at the official study, however. Cliopher drops into his usual obeisance as he has become accustomed to in the nine hundred or so years he has worked in this room.

When his Radiancy gestures to rise, Cliopher notes with shame that he hadn't had time to teach Kawa any of the protocols of the Presence, and so she had simply bowed deeply over her hands. Well, it is done, and he could only hope his lord will forgive her ignorance and his lapse.

In the room are his Radiancy, Melissa, Conju, Rhodin, and Ludvic. Plus the usual guards. His Radiancy raises the Wall of Silence, everyone looks grim.

“My lord,” says Cliopher, “The shaman and senior lore-keeper for Gorjo City, Kawa Ela.”

His Radiancy nods, Kawa bows again, and they all turn to Rhodin.

“There's five,” he says, “Both high priests, two in the second rank, and one clerk.”

Cliopher sits down at the secretary's desk, which he realises with a small note of sadness, is no longer his desk. Focusing on the task at hand, he pulls his tools and papers from his writing kit. Then he turns to his lord.

“Standard writs of arrest, my lord?” he asks. There is the option of writs under the Imperial Seal, without charges specified, and where those charged only theoretically have a right to defend themselves. Cliopher doesn't like it, and it hasn't been used since the Fall. There are ways to hold a closed court session to protect victims or state security.

“You are suggesting it might be a conflict of interest if we are both the judge and the victim,” says his Radiancy, flatly, “Yet who else could we appoint as judge and trust not to be biased? Anyone would be obliged to find in our favour.”

It is, Cliopher concedes, a very valid point. For all the legal and judicial reform, it is hard to rule against your boss. Well, perhaps they can avoid the charge against the Lord Emperor? Cliopher asks, “My lord, what has the best evidence?”

Rhodin answers, “The conspiracy of assault against her highness, Princess Melissa, my lord.”

They all carefully don't look at Melissa. 

“How good is the evidence?” asks his Radiancy, quietly.

Cliopher wants Rhodin to say it's incontrovertible, that any judge would convict with ease. He knows it's unlikely. If the priests had remained undetected so long, they had to be at least moderately competent. Also, Cliopher suspects that if Rhodin had found, say, the meeting minutes for their plans, he would have called for the arrests sooner.

“Mostly circumstantial, my lord,” says Rhodin, “They would almost certainly be convicted for negligence and incompetence, my lord. But they have been very careful at keeping their involvement secret.”

Cliopher wished he knew what the right answer was. Would the crown appear weak if they charged such senior people with only lesser crimes? It would be clear to anyone reading about it, or watching, that a charge of treason would be possible. Yet if they attempted to try for treason, and the evidence _was_ weak, how could his Radiancy convict? If the priest-wizards were only convicted of criminal negligence, they might only face a fine and the loss of their jobs. That might put his Radiancy and Melissa in _more_ danger than if they pretended they didn't know who was involved.

They all watch the finest legal mind on Zunidh weigh his options. His fingertips carefully balanced on his desk, his eyes full of magic, his face perfectly calm.

It is Melissa who moves first. She steps closer, as close as only she can. Slowly and gently, she rests a hand once again on his wrist. His Radiancy makes a small, sharp, intake of breath, and looks up at her.

“Artorin,” she says, softly, “I do not want vengeance, and I never expected justice.”

“They are a danger to Zunidh,” says his Radiancy, looking at her hand on his, “Their greatest fear is losing the cult, which is exactly what we are planning to do to them.”

Cliopher hadn't quite worked through what it will mean to the Imperial Cult if their faux-god retired to live as a normal man. Will they continue to worship him? Will they try to transfer back to the Sun? There are many adherents to the Cult. Cliopher finds it hard to imagine Kiri deciding to swap her icon of the Sun-on-Earth for the Sun.

To everyone's surprise, Kawa speaks up, “Lord Artorin, you are the high priest, yes? Could you not force them to retire to a monastery, where people you trust could keep an eye on them?”

His Radiancy looks up at her. Kawa blinks, her eyes watering, but she holds his gaze. “The Lord Magus also has the power to sever magical ability, if needed,” she adds.

“No,” says his Radiancy, “I won't do to them what they did to me. I would have rather died. Aurelius Magnus named it torture with good cause. But.” He takes a deep breath, and looks at Melissa, “Perhaps if we isolate them, deny them the tools of schooled magic, they will be less of a danger. And with the high priests gone, you have a real chance of reform.”

Kawa nods her agreement, “And some day, they may have a change of heart.” Cliopher doesn't believe it, but he wants to.

“Writs of arrest for criminal conspiracy, then, my lord?” says Cliopher, “We can always drop to lesser charges later if we feel we do not have the evidence.”

“Yes,” says his Radiancy, and Cliopher pulls out the relevant templates. Once prepared, he gets Rhodin to add the names, and then puts them on his Radiancy's desk. They all look at them. Now they need to actually action the writs.

“I shall leave it up to you, Commander Omo, to decide how many guards we will need. I will come with you,” says his Radiancy.

Rhodin and Ludvic salute. If they are concerned about trying to guard their lord while arresting five mages, they don't show it. If Cliopher is honest, his Radiancy may well need to defend *them*. Rhodin has the strongest magical gift in the guard, and as far as Cliopher knows he can only just light a candle.

His Radiancy turns to Kawa, “I'm afraid I have no idea of your skills, Mage Ela.”

She shrugs, “I mostly just do household charms, Lord Artorin. I've never even thought about combat magic. But I am very sensitive.”

“Would you be willing to stay with Cliopher and Melissa then? Just in case,” says his Radiancy.

She smiles, “Of course.”

“Well,” says Melissa, “We may as well wait somewhere comfortable.” She leads them into the private part of the apartments, to a little sitting room. Cliopher doesn't remember seeing it before, but then there are lots of rooms, he might have walked past it a hundred times. Or it could have had another purpose, or been empty, and Melissa had it made over into this cosy room.

“Lord Conju,” says Melissa, as she sits down, “Please join us.”

Conju, correct as always, bows his thanks. Cliopher thinks he looks slightly uncertain as he chooses a chair, but it is probably just the fall of the light. The Groom of the Chamber is remarkably unflappable. Cliopher concedes, perhaps Conju is also worried about his Radiancy, taking on the Ouranatha. Somewhere else in the Palace.

“Are you one of the third of Gorjo City that Cliopher claims he is related to, Mage Ela?” asks Melissa, with her usual politely interested smile.

“No,” replies Kawa, smiling back openly, “Well, possibly distantly by marriage. There must be one of my fourth cousins married to one of yours, I suppose. There's always another Mdang.”

Cliopher smiles at hearing that line in the Palace. He heard it so often at home when someone saw his name on a list, or introduced him at a party. It's also nice to hear his name pronounced correctly. His Radiancy and his friends _almost_ get it, but nearly nobody else does. Cliopher wonders if it was a relief when he offered for Melissa to use his first name.

Looking from Kawa to Melissa, Cliopher wonders if there isn't a slight tension in her poise, at the mention of 'marriage'. It's well and truly public now, but still it seems that it's hard for Melissa and his Radiancy to know how to act around each other.

“I only moved to Gorjo City a few years ago,” Kawa continues, “My niece went to university, and I tagged along after her, coming from an island with too many Ela to one with too few.”

Melissa looks curious, “Is Ela a title then? Not a name? What would you prefer to be called?”

“Just Kawa is fine,” she says, smiling, “Ela is a family name, and it is mine. But the Ela also adopt anyone with an interest or skill in the occult who wants to join.”

“I'm afraid I know very little of magic, Kawa,” says Melissa, “I have only the barest sense for it. But you said _occult_, not magic, so are you also a priestess?”

At this Kawa grins, “Oh, the gods of the Vangaveye-ve don't really go in for priests and priestesses. But we offer spiritual guidance, and hold the lore of the rituals for the gods.” 

Cliopher smiles internally at what it would be like to be a priest of a trickster god like Vou'a. Would you get to go around playing practical jokes? Perhaps he should have claimed he was a priest of Vou'a as a teenager. Well, it might have backfired if the god had turned up and been upset by the pranks done in his name.

“My brother has tried to pass the responsibility of managing the Ouranatha to me,” says Melissa, looking at her own hands, “But they have not been particularly cooperative. Well, and we didn't trust them, so he was present in any meetings to watch them.”

Kawa squints, and then looks from Melissa, to Cliopher, to Conju. Then off out the door to the room. There are no guards in the room, without his Radiancy.

“Yes, because of the curse,” says Melissa, “Both Cliopher and Lord Conju know about it.”

“It would be a hard task to try to convince them that Lord Artorin is _not_ a god,” says Kawa.

Cliopher, Conju, and Melissa all manage to contain their astonishment at this blatant blasphemy. Finally, Melissa shrugs, a surprisingly casual gesture. “I don't think it's necessarily worthwhile. I _had_ thought to separate the magic from the religion, but...”

“Now you have met me,” says Kawa, “And I'm making you uncertain.”

Cliopher offers the only contribution he feels qualified to give. “The Ouranatha do have something of a monopoly on the education in Schooled Magic. Perhaps you might force them to work with the University and standardise the education.”

“More transparency in general would be an important step forward,” says Melissa, “They wouldn't even say how members of the Council are appointed.”

“There is also very little representation of the regions on the Council,” adds Cliopher, “Perhaps some of the worst excesses might be tempered with some diversity.”

Melissa sighs, “It's going to be a lot of work.”

Conju makes a small cough. “Yes, Lord Conju?” asks Melissa.

“Your highness, when I was first appointed Groom of the Chamber to the Glorious One, I had somewhat of a similar process in the Tower,” says Conju. 

Cliopher blinks. That would have been when he was travelling to, and returning from, the Vangaveye-ve. They haven't talked much about that period, except for occasional scathing remarks about ex-Tower staff from Conju.

He continues, “In particular, it was necessary to do so without disturbing his Radiancy, which meant moving very slowly and carefully. Which might not be dissimilar to the need to not alarm the public.”

“Thank you, Lord Conju,” says Melissa, “I would certainly welcome your assistance.”

Cliopher says, with a wry twist to his mouth, “It probably wouldn't be advisable to do what I did to the Upper Secretariat, following the Examinations Scandal.”

“Yes, well,” says Conju, “Thankfully Rhodin foiled the three or four assassination attempts that followed. But I'm sure he'd appreciate less work this time.”

Sighing, Cliopher adds, “I will have to get Kiri to run some surveys tomorrow, to judge the impact of the arrests. I will be relieved that Commander Omo will get to field the questions from the news-sheets.”

Unspoken is the assumption that everything will go smoothly. That some of the best Schooled Wizards left will accept their arrest. Rather than, say, destroy as much of the Palace as possible and go on the run. Cliopher has no idea if they have the power to do that, so he's trying not to think about it. Somehow his contingency plans seem to have missed this; hopefully the staff can work from the plans for a second Fall.

“Perhaps we could play a game of cards to pass the time,” suggests Melissa.

Conju jumps up immediately, to summon people to organise for a card table, cards, and drinks. Cliopher notices that all of them opt for the iced herbal tea, rather than the spiced liqueur that would be more typical at this time of the evening in the Palace.

They have only completed two hands when spears stamp outside the door. Everyone stands and faces it, Kawa a beat behind the others. The guards open the door to admit his Radiancy and Rhodin.

“Everything is fine,” says his Radiancy, before any of them can even properly drop into the obeisance.

Cliopher bows rather gracelessly, feeling like his bones have turned to water in relief. When he rises, his lord adds, “All five have been taken into custody. One of the second-rank, Saya Puget, has already confessed and offered to testify against the others. None of them resisted once they realised who we were.”

Given how often Cliopher had waited for the Ouranatha to come for him with their ritual knives, he doesn't feel a lot of sympathy for them. At least the priest-wizards will get a fair trial. Or a trial at all.

“Thank you, my lord,” says Cliopher, more relieved than he can articulate that everyone is safe.
