Cliopher rushes to Rhodin first, since it is entirely normal to see the Private Secretary in a hurry. Rhodin is in the guard barracks, conveniently meeting with Commander Omo, and Hiscaron, who trains recruits. Cliopher gives a significant look to Rhodin.

“There is one more matter we should talk about, Commander,” Rhodin says.

Hiscaron salutes, and leaves. Presumably he knows what 'other matters' Rhodin tends to raise, and that he doesn't need to be there.

Rhodin raises the Wall of Silence. Cliopher rather feels it is becoming tiresome, the way sounds are muffled inside such that you can _almost_ hear them, but not really. Rhodin looks at Cliopher expectantly.

“I don't know what you know already, Rhodin,” he says, with a sigh. Rhodin gives his best non-committal smile back. “I will tell you both what *I* know, and then I need to go and talk to the Grand Duchess.”

Commander Omo looks tired, when Cliopher has finished explaining. Rhodin looks haunted.

“Don't take any unnecessary risks,” says Commander Omo to his second-in-command.

“I only ever take necessary risks, sir,” he replies, and then turns to Cliopher, “I've wanted to clean out the Ouranatha for years, but I had no idea they were this vile, or I would have done it without permission.”

Cliopher holds up a hand, “I do _not_ know if whoever laid the curse was a member of the Ouranatha. It could well have been a god, or a fey, or a spiritualist who ate one too many strange mushrooms.”

“Sure,” says Rhodin, “But you _think_ it was.”

“Certainly one or more members there may have been involved in some way,” says Cliopher, and winces at his own lawyer-style answer. “Walk careful, Rhodin. Whoever it was, they've laid this trap well.”

“I will attend his Radiancy immediately,” says Commander Omo, and stands up. Rhodin salutes, and drops the Wall of Silence. Cliopher follows the Commander back into the Palace.

The door minder, who greets Cliopher at the door to the Grand Duchess's apartment, is professionally bland. If there are contentious issues at the Council of Princes, Cliopher is known to try to smooth them over between meetings, so it isn't in any way a surprise for him to call. They ask why he is here, and Cliopher replies smoothly that it is a 'matter of state', which was the usual term for such discussions.

It is bare moments before Cliopher is escorted into the study where he has met her grace before. She sits behind the desk, her secretary in the corner taking notes. He has a brief moment of conflict on which greeting to use, under these extraordinary circumstances, but decides to behave much as usual. “Good morning, your grace,” he says, and bows. 

“Sayo Mdang,” she says, with the gentle polite smile she habitually wears, “A pleasure as always. Although, a matter of state before the first Council meeting hardly seems propitious.”

“Alas that I am so rarely able to come baring good news,” says Cliopher, wondering how to raise the actual reason he is here.

“Is this related to the mater Lord Conju brought to my attention earlier?” she asks.

“Yes, your grace,” says Cliopher, thankful that she is so perceptive.

“That will be all for today then,” says the Grand Duchess to her secretary. The woman collects her things, and bows herself out of the room. Her grace taps a single finger gently on the top of her desk.

“Let us talk in the breakfast room,” she says, “The view is lovely.”

Cliopher simply bows his acceptance. There must be more reason than the _view_ for the change, but he will accommodate whatever she needs. They walk together down the hall, and into a small room that has sweeping views in two directions. It is, Cliopher must admit, almost as nice as the views from his Radiancy's study.

When he closes the door to the room behind them, the familiar muffling of the Wall of Silence appears. But also something else, perhaps a slight blur to the door?

“My family,” says her grace, “Has always been paranoid. With good reason, of course. This room is spelled against spies, against being casually overheard, against anyone being able to see in from outside, and against anyone being able to sense those spells.”

Cliopher feels immensely honoured that she had chosen to share this family secret with him.

“It was not enough, this time,” she says, sadly, “Well, and I wasn't even here.”

“Your grace,” says Cliopher, swallowing a lump in his throat, “You do not owe me any explanation. I only wished to extend to you my sympathies, and the invitation of his Radiancy to spend the afternoon with him.”

Her grace looks back from the view to Cliopher. “But I do, Sayo Mdang,” she says, “You are his Radiancy's confidant, his favourite. I have never desired such a position, and I need you to know that I will never replace you in his heart.”

“I—,” says Cliopher, overcome with emotion, “I am sure Lord Conju—”

The Grand Duchess interrupts him, “Lord Conju is excellent, capable, and astute. But I have never seen a knowing smile pass between them, or notice either of them sit enraptured while the other talks. I know, Lord Conju isn't at Council meetings, his relationship is more private. Still, if his Radiancy ever _chose_ to marry anyone, I am sure he would pick you first.”

Cliopher is practically shaking in his sandals. He has no idea what to say to that. First Conju, now the Grand Duchess. But, it isn't possible. His Radiancy will not get to choose; he will marry his sister. Today.

“I am flattered that you think so highly of me, your grace,” says Cliopher, desperately trying to regain his composure.

She smiles, not the polite smile she had before, but a sad, small smile. “I shall give you an outline,” she says, “If there is any more that you would like, or need, to know, please ask.”

Cliopher nods, accepting that she wants to tell him, even if he doesn't think he needs to know.

“I know very little for certain,” she says, “So I will start with what I do. A month ago, I was attacked, magically and physically. A fanatic of the Imperial Cult, from his ranting, although most of it made little sense. He had an extraordinary amount of magical power. He managed to overcome the permanent wards on my house, and the wizard and shaman I keep close. Kept close. Even the protections his Radiancy had placed on my person.” 

She closes her eyes, and takes a deep breath. Cliopher is reminded painfully of the way his Radiancy had spun the teacup in his hands.

“He violated me, magically and physically. He made incantations in languages I do not know, and drew blood from himself, and from me. My ladies were unconscious, strewn across the floor. My guards were trying to break through the doors, shutters, and walls, but there was some kind of magical seal on the room. My wizard lay on the floor, and I could see the blood running from her nose and eyes.”

Cliopher wants to hold the Grand Duchess, to offer comfort, to promise justice. He knows all of that would be hollow, so he just listens.

“When he was done, there was a flash of darkness, and a terrible silence, and it felt like the world had disappeared. I am sure you remember the feeling,” she says, sadly.

Oh, Cliopher remembers. Nobody who had lived through could ever forget. If he ever felt the same again, he would immediately panic.

“The world returned, the guards opened the door as normal. The ladies woke, and for a moment, I shook and hid in the bed. But I had been betrothed to Prince Shallyr, and I know how to walk out of a room dressed in nothing but my own dignity. I put on the armour I had forgotten I needed, and I looked at the shrivelled corpse of the man who had assaulted me, sprawled at the end of my bed.”

“I got out of bed, and I dressed, and I told everyone that I was fine. That the magic of the Emperor had saved me. Three weeks later, I did a test I learnt as a young woman, involving a toad, and found I was, as I feared, pregnant.” 

She takes a slow breath, turning back to look outside, and runs a finger around the carved edge of the window frame.

“Last night, I used a secret my mother had told me, to gain entrance to the Imperial Apartments. I'm afraid I rather shocked Ser Rhodin. I confessed the whole to his Radiancy. He examined me magically, and found the curse. It is multi-layered, and very complicated, but he was able to give me the general gist.” She smiles, very slightly, “His gift for magic is very great, and mine is very small.”

“I asked him if he could tell if the father was the fanatic, or the god. He told me it was neither, that, impossibly, he was.”

Cliopher bites his lip. That explains his lord's fear, what he couldn't say, what he was struggling to even think. Surely his Radiancy blames himself for the entire thing, even though he had no idea and no way to stop it. An Emperor is not used to being powerless.

“And now,” she says wryly, “We enter into the world of speculation. Someone, possibly the same fanatic, violated his Radiancy while he was sleeping after the Fall. They preserved the sample they acquired. They found, or planned, the death of a god, and bound it into a curse. And the fanatic performed a ritual, surely developed over many centuries, that exchanged his certain death for certain life.”

“If there is anyone to find, anyone to serve justice to—” says Cliopher.

The Grand Duchess cuts him off. “It is too late,” she says bitterly, out the window, “All we can do is to avert what further calamity we can.”

Cliopher is not _quite_ so sure that there is no justice left. But he does not want to argue with her, when she is so clearly in pain.

“I will do anything and everything I can to help lighten this burden on you both,” says Cliopher.

She turns, and offers him another, small, pained smile. Abandoning everything he had ever been taught about etiquette, ignoring the four failed exam marks that were burnt into his memory, Cliopher steps up to stand next to the Grand Duchess. He reaches his right arm up, and takes a very gentle hold on her shoulder. The lightest possible hug, ready at any moment to drop it and apologise when she flinches or moves away.

Instead, impossibly, she leans into him. “I was raised and trained to be Consort to Shallyr,” she whispers, “What can you tell me of his Radiancy?”

He tells her of his unsuccessful attempts to analyse the categories of comment that triggered his Radiancy's smiles. By the end, she is shaking gently with silent laughter. Cliopher thinks that was probably good.

When the noon bells ring, he makes his bows and leaves, promising to see her later. It is a shame, he thinks, that the Grand Duchess and his Radiancy were being forced together like this. Under different circumstances, they could be friends.

Back in the Offices of the Lords of State, Cliopher becomes a whirlwind of efficiency. He has nothing new from the morning, but that doesn't mean there is nothing to do. Delegating furiously is the order of the day. So much so that Kiri takes him aside.

“Is there something wrong, sir? Only normally I know you would want to oversee this audit yourself,” she says, anxiously.

“His Radiancy needs me to work on something else for a couple of days,” he says, trying to sound chipper. “I'll tell you more when I can.”

She nods. “Very well, sir. We can prepare the morning reports, and I know Aioru has been itching to take a try at the Department of Works budget. We can give you a few days.”

“Thank you, Kiri,” he says, meaning it most sincerely.

“Get back upstairs, sir. Or the archives, or wherever you need to be.”

Cliopher leaves the offices, and goes to fetch his cousin's monograph from the library. It will give him an excuse to visit his Radiancy again. If his lord wants to see him.
