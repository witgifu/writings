Cliopher enters his Radiancy's study as usual, with a few issues he wanted to raise, and many more he had expected based on the morning reports. Instead, as soon as his Radiancy indicates for Cliopher to rise, before he has even made it to his desk, his lord raises the Wall of Silence.

The guards have heard them discuss many sensitive topics over the centuries. The only time Cliopher can remember his Radiancy raising the Wall in advance, is when he told Cliopher they were leaving for Woodlark. Cliopher sits down, his face serious. What could possibly have happened on that level that he hadn't heard about? It must have been something magical in nature. Perhaps one of the fragile re-established Gates between worlds have collapsed?

“My sister came to see me, last night,” says his Radiancy.

This already contained several astonishing things, and his lord has only just begun. No Imperial plural, calling the Grand Duchess 'sister', and the fact that if Cliopher hadn't heard about the visit. It must have been both _very_ late and _very_ secret. Ser Rhodin organised it, perhaps, or his Radiancy provided some magical concealment. Any person seen entering or exiting the Imperial Apartments, outside the daily schedule, was remarked upon. Even if Conju had done so at an unusual time, it might be the basis of gossip.

“After due consideration by both of us, we have decided to wed,” says his Radiancy.

It takes a second for this information to register with Cliopher. That is certainly reason enough to raise the Wall of Silence, it isn't the kind of information his Radiancy would want anyone to know before they absolutely must. 

“Very well, my lord,” says Cliopher, pulling a piece of paper towards himself. He prepares a pen, but his Radiancy signals 'stop'. Cliopher puts the pen down, and places his hands in his lap.

“You do not want anything in writing about this, my lord?” asks Cliopher.

“Correct,” says his Radiancy, beginning to pace, but saying nothing more.

“Would you or the Grand Duchess have a timeline in mind?” Cliopher asks, concerned. This seems sudden, and after so many years of both of them refusing, and now not wanting anyone to know, he wonders if there is outside interference in their decision.

“As soon as possible,” says his Radiancy.

Well, that pretty much cinches it for Cliopher. A hasty marriage is never one made for good reasons. Yet, he is the Lord Magus of Zunidh, and she is the Grand Duchess, a member of the Council of Princes in her own right. Who could possibly be pressuring them to wed, that they feel unable to refuse? His Radiancy famously refused when he was weakest, prior to his coronation. As well as every time since then. A law could be repealed, a curse broken, a contract voided. It must be something above those. The fey? A god?

“My lord,” says Cliopher, aware he is once again taking his life in his hands, “Are you sure? This seems a very sudden change of mind on something over which you had been quite firm.”

His Radiancy's eyes flash, and he stops pacing. 

Cliopher swallows hard. “I ask only out of concern for your wellbeing, my lord,” he adds, “If this match would make you happy, then I will offer you both my prayers and say nothing more.”

Still, his lord says nothing. 

This is highly disturbing, he must be angry. It is true, Cliopher has never dared make such personal enquiries before. He had thought perhaps his lord would welcome his advice, as had so often been the case before. Clearly Cliopher was wrong. Sliding out of his chair, onto the floor, Cliopher prostrates himself at his Radiancy's feet. “My apologies for questioning you, my lord,” he says quietly, “I humbly beg your forgiveness, my lord, for my unconscionable forwardness.”

Still, his lord says nothing, gestures nothing.

Cliopher trembles. He hopes he will have time to at least write to his mother and explain what has happened. Perhaps, he can convince Commander Omo to grant him that mercy. Or Ser Rhodin can sneak him some paper and a pencil. Cliopher remembers a horrifying story from Astandalas, of a woman who wrote to her son on the walls of her cell, using her own blood. In doing so, she bled out, and saved herself the death of a commoner for treason.

“She is pregnant,” whispers his Radiancy, and then repeats the gesture for Cliopher to rise several times.

Relieved he doesn't seem to be in trouble, Cliopher chooses to stand. Trying to calm his racing heart, he considers this latest statement. The Grand Duchess is a similar age to Cliopher. He supposes that with the erratic time dilations of the Fall, perhaps she might be a little younger. Still, it seems _highly_ improbable that she could be pregnant. Even if she was, surely a termination would be possible?

Cliopher is not sure if he dare ask, given his lord's disturbed mood. Yet, someone must. “My lord, I am sorry if this is an indelicate question, but if she were not pregnant, would you still be marrying?”

His Radiancy shakes his head, not making eye contact.

“And neither termination nor a quiet retreat and adoption of the child, are possible, my lord?” asks Cliopher, gently.

His Radiancy shakes his head again.

“My lord, do you know who the father is?” asks Cliopher.

His Radiancy nods, but seems unable to answer further. This is bad. Usually his Radiancy is serene, eloquent, in control. He can clearly barely keep it together.

“What can I do to help, my lord?” offers Cliopher.

“Please tell Conju,” his Radiancy whispers, “And organise… things. As quietly as possible.”

“Of course, my lord,” says Cliopher, and bows. “I should like to consult with the Grand Duchess, would that be possible?”

His Radiancy nods once, and then turns and strides through the door of his private study, closing it behind himself.

The Wall of Silence is still up. Cliopher can't dismiss it, being magic-null, but it's perfectly possible to walk through it. So he turns and walks towards the door out of the study, until he can hear the world outside again.

The first thing he hears is his lord weeping in the private study. Cliopher's heart breaks, wishing he could offer more. His lord has nobody to turn to, nobody to appeal to, nobody even to hold him. Well, after the Grand Duchess is purified, presumably she could hold his Radiancy. Which reminds him, Conju will know how long that will take, which will be the minimum amount of time until the wedding.

His lord's sobs have gentled, but not stopped. Does he know they can hear him out here? The guards move over to take the position by the door. They look as unaffected as always, so Cliopher doesn't know. Well, he's been given permission to talk to Conju, that must be his first priority. His lord would surely not appreciate Cliopher bursting into his private study to offer condolences.

The inner rooms of the Imperial Apartments are vast and confusing, like the rest of the Palace of Stars was when he first arrived. He has been in the first few rooms, when his lord invited him to a game of chess of an evening. After that, he is forced to ask directions from the curious staff, to find Conju.

The Groom of the Chamber is in his Radiancy's dressing room, carefully preparing a court costume. As always, it looks magnificent, but Cliopher can see now how much more beautiful it will be with his lord inside rather than a mannequin. Conju looks up from a tray of tiny sequins, which are presumably real gold.

“To what do I owe this pleasure, Cliopher?” asks Conju, “Is his Radiancy wishing to plan a tour or—?”

“Does this room have a Wall of Silence?” asks Cliopher, trying not to twist his hands in his uniform, which he knows Conju disapproves of.

Conju raises both eyebrows in unison with his hand, and the gentle background sounds of the tower disappear.

“His Radiancy wishes us to organise his wedding,” says Cliopher.

Taking a deep breath, Conju starts to say, “Congra—”

“To his sister,” interrupts Cliopher. He will deal with Conju's assumption that his Radiancy had proposed to _his secretary_ for another time. Perhaps Conju has been reading too many trashy romance novels. They're fun, but really.

Conju blinks, several times. “The Grand Duchess? Are you quite sure, Cliopher?”

“Yes,” says Cliopher, and then rushes to add, “She's pregnant, and his Radiancy knows who the father is, but he won't say who, and for some reason she can't terminate or adopt the baby out, they have to get married, and Conju, he's sobbing in the private study, and I don't know what to do.”

Taking a deep breath, Conju swears, quite loudly.

“We're allowed to talk to the Grand Duchess,” remembers Cliopher, “But apart from that he wants it to be 'as quiet as possible'.”

Conju snorts. “You can't have a _quiet_ wedding when the Last Emperor of Astandalas is the groom.” Then he realises what he's just said, contradicting the Sun-on-Earth and suggesting they disobey a direct order, “We will find a way, Cliopher.”

Conju thinks for a moment, tapping needlepoint tweezers against the tray, making the sequins dance and sparkle. “I will talk to the Grand Duchess. Immediately, if she will see me. We will need to organise the purifications. She has always had a good relationship with the Ouranatha.”

Cliopher hears the 'better' rather than 'good' that Conju feels unable to say, even behind the Wall.

“You,” says Conju, “Try to talk to his Radiancy through the door. Tell him… Talk to him about your family, he likes that.”

“He does?” says Cliopher, surprised. He had never been quite sure. An attempt to do an analysis of which types of anecdotes received the most enthusiastic response had not found anything statistically significant in any of the categories Cliopher had used.

Conju gaves him a withering look, “Yes, of course he does. He enjoys any conversation with you, but if you can get him to think about anything other than this problem for a little while, perhaps he will be able to… be ready to… will grace us with his presence again.”

Cliopher nods. “Thank you,” he says, “You always know what to do, Conju.”

Conju looks up to the ceiling, and takes a deep breath. “If only, Cliopher, if only.”

Returning to the study, Cliopher feels awkward. The guards are still on the door to the private study, so his Radiancy hasn't moved. He can no longer hear anything from inside, but when Cliopher crosses the room, he can hear that the Wall of Silence in the official study is still in place.

Heart in his throat, Cliopher listens at the door, between the two guards. He carefully doesn't touch the door, uncertain if that is allowed. There is a very faint rustle coming from within, possibly pages of a book turning, or his Radiancy's robes swishing against furniture. Cliopher takes a deep breath, and follows Conju's advice.

“My lord, have I told you of the time Basil and I broke into a hotel? I think we were thirteen at the time, maybe fourteen. We were playing Aurelius Magnus and Elonoa'a, and decided we needed the shell on the wall of the hotel to make it authentic…”

Cliopher isn't sure how long he stands there, telling anecdotes. The guards are surely tired of all the various ways he had gotten in to trouble as a youngster, or the antics of his many cousins. He's even begun talking about his family's academic research. Finally, he finds himself trying to explain Zemius' research on the Lords Magi of Zunidh, which surely is something that the _Current Lord Magus_ already knows about. Somehow his mouth rattles on ignoring his internal anxiety.

This last topic is the trigger to his Radiancy opening the door. “I would be interested in reading that monograph,” he says.

Bowing, Cliopher moves away from the door. “Of course, my lord,” he says, and makes a note to have it fetched from the library.

Just then, Conju arrives with morning tea. Had Cliopher spoken for so long? He must have, his throat did feel sore enough. Taking the glass of water Conju handed to him with a smile of thanks, he turns back to look at their lord.

His Radiancy is standing in the doorway of the private study, looking between Conju and Cliopher. There is a fine tea set on the desk, but the usual plate has been replaced by a three-tiered stand. With, yes, ginger biscuits, but also tiny marmalade tarts, cubes of honey cake, and sticks of candied ginger dipped in bitter chocolate. His Radiancy steps out, crosses the floor, and takes the cup of tea.

“Thank you, Conju,” is what Cliopher is sure his lord says, but the Wall of Silence is still up. Discretely, he steps over to be inside of it.

Conju bows, perfect as always, and says, “My lord?”

Slowly, his Radiancy looks up from the cup of tea. “Yes, Conju?” he says, and now Cliopher can hear how his voice sounds sore.

“I have met with the Grand Duchess, my lord,” says Conju, looking at his lord's hands. As his Radiancy is. Only Cliopher looks at their faces, perfectly composed.

“Under the circumstances,” Conju continues, “The ritual and legal requirements could be completed today. Then the public celebration could be held in two months time, on Emperor Day.”

That would be helpful, thinks Cliopher, having it on Emperor Day. They could re-use a lot of their existing plans for celebrations, and potentially add an extra public holiday for the day after as well. Then his mind collects the first part of Conju's statement. 

_Today_. That is… well, it isn't legally possible for anyone _else_ on Zunidh. You have to file the intent to marry paperwork at least three weeks in advance. His Radiancy is keen to let everyone practice whatever local or traditional marriage practices they liked, but _consent_ is something that has been enshrined even in the old Astandalan Law Code. Having a cool off period is one of the easiest ways to ensure _some_ level of consent. It can perhaps be argued that while his Radiancy had broken off his betrothal to the Grand Duchess prior to his coronation, the intent had been there. The simpler method was just to write a proclamation saying they were married.

Cliopher almost wishes it weren't so easy. That they had time to find another solution to this horrible predicament that his Radiancy and the Grand Duchess somehow found themselves in. Yes, they worked together on the Council of Princes for centuries, but they hardly know each other. Every occasion had been formal, and public, and they had both carefully maintained the image of monarch and vassal. Never said 'brother', or 'sister', or even offered a smile. His Radiancy had invited the Grand Duchess to Silverheart every year, and she had always politely declined.

His analytical mind racing ahead, he blurts out, “But Conju, how could the Grand Duchess be purified in half a day?” This breaks several rules; most prominently addressing anyone in the room other than his Radiancy. Cliopher bites his lip, but doesn't try to apologise.

“She was purified before I woke,” says his Radiancy, sounding far away, “As soon as Solaara reconnected with Old Damara. The Ouranatha advised her that if I woke and was… incapacitated… it would be helpful to have someone who could touch me. Someone above reproach.”

That was a very cunning move. Cliopher doesn't like it, or what it implies about the Ouranatha's involvement in the rest of this problem.

Conju nods. The Grand Duchess has clearly explained the same thing to him.

Cliopher has so many questions. He starts with the most pressing. “If you didn't go through with it, my lord, the consequences would be dire?”

His Radiancy takes another sip of tea, turning the cup quickly in his hands. “Yes,” he says, “At a minimum, she and I and… the child… would die.” His hands start shaking slightly, and he puts the cup down slowly, and then presses his hands firmly into the desk. “There would probably be a second Fall, although that is much harder to read. The curse was bound with the death of a god.”

Cliopher blanches, looking between his lord and Conju. Even Conju looks a little wan, though it is hard to tell with his cosmetics. Well, they had all lived through the Fall, they had, in their own way, done whatever they could to rebuild the world. To face the same again… yes, Cliopher could see why his Radiancy would choose marriage to his sister instead.

“My lord,” he says softly, “If I could do anything to lift this burden from you, I would. We will do all we can to ease it for you.”

Conju's mind is clearly running along a similar path. “My lord,” he said, “Perhaps you could invite the Grand Duchess to spend the afternoon with you? A public summons to tea will be less remarked upon.”

His Radiancy nods.

“Does anyone else know, my lord?” asks Cliopher, carefully. 

“Ser Rhodin knows the Grand Duchess came to see me,” says his Radiancy, “There may be some in the Ouranatha who know parts, such as that the Grand Duchess is purified. And that…” he paused, frozen. Cliopher was suddenly worried that whatever had struck him before, perhaps part of the curse, had happened again.

“My lord, you need not explain any more to us,” says Cliopher, “Ser Rhodin is more than capable of making discreet enquiries and fishing out what anyone in the Ouranatha might know. And if he were to let Commander Omo know, then you would have two guards for whatever happens today.”

“Thank you,” says his Radiancy, barely a whisper.

“Shall I let Ser Rhodin and the Grand Duchess know?” says Cliopher, “So Conju can stay with you. We will not abandon you, my lord, no matter what may come.”

“Thank you,” says his Radiancy again.

Cliopher backs out of the room, churning with emotion. If nothing else, perhaps Conju can tempt his Radiancy to a more comfortable chair. The only thing Cliopher can take comfort in is the knowledge that the marriage could be annulled at any time on the grounds of coercion. If they ever found another way to handle the curse. The child could not be so easily dismissed.
