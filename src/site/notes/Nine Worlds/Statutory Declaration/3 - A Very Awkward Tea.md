Cautiously, Cliopher approaches the Imperial Apartments again. “I brought his Radiancy a book he requested,” he says, awkwardly, “If he is busy, I can leave it with Lord Conju.”

He's admitted to the fifth antechamber automatically, which he assumes has something to do with his position as the Hands of the Emperor. Not once before has he attempted to see his lord when it was outside their schedule, or a specific summons. It feels strange, not like visiting Conju in his rooms or meeting Rhodin at the barracks.

The wait isn't long. One of Conju's assistants comes out, and waves him through. “His Radiancy and the Grand Duchess are taking tea outside,” she says, primly, “The Glorious One asked that I bring you through to join them.”

Cliopher stumbles slightly. Invited to join his Radiancy and the Grand Duchess for _tea_? But surely they would want to use the time to have a private discussion? To negotiate what this change in their relationship will mean for them both? Perhaps something about the child?

Through the rest of the antechambers, through the official study, and out into the sunlight. Conju is serving, and he gives a look from behind them that Cliopher can only interpret as a plea for help. Cliopher folds into the obeisance, wondering what service he can possibly perform. He is a secretary, a government functionary, not a relationship counsellor. Well, he does have _some_ training in helping with relationships, from a long time ago. As always, he can only try his best.

When he stands, Cliopher offers the Grand Duchess the bow she would be due as Consort. He notices a slight tension in his Radiancy as he rises, and a look of approval from Conju. It's just like Cliopher to walk out there and immediately make a hash of things.

“My apologies, my lord,” says Cliopher, “I did not inquire earlier as to what rank her grace would have, after you wed.”

And now he has spoken first. With an internal sigh he places the book he had brought on a side table, and waits to be addressed. Everyone looks tense, although he isn't nearly as experienced at reading her grace's moods.

“Come, join us, Cliopher,” says his Radiancy, indicating the place beside the Grand Duchess. “We had only thought it would be unconscionable for anyone to refer to you as a concubine, your grace. We would gladly grant you any title.”

“You are most generous, my lord,” she says.

When she fails to express a further preference, Cliopher dares to ask, “Is there any requirement you can discern in the curse, my lord? Is it only that the child is born legitimate by the old Imperial laws?”

His Radiancy suddenly becomes stiff, and Cliopher realises that his lord doesn't know what, if anything, the Grand Duchess has told him. So he amends his statement, “That is, my lord, it does not require the child to be born specifically 'of the Emperor's Consort'?”

With a weak smile, his Radiancy says, “If only curses came with clear contractual terms. We can tell that it is bound to us as Emperor and Lord Magus, the child, to you, your grace, and to Zunidh. If we were the only one in danger, it might be possible to take risks, but not for all our world.”

“Of course, my lord,” says Cliopher, mentally cancelling possibilities, such as hoping their existing sibling relationship might be sufficient. He pauses, and wonders how much he should ask. So far it seems only that his Radiancy has struggled to answer, not that he has been offended to be asked. Her grace had been very open.

“Your grace, would you have anyone you would _like_ to hand rule of Old Damara to, now?” asks Cliopher, once again breaking the rules and addressing her directly. Yet, if she already has someone lined up, perhaps she would like to enact those changes first.

“No, my lord,” she answers, looking at his Radiancy, as if he had asked, “I had hoped I might be able to adopt an adult to pass it to, but I have found no suitable candidates. And now there is no time…”

His Radiancy inclines his head, “A perfectly sensible solution, your grace,” (Cliopher can see a number of flaws in the plan, but it is perhaps better than the primogeniture most of the Council of Princes prefers) “We should wish to see you continue the many good works you have done in Old Damara.”

Well, maybe that is something they can address very simply. “My lord, your grace, I think the world would understand if you only lived together while her grace is at court for the Council. Many couples cannot live together all the time due to work commitments.”

His Radiancy nods, “Of course. You are quite right, Cliopher. Would that help, your grace?”

“My lord is too kind,” she says softly. Cliopher thinks perhaps she flicks a slight smile in his direction. He hopes that somewhere in Old Damara there is a friend she can visit.

Yet there is still the matter of titles and perception. “The questions the newspapers will all be asking, my lord, is 'why now?'. We will need an answer to that,” says Cliopher. “I do not think it likely anyone will assume there is a child. And anyone born after the fall of the Empire will be… confused.”

_Confused_ is probably putting it lightly. Disgusted, offended, irate, are all much more likely. They will need extra people in the Offices to handle the mail, and more people who can detect curses and other magic. This move will only be popular with a very narrow, and increasingly old, segment of the population.

Neither the Grand Duchess nor his Radiancy appear to have any ideas. Both have returned to looking at their teacups.

“Would it be possible, my lord, to present this as anything other than a marriage? Tlaxos has a register of relationships that includes 'carer' or 'significant relationship'. Jilkano allows people to register a long-standing business partnership as next-of-kin.” Cliopher takes a deep breath, and admits what he thinks is most likely to be a passable solution, “My home, the Vangaveye-ve, recognises _fanoa_. It is a broad category, including those who wish to co-parent but not marry, the greatest of friends, close trading relationships.”

Finally, his Radiancy looks up from his teacup. “It would be safest if it was a marriage. But, perhaps we could, as you suggest, frame it as a way of sharing in a business partnership of sorts. Sharing power.”

Cliopher nods, he can see the logic. And if they can present it as the only current possible solution, that should give support to more reforms. There is still the matter of *why now*.

“My lord,” he says cautiously, “Would this only be ceremonial, or would you delegate some powers in truth?”

“Both,” says his Radiancy, “If you would not mind, your grace? At least while court is in session. And if you are not indisposed.”

“I…,” the Grand Duchess does not look up, “I have never desired your throne, my lord, nor any part of it. I am honoured that you would consider it. But, I would not want to give lest than my best to either your government or Damara.”

Cliopher spots a tiny smile in his Radiancy's eyes. “I have always found delegating to Cliopher to work most effectively.”

Now it is Cliopher's turn to look at his teacup. “Thank you, my lord,” he says, quietly.

Trying to turn the conversation away from himself, Cliopher says, “The titles with precedence for your grace would be 'Consort', 'Princess Consort', or 'Empress Consort'.” He is well aware that his Radiancy prefers not to be addressed as 'Emperor', but feels obliged to include it for completeness. “I know your grace prefers not to be known as 'princess', but given that 'Consort' was not usually delegated authority, and we are endeavouring to dilute the memory of the Empire, it may be the most fitting.”

“It is entirely your choice, your grace,” says his Radiancy, quickly, before she has a chance to demur.

Melissa offers him a small smile, and a moment of understanding passes between them. _Choice_, is not something either of them really have here. “It is a wise suggestion, Sayo Mdang,” she says, “I will accept being known as Princess Consort in Solaara.” Unspoken is that she will expect to be the Grand Duchess at home in Damara. Cliopher considers that is entirely fair.

With an inkling of an idea Cliopher asks, “Do the old Astandalan aristocracy still not acknowledge the existence of a child, before their ninth birthday? Do you think it would be possible to do the same with this child?” He worries that it skirts close to attempting to foil the curse.

“The head of the family is told, as Eritanyr was told about our birth,” his Radiancy replies, slowly, “It would be breaking tradition to publicly acknowledge—” He stops, and takes a deep, shuddering breath.

“A potential heir to your throne, my lord?” finished Cliopher, when it was clear his Radiancy was unable to do so.

His Radiancy nods, once, sharply.

“May I ask, my lord, if the child is of sound mind and body, would you want them to inherit your throne? Your countenance would suggest not, but I would like to be certain,” asks Cliopher, emboldened by his success so far.

“_Never_,” whispers his Radiancy, “I had hoped that the throne might remain empty, when I left it.”

Cliopher can tell this surprises Conju; he's uncertain if it surprises the Grand Duchess. “Very good, my lord,” he says, which earns him a withering look from Conju, “Nine years should be sufficient to dismantle the last of your powers as Emperor, and hopefully find a new Lord Magus via an alternative method. Would that invalidate the curse?”

Conju's eyes have gone wide. The Grand Duchess puts her teacup down, and carefully places her hands under the table and out of sight. His Radiancy pauses for a minute, and with each breath he takes Cliopher becomes more certain that he has finally, actually, gone too far. His statement was, very literally, *high* treason.

 Cliopher opens his mouth to beg forgiveness, and begins to push himself out of his chair to fall on the floor. But his Radiancy holds up a hand in the gesture to remain in place. Cliopher has a well-practiced court face, but he is sure everyone can see that he is shaking. He drops himself back into the chair, and bows his head awaiting the order for Commander Omo to remove him.

“You think that is possible, and that people will accept it? Cliopher?” asks his Radiancy, softly.

“Y-yes, my lord,” says Cliopher, not daring to say more, or look up.

“You would help implement it? For our people?” his Radiancy continues.

“Of course, my lord, it would be the greatest honour of my life,” answers Cliopher, and lifts his head and eyes slightly so he can glimpse his lord's face. It is serene, but his eyes are sparkling.

“Yes, it would invalidate the curse,” he says, “We never thought that it might be possible to… retire.”

“I will endeavour to do it as fast as I can,” says Cliopher, imagining that old plan still in folders in his office, “And then you would be able to have the marriage annulled. I only wish it could be done sooner, so that it might be safe to avoid the marriage altogether.”

They both look slightly surprised now.  “Perhaps, my lord,” says Melissa, volunteering something for the very first time, “It would be made easier if Sayo Mdang was your Lord Chancellor, rather than your secretary.”

Cliopher freezes. He has never wanted to be ennobled, never sought overt power. And he enjoys being his Radiancy's secretary, being overlooked and slightly anonymous.

“Cliopher?” says his Radiancy gently, “You would need to be a more central part of court. But it would make it much easier to enact your changes.”

“My lord, you know I have never wanted a title,” says Cliopher, looking up at his Radiancy. There is that tiny light of amusement again in his lord's eyes. “However, if it would ease the burden for either of you, I would accept gladly.”

“Then we should do that as soon as possible,” says his Radiancy, “It may also bolster the argument for reformation of the structure of power, if a Lord Chancellor is not sufficient. Conju, would you be able to help organise Cliopher's investiture?”

“Of course, my lord,” said Conju, who proceeds to refresh the teacups.

Confident he has addressed the major issues of the _marriage_, Cliopher feels he must now return to the child. “My lord, your grace, had you any thought to raising the child?”

His Radiancy becomes almost unnaturally still, and it is clear the Grand Duchess is waiting for him to speak first. “A house in the countryside of Damara would be safest,” he says eventually, in a voice that sounds like it comes from the Throne of Judgement rather than the man Cliopher works with every day. “We will establish a household for them, nurses, tutors, so on.”

“Was… was that how you were raised, my lord?” asks Cliopher gently. He had known his Radiancy only met the Grand Duchess when they were both adults, but it had never occurred to him before what that might have meant for their childhoods.

“Yes,” says his Radiancy, looking out into the distance.

“Would that be what you would want for your child, either of you, if you had a choice?” asks Cliopher. Perhaps it was a happy childhood, but Cliopher can't imagine his Radiancy would be so withdrawn now if that were true.

“No,” says his Radiancy, very quietly.

“I was raised by my, by our, mother,” adds Melissa, quietly. “After Eritanyr forced her to give up you, my lord, she would not do so for another child.”

She takes a long, deep breath, “Despite the circumstances, I would like to raise the child myself, as my mother raised me. I know it is unlikely…” she trails off, and looks shocked at her own presumption. “Of course, my lord, I will do whatever you would ask of me.”

Cliopher decides he must interrupt, or they will end up agreeing to something neither of them want out of a surfeit of politeness. “My lord, do you think it possible the Lord of Ysthar might be willing to assist?”

His Radiancy blinks, drawn forcibly back into the present by Cliopher's question. “It would depend on the request,” he says carefully, “We intended to send a delegation soon to establish more regular relations now the Game is over.”

Carefully, Cliopher lays out his tentative plan. “A delegation to Ysthar would make a good reason to travel there. Your grace as a representative of you, my lord, and a carefully selected handful of others. If the Lord of Ysthar were able to host, for a month of Zunidh time, it would probably be at least six months on Ysthar. They are supposed to have very good medical facilities, for the birth. When returning, raising the child could be framed as part of the re-established relationship. We could even, your grace, arrange for you to adopt the child.”

His Radiancy, the Grand Duchess, and even Conju, all look surprised and thoughtful.

“It's a good idea,” says his Radiancy, eventually, “The Lord of Ysthar is very discreet.”

“Please, my lord,” says Melissa, softly.

His Radiancy looks at her, and for the first time that Cliopher knows of, she looks at him. Purified, she has nothing to fear from the taboos. Yet she has always been painfully correct with him.

“Anything,” says his Radiancy, “I know nothing can make up for having to renege on my first promise to you—”

“My lord, you gave me a choice, one I thought I would never have. Decades of a freedom I could never have imagined. I know what that cost you, my lord. Thank you,” says Melissa.

Suddenly it occurs to Cliopher what everyone, even Conju, but especially the Grand Duchess, is expecting to happen tonight. His mind is white with outrage. If he says nothing, they will all just do it, even though none of them want to. It's insanity.

“You don't have to consummate the marriage!” says Cliopher, rather louder than he intended. “You don't even have to sleep in the same *room*. The only requirement is that you both agree to the marriage, and a statement signed and witnessed to that effect. Anything else is for appearances only.”

The Grand Duchess is carefully not looking at his Radiancy. Who is frozen in place.

“It would probably be wise to keep any lovers discreet, my lord, your grace,” adds Cliopher.

The serenity cracks slightly, and his Radiancy is shaking, one hand over his mouth, with his eyes closed. No, not shaking, he is laughing. Cliopher is almost sure. Slowly the serenity reasserts itself.

“I have no objection whatsoever if you would like to have an entire stable of lovers, your grace,” says his Radiancy, with only a small hitch in his breath, “I will not.”

The Grand Duchess looks bewildered. It is the first time Cliopher has known her court mask to even slip. Conju looks like he is about to try to disappear into the floor. He has taken a couple of steps back and closed his eyes.

“My lord is most,” begins the Grand Duchess, but suddenly even she sees the amusement in completing that with 'generous'. She pauses, a slight shadow of amusement on her face, “My lord, it has been many years since there was anyone.”

A look of understanding passes between his Radiancy and the Grand Duchess, and Cliopher has a moment of hope that they might actually defrost a little. Potentially get to know each other.

Cliopher gives them a moment, hoping that one of them will say something else. But no, that seems to be the extent of their familiarity. He wishes he could force them to hug like his mother used to when he had a fight with one of his sisters. “I would also suggest that you might wish to practice using first names,” he says, eventually, “Perhaps go to the theatre together, or a walk in the gardens. Tell each other your favourite cheese, and the worst book you ever read.”

His Radiancy takes a deep breath. “Melissa. I think the worst book was some poetry by En Lovand. I can only assume he paid for it to be published. It wasn't even so bad it was funny, it was just bad.”

A small smile appears on Melissa's face. “I'd assumed he'd bewitched the publisher,” she says, “How else would there be so many copies that you and I have both read it… Artorin?”

“Perhaps some experimental magic went awry,” says his Radiancy, now actually smiling, “And they ended up with ten thousand copies.”

Cliopher smiles, and pulls a pen and paper from his writing kit. He can draft the documents and listen at the same time.
