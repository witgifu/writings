The Lord of Ysthar is very accommodating, although the difference in time progression means they are only able to send a few letters. His Radiancy seals them so they can only be opened by another Lord Magus, then they can be comfortable sending them with the usual couriers. The Lord of Ysthar is polite enough to do the same for his replies.

Cliopher is keen to experience London's public health system first-hand, as well as many other things during his visit. There's a sewage treatment plant he's read about, plus of course it has many famous museums and libraries. Plans are made for transport and accommodation, and tentative dates set.

It is clear from the Lord of Ysthar's letters that his government is entirely magical, and the civil government is completely separate and has nearly no knowledge of his existence. The logical choice then would be to take members of the Ouranatha. His Radiancy refuses to even consider the idea, and Melissa concurs.

They sit, once again taking tea, trying to make suggestions. 

“Are there any academics from the university?” asks Cliopher, “Maybe August might know someone, my lord?”

His Radiancy sighs, “No, the Ouranatha control magical education in the capital.”

Cliopher silently adds removing that privilege to the list of reforms to the Ouranatha, once Rhodin has finished his drag net. Actually, it might be worth telling Rhodin, whoever enforces that is suspicious.

“There is no schooled magic left on Ysthar anyway,” adds his Radiancy, “We would be better off seeking out a hedge witch or a shaman.”

It is so clear to Cliopher that his Radiancy would like to go with them. “Perhaps, my lord, you could go on the next mission?” he offers, “I'm afraid I know so little about magic… Do you work schooled or wild magic, my lord?”

“I am a wild mage,” says his Radiancy, calmly, “Although bound as Emperor at the centre of schooled magic, I was divided from my own. After the Fall, my magic slowly returned. I am able to work both, although schooled magic is still uncomfortable. The Lights, for example, are hybrid, so any mage can access them.”

“Separating a person from their magic was defined as torture as far back as the Aurelian Code,” says Cliopher, shock showing clear on his face. “I am so very sorry, my lord,” he says, knowing there was nothing he could have done, but wishing he could have at least been able to see how much the man at the centre suffered for their peace. His lord was tortured for over fourteen years. How did he survive?

To the side, Cliopher can see Conju is struggling to maintain his usual calm.

“But, it is not like that now, my lord?” asks Cliopher, gently.

“I can walk in the sunlight, and access my magic, and there are fewer rituals,” says his Radiancy, “But we are still bound by law, and custom, and the taboos. And we have been forced to marry our sister and...”

His face is still, his eyes distant. Cliopher feels his lord is pushing away the storm of hurt and anger. His Radiancy puts his hands on the arms of his chair, as if to rise.

Melissa reaches out, slowly. His Radiancy sees, and seems transfixed. Gently, so gently, she lays her hand on his slender wrist. “Artorin,” she says gently, “We will set you free.”

He is staring at her hand on him. Nobody has touched him in a thousand years, thinks Cliopher. It must be so strange for someone to do so now. “But the child...” he says, weakly.

“You… You don't have to see them, Artorin,” she says, her voice shaking very slightly. “I can keep them in Damara.”

“No… what if… they find them,” whispers his Radiancy, once again, looking out at the distance.

“My lord, if this was not just one fanatic, Rhodin will find the rest of them,” says Cliopher, wishing he could reach out as well. “And together we will make sure there is nothing to bind the child to.”

They all take a moment to breathe. 

“Yes,” says his Radiancy, “Of course. I do not doubt you, Cliopher, or Melissa, or Rhodin.” He looks down at Melissa's hand on his, and then at her face. She lifts her hand slightly, as if to remove it. His Radiancy is perfectly still, as if he is afraid to indicate if he wants her to stay or go.

“I believe the University of the Wide Seas has a scholar of magic,” says Cliopher, unable to think of anything else that he can do to reassure his lord.

“Megana Courtenay?” asks his Radiancy, “She is a scholar of Astandalan magic, quite a fine one, but schooled magic all the same.”

Cliopher is pleased the academics of his university have come to the note of his lord. "Yes, my lord, she is. However, she has a colleague, Hilda Delanis, who I believe works with the shamans. I do not know if she has any skill at magic herself, I'm afraid I have only met her socially.”

“Perhaps she can at least suggest a practitioner from the Vangaveye-ve we could invite,” adds his Radiancy.

It doesn't seem worth clarifying that the zamà would be best-placed to do that. It might be that Hilda will know someone from closer to Solaara who might be more suitable. Cliopher adds a note to write to her as quickly as possible, as Lord Chancellor rather than as the cousin of her colleague.

“I would like to take Huara from the Offices of State, my lord,” says Cliopher, “So I may leave Kiri and Aioru with you.”

His Radiancy nods. Melissa chose not to take her hand away, and she must clasp slightly tighter, for his lord looks at her inquiringly.

“How many guards would be appropriate?” she asks, uncertain. “The Lord of Ysthar seems to suggest in his letters that he will find us accommodation that is magically secluded.”

Rhodin is on guard, and his Radiancy looks up, and beckons him inside the Wall of Silence. He salutes crisply.

“What is the *minimum* number of guards for the embassy, Ser Rhodin?” asks his Radiancy.

“Eight for the journey to and from the gate, my lord,” says Rhodin, and then steals a glance at Cliopher. “Based on the information the Lord of Ysthar has provided, my lord, and if his excellency and her highness agree not to leave the accommodation separately, then four should be workable on Ysthar.”

Cliopher is relieved to hear they will not need to be quite as closely guarded as his Radiancy.

Later, Cliopher gathers Kiri, Aioru, and Huara in his office. They sit around a bowl of crunchy snacks, pencils in hand.

“His Radiancy has declined to invite any of the Ouranatha,” says Cliopher, and there are winces around the desk. “Apparently there is no schooled magic left on Ysthar at all, and so there is no point. It's not even worth asking the Mother of the Mountains.”

The others look thoughtful. “There isn't any school for wild mages,” says Kiri, and then smiles wryly at herself. “Of course there isn't.”

Cliopher nods, selecting a zucchini chip. “But there are some academics who study non-schooled magic. I have met one from the University of the Wide Seas. I was going to write to her and ask for suggestions.”

Aioru munches on a rice cracker, a glint in his eye that Cliopher recognises. He raises an eyebrow at the younger man, who blushes slightly.

“I know a wild mage,” he says, cautiously. “But… she's about fifteen or so. She and her family are living in Solaara at the moment.”

“I don't think taking a child as our mage sends the right message,” says Kiri. 

Cliopher quietly agrees. He'd rather take a crotchety old Ela grandfather than a vulnerable person just learning. Yet, perhaps there could be room for both? They certainly can't take her *alone*.

“Indeed. Out of curiosity, what does her family do?” he asks.

“Her father is a traveller and author,” says Aioru, “You might have heard of him? Kon Isacu.”

Kiri and Huara both nod. The name seems somewhat familiar, but Cliopher must have just heard it in passing.

“They travel Zunidh?” asks Cliopher, curious.

“All the nine worlds,” says Aioru, “Even Ysthar and Eahh.”

Cliopher blinks, “Have they been to Ysthar recently? Do they know any of the languages? I've been trying to teach myself some English, but there's not much available.”

“Yes,” says Aioru, sagely, “I believe all of them speak a little English, Mandarin, and Spanish. Tanaea, my friend, said there are tens of thousands of languages, but most people speak at least one of those.”

Rubbing his temple, Cliopher says, “I don't envy Rhodin trying to do a security clearance for two people who have spent little time on Zunidh.”

Kiri looks at him sternly, “You're not serious, sir, surely.”

“I don't want to rule it out,” said Cliopher, awkwardly, “I have no idea if we'll be able to find anyone else who might be willing to come. At least they're used to travelling. Do you think they'd be more comfortable if you came, Aioru, rather than Huara?”

“I'd be more comfortable,” muttered Huara, and then blushed deeply. “Sorry sir, I'm just a bit of a home girl.”

Internally, Cliopher winced. Had he not actually _asked_ her? Or had he asked in a way that implied she had to agree? “I'm so sorry, Huara, I should have made it clear that you're _invited_ but not at all required to come. It's a long time to be away, and I don't want to even slightly suggest you need to go.”

Aioru and Huara looked at each other and grinned. “Well,” said Aioru, “I'd rather not be substitute secretary to his Radiancy.”

Huara added, “I wouldn't mind that at all.”

“I'm glad that's sorted,” said Kiri, with a smile at them both, “But sir, don't try suggesting you and I should swap.”

Cliopher shared the smile, “I wouldn't dare, Kiri.”

The reply to Cliopher's letter comes faster than he expected. Since he addressed his formally, the reply is also formal. Domina Delanis apologies that she is only an anthropologist, and recommends Lord Mdang contact her coworker Domina Courtenay. He is disappointed, he was so sure she worked with the Ela, but perhaps that was only on the spiritual and social aspects of Wide Seas culture.

He's written to the Mother of the Mountains, and the major universities in Boloyo City and Cisven. None of them know of any wild mage of sufficient power to bother the _Lord Chancellor_. Cliopher quietly wishes they were willing to tell him of the hedge-witches he had seen in both cities selling charms against fire and food spoilage. But maybe the illustrious personages he's writing to don't visit those parts of their cities.

In desperation, he writes to Uncle Lazo, including a note to pass on to the zamà. He's not actually sure who that is at the moment in Gorjo City, but Lazo will know. At least Uncle Lazo won't try to pretend they don't exist.

Indeed, Uncle Lazo replies to tell him that the zamà is Kawa, and he will forward the note. There's also a gentle update on all the other lorekeepers. It reminds Cliopher how long it has been since he visited Lazo's shop when he was home. Too long. He sighs, and hopes the zamà can suggest someone. The Ela are those who went furthest, which is both literal and metaphorical. 

Hopefully one of them will be willing to go as far as Ysthar. There's no longer enough time for them to take the sea train to Solaara, so they will have to send a sky ship.

Cliopher twice wakes from nightmares where he's had to tell his Radiancy there isn't any available mage. Quietly, Cliopher thinks that if that _does_ happen, his Radiancy will have to go and Cliopher stay. But organising the full Imperial Household to travel is always a gargantuan effort. One evening, at dinner in the Zunidh apartment, Cliopher quietly confesses the option to Conju.

“The only leads I have are the senior shaman from Gorjo City, and a fifteen-year-old friend of Aioru in Solaara,” says Cliopher with a sigh.

The Groom of the Chamber gives him a most scathing look. “Are you telling me that _he_ is the only adult wild mage on Zunidh? You aren't looking hard enough.”

A very drunk Conju has twice mentioned a wild mage he used to know. Cliopher quietly wonders if he has walked into a sensitive topic.

“It might be that there aren't many wild mages willing to work for the Emperor,” Cliopher offers, “Or that they are intimidated by the idea of meeting the Lord Magus.”

Conju looks far away. “Yes, possibly,” he says, absently twisting a teaspoon between his fingers.

“I don't suppose you know…?” asks Cliopher, quietly.

“No,” says Conju, firmly.

Trying to lead his friend away from obviously painful thoughts, Cliopher turns to asking how the new arrangements in the Tower are going. “How is Melissa settling in at the Imperial Apartments?”

“Sometime I _will_ get used to that,” mutters Conju, then in a more normal voice says, “Very well. I found some gowns in the archives that had been made for her, and we have a good laugh over the outdated fashions.”

“There were gowns made… assuming she would be Shallyr's Consort soon?” asks Cliopher, surprised.

“Yes,” says Conju, “I can't think why, when she would have to go through nine months of preparations before the wedding. They would have been horribly out of style by the time it was done. I'm afraid they didn't come with explanatory notes.”

“I assume you've never done that?” says Cliopher, “Well, I suppose you have now that they're… living together.”

“No, I would never have dared commission anything for her when she was the Grand Duchess,” says Conju, with a slight smile.

Cliopher waits for him to clarify, trying to copy one of Conju's sarcastic eyebrows.

“All right!” says Conju, “I didn't actually commission anything for you, but I did have Ylette draw up some designs.”

Trying to swallow, Cliopher chokes on nothing at all. “You… what?” asks Cliopher, trying to breathe.

Conju smiles, a predatory light in his eyes, “Maybe I'll get some of them made up for your trip to Ysthar.”
