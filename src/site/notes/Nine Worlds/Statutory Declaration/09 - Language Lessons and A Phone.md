They meet everyone else at what Sherry calls 'the court', but her snicker at her own words suggests that she recognises an old pub is not what they are expecting.

Rhodin is clearly relieved to see them. It had been hard to convince him to let his two charges go to the hospital without him. Sherry and Prince Robin had both agreed, however, that it was far more likely to cause more problems than it solved.

There is a mixed and slightly ragged round of greetings, as nobody seems quite sure on the correct level of formality, or indeed if Melissa or the Lord of Ysthar should pick it. After an awkward silence, it's Melissa who resolves it.

Sitting down, she asks, “I don't suppose there's anything non-alcoholic on offer, Raphael?”

The Lord of Ysthar looks still, but then gently pleased. It reminds Cliopher of the way his Radiancy carefully controls his expression. “I believe there should be cordials and the usual fizzy drinks.”

“Any of those sounds *wonderful*,” says Melissa, “I can't believe how thirsty I am.”

Once everyone has a glass of something, Robin says, “I presume you didn't have any issues with the NHS? You seem to have got out in reasonable time.”

“Oh yes, everyone was very obliging,” says Melissa, in the polite tones she uses in the Council of Princes.

“Translating medical words like *ultrasound* into Shaian was an adventure,” says Sherry. Robin gives her a strange look, and she clarifies, “I don't mind, it's kind of fun, but 'machine that makes pictures from sound' is a bit long.”

Robin blinks, and doesn't look any less confused. “You didn't just teach them English?” he asks, eventually.

Now it's Sherry's turn to look baffled. Raphael looks curious, but everyone else also seems to have no idea what Robin could mean.

Looking around the tables, Robin gets no nods of agreement.

“My apologies, Prince Robin,” says Cliopher, trying to bridge the awkward moment, “We have been trying, but I am afraid it is rather different from any of the languages we speak. Only Tanaea knows more than the tiniest bit. We were very thankful for Sherry's assistance.”

Robin turns to Raphael, “You can't?”

“I'm afraid,” says Raphael, “That I am only human. I have learnt every language *I* know through study and use.”

“Ah,” says Robin, awkwardly, “I apologise, my lord, I didn't realise, you have always been so gifted in speech. May I offer my services?”

Raphael pauses for a moment, before saying, “Do you mean your skill for languages is a gift of the fey?”

Looking slightly embarrassed, Robin says, “I had assumed it was simply magical. But if even the Lord of Ysthar cannot, then it must be. I've, ah, only ever used it on others in jest. So I don't know how long it lasts.”

“Please,” says Raphael, formally, “With their permission, of course.”

Cliopher flicks his eyes to Rhodin, who shrugs. If the Lord of Ysthar trusts Prince Robin, then it is probably safe. As far as these things go.

“I would very much appreciate it,” said Cliopher, “I'm sure you'd rather let me loose on the libraries and museums and not have to trail around after translating.”

Robin winks, and clicks his fingers. Cliopher feels his ears pop, but nothing else. Casting his eyes around, he spots the cardboard coaster his drink is on. He'd noticed it had writing on it, but now he can read 'Extra Smooth' along the bottom.

“Thank you, very much, my lord, your highness,” says Cliopher, suddenly realising that just as he can switch between Islander and Shaian, he can now do so to English as well. It feels the same, although where Islander reminds him of Buru Tovo, English reminds him of Will. “This is incredible.”

The rest of the Zuni contingent look either confused or impressed. Tanaea is absolutely grinning, Kawa looks thoughtful. But all of them also accept the offer. Those who have only ever known Shaian before seem to find the process more disturbing. If Melissa does, she tucks it quietly behind her court face.

When they have all settled down again, Will opens the pub door. “I see I'm the last,” he says, and shuts it behind himself, “Well then, what is past is prologue. Where to? Or will you once again despair, and order pizza?”

In the afternoon Melissa returns to the house to write to his Radiancy, while Raphael invites Kawa and Tanaea to meet a bridge troll. Will and Aoiru fall into a discussion of the contributions of the various cultures of Ysthar to the modern city around them. Cliopher is tempted to join them, until Sherry gives him a speaking look. 

"So, shall we see about getting you a phone then?" she says, "Now that you'd be able to read the screen."

Robin turns on the spot to look between them. "A phone? Have you even read any reviews, Sherry?"

Sherry shrugs, "No, I was going to visit a phone shop and see which one spoke to Cliopher."

"Electronics aren't like magic or musical instruments," says Robin, firmly, "They don't have _soul_, they have specifications."

"Are they all very different?" asks Cliopher, concerned.

"No," says Sherry, at the same time Robin says, "Yes."

"Well," says Cliopher, thinking perhaps this might be a long-running disagreement, "We will only be here for six months, so there's probably not a lot of reason to get anything particularly fancy or expensive." Quietly, he's wondering if a used phone might be the best option.

They walk along past the shop fronts, each of which seems to have its own colours like the noble houses of Astandalas. And just like at court, all gathered together, they clash rather spectacularly. The names seem to be just as much nonsense as they were before he could read English, so perhaps they *are* families.

Sherry and Robin are now arguing about the type of store to buy from. Cliopher is letting them enjoy it, and admiring the shop displays. Everything in bright colours, shoes, sweets, stationary. Even though everyone around them is dressed in black and grey. Even the bank is a bright green, with a dancing horse.

"They have the best network," says Robin, "Cliopher can just cancel the contract when he leaves."

Sherry rolls her eyes, "Do you think you will be going hiking in the lake district, Cliopher? Or visiting the channel islands?"

"He will now *you've* said it," retorts Robin.

"I'm afraid I will have to defer to your expertise entirely," offers Cliopher, "But is there perhaps some shop that has a variety of options?"

"Let's just go inside," says Robin, "The discussion will be more comfortable."

They enter a store that is a bright crimson colour. It has wall displays with different options, each with a little sign next to them highlighting the features and cost. Cliopher pokes the first one cautiously, but nothing happens.

"They're dummy models," says Robin, with a sigh, "I wish they wouldn't do that. Given they're glued in place anyway."

This actually gives Cliopher more confidence. He tries pressing all the buttons, which feel very satisfying, like the closure on his writing kit. The label next to it declares it to be a  basic model, with a loud alarm. Given how much Robin and Sherry were debating the cost, the listed price seems very reasonable. Then he sees the price has a footnote. Well, he's hardly going to be caught not reading a contract.

A sales assistant slides up to him, dressed in the store livery of red and white. "Is there anything in particular you are looking for, sir?" he asks, differentially.

"Just a phone," says Cliopher, wondering how many messages people on Ysthar send that packages come with hundreds per month.

"Then you're in the right place, sir!" says the salesman, with a smile, "Are you upgrading? Any particular features that you're looking for?"

"No," says Cliopher, hoping to be left alone.

"Some of the recent models can be used for playing music, replacing the need for an iPod or discman," offers the salesman. Curiously while the last two words are clearly separate in English, the only Shaian equivalent that comes to mind is a music box. Cliopher can't imagine any of the tiny boxes are large enough to hold a music box of any complexity. Even the magical ones were never very impressive to him. Maybe he has been spoilt by coming from the Home of Music to the Palace of Stars.

"No, thank you," says Cliopher, "I prefer live music." He's pleased with the phrase 'live music', as if it is not only the musicians but the music itself which comes alive in the playing.

Sherry comes over to them, and Cliopher is hopeful for rescue. "He doesn't even have home internet," she says, with a sigh. She seems like a whole different person, reminding him most of his young niece, trying to explain anything to his mother.

There's a twinkle in Robin's we as he bounces over, "Because he only just *moved*, darling," he says, with a flourish, "Give a man time to *breathe*."

"In London?" retorts Sherry, "As likely as not that'll make him sick. He needs texts, calls, and maybe maps."

"*Everyone* needs a camera," says Robin, taking Cliopher's arm, "Come and look at these."

As instructed, Cliopher looks at a selection of black glass rectangles, about the size of a small pocket notebook. "No buttons?" he asks, tentatively.

Robin smiles, and pulls his own out of his pocket. "It has a touch screen, so you can look at more at once, and it just has the buttons you need." He demonstrates this by swiping a map around, which Cliopher tries not to be impressed by.

"What else can they do?" asks Cliopher. The salesman opens his mouth, but Robin gets there first.

"Apps," he says, with relish, "Notes, voice memos, calculators, calendars, books, newspapers, games."

Cliopher is mildly astonished so much can fit in such a small object. Perhaps there is some technological version of Fitzroy Angursell's famous bag at work.

"Yes," says Sherry, "Inferior versions of all those things."

"Are there any models I can try?" asks Cliopher cautiously.

Smiling the salesman begins what must be a standard patter about the wide variety of latest models they have, and leads him over to a table. Eight phones have been bolted down in metal frames. Cliopher wonders what crimes they have committed.

Sherry looks at them with disdain. "Needlessly expensive," she says.

The salesman, knowing well his cue, begins explaining the deals they have on offer. "All of them are currently available for half price for the first three months of the contract."

Cliopher looks at him closely. "That's only six-point-two-five percent off the total contract cost, assuming these are twenty-four months like those on the wall." Then he peers down at the signs to check.

"Oh," says the salesman, "But that's still a significant..."

Robin interrupts him to ask about the features of the phone that is so important it gets a table to itself.

Cliopher ignores them, and begins to poke the screens. Working down the table, he looks at each one. They all still seem very similar. Swipe to unlock, and then a grid of colourful icons like a box of chocolates. The last one in the row, however, is different. It has big text, and everything flips open like a book.

Paging through it, he is surprised to find a household budget. Tapping around, he accidentally increases the rent by adding a zero. Before he can work out how to fix it, the rest of the table adjusts automatically. The family is now, unfortunately, several hundred pounds short each month. The number is even red!

Cliopher whispers to Sherry, "Is there a cheaper version of this one?"

She looks at the sign, and then at the wall. "These ones," she says, gesturing to a column.

Moving over, his eye is immediately drawn to one two-thirds of the way down with a bright blue frame. Peering around the back he sees it is blue as well. Suspicious of the deals on offer in this store, he reads the sign carefully.

"How much is a top-up?" he asks.

"Ten pounds, usually," says Sherry, "Mine lasts me two or three months."

"Do you think this one would be adequate?" he asks, "It seems a lot cheaper than the others."

She looks at the label and smiles, "If it calls to you...?"

"I like the colour," admits Cliopher.

"A perfectly valid reason. Now, has Robin convinced the poor shopkeeper we're going to buy one of everything, do you think?"

The salesman did have a sort of happy glazed look that Cliopher mostly associated with shop assistants in the wake of one of Conju's post-break-up shopping sprees. Robin gently directed him back over, saying, "Now, just for today, what could you offer us on the phone my friend is interested in?"

The man readily agreed to a further discount, and they finished the sales paperwork. He offered to set it up for Cliopher, but Robin protested that they didn't want to take up more of his time. So together they walke back to the house, through the drifts of afternoon shoppers. Robin explaining the fashions and technology in the windows.

When they enter the house, it seems everyone is there, and has adjourned their various activities for afternoon tea and coffee together. Cliopher joins, putting his bright crimson paper bag on a side table. The coffee, is, of course, not quite as good as home. But Robin claims he had sent his staff to Melbourne (wherever that is) to apprentice to the best makers Ysthar had. Cliopher hums appreciatively, and chooses one of the ginger biscuits.

It is Aioru who notices the red bag first. "Did you find what you were looking for, sir?"

"Ah!" says Robin, "We should help you set it up."

"You mean *you* should," says Sherry, with a smile, "I am happy with my old phone that has been run over by a taxi and still works."

"You set up mine," says Raphael, blandly.

"You left it in the box for two weeks!" says Robin, and then realising he is being goaded, purses his lips and goes to look in the bag.

"Sherry!" he says, sounding alarmed, "Why did you let him buy a Windows phone?"
