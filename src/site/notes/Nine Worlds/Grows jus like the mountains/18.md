Kip had thought the washroom he had been provided was more than adequate for a private space. He had not considered that there might also be a bathhouse. Or a bath… suite? It seemed to be an Astandalan public bath in miniature. This first room had couches built into the walls, with geometric mosaics on the floor and walls. Through a glass door, Kip could see another room, with stations for washing and a plunge pool. There would presumably be a sauna room beyond that.

“Liliane,” breathed Conju, “You are a treasure.” He walked in, and began stripping down. “Coming, Cliopher?”

Kip followed, swapping his torn and grimy clothes for a small towel, copying Conju. In the public baths one would have had robes and slippers, but presumably such concerns didn't matter when the bath was just for you and your friend.

The main room was deliciously hot. Every part of the Palace of Stars was made the same temperature by magic, so one only needed to worry about things like dressing for the season outside. Here, it felt like the very hottest summer in the Vangaveye-ve, but with soft warm mage lights, rather than the beating sun. Kip sighed immediately, feeling it seep into his bones.

The scrubbing mitt and soap were very welcome. Somehow, with so many doors between himself and the world, he felt safe in a way he hadn't for a while. Perhaps it was just a natural reaction to being so recently kidnapped. Once they were both clean, they settled into the small sauna room.

“I had no idea this was here,” said Kip, leaning back into the bench.

“Well, since I don't think my family has come to court since my sister was presented, what, ten years ago? I'd imagine it took a while to clean it out,” said Conju, reclining on the bench like it was an eating couch. “I think Liliane might have been saving it for a special occasion.”

Kip wasn't sure what to say about what had happened in the past day and a half. He hadn't told Conju anything yet, Ser Rhodin was probably the only person who knew much at all. What did he want to say? He was starting to feel that he'd been foolish not to have expected the Lord Treasurer to take revenge. Then he realised there was a piece of the story he was missing.

“How… how did the Glorious One find out?” said Kip, tense. There was an obvious way, but it seemed so forward, when Conju had always been so careful.

Conju took a deep breath, and closed his eyes. “The idiot Lord Treasurer put his demand note in the budget papers he sent me. I opened them in front of his Radiancy.”

After waiting to see if Conju would clarify on his own, Kip asked, “And he saw it? And asked you… what?”

“Oh no,” said Conju, “I copied it out and handed it to his Radiancy, with the documents for his seal.”

Conju looked like he was about to be sick, or possibly sneeze. Kip reached out a hand, and gently placed it on his. “That was… incredibly brave of you,” said Kip, giving the hand a squeeze, “'Thank you' seems inadequate.”

Fluttering his eyes open, Conju looked at their hands. “If I had been brave, I would have asked the Glorious One directly,” he said, quietly, “But I wasn't sure, I had no reason to believe he would move against the Lord Treasurer, and it was only very technically treason.”

Abruptly, Conju looked up at Kip. “It was, wasn't it? They abducted you as you were leaving the Imperial Apartments, and not later in the day?”

“Yes?” said Kip, confused, “I went straight down to the Archives, and they accosted me before I got to the front door.”

Conju let out a huff of relief. Then presumably realising that wasn't the correct reaction to someone being kidnapped, said, “What happened then? Your note said you were okay, but…”

Kip shrugged. “They knocked me out with a spell, and then stashed me in an out-of-the-way dungeon. I don't think they were very experienced. If I'd wanted to kidnap someone, I wouldn't have left them unrestrained. I don't think they even searched my pockets. By the time they delivered lunch today, I'd made two traps, a knife, and ties.”

Conju burst out laughing. Kip suspected it was both at the absurd incompetence, and a release of tension. He joined in. It was like an adventure out of a novel, or a play. And now they were safe, and warm, and cosy. Conju turned his hand around, and lifted Kip's up, brushing the tiniest kiss against it.

“I am _so_ glad you are safe, Cliopher,” he said, his voice sounding a little raw.

“For now, at least,” said Kip, grinning back, “Perhaps next week we can irritate an admiral, and you can ask the Emperor to rescue me again.”

“Please,” said Conju, sounding serious, “Please no. I don't think my heart can take doing that again. When you were missing, and I had no idea where you had gone, if you had been injured somewhere, or murdered…”

Kip picked up Conju's hand, and brought it to his own lips. “Thank you, Conju,” he said, “I don't think I can say thank you enough.” Conju looked at Kip, his eyes wide, his lips slightly parted. 

Then, suddenly, Conju was gripped by a fit of sneezes. When they abated, he rolled onto his back, and looked up at the ceiling. “I think the medications are wearing off,” he said, sniffling, “I suppose the steam should help clear things out.”

“It would probably work better if you were sitting up, so the mucus doesn't pool,” said Kip, remembering something his mother had once said when he was young.

With a dramatic groan, Conju pushed himself up, head lolling on his shoulder. Kip shuffled closer, and bumped his shoulder against Conju's head.

“If it's too hard to keep yourself up, you can lean on me,” said Kip, with a small smile.

The puppy eyes Conju made spread Kip's smile into a grin. “Come on,” said Kip, and pulled Conju against him, “You're sick, it's okay. If you fall asleep, I'll carry you out so you don't get burnt or anything from being in here too long.” Conju buried himself into Kip's side, and let out a contented sigh.

After a pleasant while simply enjoying the sauna, Kip's mind started circling back to problem-solving. “What do we do next time?” he asked, “And what do we do tomorrow? We're going to be _so_ behind, and I'm probably going to be stuck all afternoon being interrogated.”

Conju sighed, “There won't be a 'next time', nobody else is going to dare touch you. Or hopefully me. Also, I very much doubt it will be an interrogation, they'll probably be very polite. In fact, I look forward to you complaining about how obsequious they will be.”

“But… the Glorious One was doing a favour to *you*,” said Kip, “And the entire guard were disturbed from their usual routines. They're surely going to be annoyed.”

“Can we talk politics later?” pled Conju, leaning even further into Kip, “My head hurts.”

“I'm sorry, Conju,” said Kip, pulling the other man up, “The heat's probably starting to make things worse. Let's go for a quick dip in the pool and then get you some more painkillers.”

By the time they made it to the outer room of the bathing suite, Conju was definitely looking ill. Kip helped him to take the medications Lilliane had left out, and then dithered over trying to get his friend into bed now, or letting him rest on the couches. Hoping to be able to coax him into eating something, Kip decided to tuck him under the fluffy warmed towels.

Conju looked like he had almost fallen asleep when Kip made himself comfortable on the long padded seat. Kip picked up a handful of roasted chickpeas, and began eating them. They were delicious, salted and spiced perfectly, and Kip could see himself sitting there and eating the entire bowl.

Clearly Conju was not quite asleep however, as he wormed his way up with his mouth open like a baby bird. Kip snorted, and dropped one into Conju's mouth. The other man rumbled appreciatively, wiggled higher, and opened his mouth for more. This continued until Conju's head was all the way into Kip's lap, and Kip was feeding them alternately.

When the chickpeas were gone, Kip picked other things off the platter. As well as making Conju sit up and drink plenty of the lemon water. Eventually, the medication and the sickness caught up with him, and Conju actually did drift off to sleep.

Trying not to wake him too much, Kip managed to half-carry, half-help his friend to the primary bedroom. Another blessing, Lilliane had laid everything out here too. Kip would have had no idea where to find what Conju wore to bed.

When Kip finally got Conju under the covers, he found his friend grasping his hand tightly. “Missed you,” mumbled Conju.

“I was only gone one day,” said Kip, amused how Conju's careful manners had dropped.

“Was scary,” said Conju, “Didn't know…”

“It's over now,” said Kip, rubbing Conju's hand with his thumb, “You can rest.”

“… stay?” said Conju, burying his face in the pillow.

“Alright,” said Kip, pulling himself up onto the bed, “At least until you fall asleep. I'll even tell you a story, so you have something else to think about.”

Conju hummed appreciatively, and then made a tiny, adorable sneeze.

“Once, when the islands were all still new, there were three brothers,” Kip began. Telling the story, not as his Buru Tovo had told it in language the Lays, but as his mother had told it in Shaian, when he was small.

As the story went on, Conju relaxed, and let go of Kip's hand. But he stayed to finish the story, even though he was pretty sure that Conju was properly asleep. When he reached the end, he slid off the bed.

“Love you,” whispered Conju.

Kip wasn't sure if perhaps Conju thought he was one of his siblings, or his mother. Still, he said back anyway, “Love you too, sleep well.” 

It was true, he thought, as he slipped down the hallway to his own bedroom. Conju was the closest friend he made since university, maybe even more like a brother than a friend. It was a pity it was so far to the Vangaveye-ve, and he couldn't really introduce Conju to anyone. 

Well, perhaps he could convince Basil to come and visit. It was too late to invite him for Silverheart, which was now days away. And anyway, Basil would want to spend Silverheart with his in-laws. Possibly after then; it seemed unlikely that Kip and Conju would be able to take leave at the same time to go to Alinor and visit Basil.

Remembering how behind they were likely to be, once he was dressed, he went to find Lilliane, to see if she might know what had happened to his writing kit. Given how Conju was fairing, it seemed likely Kip would need to attend his Radiancy the next morning.

He found her in the kitchen, with two other people he didn't know. All of them stood up quickly when he entered. “My apologies for interrupting,” he said awkwardly. Still uncomfortable with having servants, even if Lilliane worked for Conju, and Kip sometimes hoped they were more like colleagues.

“You should have rung, Sayo Mdang!” she said, with the slightly raised eyebrow that Kip knew meant he was _doing it wrong_. But she added quickly, “What can we do for you?”

Kip blinked, realising that she was implying that the other people with her were also Conju's staff. When had he hired more? Or were these people employees of Lilliane? He had never needed to know how the houses of the high nobility were organised; he supposed now he might have to learn.

“I was hoping you might know who to ask if my writing kit has been found?” asked Kip, now trying to look over the other two people surreptitiously. The older woman had a hat he thought meant she was some kind of cook or chef. The younger person, not wearing clearly gendered clothing, also seemed to have no marks of rank or status, but was polishing a brass figurine.

Lilliane and the chef gave each other a quick look. “I will ask the guards outside,” said Lilliane, “Please wait here, it will only be a moment.”

Watching the closed door, Kip wondered why there were still guards outside. Was he under house arrest? Or was Conju, for asking for a favour from his Radiancy in the wrong way? Perhaps they were just there to make sure Kip didn't run off before he could be interrogated tomorrow. He would be the primary witness to the crime, although not the victim.

It was strange, to be the _object_ of a crime. He'd never wondered how the horse felt about being stolen, or the salmon about being poached. The Palace had inured him somewhat to nobles having a completely different set of laws; he had expected that the best he could hope for was that the people who had taken him be fined. If they had kidnapped him the day before, they would have. Now, the guard probably wouldn't even bother laying that charge, with a writ for treason in their hands.

The ornament polisher must have moved slightly; the sound drew Kip back to where he was, and that he wasn't alone. “Sorry,” he said, looking between them awkwardly, “I don't think we've been introduced?”

“I'm Yarrin, sir,” said the chef, with a bow, “and this is Munayan.” Munayan bowed when their name was mentioned, and looked nervous. “We're both very thankful to be recommended to his lordship's service,” added Yarrin, “He seems like a very good sort of lord, sir.”

“He is,” said Kip, without having to think about it, and smiled.

Lilliane returned, and immediately bowed to Kip, “My apologies, sir, your writing kit is being held as evidence.”

With a sigh, Kip said, “I'll make do with things from the library then. And I suppose I'll do my best to reconstruct his Radiancy's letters from memory. Did you put Conju's document case in the library? I should try to get through the work from this morning as well.” At the end he realised he was thinking aloud more than asking for assistance, and shut his mouth with a small wince.

Lilliane smiled and replied, “Yes, sir, I put my lord's things on his desk in the library. I suspect that under the circumstances, perhaps the guards can be persuaded to at least return the documents. I shall ask.” She went through the door again, and Kip followed, but turned left towards the library when she went right to the front door. He just caught a glimpse of the guard standing outside the door. And, bizarrely, a small group of people who looked like they were trying to persuade Lilliane to take gifts or let them in.

Kip put the issue aside for now. Perhaps some rumour had run around the Palace about the great favour given by the Glorious One to Conju. There was work to do, and he would feel much better if it wasn't looming over him when he went to bed.
