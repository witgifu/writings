“Maybe when we retire, we could go on a grand tour of the Empire,” said Conju, snuggled into Kip's chest.

“What would you most like to see?” asked Kip, both arms wrapped around Conju, “I've always wondered how Voonra could be flat. What is at the edge?”

“So is Cohélhé,” said Conju, “They say the sea just runs off the edge into the stars. Perhaps you could sail there.”

Kip chuckled, he was a good sailor, but he wasn't exactly a navigator like Elonoa'a, to travel the strange seas of stranger worlds. He stopped laughing, and thought Conju was laughing too. But then he realised it wasn't that. The bed was shaking.

The room was shaking.

The world was shaking.

There was no light. 

The water pushed and pushed and drove Kip further, and he didn't even know which way was up except it must be the way the water wasn't pushing because that's what had happened it was a rip, and he was diving for pearls, the flame pearls that he needed to prove to Buru Tovo that he was worth teaching that he could be tanà and hold the lore and the fire, but there was no fire down here only dark and cold and the endless thrust of the ocean he was going to drown going to die like his father swept away by Ani, and he reached out to find something, anything he might hold onto and…

There was a warm body in his arms. A shaven head, delicate limbs. Kip held on fiercely. This could not be pearl diving, not if Conju was here. Somehow Conju must have got into trouble, and Kip had jumped in after him. Why he hadn't even brought a rope he didn't know. But with one hand he scrabbled in the coral, and kicked with all his might, to push himself, push them, out of this current, away, somewhere else…

---

There was no light.

The wind howled around them, biting. Not the pleasant snowfall of Astandalas, but driving like a summer storm of ice. Hail, but tiny and sharp and piercing into them. The wind pushed them, back down, the path, back the way they had come.

A howl, behind them. Was it a wolf, or something bigger? They must not stop, must keep moving, get away from whatever it was. Another howl, and a third. They were being hunted from behind and frozen from in front. The path in the dense dark forest had only forward and back. They hurried forward, stumbling, shivering, clutching each other.

---

There was no light.

It was unbelievably cold. Kip shook with how cold he was, even though he was in bed, with plenty of blankets.

“Conju?” he said, hopefully. His voice sounded far away, as if it was echoing through a valley.

“Cliopher?” came Conju's voice, sounding even further away, “What's happening?”

“I don't know,” said Kip, and he was afraid. Cold, scared, in the dark. His hands felt numb, his arms too, although they were wrapped around Conju.

“Don't leave,” whispered Conju, “Don't leave me!”

“I'm here,” said Kip, “I've got you Conju.”

They grasped each other tightly, and the silence was so complete it felt like the universe was screaming.

---

There was no light.

Time was meaningless, without light. There was fear, and the wailing of the things in the dark that Kip didn't want to know about. There was cold, and the feeling that the world was spinning, or perhaps the universe, or perhaps it was just Kip. Everything hurt, and Kip had no idea if he was awake, or dreaming, or if there even was a difference anymore.

He held onto Conju. Conju held onto him.

---

Kip opened his eyes, and there was, not light, but different shades of darkness. It was not _quite_ so unbearably cold. He looked around, trying to see where he was, where they were.

“Conju?” he said, to the man in his arms.

“Cliopher?” Conju said, his voice cracking like he'd been yelling. Perhaps he had, perhaps they both had.

“Something has changed,” said Kip, squinting, trying to see if the shades of darkness might resolve into something. Conju whimpered, quietly.

Then Kip spotted a tiny pinprick of light. “There's a light,” he said, and pulling Conju after him, scrambled out of the bed. The bed that they had been in, before all of… whatever it was… happened. Over to where the window that looked out on Astandalas was, the curtains shut against the lights of the city.

He pulled back the curtains, and looked out. The world was dark, and if Astandalas was there, every light and every fire was gone. But what he had seen through the crack in the curtains was the stars. The wake of the ancestors' ships, spread across the sky. The bright guides—

No. Those were the stars of Zunidh, his home world. The stars of Ysthar were different, although they also had a river of stars. They should be different. He didn't know those constellations, the star-paths or the zenith stars or anything else. 

He knew these constellations. They echoed in his mind, in his bones, with Buru Tovo's voice.

“We're on Zunidh,” he said, not understanding how that was possible, and yet certain it was true.
