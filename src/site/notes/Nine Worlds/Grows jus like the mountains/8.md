Kip was able to work in the Archives for a while, before returning to the Offices of State to sort and summarise the morning reports. It was tricky, again, to find space. He ended up squeezing himself in a corner and using the top of an index-card cabinet as a desk.

In the next day's stack there were, as he had hoped, incoming letters. Fewer than he expected. Someone must be sorting them, and deciding what actually went to his Radiancy. Kip would need to find out who, to make sure nothing was being excluded that shouldn't be. He had the sneaking suspicion that the process might be somewhat ad-hoc.

While he had a bit of time, Kip took the chance to jot out some potential replies for two of the letters. Ones he thought might actually be better dealt with by a department than by his Radiancy. He filed the potential responses with the letters, hopeful that it would be seen as helpful rather than wasteful or encroaching.

While he was copying the report stack, he spotted one of the Palace staff refilling a stationery cupboard. Taking advantage, he casually asked how the process worked. Operations Officer Gethin enthusiastically explained how everything from spoons (why were there never enough? Gethin also did not know), to the special inks used by the various departments, were requested and supplied.

Gerthin was happy to tell Kip that if he was in need of anything in particular, he was always welcome to drop by the supply rooms in the lower levels. If it was something they usually kept in stock, he only needed to name his department to whoever was on duty there to make sure the costs were allocated correctly. Kip resolved to never bother trying to get ink out of the Offices of State again.

Once Kip had followed Gerthin's directions and acquired a good supply of all the stationary he thought they might need, he went to try to find the door to their new apartment.

It was, Kip admitted, obvious from the decoration. Not only was the door carved with oaks, but the snakes were even painted. He wondered briefly how old your title had to be to get a custom door to your suite in the Palace of Stars. Had the an Vilius family served since before Aurelius Magnus? Perhaps.

He knocked on the door, worried that nobody would answer. Standing there for a moment, watching the end-of-day traffic through the hall, he wondered if maybe he had misheard, and Conju had actually said to meet somewhere else.

Liliane answered the door. “You don't need to knock, Sayo Mdang,” she said with a smile.

“Ah,” he said, feeling awkward, “I don't have a key yet?”

“The locks are magic, Sayo Mdang. A key isn't required,” she said, and stepped back to encourage him inside.

He had visited the apartments of nobles before, like the party where he had met Conju. He was, therefore, not entirely overwhelmed. Slowly, he walked down the entrance hall. Stained-glass windows at the end cast coloured lights up the white marble floor. The doors to his right were thrown open to show an Astandalan-style dining room, complete with a mosaic of the moon in her aspect as the first lover.

Liliane led him further on. Past the sparkling windows, another set of doors was closed. Through a smaller door he spotted what must be a library, with darkening views of the city. She led him on, through the door to the private rooms. At the end of that hallway, she stopped.

“My lord and yourself may wish to change the arrangements, but for now I have set the Green Room for you, Sayo Mdang. I hope it is to your liking,” she said. She opened the door on a bedroom larger than any he had yet had at the Palace.

“The dressing room is through that door, sir, and the bathroom past that,” she said, with a smile.

Kip stood in the middle of the room and spun around slowly, taking it all in. There was a generously sized bed, with green linens. A comfortable green armchair beside the fireplace, and a small desk set to take the best advantage of the natural light. Both sides of the fireplace had bookshelves, which Kip noticed had been filled with his notebooks, and other objects. 

His oboe case was placed at exactly the right height to open and take out the instrument, with his small collection of sheet music beside it. There was even, to his surprise, a beautiful carved music stand. He wondered if that had already been in the room, or if Liliane had found it somewhere.

“Thank you, Liliane, it's lovely,” he said, touched. He went to put his things on the desk, and then realised he was carrying a large amount of very particular stationary that should be shared with Conju. “I suppose this should go in the library.” he said, and before she could reply asked, “I don't suppose Conju has arrived yet?”

“My lord has not yet arrived, no,” she said, “If you have no more questions, Sayo Mdang…?”

“Oh, I am sorry Liliane, please do not let me keep you,” said Kip, feeling nervous. He was definitely not used to anyone deferring to him. Lilliane bowed, and left.

He walked back along the hallway with his stationary haul. The windows here had no view, but did let in the last of the days light. With the wood panelled walls it did feel warm and a little homey. In this more private space the artwork tended towards paintings of bucolic landscapes. Kip wondered if they were of  Vilius itself.

The library was a corner room, with large windows. So large that rather than feeling like a cosy den with bookshelves wrapping around, it felt almost like an opening on the world. It reminded Kip of dinning in the backyard of his family home, that combination of indoor and outdoor.

Of course here in Astandalas it would be madness to have a library actually open to the outside. The damp, not to mention the snow, would damage the books. However, the effect with the windows was still pleasant.

The room held two desks. Since Kip had also been given a desk in his room, he wasn't sure how these would work. Would Conju and Kip share a desk, with the other reserved for Conju's mother? Would they take a desk each? Were the desks meant to be available to anyone and so kept clean of anything personal?

While he stood there, wondering where to put the special papers and inks, Conju arrived.

“We did *not* get through all the scheduled audiences!” he said, walking through the doorway and putting his rather small stack of notes on the closest desk. “Four lords were moved to next week. Next week is already full! Who is managing this booking system!” He started sorting his notes.

“Probably whoever is standing closest to the ledger when the Offices of State get a request,” said Kip, sadly. Taking his cues from Conju, he placed his own stacks on the other desk. “Where should we keep things like the ink for proclamations?” he asked.

“Ridiculous,” said Conju, “There are only so many hours in a day, overbooking does not help.” He turned and looked around the room, and gestured to the corner. “Would that cabinet do, do you think?”

“Overbooking makes it your problem rather than the problem of the Offices of State. They can say yes to everyone and make you say no,” said Kip, and opened the cabinet. It contained just a stack of blank paper, and a small hand-made notecard that said 'Mother' on it in a child's imitation of Chancery hand. “Excellent!” said Kip, and started sorting the supplies he had acquired into the space.

“Of course. Because the goal of the Service isn't to *serve*, it's to avoid confrontation,” said Conju, sarcasm dripping from his voice.

“Were the audiences useful? Any action items I should know?” asked Kip.

“Oh it was mostly people showing how powerful they are by wasting his Radiancy's time,” said Conju, “I would not be nearly so annoyed if at least half of them had something more serious or even an actual question.”

Kip was disappointed, but not surprised. He sighed, and finished setting up the supply cupboard. “There still a proclamation and a few letters to write up from this morning,” said Kip, “But not nearly as much as yesterday.”

“Oh good,” said Conju, “It would be nice not to be working past midnight *all* the time.”

“Do you want to try to get through as much as we can now? Or does your hand need a break?” asked Kip, hoping also to be able to get the work out of the way. He was behind on writing letters home.

“I spent most of the time carefully not writing anything because the petitioners were waffling on. I would rather try to get it done now,” said Conju, and turned to Kip's much larger quantity of notes, “Pass over whatever you think I should tackle first.”

Kip handed over the shorthand notes on an inheritance dispute that was likely to make it all the way to the Throne of Judgement. If only because the three sides were all princes on Zunidh.

Conju looked down at the notes and snorted, “I see you gave me the one with all the interesting personal names.”

With a wry smile, Kip asked, “Oh, I thought you might know some of those people?”

Conju rolled his eyes. “I don't know _everyone_ in the aristocracy, Cliopher. It isn't called the Upper Five-Thousand without reason. I might as well ask if you know them, since you're from Zunidh.”

Kip snorted, “The Prince of the Vangaveye-ve is known as The Poz, and I don't think he's been sober enough in the last decade to file a legal dispute.”

“The Poz? Isn't that a bit disrespectful, Cliopher?” asked Conju, taking a seat and pulling a fresh sheet of paper forward.

“He calls _himself_ that, so I don't think so,” said Kip with a smirk, “But also the Vangaveye-ve doesn't care much for nobles. If he tried the things I've seen here, he'd probably be thrown in the harbour.”

Conju looked both impressed and offended. “Surely that would be treason?” he asked.

“The law,” said Kip sagely, “Is worth nothing, if you cannot enforce it.”

“Is that a quote from somewhere?” asked Conju, dipping his pen.

Kip was surprised Conju didn't know. Well, Kip wasn't going to admit he knew banned works to someone who didn't. “Oh, some poet, I think,” he said, picking up his own pen. They worked in comfortable silence for a while.
