“I went and told the Master of Offices,” said Conju, now looking a little less shaken, “He seemed relieved, honestly. I asked about the undersecretary, and he replied that since *he* didn't have anyone else at the moment I was free to appoint anyone I thought qualified. Then he handed me off to one of _his_ secretaries, and we filled in some paperwork for my appointment and yours.”

Kip blinked rapidly a few times. “Just like that? Surely there should be more process? Last time I moved sections there were four meetings about headcount before it was approved. My last promotion took six months and the final document had to be approved by my department head, Treasury, and the Office of State.”

Conju looked pained. "I thought I was going through the correct channels? They pulled up your personnel file. I do remember the secretary drafting a memo to an undersecretary for health and grumbling that someone was going to be annoyed."

"Yes… I suppose that does sound right," said Kip. “Presumably since both positions were longstanding, they didn't need Treasury approval? The Emperor himself appointed you, so there could be no questioning that. And the Master of Offices delegated to you to appoint the undersecretary. It just seems strange that it could happen so very fast.”

"Well, I suppose I should say 'Congratulations' then," added Kip, with a smile.

“I am not so sure about that. If I do not get all this work done, I will certainly be fired tomorrow,” said Conju, looking at the table. There was an appointments book that looked like a full-size banking ledger. Several stacks of shorthand notes with longhand drafts atop them, one or two with fair copies atop that. A _Beck's Peerage_ had been relegated to the floor, and a detailed tree-chart was being held unrolled by bottles of ink.

“Were you given a desk?” asked Kip, thinking a small filing cabinet and some drawers would make this all much easier.

(When would Kip go and clear out his old desk? Did he have a new desk?)

“Yes, but I cannot possibly sit in the Imperial Study all afternoon. People are walking through there, the Glorious One is walking through there!” said Conju, horrified.

“I was thinking of one in the Offices of State,” said Kip, “But they are a little overcrowded, so you might have to buy a desk off someone else.”

“Well, this is the space I have for now, I shall have to make the best of it,” said Conju, looking over everything, “Please, Cliopher, will you help me?”

Kip smiled, “Do you want me to see if I can read your shorthand? Or will you let me write the fair copies?”

“I could kiss you, Cliopher, whatever you would prefer. You can have the morning off in exchange—” said Conju, the relief cracking through his usually controlled face.

“No,” interrupted Kip, “I'll need to spend the morning preparing notes for your afternoon, if we've finished this. I can see from his schedule that the Emperor will be giving audiences, and I assume you'll be taking notes.”

Conju looked from the appointments book, to his neat piles of notes, to the surrounding room. “How, exactly, is this meant to be a one-person job? When am I supposed to sleep, or eat, or even get fitted for my new uniform?”

“I would venture to guess,” said Kip, imitating Conju's most acerbic tone, “That most of your predecessors simply did a poor job.” He pulled one of the drafts towards himself, and began pencilling in corrections.

“Unthinkable,” said Conju. 

They sat together, turning notes into drafts, and drafts into memos, letters, and one proclamation. The latter, Kip pointed out, would require a special ink and parchment for the final version, which they would need to source from the Offices of State. 

At some hour that Kip thought was probably later than he would usually eat dinner, Lilian started hovering. Conju looked up with his polite bland face on. “Will my lord take dinner in a refectory, or make space for me to serve it here?” she asked.

Together, Kip and Conju moved away their work until the table was empty and the floor and furniture was covered instead. As Liliane put out two bowls of lentil salad, Conju sighed. “It will take at least a week to open up the family suite. We—”

There was a cough from Liliane. “Excuse me, my lord, but if I can pay some palace staff for help, we could have it open enough for yourselves by tomorrow afternoon. As long as you promise not to bring any guests.”

“Of course, we would not dream of inviting guests until mother writes and says so,” said Conju, politely.

“And will you be wanting the primary bedroom, my lord?” asked Liliane, deferentially.

Kip watched Conju's face flit with emotion, before he regained control. He ate a slow spoonful of salad, before answering. “I should not presume…” he began, but trailed off without actually answering. 

Kip wondered if the etiquette book he had been reading when he had first met Conju would have held an answer to the conundrum. If he had been staying in Buru Tovo's house on Loaloa while his great-uncle walked the ring, then he would have taken the best sleeping spot until Buru Tovo returned. But Kip suspected that there was a lot more to changing bedrooms in a Palace family suite than sliding a sleeping mat.

“Perhaps that is a question also best addressed after you write to your lady mother, my lord?” said Liliane.

“Thank you, Liliane, yes,” said Conju, with some relief.

After dinner was finished, they went back to work. Kip switched to turning notes into longhand drafts. “Did you know which of the six education undersecretaries this memo is meant to be addressed to?” Kip asked, confused, “the context is a little vague.”

Conju looked over at the notes Kip was holding. “No, I'm afraid the Glorious One left it entirely unclear. I had been hoping it might be more obvious to you?”

“You knew it wasn't clear, and you didn't ask?” said Kip, slightly incredulous.

“No, I did not question the Lord of Rising Stars which undersecretary for Education he had read a report from a week ago,” said Conju, matching Kip's tone, “Are you expecting me to just _interrupt_ the Sun-on-Earth and _ask_?”

“Well, yes. Otherwise, you will end up spending hours on it, chasing down the recent Education reports and comparing the content. Asking would have taken seconds,” said Kip, pragmatically.

“Absolutely not,” said Conju, “It will get easier once I am more familiar, it is only because everything is new that it is tricky.”

Kip sighed, and wrote “To Unknown Education Undersecretary” at the top of the memo draft. 

There were some other unknowns he was able to fill in with information from the tree chart which turned out to contain the hierarchy of the Astandalan military. A few he could guess, and some he could at least narrow to a couple of candidates.

"Do you have a copy of the reports going to the Glorious One tomorrow?" asked Kip, hoping that there might be some more context there.

"Oh yes," said Conju, and passed them over. The stack was very large.

"The Glorious One reads all of these over breakfast?" said Kip, flipping through the stack. "They don't even seem to be sorted. And some of them don't even have an executive summary."

"That is exactly what I was given. They took the stack they had prepared and put it on a duplication plate," said Conju, "I haven't looked through it yet."

Kip grimaced. "If I'd presented this I would have been laughed out of the room. I'm not sure if we have time to fix it tonight," he said, looking around them, "But I'll look through them and add a cover summary."

Conju looked up briefly, sighed, and looked back down. Kip agreed, there was too much to do.

They stopped some time after midnight, Kip was not exactly sure when. Wishing he had packed more changes of clothes, he said, "I'll try to get the last of the unknowns filled in tomorrow morning."

"And I will see if there is anything else the Offices of State have on his Radiancy's calendar. This appointments book seems to be missing a lot of details," said Conju, giving the ledger a dirty look.

Liliane returned through the front door. Kip realised he hadn't actually noticed she had left.

"I have taken the liberty of fetching you another change of clothes, Sayo Mdang," she said, and handed him a linen bag. "I have also arranged for Palace staff to move both your things tomorrow morning. If you could both absent yourself as early as possible tomorrow, my lord?"

Kip felt slightly uncomfortable with the idea of other people packing and moving for him. Would they be careful with his oboe? Would they look in his notebooks? But he very much appreciated the change of clothes. And if they were going to making a habit of working into the small hours and then getting up early, it would be hard to find time to pack for himself.

"Thank you very much, Liliane," he said.

"Yes, thank you, we could not do this without you," said Conju.

As Kip got into bed with Conju, he asked, "Where exactly is your family suite?"

"Collian wing, four floors down from the Imperial Apartments," said Conju, sleepily, "Just walk down until you see the doors with oak trees with snakes in them. I can't remember how many it is."

Kip stared up at the ceiling. Close enough to the centre that one counted doors rather than corridors. How was he ever going to explain any of this to his family.

Kip stat in the last anteroom, and tried not to look at the guards. They were there to protect the Emperor, not to monitor Kip. Although why even the Emperor of Astandalas needed quite so many guards, and quite so many doors, he wasn't sure. Conju was even further inside, in the literal shinning presence of the Lord Emperor.

As he had planned, Kip had spent the first part of the morning preparing briefing notes for Conju. Then he had gone down to the archives, completing drafts that had gaps. Now he needed to make fair copies, and have everything done by the mid-morning break. Then, they hoped, Conju could pass him the first batch of shorthand, and Kip could pass Conju more documents for the Glorious One's approval. 

The special ink and parchment for proclamations had been a pain to acquire. The Offices of State were not keen to hand it out to a random fourth-level secretary who asked for it. It had taken at least a quarter-hour of polite insistence to get them to check who he was. 

Even then they had wanted to fetch Conju to vouch for him. It was only when Kip pointed out that Conju was already with the Emperor that they relented. They issued him one sheet of parchment, and a nearly empty bottle of gold-flecked ink.

It wasn't as if it would be possible for him to forge a proclamation with just the parchment and ink. It also needed the special wax kept only in the desk of the Lord Emperor, and the signet ring that never left his hand. What would Kip do, burst into the study and demand the Emperor seal it? He would be skewered by the guards before he got out a word.

Still, despite the miserliness of the staff in the Offices of State, Kip had enough supplies to complete the one proclamation from the day before. Hopefully he would be able to drag Conju along later and get a full bottle of ink and a larger quantity of parchment. How many proclamations did the Emperor make in a typical week? Kip had no idea.

Kip finished the documents, and had started on writing up notes for himself on all the things they needed to do. The door to the Imperial study opened a crack, and Kip rushed forward with the stack of documents. The guards handled the transfer, and Kip turned back to his desk with the pile of new notes.

The door opened further. Quickly, Kip turned around to make sure he wasn't showing his back to the Lord Emperor, if he happened to look that way.

Nobody exited, and there was nobody else here to enter. Trying to tamp down his fear, Kip stared at the floor as the door opened completely.

“Come forward,” said a voice from inside the official study. It wasn't Conju, and that intonation could only be one person.

Kip stepped forward, eyes on the floor, clutching the notes to his chest as if for protection. One step inside the doors, he fell inelegantly to his knees, and bowed his head.

“Cliopher sayo Mdang, Glorious One,” said Conju. Kip envied how controlled and even his voice sounded.

“Rise,” said the Lord Emperor.

Carefully, Kip stood up. He kept his eyes firmly on the floor in the centre of the room. Breathing slowly he relaxed his hold on the notes slightly, so he didn't crush them.

“You are our undersecretary,” said the Emperor, and Kip thought perhaps he heard the faint scrape of fine porcelain.

“Yes, Glorious One,” said Kip, uncertain. The Emperor had not phrased it as a question, and yet seemed to have left space inviting a response. He hoped desperately he had done the right thing.

“You prepared these documents for us,” said the Emperor.

Kip's eyes flicked up slightly, and he saw the Emperor's shining hand indicate a gold tray of documents. Those he had passed through the doors not moments ago. He swallowed, he must not contradict the Emperor.

“Yes, your Radiancy,” he said, trying to stop his voice shaking, “Lord Conju and I prepared them.”

“You wrote the summary for our morning reports,” said the Emperor.

“Yes, Glorious One,” said Kip, and looked down at the floor again. Was he in trouble? Was Conju in trouble? Should they not have split the work, or added the summary?

“Very good,” said the Emperor, with a surprising note of warmth in his voice.

Slightly startled at the praise, Kip assumed this was also a dismissal. He dropped once more into the obeisance, and then retreated backwards out the doors to his desk. Once the doors had closed, and the guards resumed their position, he finally allowed himself to collapse, shaking, into the chair.

Dropping the notes, he rubbed his face. _The Emperor_ had praised their work. Oh, true, it had mostly been Conju's work. But still! Kip had helped! As he tidied up the notes, Kip tried to fix every moment of the encounter in his memory. He might never be summoned through those doors again. He had certainly made a complete hash of the obeisance, and Conju would probably make him spend the evening practicing.

Kip pictured again that elegant hand, gesturing to the gold tray. Had he seen, out of the corner of his eye, the face of the Emperor? Had it looked like the portrait he had seen so many years ago, or was his memory already playing tricks on him?

Well, he couldn't sit here all morning reliving it. There was more work to do, and not enough time to do it. Perhaps, he though, someday, he would get to hear the Emperor say “very good,” to him again.

To his list on the side, Kip added 'Incoming correspondence' below 'New uniform for Conju' and 'Stationary'. Maybe it was usually included with the morning reports, but if it was passed to the Emperor at a different time they should track it down. At least so they had the context, but it might also be possible to prepare likely replies.

Below that, he added 'Personal correspondence?'. He knew many of the ministers had two secretaries, one for personal letters and one for public work. There was no such position for the Emperor's household, and so it was presumably also handled by the Private Secretary. If there was any? Was all correspondence between the Emperor and his family (small as it was) a matter of work, of public life? Perhaps if all your family were Princesses and Grand Dukes, there was no clear separation. Did the Emperor have friends? Perhaps Conju would know.

When the bells rang for noon, Kip had managed to turn most of the shorthand notes into longhand drafts. There were, in fact, fewer unknowns this time. Mostly, Kip thought, because he had read the same reports as his Radiancy, and so had a lot more of the context. There were still places where he wished Conju had just _asked_ what the Emperor had meant.

Conju backed out of the official study, with a stack of notes and sealed and signed documents.

“Were there any corrections?” asked Kip, once the door shut.

“One,” said Conju, a slightly pained look on his face, “I hope he wasn't too upset. He didn't look upset, but of course he wouldn't. He is most serene and gracious.”

Kip decided not to try to square the seemingly contradictory statements Conju had just made. “Let's drop those off in the Offices of State,” said Kip, pointing to the completed documents, “Then I can continue on your notes while you're in the audiences. When do they start?”

“Half one,” said Conju, “In the Lesser Audience Chamber. Hopefully we have time for some lunch.”

They did have time for lunch, although the refectory they chose was very crowded. Both of them ate as quickly as possible so they could escape somewhere quieter. Finding a meeting room that was at least not _currently_ occupied, Kip handed over the information he had collected that morning. “I'll head down to the Archives for the afternoon,” said Kip, re-sorting his stack of documents.

“I am not sure if the audiences will finish on time,” said Conju, looking over the list Kip had copied out of the appointments book. “If they do, I will come and find you in the reading room. If not, I suppose, I will see you at our new apartment?”

Kip was relieved to hear Conju describe it as ‘our’ apartment, rather than just his or his family's. It wasn't that he didn't trust Conju. But since coming to the Palace he had been disappointed so many times, made so many mistakes, that he expected it to continue.

At any moment, he was sure he would wake up in his old room, with a headache from working too late into the night to avoid someone who had been hanging around. He missed the gentle, friendly touch, that he had back home. Here he had learnt standing too close could be judged an invitation, and not immediately pulling away as consent. “If you didn't want it, you shouldn't look at me like that,” had been the refrain of his first year in the Palace.

To his embarrassment, something of his thoughts must have shown on his face. Conju asked, quietly, “Are you feeling well, Cliopher?”

Kip shook his head. There was work to do, he didn't need to waste time on his own worries. “Yes, thank you Conju, I was just trying to think if there was anything else I need to do. I will make sure to get to the Offices of State to check over tomorrow's reports, and see if they can't be at least sorted better.”
