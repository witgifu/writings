Audiences were, for the most part, as dull as court. You had to pay closer attention, however, as the slightest misplaced movement could be understood as agreement or consent. So you sat, statue on a throne, while various interchangeable dukes complained about their in-laws, or taxes, or boasted about their recent attempts to impoverish their farmers or strip-mine their lands.

The Cavalier took notes, if there was anything worth noting. Usually, there wasn't, unless one wished to track the most obsequious comments received or overblown praise. Some people should not be allowed to use a thesaurus.

It was a slight surprise when the page announced a member of the minor nobility. Perhaps he had scraped together enough money for a bribe, or slept his way to getting an appointment. You had no illusions about how your government worked, and while you had tried to reform much, you had yet to find the right lever to change this.

The young man was clearly a duellist, although of course he wasn't allowed so much as an ornamental dagger in your presence. He made an elegant obeisance, but when you said, “Rise,” he only lifted as far as his knees.

“What issue would you bring before us?” you asked for the tenth time today.

“I have come to beg for my life, Glorious One,” he said, his eyes on your hands, “Although I know I am unworthy of your consideration, Lord of Five Thousand Lands, I come before you in fear and trembling with nowhere else to turn. I have offended the Lord Treasurer, Serenity, and he has called for satisfaction. If the gods grant that I win, I cannot imagine, Glorious One, that I would live much longer. Most merciful lord, I humbly beg for your aid.”

I wanted to wish him luck and send him out. He was probably young enough to be the Lord Treasurer's grandson, and even if the Treasure had kept himself in fighting shape I would guess the older man was possibly already regretting calling out a *professional*. Also, it would be so sweet to know the last of Eritanyr's henchmen had joined him. The Lord Treasurer was cruel, but smart enough to keep what he did behind closed doors and away from where you were allowed to perceive it.

“What does the Lord Treasurer accuse you of doing?” you asked, the more appropriate response. Although I struggled to think of a matter of honour where the Lord Treasurer would be in the right.

Ser Rhodin looked perfectly calm, as any good courtier or fighter must. “I helped the girl he kept escape, Glorious One,” he said. Girl, not mistress or woman. Oh, you should have known. I should have known. Instead, this gallant minor noble had met her at some party and… Hm.

“And what did you do with her?” you asked, coolly.

“Glorious One, I escorted her to the Gate to Alinor, where she was met by my cousin, a scholar, who took her to Tara University. Her maid was with her the entire time; I swear nothing untoward happened, Illustrious One.”

It would help if you had literally any way of confirming this. You could summon the Lord Treasurer, who would presumably agree about the duel. Not, of course, about the girl. Who, it sounded, was at least old enough to go to university, some slight relief.

“Is there anyone still on Ysthar who can speak for you?” I probed.

“I would not wish to presume, Glorious One, but perhaps the Cavalier Conju an Vilius might?” he said, his voice uncertain.

At his desk, the Cavalier paused ever so slightly. He did not seem surprised, but then a good courtier should be unflappable and sharp. Was this how Ser Rhodin had got his audience? He knew your secretary?

“And would you say Ser Rhodin's words have been true, Cavalier an Vilius?” you asked, perfectly still. As if you were only inquiring about a matter of record.

“Yes, Glorious One,” said the Cavalier. His eyes flicked briefly in your direction, and he made a small bow in his seat.

Once again, you were left to weigh insufficient evidence. Against the Lord Treasurer, who you knew to be ruthless and twisted, was a complete unknown. Who asked for your secretary, your most unusually efficient and competent secretary, to stand as witness to his character. I knew little about the nearly silent man I worked with every day. This was the first time I had any inkling that he participated in the usual games; otherwise he had seemed remarkably interested in the good working of government.

Well. Perhaps you could try to fish out what they actually *wanted*. “And how would you request we intervene, Ser Rhodin?”

You could, of course, simply tell the Lord Treasurer he wasn't allowed to duel. That would lose him face in the sight of the court, and he would be resentful, and probably have Ser Rhodin killed quietly anyway. Forbidding Ser Rhodin from duelling would also not help, as then he wouldn't be allowed to fight back. Making it less of an order to protect him, and more of an amateur execution.

Ser Rhodin's eyes flicked to the Cavalier, who was carefully looking at his paper. You noticed he had a folder on his desk, much like the ones for policy proposals that came from your ministers.

“I dared to hope, O Protector of the People, that it might be possible for your generous self to prohibit duelling at the Palace of Stars,” said Ser Rhodin, quietly, now looking again at your hands.

“Would you happen to have prepared a briefing on such a possibility, Cavalier an Vilius?” you asked. I wanted to laugh my head off. This was a conspiracy? For what benefit? Was it purely altruistic? Surely not, perhaps the Cavalier knew the girl.

“Yes, Glorious One,” said the Cavalier. Your secretary stood, and bowed, and walked over the few paces to place the folder on the small table beside you.

You picked it up, and opened it. This was, you were certain, Sayo Mdang's handwriting. A conspiracy of three now. Well, four if you counted the girl. The document was short, and to the point, and very carefully worded. It was also devilishly clever. An outright ban would be hard to swallow, instead it treated it as a matter of decorum.

It was a good idea to ban duelling anyway. Moving towards a less violent court had been one of the hallmarks of your reign, even if you often felt it had achieved little otherwise. 

If Ser Rhodin had been less than honest with you, there was not a lot you could do. Perhaps ask the Guard to investigate? But they would likely hand it over to Internal Security, who would be no help. If only you dared ask more. If only you had people you could trust.

Well, the Cavalier and Sayo Mdang had shown themselves to be competent and organised. To notice discrepancies and bring them to your attention. Every daily summary had been scrupulously fair and accurate. If Sayo Mdang had planned this proposal, it would be worthwhile implementing regardless of Ser Rhodin.

You gave the tiniest smile, “Yes, very good. It seems high time such behaviour fell from style.”

“Yet,” you added, “Would this not put you out of work, Ser Rhodin?”

He bowed, still kneeling. “I begin training as one of your Imperial Guards tomorrow, Glorious One.”

Which would also handily place him out of reach of the Lord Treasurer. After all, there could be no *personal* attack on a guard. To do so was to implicitly attack the Emperor. And, of course, Ser Rhodin would be surrounded by some of the best guards in the nine worlds while the Lord Treasurer's temper was hottest. They had all done this very tidily. 

You would be interested to watch how Ser Rhodin did in the guard.

You hoped your secretaries would get involved in more similar conspiracies in future. It was the most interesting audience we held for some years.
