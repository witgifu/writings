When Kip saw Conju in the library, he could tell that the other man was not exactly _well_. But he had clearly got annoyed with simply lying in bed, a predicament Kip could sympathise with.

“Looking for a book?” said Kip, as he walked through the doorway.

“I suppose,” said Conju, trailing his fingers along the shelf. Then he turned around and smiled. “How did it go? Is there anything I can help with?”

Giving Conju a closer examination, Kip decided it was probably safe to let him write up notes. At least until he got tired, and Kip could chivvy him back to bed. He opened his mouth to accept, when Liliane walked in behind him, and bowed to them both.

“You're going to insist on lunch first,” said Conju, but he was still smiling.

“There's only these notes to write up,” said Kip, dropping them onto the desk, “It can wait until after lunch. They're not due until tomorrow morning.”

“Then I suppose we have no excuse,” said Conju. He turned, and brushed his shoulder against Kip's arm. Then lent into him, “Since we have all day and all night, my dear.”

Kip could sense the slight tension in Conju, who was obviously uncertain if Kip was comfortable with this change. Feeling daring, Kip pecked Conju on the cheek. “I do hope we have time for more than just work,” he said.

Conju's smile made him look nearly as bright as the Emperor. “As do I,” he said, and taking Kip's hand, pulled him along to the dinning room.

They ate lunch at a relaxed pace. Without saying anything, they agreed to leave discussion of the morning's events for afterwards. Instead, they traded stories about their friends and family back home. 

They also traded small touches, that could perhaps have been casual touches between friends and family, if Kip had been in the Vangaveye-ve. In Astandalas, in the Palace of Stars, he knew they were not. He was still not entirely sure exactly what Conju wanted, but did his best to relax and let the younger man take the lead.

When they returned to the library, and the pile of notes, Kip handed Conju the first third of the pile of notes.

“I'm not _that_ much slower than you,” said Conju, sounding a little hurt.

Kip smiled, “And I'm not going to risk Liliane's wrath by overtaxing you.”

“Fair," said Conju, with a wry smile.

They worked together in silence. Kip deeply appreciated that Conju was not the kind of man to need to constantly talk ask he worked. In nearly every office he'd ever been in, there'd been at least one person who never seemed to stop. It could make it hard to focus, and if he was really unlucky, that person picked Kip as someone who couldn't shoo them away.

When Conju finished, he yawned, and announced he would write a letter to his sister. Kip stole glances at him as he finished his own pile, but Conju never seemed to get further than the salutation. Stacking the notes in the correct order, Kip stood up and stretched.

“I'll find a page to take these to the Tower,” he said, carefully placing the pages into a dispatch box. “When I return, where shall I find you?”

With a sigh, Conju put away his pens and pencils. “Perhaps the baths?” he offered, looking up at Kip with an expression Kip couldn't quite parse.

Kip nodded, and hurried off to finish their last duty before the holiday.

He found Conju as promised, in the baths. But rather than having already made his way into the wash stations, he was sitting in the first room. On the edge of the bench, staring at the wall.

“Conju?” said Kip gently, “Are you feeling tired? Do you want to go back to bed?”

“Oh, I'm fine,” said Conju, far too quickly to be convincing. Also, he didn't move.

Kip walked over, and put a hand on Conju's arm. Then he squatted down, so his face was level with the other man's. He didn't say anything, just waited.

*Listen first, ask questions later.*

Conju sat there for a long time, saying nothing, mind clearly somewhere else. Finally, he said, “I can't fix the Empire for you, Cliopher.”

This wasn't at all what Kip had been expecting Conju to say. He had wondered if the trial was making Conju worried, or the Emperor's interest. This seemed like something else.

“I don't expect you to,” said Kip, gently.

“But,” said Conju, finally focusing on Kip's face, “It's not safe. You're in danger, and all I've done is put you in more danger.”

Kip wanted to say life wasn't going to be safe, that he didn't really want it to be so. Yet Conju knew that. Kip had told him about the journey to Astandalas, about diving for pearls, how his father had drowned. There was something he wasn't seeing.

“Did someone send another threatening letter?” Kip asked.

“No,” said Conju, tightly, “But they will, there will be someone else. Next time they'll be smarter, and I won't be able to ask the Emperor to intervene, and I don't actually have a powerful court faction backing me, and so they'll probably torture and kill you.”

Kip waited. None of that was new. Since the first day he'd been at the Palace it had been clear to him that he had been walking a fine line. He'd accepted the danger, in the hope that in risking himself he could help thousands of others. 

Conju was the one who had taken the dying embers of that hope and built them back into a warm fire. They *were* making change. Painfully, slowly, but meaningfully. The Emperor wanted to govern well, and they were enabling him. It wasn't exactly what he had dreamed of when he had seen the Emperor's portrait. It was real.

What, then, was Conju afraid of?

“I know, it might happen. Or it might not,” said Kip, softly.

“I'm supposed to protect you,” whispered Conju, “And I can't.”

“I was here for four years before I met you, Conju. You've given me hope when I had almost given up. I'm not leaving,” said Kip, reaching out his other hand to hold Conju's.

“*Noblesse oblige*,” muttered Conju.

Ah. This was a court culture issue, not just Conju's fear for Kip's safety.

“What does that mean?” he asked, as if this was one of the times he'd asked about dress codes.

“Service must be rewarded with protection,” said Conju, reciting, “It is that which binds the Empire. As Emperor to the Empire, so each head of household is to their family. Justice, protection, gifts, flow down. Service, honour, tithes, flow up.”

Kip's instinctive response was to say “Am I a servant to you?” but he bit it back. Now was not the time for witty banter. Although, perhaps it was the time for confession. *Did* Conju think of him that way? He had thought not, had thought— Well, he had always been bad at flirting, and seeing it.

“Is that… how you think… about us?” he asked, afraid for the answer. *Of course,* was the answer his own mind supplied, *you have offered your lord a service, and since he cannot protect you he is refusing. Be thankful he is doing it kindly.* It was the way the Palace worked, the way the Empire worked, as Conju had said.

Conju looked up, and shook his head. “No,” he said firmly, “No, but...”

Heart hammering in his ribs, Kip waited.

“There was someone, once,” said Conju, looking down at their clasped hands, “A, a friend. I couldn't protect him from the Empire either. He left.”

There was a world of pain in those last two words. Kip wondered about the friend, if it was the same one who was like a ghost in Conju's stories of his childhood and teen years.

“If I have to flee the Palace,” said Kip, unable to promise it wasn't a possibility, “You'll probably have to come with me.”

Conju blinked in surprise. “What?”

“Well, I wouldn't want to inconvenience your family by sending you back with a price on your head. But nobody in the Vangaveye-ve would mind,” said Kip, tilting his head, and smiling a little. “I know you think it's so uncivilised it's nearly out of the Empire, but really the only uncivilised part is The Poz.”

“I will never get over that he calls *himself* that,” said Conju, tartly.

“It would be up to you, of course, I wouldn't kidnap you,” said Kip, trying to insert more levity, “*We* do not need to stoop to such things.”

Conju snorted, and did smile genuinely. “Indeed,” he said, and squeezed Kip's hand. “*We* are competent at our work. Or at least you are, and I look fabulous.”

“You are a very good secretary,” said Kip, honestly, “And you do look fabulous.”

“Thank you, my dear,” said Conju, now with a slight quirk to his smile. “Let's get clean so we can relax properly.”

Kip stood up, pulling Conju with him. They stripped next to each other this time, in a comfortable silence. Then walked together into the washroom.

“Would you mind…?” said Conju, quietly, “Only I've been lying in my own sweat, and my back is *itchy*.”

“Not at all,” said Kip, picking up a scrubbing mitt. “You are sick, after all. You can return the favour when I get the cold.”

Conju made the sweetest puppy eyes, and Kip couldn't help giggling. “I am glad to see you feeling so much better,” he said, “Now which soap?”

“The lemon balm,” said Conju, without hesitation.

Kip sniffed along the bars until he found what he thought was the right one. He lathered it into the damp mitt, and then began on Conju's back.

The smaller man practically melted into him. Kip rubbed gently, making sure he covered everything. Then feeling that half done was easily undone, he moved onto Conju's arms.

“I can...” protested Conju weakly, looking up at Kip.

“I know,” said Kip, kissing him in the middle of one of the swirls of his tattoos, “Sometimes it's nice to do something for someone else.”

“You...” said Conju, with a sigh, “Are amazing.”

Kip smiled, “As are you, my Cavalier.”

“Are you mocking me, Sayo Mdang?” asked Conju.

“No,” said Kip softly, running the mitt up Conju's chest as he held him from behind, “I would never.”

“Yes you would!” protested Conju, twisting around to face Kip.

“Only a little bit,” said Kip, leaning down to touch noses.

They did, eventually finish washing. It took a lot longer than usual.
