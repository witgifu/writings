Kip was doing his best, he hoped. It had been a long few days, and he had been hoping he might be able to enter court unobtrusively and stand at the edge. Watch and listen for a couple of hours until he felt he had met the Glorious One's request to attend, and then go back to his apartment and sleep.

It was obvious as soon as he entered that he wasn't going to be able to do any of that. Even before the Emperor entered Kip was cornered by the Undersecretary for Tithes who seemingly just wanted to chat. She was followed by a whole stream of people, including a few of his previous bosses who all seemed slightly embarrassed.

Once the Emperor was there and the dancing had begun, Kip found himself between the Princes of Karthmoon and Princess Indrogan. He couldn't tell if they wanted him to adjudicate their decade old argument, or if it was just that neither of them wanted to be seen as the one who walked away. It was bizarre to be very nearly deferred to by two people who wouldn't even look at him six months ago.

Eventually they got invested enough that he was able to drift away. Only to find himself requested to dance by nearly everyone. He tried to decline carefully; he was genuinely tired but didn't want to offend. Listening to the bells and watching the Emperor, he tried to judge when he might be able to slip away.

So many people were set on talking at him (most of them still could not be said to be talking *to* him), that it wasn't until the Emperor rose from his throne that Kip finally felt he wasn't being watched. At his Radiancy's gesture of dismissal, Kip rose nearly indecently quickly, and hurried from the Throne Room. Only his guard shadow seemed to notice.

Kip went directly to the Offices of State, where he checked for anything new. There were no reports, but the Glorious One's diary showed that all tomorrow would now be taken up with the High Court. That must have been at the Emperor's request. Kip fretted, as he took the back stairs, that he might be called to give evidence. He'd never had reason to visit before, and had no idea how it worked.

As he approached the front door of the apartment, he noticed someone waiting there. But when they saw Kip approaching, they wandered off. Possibly, he thought, because there were two imposing Imperial Guards following him. They had been with him all day, and while they clearly tried to be unobtrusive to Kip, they made a lot of people nervous. Justifiably so, in Kip's opinion.

“You know, I'm not going to run away,” he said, finally snapping as they took up positions either side of the door. “I'll come without you dragging me if I'm summoned to testify.”

Both guards looked stoic. “We are assigned for your protection, sir,” said the shorter one, on the left.

Kip decided not to press the issue, and walked between them into the apartment. Lilliane appeared just as he entered.

“Is anything wrong?” Kip asked, worriedly, “Is Conju—”

“My lord is no worse,” interrupted Lilliane, “But he said he would like to see you as soon as you got back.”

“Of course,” said Kip, “I'll visit right away, and then hopefully he'll be able to get some sleep.”

“And yourself, sir?” asked Lilliane, knowingly.

“I need to read this report,” he said, waving it, “In case there's anything truly urgent in it.”

Lilliane gave him a withering look, but let him go to Conju without further comment.

Conju was in his bed, propped up on pillows, with a magazine in his lap. But as Kip stood in the doorway, he could see that Conju wasn't really looking at it. He was blinking slowly, looking out the window at the night rain.

“I'm back,” said Kip, and pushed the door shut behind himself.

Conju smiled, then sneezed, coughed, and slumped down. “You're allowed to go get changed, Cliopher.” But he smiled again to soften it.

Kip smiled back. “I'll leave this report here as a promise of my swift return, then.”

When he came back, in his night clothes and comfortable dressing down, he got another watery smile from Conju. 

“Even that old dressing gown is better for your complexion than the uniform,” said Conju, patting the bed beside him.

Kip retrieved the report, and climbed up next to Conju. He began explaining the evening. 

Conju rolled his eyes when Kip said what he hoped would happen at court, “My dear, we are far too important to be left to ourselves.”

Kip winced, “Is that why the guards are following me like I'm one of the Lords Magi?”

“Not at court, I hope?” asked Conju, patting Kip's hand.

“Oh no,” said Kip, “Just about everywhere else. Of course not into the Imperial Apartments. And not into the Ministerial Dining Room, but then I was with the Deputy Commander of the Guard.”

Conju looked at his bedroom door suspiciously.

“And not into your apartment, of course,” added Kip.

“*Our* apartment,” corrected Conju, rubbing his nose, “I do hope, Cliopher, that you consider this place as much yours as mine. I know it will never be *home* the way Gorjo City is.”

Kip blushed. “I do, I just… I wasn't sure what you wanted me to say around other people. And I find it easier if I don't have to switch terms. I've never heard any commoner who lives with their noble patron say it like that. It's always 'my lord's apartment' or 'my lady's town house'.”

Conju was the one to break eye contact for once, although he at least pretended to be fishing around in the sheets for his handkerchief. “I hope you don't feel you have to… I'm not trying to buy you.”

“No,” said Kip firmly, taking both Conju's hands now, “But what we are in private, and how you want people to perceive us… There's a lot more people looking than I ever expected.”

With a wince, Conju said, “That's true. I'm sorry, I don't know. I want you to be safe, and happy.”

“I am,” said Kip, squeezing Conju's hands, and then passing him the handkerchief, “And I want the same for you.”

Conju sniffed, but smiled, “So what *did* happen at court then?”

By the time Kip finished retelling the night, Conju really was looking quite sleepy. He smiled, and picked up the report, glad to see that Conju was starting to look like he was feeling better. A quick skim of the document showed it was unlikely to need urgent action, so he settled in for a detailed read rather than fetching any writing implements.

Kip woke to the feeling of lips brushing the back of his hand. He blinked his eyes open in the pre-dawn light. In front of him, Conju was gently holding his right hand, looking surprisingly vulnerable.

“I was awake,” whispered Conju, “And I wasn't sure if you needed to update your morning summary?”

The question flung Kip's mind from the warm hand holding his to the document he'd seemingly shoved to the bottom of the bed. He lent up a bit to look at it.

“No need,” he said, and slumped back down, “It's mostly a long excuse for a lack of progress on a dam.”

“Oh,” said Conju, with a small smile, “Then I'm sorry I woke you early.”

Kip blinked, and thought again that the report looked quite tidy for having been shoved around in his sleep. And Conju was still holding his hand. A memory stirred from the first night Kip had slept in Conju's bed.

*Kip asked, “Have you been flirting with me?”*

*“Hmm? Oh, only outrageously since the day we met.”*

Oh. Kip appreciated how gentle Conju had been. Any other patron would have just ordered him into his bed. Had done so, in fact. One of his previous bosses had been well-known for 'trying out' every new person who came into his office. Kip had been thankful he hadn't been chosen as part of his coterie of *favourite* staff.

No, Kip put those thoughts aside. Conju wasn't *like* that. Just the previous night he'd nervously checked that Kip felt safe in this apartment. *Their* apartment. He could probably have continued to feign ignorance of what Conju was suggesting, and been safe.

For the first time in Astandalas, someone was genuinely *asking* him. He nearly wept in relief, and only managed to hold it back knowing that Conju would take it entirely the wrong way. Since they had moved in together, Conju hadn't brought back anyone else (except *perhaps* Ser Rhodin). Nor had he spent the night away. He'd been content, it seemed, to wait for Kip.

Truly, Conju was his greatest friend. Never had anyone other than Ghilly treated Kip with this kind patience. And if his friend wanted something, and Kip didn't mind giving it? Well.

Kip looked into Conju's uncertain eyes, and gently grasped his hand. Then drew it back towards himself, as he slid a bit closer. Tentatively, he returned the soft kiss Conju had used to wake him.

Conju's breath hitched. “Are you sure, Cliopher? You don't have to, we can just be friends.”

Every time Kip had expected Conju to turn away, to hurt him, he had not. Still, his friend was worried. He tried to think of something he could say that was reassuring, and also true. It was hard, before coffee.

“I'm not sure that I can love you like you deserve,” whispered Kip.

Conju's face lit up, and he said, “Oh, Cliopher,” with such fondness that Kip blushed furiously. Then Conju lent over, and kissed the very tip of Kip's nose. 

“You know I would never want to come between you and your calling to be perfectly honest,” he said, and then added, “Or to serve the Emperor, of course.”

“Of course,” echoed Kip, trying to work out if Conju was teasing.

“Good,” said Conju, and kissed Kip firmly on the lips.

Kip pressed back, enjoying himself. He never dreamed of kissing, never sought it out, but it did feel good. So much sensation, rippling through his mind and body. It was like sailing, but with someone else rather than the wind.

Of course, time conspired against them. The dawn bells rang, and they broke apart suddenly like teens caught by their parents. Conju groaned, and buried his face in the gap between their pillows. 

“There's no court tonight,” Kip reminded him, as the echoes of the bells died away, “So we can stay in all evening. And all day tomorrow.”

Conju got up, looking baffled, and shuffled over to his little shrine. “Why?”

“It's Silverheart tomorrow,” said Kip, with a grin, “And rather than favouring one person by going to their party, I thought we could slight them all and stay here.”

“Normally I would argue,” said Conju, lighting a fresh stick of incense. He waved it in some complex and no doubt meaningful pattern, before putting it in the holder, and clapping three times. “But I'm too tired,” he finished, “And certainly not up to attending the Prince of Karthmoon's party in the city.”

Kip rolled out of bed, and padded over to Conju. “Then I will see you at breakfast,” he said, and gave Conju a quick peck on the cheek, “And again after work.”

When he turned to close Conju's door behind himself, Kip spotted the other man with his hand raised to his cheek, mouth slack, but smiling. He was glad he could make his friend so happy.
