They had sat down to a relaxed luncheon. Cliopher was trying to get bites of grilled vegetables into his own mouth, around Aurenia who sat on his lap. She seemed entirely uninterested in the cold taro she had been given, and very interested in Cliopher's spiced eggplant and water spinach.

Rhodin kept making faces at Aurenia, which she giggled at. This was hampering his progress with his own meal, but he did not seem to mind. However, this behaviour gave someone for Conju to sniff at — since he seemed to have discerned that disapproval of Aurenia would meet with disapproval from their lord.

At a lull in this action, his Radiancy said to the table, “We should return to Solaara.”

Cliopher looked up from trying to spot where Aurenia had dropped her last bit of taro. “Of course, my lord,” he said automatically, “We are supposed to send word to Captain Achillon with Balgo from the village, but he has not yet returned at all. Shall I send someone to ask who else has a boat that can cross the Bay?”

His Radiancy looked out the window to the flock of parrots being noisy outside the dining room. “We will send a message another way. Is there anyone else you might need to send a message to in Gorjo City, Cliopher?”

Cliopher looked down at Aurenia. “I think I might need to explain in person, my lord.” He looked up again, to see his lord also looking at Aurenia. “I am hoping, if they are acceptable to you, my lord, I might be able to convince one of my family to come to Solaara for a time to help look after Aurenia.”

With an inquiring eyebrow, Conju looked at Cliopher. “You aren't seriously going to try to fit three people in your little rooms, are you Cliopher?”

Before Cliopher had a chance to answer, his Radiancy did. “Of course not, Cliopher will take up the Lord of Zunidth's apartments. Conju, fetch me paper and pen and some string. Rhodin, open the window.”

Then, to the surprise of everyone, his Radiancy wrote a short note, and sent it with one of the parrots.

While Aurenia had an afternoon nap, Cliopher discussed with Mikiya the logistics of taking Aurenia on the boat.

“She will probably sleep the whole time,” said Mikiya, “I've known parents who could only get their children to sleep by taking them out on a vaha.”

Cliopher considered that perhaps Aurenia might react more like his lord had on the way over. “Do babies ever get seasick?” he asked, slightly worried.

“Not Islander babies,” said Mikiya, “but it's not very long if she does the first time.”

Cliopher nodded, and then considered the next problem. “It is long enough she'll need feeding on the boat… I'm not sure if I can get her to eat enough solids to fill her up.”

“I'll give Loven my old recipe for a percent formula — it's not to be used all the time, mind! — but it's fine for occasionally if you don't have anything else. Breast-milk or the formula the hospital imports from Ysthar is better.” Mikiya gave Cliopher a piercing look.

“Understood,” said Cliopher, “I will find some Ystharian formula as soon as we arrive in Gorjo City, and follow the directions.”

“Now, you should go and talk to your man.”

Cliopher balked. The Lord of Rising Stars was not *his man*, he was *his lord*. And yet, if they were to raise a child (together?) there were some things they would have to discuss. 

He sighed, and nodded to Mikiya. Taking the sleeping Aurenia in her bilum, he went to find his lord.

Did his Radiancy want her to be kept secret, like many old nobles had? Until this holiday his lord had never spoken of his childhood. Did he expect corporal punishment? Would he want her tutored privately or sent to school?

These were questions Uncle Lazo would advise young people to talk about *before* they had children. But, well, Cliopher would just have to do his best.

Cliopher found his lord on the verandah, watching the water. Cliopher bowed, and his Radiancy gave him a searching look. “Let us go for a walk,” his Radiancy said.

“Of course, my lord,” said Cliopher, and stepped down onto the path.

The two of them strolled down the beach, Aurenia sleeping happily on Cliopher's back, the guards following.

“Do you have a protocol for this,” said his Radiancy, as he waved to Aurenia.

“Not precisely, my lord,” said Cliopher, “I do have one for the arrival of unexpected Imperial relations but it, somewhat naïvely I now see, assumed they would be adults.”

“Yes, I'm not sure how...” began his Radiancy, but he didn't finish the thought. Instead, he switched topics. “I do not want her childhood to be like mine.”

“In what ways, my lord?”

“Any of them, all of them.”

Cliopher considered for a little while the conversation they had earlier, sitting on the sandbar. It seemed like a world away. Surely there had been something good in his lord's young life?

“You would not want her raised by a nurse or a nanny then?” he asked, tentatively.

His Radiancy's steady stride faltered slightly. “I would prefer not. But there is so much work for us to do. You mentioned one of your family might help at first?”

“Often, in Gorjo City, if both parents work, the grandparents, or other family members will help out. I am hoping some of my family will be willing to come to Solaara, at least until she is old enough for school.”

His Radiancy stopped, and looked at Cliopher. “They would do that?”

“I think so, my lord, but it will take some explaining and organising. Would you...” Cliopher trailed off, unsure how to phrase his question. He tried a different approach. “My lord, I am not sure of the correct way to introduce my family to you.”

His Radiancy raised one eyebrow. “How would your family expect you to introduce your *fanoa* and daughter? If it were someone else.”

“I would take them to visit my mother, and introduce them to her. Then she would invite over a good number of my uncles and aunts to meet them. But, my lord, ... “

He could not introduce Lord of Rising Stars, the Lord Magus of Zunidh, the *Last Emperor of Astandalas*, to his mother. He might be very bad at etiquette, but even Cliopher knew the correct way would be for his mother to be brought and introduced to his Radiancy. His mind started working through a plan of taking over Orianna's palace, and using her throne room.

But his Radiancy said, “I should like that.”

Cliopher had to wind his mind back, hurriedly. “You would, my lord?” he said, his voice only squeaking very slightly, “That is, thank you, it would be an enormous honour to introduce my family to you.”

His Radiancy nodded. “I may be indisposed when we first arrive, the day after would be best.”

“Of course, my lord,” replied Cliopher, now thinking of where in Gorjo City (apart from the palace), could possibly host the Emperor-in-disguise. He would need to talk with Conju and Ludvic.

At that moment Aurenia woke up, disgruntled. Cliopher realised he hadn't brought anything he needed to change her. Should he run back to the house? They had walked a fair way down the beach. He couldn't smell anything yet.

“Excuse me, my lord,” he said, feeling awkward, “I think Aurenia wants the toilet.”

His Radiancy looked around, confused. “Oh?” he said, “Can I help?”

This stumped Cliopher for a moment. If his lord was going to parent Aurenia, that might someday include changing her. Even if, at the moment, he couldn't touch. But Cliopher was going to have to do this a more traditional way. Well, Aurenia came before his pride.

“I'm not sure, my lord, I'm still new to this as well. Let me see...” he began walking up the beach and peering into the bush. He spotted what he was looking for immediately, a purple-red plant that grew velvet-soft leaves. 

Checking there were no food plants around, he got Aurenia out of the bilum. She was truly crying now. Remembering the wonderful magic that had entranced the market, Cliopher asked, “My lord, would you be able to distract her with an illusion?”

His Radiancy smiled, “I can try,” he offered. He waved his hand, and a golden butterfly appeared in front of Aurelia's nose. She was briefly confused while she struggled to focus on it, and then burbled with delight.

Relieved, Cliopher picked a selection of the soft leaves. Having laid his collection next to Aurelia, he quickly unwrapped her, and then snatched her up and held her away under another shrub.

Entranced by the butterfly, it took Aurelia a while to notice she was free of the napkin. She let loose, still waving her hands at the illusion. While she went, Cliopher snuck a peek at the napkin. It was blessedly clean. Relieved, he wiped her down with the leaves and re-wrapped her. 

Back in the bilum, she continued to try and catch the butterfly, which would flit just out of reach as her hands drew close. This didn't seem to bother her, and she said “eeei” to it every time it got away.

“I should go back to the house, my lord,” said Cliopher, apologetically, “I should not have taken her out without more supplies.”

His Radiancy looked curious. “You seemed perfectly confident with the task, Cliopher. What else did you need?”

Cliopher knew he was blushing. “Well, my lord, one usually uses damp cloths, rather than leaves. And I should have had a clean napkin in case she soiled it.”

His Radiancy nodded, and Cliopher suddenly wondered if he'd ever actually seen a baby before. Prior to the Fall, aristocrats had kept all their children hidden until they were nine. Afterwards, that had been relaxed, and the Emperor had more exposure to commoners. But still, possibly not. Maybe he should mention a few things, just in case his lord didn't know.

His Radiancy was watching Aurelia play with the butterfly. He smiled, and it wasn't his usual serene and benevolent smile, but the genuine joy he had shown flashes of on this vacation. 

Cliopher dove in. “Mikiya has kindly been feeding her, as she's still too young to eat just solids. When we arrive in Gorjo City, I'll take her to the hospital to get a checkup, and get infant formula. I don't believe wet-nursing is much done in Solaara anymore, my lord, but I could make enquiries if you would prefer?”

“I would be pleased with whatever you prefer, or the hospital recommends, Cliopher,” said his lord, still watching Aurelia.

“Of course, my lord,” said Cliopher, now heading in to murkier waters, “Do you have any ideas for who would make appropriate playmates? Would you prefer she only play with other girls, or only the children of… of the Princes?”

His Radiancy stopped, and stared at Cliopher. Before this vacation, if his Radiancy had done that, Cliopher would have panicked and fallen to the floor. But he thought, he hoped, this was something he could ask now.

“I have no idea,” said his Radiancy, “Is that common? It seems rather restrictive.”

“I know some of the Princes would be horrified to learn their children were playing with those of the palace staff,” said Cliopher, cautiously.

“I don't think it would be a good idea to take parenting advice from Prince Rufus,” said his Radiancy dryly. In a warmer tone, he added, “I would be pleased if you,” he paused for just a breath, and corrected himself, “if we, could raise her in your culture, Cliopher. As you would your own natural child.”

“There are no songs, or stories, of yours that you would like to share with her?” asked Cliopher, uncertain if his Radiancy truly meant what he was saying.

A look that Cliopher had no reference for flashed across his Radiancy's face. Was it fear? Or pain? What horrors of his childhood had he kept from Cliopher?

“I am sure all the best of Shaian culture was banned by the censors,” said his Radiancy, quietly.

“Do you mean _Aurora_, my lord? Have you been enjoying it?” asked Cliopher, nervously. He did not want to mention how many of Fitzroy Angursell's songs he knew. “If you would rescind the ban I would be happy to teach her,” and then realising he probably should not admit to treason directly, added, “I learnt it before the ban of course.”

“Of course,” said his Radiancy.

Cliopher wished very much that his lord would clarify if he meant Aurelia should be taught _Aurora_, or if he was agreeing that Cliopher would not have engaged in avoiding the censors. He felt a certain book stored in his writing kit was practically shinning like a beacon.

“You are enjoying it?” Cliopher decided to offer, as a perhaps a slightly less controversial question.

“It appears to be an annotated edition,” his Radiancy replied, calmly, “the comments are indeed amusing.”

There was no way to avoid it, Cliopher was going to have to ask. “Do you think it should still be prohibited, my lord?”

“Oh, probably not,” his Radiancy said, in an offhand way. Then he abruptly changed the topic. “Tell me, Cliopher, what are the significant dates in a child's life here?”
