As soon as Cliopher reached the top of the Light, Aurenia started fussing. He hurried across the bridge to the sky ship, for the first time not thinking at all of the Grey Mountains. Instead, he made straight for the bathroom.

However, Aurenia showed no interest in the toilet. Not was she interested in a bottle, or food, when he went to the kitchen to try those. Cliopher eventually gave up, and carried her into the Sun Cabin. 

She would not let him put her in a bilum, or down on the bed. Each time he did she started crying. His Radiancy even tried the butterfly illusion, but to no avail.

“Are you teething, little one?” he asked her, as he held her close, the only way that seemed to keep her quiet. “Or do you not like the air up here?”

Conju shot Cliopher a disbelieving look. 

“I know she can't answer, Conju,” said Cliopher.

“It's important for children's development that their parents talk to them, and include them in discussions,” said his Radiancy, sipping his tea. He had insisted on reading the parenting books that Cliopher's family had given them.

Aurenia didn't say anything, just tucked her head into Cliopher's neck.

Cliopher stroked her back. “We should probably start making plans for our return, my lord,” he said, “but I don't know if Aurenia will let me write.” 

Conju disappeared into his Radiancy's dressing room. 

Internally, Cliopher sighed. It was the first time he had seriously thought about work since Aurenia had arrived, and he was already having to choose between her and what his Radiancy needed him to do. Trying to support her with just one arm, Cliopher pulled his writing kit towards himself, and opened it. Then he realised he had done so with his left arm, and of course he would need his right to actually write anything.

As Cliopher started trying to shuffle her to his other arm, Conju returned. He had the baby carrier. “I may not know anything about children,” he said stiffly, “but I have seen enough people in Solaara wearing these that I can probably help you. Even if crimson _does_ clash with your orange tunic.”

After a protracted negotiation between Aurenia, Cliopher, and Conju, Aurenia was finally tied onto Cliopher's chest. This gave Cliopher both hands free. He finally set up his writing supplies, and looked over at his lord. His Radiancy was reading another parenting book.

Unwilling to disturb his Radiancy, Cliopher began taking notes for himself. The old notebooks where he had jotted his ideas would have more details, but they were in Solaara. Plus he would need to consult with many members of his staff. However, since he had neither to hand, he started on what came first to his mind. The stipend.

After Cliopher had made a good many pages of notes, he looked up to see his Radiancy watching him.

“My apologies, my lord,” he said, and pulled a clean sheet towards himself. “Where would you like to start?”

“We will need a proclamation announcing you as Lord Chancellor, and to organise an investiture,” began his Radiancy, and Cliopher's pen dashed across the page in scribal shorthand.

That night, Aurenia still refused to be separated from Cliopher. She would sleep in his arms, and in the baby carrier. But if he gave her to Rhodin, or Aunt Oura, or put her down anywhere, she cried. 

Well after midnight, he tried to put her in the bilum for the fifth time since sundown. She cried almost immediately. Cliopher also needed to sleep, and he was terrified he might smother her accidentally if he shared his bed with her. At least when she wanted to be held so close. He had just resolved to sit up in the chair and doze through the night, when his Radiancy looked up from near the end of the parenting book.

“One of these books says you should let a child cry themselves to sleep,” said his Radiancy. 

Cliopher looked at his lord, making no effort to disguise his horror. What an abominable thing to do to a baby! He couldn't believe anyone in his family had taken that advice seriously. He stroked Aurenia's head.

“This one advocates for parents taking shifts,” said his Radiancy. He looked calm, but Cliopher could see the regret he was trying to hide. Until Aurenia was purified, or his Radiancy released from the taboos, they all knew it was impossible.

“Did they examine her, Cliopher?” asked his Radiancy, putting the book down, “At the hospital?” he clarified.

“Yes, my lord,” said Cliopher, and tucked his chin to see as much of Aurenia's head as he could. “They said she is within normal ranges for her presumed age, and seemed to be a very lively and happy baby.”

“And did they say she was teething?” his Radiancy asked, “The books say teething happens in spurts.”

“No, my lord,” said Cliopher, “And they did do a very thorough exam, because they were worried that she might have been… might nave not had the best start in life.”

There was a pause. Cliopher wondered, not for the first time, what Aurenia's life with her mother had been like. Who had fed her, cleaned her, played with her? Was there someone in the Divine Realms worried about Aurenia? 

“With your permission, Cliopher, I would like to examine her magically,” said his Radiancy. “I am sure the hospital staff are excellent, but I think it unlikely they have seen many of those begat by the gods.”

“Of course!” said Cliopher, worried that he hadn't though of this before, “Please do so, my lord, at any time.”

His Radiancy nodded, and Cliopher noticed the light rise in his eyes. His lord was focused on Aurenia, in Cliopher's arms, and not on Cliopher this time, so he did not feel so _weighed down_. It was beautiful, to see his lord working magic.

“Hmm,” said his Radiancy, “I am not sure… Conju?”

“Yes, my lord?” said Conju, emerging from behind a screen.

“We will need to trance. It should only be short, so if we are gone more than a bell, please wake us,” said his Radiancy, and lay down on his bed.

“Of course, my lord,” replied Conju, and bustled out of the cabin door.

Cliopher, uncertain if he was allowed to move, lent back gently in his chair. He stroked Aurenia's fuzzy head, and quietly hummed some of the lays, to keep himself awake.

Conju returned shortly with a covered tray. Then he began setting out tea things. Would it disturb his Radiancy, Cliopher wondered, if he talked to Conju? He wasn't sure, and Conju moved silently, so Cliopher just hummed.

It was far short of half a bell when his Radiancy began to stir. Conju immediately poured hot water into the teapot, releasing a wonderful fragrance. It made Cliopher smile, even though he had no reference for it.

Conju bustled over to the bed with the tray and tea, uncovering a selection of small sweetmeats. Eventually, his Radiancy blinked his eyes open.

“There you are, my lord,” said Conju, with such affection that Cliopher felt perhaps he was intruding.

“Mmm,” said his Radiancy, and let out a long, slow, sigh. He picked up the cup of tea, and breathed in the scent.

“Oh!” said his Radiancy, suddenly sitting up, and looking much more alert. He waved his hand, in a similar but not quite the same way as he had summoned the illusions. At the same time, Aurenia stirred in Cliopher's arms. “Sorry Aurenia,” said his Radiancy, “I don't really like the schooled magic either, hopefully that feels better.”

Conju made a hushing gesture to Cliopher behind his back. With a comparatively tiny gesture, he drew their lord's attention to the tray.

His Radiancy picked up one of the squares, and chewed on it slowly, relaxing back into the cushions.

Cliopher looked down at Aurenia, and she looked up at him. She yawned, and made a small snuffling noise that Cliopher could only think of as adorable. Slowly, he lay her down in his arms, and transferred her to the bilum.

This time, she didn't immediately cry. She stuck her fist in her mouth, and closed her eyes. Cliopher breathed out deeply, waiting for her to change her mind. She wriggled a bit, but didn't begin to fuss, and didn't make a sound.

Amazed, Cliopher turned back to his Radiancy. 

“It’s the schooled magic in the Lights. Aurenia is a wild mage, and it feels… uncomfortable. Itchy. To float along in it,” said his Radiancy, looking at the ceiling. “I should have realised earlier… she should be fine now.”

Conju turned away from his Radiancy, and Cliopher could see the pain clear on Conju’s face. The memory of Conju explaining what had happened to his childhood friend surfaced in Cliopher’s mind. He didn’t want to leave his friend in doubt.

“How, my lord?” whispered Cliopher, “Did you modify the Lights?”

“No,” said his Radiancy, “That would have… repercussions. I have wrapped her in my magic. Because the Lights are hybrid…” He turned his head, and saw Conju carefully looking at the wall. “Nobody could have done something like it before the fall,” he amended, “even now it will take me… you should sleep Cliopher… I can probably keep it up until dawn…”

Now it was Cliopher’s turn to receive Conju’s ire. Without saying a word, Cliopher rushed through his ablutions and into bed. He could only hope that Conju would feel able to sleep as well, but he suspected only he and Aurenia would have a peaceful night.

Cliopher woke just before dawn, when Aurenia began fussing. As silently as possible, he pulled her out of the bilum. She quieted immediately. He looked around the room. His Radiancy was asleep, and so was Conju. The guards gave no indication they thought anything amiss.

Now that she was awake, Cliopher was sure that Aurenia would want food or toilet soon. Grabbing a bag of supplies he left for the common areas, hoping to let his Radiancy and Conju sleep for at least a couple of hours.

Cliopher and Aurenia had breakfast, and Aurenia had a bath. He was allowed to eat some of his own breakfast, and drank rather more of Aurenia's bathwater than he would have liked. When they were done, Cliopher and Aurenia sat on the deck together, and watched the sun rise.

It was peaceful, now. Floating in the sky, above the worries of his family, of Zunidh. Aurenia curling into him, as if they had known each other for months, rather than days.

It was going to be madness when they got back. His Radiancy had already pointed out that Cliopher wouldn't have time to be his secretary. They would also need to think about the other roles. It was all well and good for Cliopher to be up before dawn and in bed after midnight when he was only responsible for himself. But he didn't want to be an absent father. There should also be time for his lord to spend with Aurenia.

Reluctantly, he came to the conclusion that he would have to give up much of his current day-to-day work. But if it meant Aurenia could have a family, if it meant his Radiancy could know his daughter, if they could free Zunidh from the ashes of the Empire, it would be worthwhile.

Conju found him, a couple of hours later. “He's awake,” was all Conju said, as he strode past to the kitchen.

Cliopher pried himself up, and returned to the Sun Cabin. His Radiancy was sitting on the balcony, admiring the view, sipping a cup of tea. He looked majestic, Cliopher thought, against the blue sky, and hazy distant horizon.

His lord waved for Cliopher to join, and so he did, taking the other seat and re-settling Aurenia. The guards were inside, Conju was absent. This was likely the most privacy they would have in months, possibly years. 

“Cliopher,” began his Radiancy, and paused. 

For Aurenia, Cliopher tried to still his heart, which beat faster for every moment the silence extended.

“Cliopher,” his Radiancy tried again, “What do you want Aurenia to call you?” He wasn't looking at Cliopher, he seemed to be watching the empty sky.

“I hadn't thought about it, my lord,” said Cliopher, “I would feel strange if she called me 'papa', but she could not do the same to you.”

“I do not want her to call me 'my lord',” said his Radiancy, quietly.

Cliopher could only imagine that the controlled flat tone was hiding deep emotion. Had his Radiancy ever even met his own father? What had he called him?

“Nor any of my other supposedly ten thousand titles. But,” said his Radiancy, and now Cliopher could hear him breathing heavily, “She cannot call me papa, or father, or…”

“In Cisven,” said Cliopher gently, “Where families are often blended and complicated, most children use their parent's names. Or nicknames.” He stroked Aurenia, who did not seem inclined to do anything more than sleep on him.

When his lord did not respond, Cliopher continued. “Would you like to be Tor, my lord? And I could be Kip.”

His Radiancy looked at Cliopher, his eyes so bright that Cliopher though for a fleeting moment that the Sun had finally come to take his lord. But no, he was just happy.

“Yes,” said his Radiancy, “Thank you. We will have to still use titles much of the time, much as I know you would enjoy scandalising the Council of Princes. But when it is just us…”

“When it is just our family,” said Cliopher, and then realised what he'd said. But it was true, wasn't it? They were a kind of family. Every family grew and changed with time, children left home, people had work that kept them apart. So he forced himself to continue. “Then you and Aurenia can call me Kip.”

“And you and Aurenia can call me Tor,” said his Radiancy.

“Thank you, my—Tor?” said Cliopher, softly, uncertain.

His Radiancy, Tor, smiled at Cliopher. And for the first time, Cliopher didn't think about the problems they were walking into, raising Aurenia together. Just how much joy filled him, being with them both.
