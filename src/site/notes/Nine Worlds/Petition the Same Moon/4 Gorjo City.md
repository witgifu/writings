They were walking along the beach, his Radiancy in his wizard disguise. Cliopher had a vague guilty feeling that he should talk to Uncle Lazo, or maybe one of the *ela*. In case they had any particular insights on raising a half-divine child. He was certainly not going to consult the Ouranatha on such a topic.

They had to walk well above the high-tide mark to go around and outcropping of rock, and Cliopher wondered how resistant to sand the wizard outfit was. But then his Radiancy slowed and stopped in front of them. Cliopher checked on Aurenia (smiling happily at Rhodin behind her), before looking up to see who or what had stalled the walk.

Buru Tovo and his boyfriend were walking towards them. Cliopher stepped up beside his lord to make introductions.

“My lord, this is my Buru Tovo, and his partner,” Cliopher bowed over his hands at the couple, suddenly awkward that he couldn't remember his great-uncle's partner's name.

“Buru Tovo, uncle, this is my *fanoa*, and,” he moved the bilum around to his front so they could see, “my daughter Aurenia.”

Buru Tovo gave his Radiancy a thorough inspection. “Not bad,” he said, “Does this mean you're here to stay?”

“Not yet, Buru Tovo,” said Cliopher, wondering how many times he would give this answer, “in a few years.”

“Our apologies that we cannot return your nephew sooner,” said his Radiancy. 

“Your daughter?” said Buru Tovo's partner, looking from Aurenia, to Cliopher, to his Radiancy, and then back to the baby. “Yeah, her mother can be like that. Bit cold, for one who claims all lovers.” He then bumped his hip against Buru Tovo, in a way that made Cliopher feel even more awkward.

His Radiancy was watching Buru Tovo's partner with a curious intensity. “You seem to also be… acquainted… with the lady. Do you think her likely to return for her daughter?”

At the question, Cliopher instinctively wrapped an arm around Aurenia. Of course, her mother did have such a right. And he could do nothing to stop her if she did. Given the way she had practically dumped her child on a stranger, he had thought it unlikely.

Buru Tovo's partner gave his familiar kookaburra laugh. “I think she's had her fill of children for a good many years,” was all he said.

Cliopher wondered who exactly he was, to know the Moon Lady so well. Perhaps one of the *ela*, or a lore scholar.

His Radiancy nodded politely.

“You know the talk I give new parents, Kip,” said Buru Tovo, “You asked your mother for help?”

“Yes, Buru Tovo,” said Cliopher, hoping he wasn’t going to feel the need to include the part of the talk about making sure you give yourself time to recover before trying for more kids.

Buru Tovo nodded. “You going to live with Eidora when you come back for good?”

Cliopher stared at Buru Tovo and then belatedly looked to his lord. He hadn’t even thought about it. Only hours ago his Radiancy had said he would move to the Vangaveye-ve after retiring. They’d had no time, and Cliopher had not been brave enough, to ask exactly what that might mean.

“Something to talk about then, hmm?” said Buru Tovo, looking between the two of them.

That was a much easier question. “Yes, Buru Tovo,” replied Cliopher.

Then Buru Tovo turned to his Radiancy, and Cliopher braced himself for his great uncle's treason. “Now,” Buru Tovo began, smiling at his Radiancy, “I met your uncle once, and you don't seem much like him. So you get the other talk. Family is also about choosing, yes? Aurenia's mother sent her away, chose not to be her family. You chose our Kip, to be his family.”

His Radiancy inclined his head, and to Cliopher's surprise, his lord's full court mask was on. But then he remembered again what his lord had said on that sandbar. How his Radiancy's blood family had abandoned him, and then locked him away, until he was suddenly needed at thirty. No, it wasn't surprising.

Buru Tovo raised an eyebrow at his Radiancy's reaction, but lets it go. Cliopher did as well. It wasn't as if they were really *fanoa*. It would just be until his Radiancy retired, and could claim Aurenia as his own. Cliopher was glad his lord wanted to live in the Vangaveye-ve, it would make visiting them both much easier. If his lord would like that.

They continued their walk down the beach, in silence. Cliopher wondered how to ask Ludvic, Rhodin, and Conju if they would like to retire with him. Even if they declined, it might be good to buy a house, so they could visit. He had plenty of money.

Every one of Cliopher's aunts, uncles, and cousins that lived in Gorjo City wanted to be introduced to Aurenia, as soon as they heard about her. It felt disconcertingly like being at court, as relative after relative just happened to pop by wherever they were. They were interrupted at restaurants, shops, walking in the streets. Not to mention all the people who dropped in to Eidora's house.

Cliopher started treasuring the time they spent at the Dolphin Hotel. It wasn't that he didn't want to see his family, but all this attention was a bit much. He desperately hoped his lord did not mind. They could have left and sent the sky ship back for Aunt Oura, but Cliopher worried about negotiating his first week of work without family support.

On their third day in Gorjo City, Aurenia woke Cliopher at dawn. He got her ready for another day of being passed around and coddled, and went into the dining room of the suite to get himself some breakfast.

His Radiancy was having breakfast, with Conju serving him. Holding Aurenia, Cliopher bowed, and then hovered awkwardly. Just as he decided he should go, his Radiancy said, “I would be pleased if all three of you would join me.”

“Thank you, my lord,” said Cliopher. 

Unwilling to ignore what amounted to a direct instruction, Conju fetched himself a sweet bun. He sat nearest his Radiancy, and picked tiny bits off the bun.

Once Cliopher had Aurenia arranged on his lap, he peeled a banana and handed her a chunk. She squished it between her fingers. 

“It's food, Aurenia, not hand cream,” said Conju, as Cliopher popped a chunk into his own mouth.

His Radiancy smiled a little at the exchange. “I will miss this, when we are back in Solaara,” he said quietly.

Cliopher was surprised by the sentiment and the candour. “I would be happy to join you for a meal any time, my lord. Although I would not recommend inviting Aurenia if you are eating with the court.” He looked down at her, as she banged her banana covered hand on the table, “Her table manners need some work yet.”

“Eeeei,” said Aurenia, “Baaa!”

“Yes, banana is delicious,” said Cliopher, “Would you like to try eating some?” This time he held a small piece up to her mouth, rather than putting it into her hand. She sucked on it a little, seemingly happy.

Cliopher could see Conju was carefully keeping comments about Aurenia to himself. He would not be looking forward to extracting food from all the Tower's beautiful white linens.

“Perhaps on the terrace, my lord? It will be easier if Aurenia persists in these… experiments,” suggested Conju. 

Would Conju, Rhodin, and Ludvic join them, Cliopher wondered. He expected not, but he hoped that sometimes it might be possible.

His Radiancy smiled a little, watching Aurenia. “Yes, for your sake at least, Conju,” he said.

“I had also wondered,” said Cliopher, being brave for Aurenia, “If you might like to help with bed times, my lord? Schedules permitting, of course. You could read Aurenia a story? Or play her something?”

Then rose his Radiancy's full smile, and Cliopher wondered if there wasn't a little dampness in his eyes. He took a couple of deep breaths, and then said, “Yes, I would like that very much.”

---

In four days in Gorjo City, Cliopher was amazed at the quantity of baby supplies they had acquired. He had thought the people of Lesuia had been generous, but his own family had clearly decided to show them up.

Much of it Aurenia wouldn't need for months, and some of it they had been able to decline as being too much to take on the sky ship to Solaara. He had still been given a vast amount of tiny clothes, toys, and books. His cousin Clia had quietly given him her embroidered Solaaran-style baby carrier, and Dora had proudly handed over a book she declared she was now “too old for”.

Gaudy and Rhodin had taken Aurenia to the beach, to give Cliopher some time to pack.

“We will need to adjust the sleeping arrangements on the sky ship, my lord,” said Cliopher, trying to find space in the trunk he was packing for a beautiful hand-carved vaha. He was carefully not thinking about what had happened either to his childhood toy boat, or the vaha he had crossed the sea on.

“I had thought I would join Ludvic and Rhodin, my lord,” he continued, “and ask Aunt Oura to take my cabin with Dora and Aurenia, if that would be suitable.”

His Radiancy was paging through a picture book set on Alinor. “There is plenty of room in the Sun Cabin for you and Aurenia.”

“I wouldn't want to inconvenience Conju,” said Cliopher, tucking the boat between layers of clothes.

“That ship has sailed,” said Conju, watching Cliopher dither about a pair of tiny socks. Conju had already packed much more, much faster, into a smaller trunk than the one Cliopher was using. “Oh give it here,” he said despairingly.

Not looking Conju in the eye, Cliopher handed over the socks. Conju slipped them into the perfect space, and then rapidly worked through all the other items on the table.

“Thank you, Conju,” said Cliopher quietly.

“The Sun Cabin can be set up to host his Radiancy and four attendants,” said Conju, as he closed and latched the trunk, “unless Aurenia requires more bed space than you, Cliopher?” He looked at the two trunks, in addition to the two valises, that had items needed while travelling. “She certainly has more clothes than you do.”

“We will need to arrange a household for Cliopher, Conju,” said his Radiancy, looking up from the book, “And a wardrobe. He can't go around in his uniform when he is Lord Chancellor.”

“Of course, my lord,” said Conju, with an assessing look at Cliopher. “Would my lord have any recommendations? Lady Ylette has a young apprentice who would do superbly as his wardrobe mistress.”

Cliopher had to admit that Conju had got him back most spectacularly.
