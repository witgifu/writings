Cliopher rang the bell pull sharply. Every time he had come home since he first left for Astandalas, he had felt a little strange coming back to his mother's house. He wasn't sure if this was more or less strange than when he had turned up after the Fall. Possibly, he was equally overwhelmed, if a little better dressed. Also, this time he had support.

His sister answered the door almost immediately. “Kip!” she said, astonished, and then saw that he wasn't alone, “and Conju! And—” she looked from Conju, to the baby in Cliopher's arms, to his Radiancy. And then to Ludvic and Ato behind them.

“Is Mama home? I was hoping to introduce everyone,” said Cliopher, trying not to imagine what wild theories Vinyë was developing.

“Of course! Come on up,” she said, carefully covering her confusion with a smile. “It's Kip, Mama,” she called up the hall, “for the *third* time this year! And he's brought Conju, and…”

They entered the sitting room before Vinyë attempted to guess Cliopher's relationship to the other people he had with him. Eidora was in her favourite chair, with his Aunt Oura beside her. To the side, his niece Leona was sketching. His nephew Gaudenius was there too with, to Cliopher's surprise, the set books for the Imperial Bureaucratic Examinations.

Everyone recognised Conju, but Gaudy was the first to recognise any of the other guests. He stood up, knocking his books aside. Eyes wide, he dropped into the obeisance. Leona followed her brother, and then Aunt Oura and his mother, and finally Vinyë.

“Please rise,” said his Radiancy, smiling to the room in general. They all stood again, in various states of surprise.

“My lord,” said Cliopher, feeling like his worlds were colliding, “May I introduce my mother, Eidora Mdang,” she bowed again, “my aunt, Oura Mdang, my sister Vinyë el Vawen, and her children, Gaudenius and Leona.”

“Mama, this is my *fanoa*, and my daughter Aurenia.” This was as far as he had been mentally able to plan. Aurenia twisted in his arms to see the new people, and he changed his hold on her so she could see his—and her—family.

Vinyë mouthed *fanoa* from behind Conju, looking only slightly like a fresh-caught fish.

“Please, come in everyone,” said Eidora, giving Cliopher a sharp look. It was a look he associated most with getting a talking-to later when they weren't in public. “Will you have coffee, sire?” she asked his Radiancy.

“Thank you,” said his Radiancy, and took the chair on the other side of Eidora's.

Cliopher sat on the couch, where Conju joined him.

“Does… Aurenia… need anything, Kip?” asked his mother, as she moved to the doorway. She now made an attempt at a polite smile.

“No, thank you, Mama, she just had breakfast,” he said, settling her on his lap.

To her annoyance, Leona was sent by her mother to help her grandmother. Gaudy carefully reopened his book, although Cliopher could see he wasn't actually reading. The silence started becoming awkward, and Cliopher desperately tried to think of something to say.

“When did you all arrive?” asked Aunt Oura, smiling particularly at his Radiancy, “I didn't see a sky ship coming in today.”

“A month ago,” said his lord, “We have been staying on Leuisia.”

Vinyë gave Cliopher a conspiratorial look, which he did not feel comfortable refuting. He was sure she was thinking of it as a romantic getaway.

“Did you see much of the jungle there?” asked Aunt Oura, obviously fishing for an innocuous question, “They have some lovely species of Coleus, with pink velvet leaves.”

Remembering all the plants he had been unable to name in Shaian, Cliopher blushed slightly. He had seen the plant, but only knew it as *wampung*, which he thought was green and pink.

By the time his mother and Leona returned with coffee (and to Cliopher's quiet relief, jam drop biscuits), his Radiancy and Aunt Oura were deep in a discussion on the plants of Leuisia. Gaudy had come over to meet Aurenia, and was showing her the pictures in his books. She very much wanted to grab the pages, and Gaudy was keeping them carefully out of reach.

“So,” said his mother, settling down in her chair with her favourite mug, “I don't remember hearing of a daughter, or a *fanoa*, last time you were here, Kip. Aurenia is surely old enough to have been born before your last visit?”

“Yes, Mama,” he said, looking down at his daughter, “Aurenia is about six months old. But I only met her at the lunar eclipse.”

“And so for the six months before that, and the nine months before that, you were too busy to look after your daughter?” Cliopher's mother was on the warpath. “I know you think your work is important Kip, but your own daughter!”

Cliopher suppressed a sigh, he had been worried his mother would take this tack. He should have tried to get ahead of it. “I learnt of her for the first time at the lunar eclipse, Mama. Of course if I had known of her earlier I would have done more.” He gave Aurenia a kiss on her little fuzzy head. “Her mother, the Lady Moon, left her with me at the eclipse party without even telling me a name for her.”

Aurenia must have been able to sense the stress he was feeling from his mother's disapproval, and started fussing. He turned her around so she could see him, and began planting little kisses all over her face. “Eeeei,” she said, and grabbed at his hair.

His mother and aunt burst out laughing. “Really Kip,” said Aunt Oura, “That is the most ridiculous tale. You shouldn't really joke about something like this.”

Not knowing what to say in response to their disbelief, he turned to Gaudy instead, “Would you like to hold her?” Cliopher carefully didn't look at his mother or aunt.

Gaudy nodded, and Leona came over to join them. Cliopher passed Aurenia over to Gaudy, where he squatted on the floor. Aurenia immediately poked Gaudy in the nose, and then grabbed onto his hair, which was longer than Cliopher's. “Eeeei,” she said.

“It's true,” said his Radiancy to Eidora, with the charming smile he had used at the market and party on Leuisia. From next to Vinyë, Conju nodded, “Yes, it was most surprising.”

Cliopher very much appreciated their support. He knew Conju didn't really approve, didn't understand why he wouldn't leave Aurenia to be raised by his mother.

“The gods,” said his lord, with a twinkle in his eye, “do as they will.”

Eidora sniffed, in a manner so like Conju, that Cliopher almost laughed. She could hardly disagree with the Emperor about his own ancestor.

“Well,” said his mother, “Does this mean you are home to stay?”

Cliopher tensed again. This time Aurenia didn't notice, as she was happily watching Leona make silly faces.

“I'm afraid we still have much work to do in Solaara,” said his Radiancy, still with full charm, “I hope we will be able to return permanently after I retire at the Jubilee in seven years.”

This wasn't actually something they had discussed yet, what would happen after his Radiancy retired. That he would even consider moving to the Vangaveye-ve made Cliopher feel oddly light and floaty. He turned to his lord, and gave him a wide smile of pure joy.

His lord's charm seemed to be working a little on his mother; she smiled back. “Seven years!” she said, and Cliopher could only detect a hint of reprimand in her voice, “And just the two of you, working full-time, with a baby?”

Conju bristled at the idea that he would not be able to hire the best nursemaids and tutors for Aurenia. He had, in fact, tried to convince Cliopher of just that idea. It was Cliopher, backed by his lord's assurances, that didn't want his daughter raised by staff.

“Mama,” said Cliopher, hesitating, “I was hoping that maybe some of the family, or someone in the family, might be willing to come and help us in Solaara. We could hire a nursemaid, but…”

Both his mother and Aunt Oura bristled, as he knew they would. They looked at each other and sighed, such a perfect mirror that you could see clearly they were sisters.

“It's a long way to Solaara," said Aunt Oura, “I'm not sure who would have the time...”

“And Aurenia is very young, she'll need a lot of energy,” added his mother.

As if they were in the Council of Princes, negotiating, his Radiancy asked a leading question of Cliopher. “The journey by sky ship is how long, Kip? And how much notice does the captain need?”

“Twelve hours notice, my lord” said Cliopher, “And the journey is three days in fine weather, otherwise four.”

At this point, Aunt Oura's granddaughter Dora ran into the room. “Hello! Cousin Kip!” she said brightly, skipping over to give him a hug. He picked her up, and put her on his lap, where Aurenia had been before.

Cliopher turned to his Radiancy, and said “My lord, this is my cousin Dora.” To Cliopher's embarrassment, Dora then proceeded to have a perfectly normal conversation with the Sun-on-Earth.

“Cousin Kip says that when I’m big enough I can come visit him in Solaara and see the Palace,” said Dora, twisting in Cliopher's grasp.

“What a good idea,” said his Radiancy, looking around the room, “Your Cousin Kip and I were hoping some of your family might come and visit us to see Aurenia as well.”

“Who is Aurenia?” said Dora, looking around for someone she didn't know. “Oh!” she said, spotting the baby, and wiggling off Cliopher's lap to go and inspect her, “Are you Aurenia?”

“Eeeei!” said Aurenia, who clearly loved meeting new people. 

“Yes,” said Cliopher, “that's my daughter, your Cousin Aurenia.” 

Dora ran off to go and get some of her favourite toys to show the baby. Cliopher wondered how much chewing of her toys Dora would tolerate, but it wasn't as if Dora didn't have experience with other young cousins.

“Dora starts school in six weeks,” said Aunt Oura, biting her lip, “What if I took her to Solaara for a month, and then you can organise a schedule after that, Eidora?”

Cliopher's mother took a big breath, and looked from Kip, to his Radiancy, to Conju, to little Aurenia (who was sucking on Gaudy's finger). “I will see what I can do,” she said finally.

A knot of tensions unravelled in Cliopher's stomach. “Thank you very much, Mama, Aunt Oura,” he said softly. When his mother focused on a task, she was a force to be reckoned with. And while she often didn't understand Cliopher, she always put her family first.

Just as Dora had arrived back with a knitted octopus to show Aurenia, more footsteps bounded up the stairs.

“Good gracious, Kip!” cried Cousin Zemius, striding up to give Cliopher a fierce embrace. “I heard from one of the medical professors who works at the hospital that you have a daughter! Where have you been hiding her? Oh, is this your husband, Kip? I—!” Then Zemius realised who he had just gestured at.

Thankfully, despite Zemius fainting, he did manage to have a long and interesting discussion about how the mantle of the Lord of Zunidh was passed down.

When Kip went to make up a bottle for Aurelia, Vinyë came with him into the kitchen. 

“I think she's missed a nap now, in all this excitement,” said Cliopher, “I hope it doesn't make her too grumpy, or mess up her sleep schedule.”

Vinyë gave him a grin. “She'll do what she wants, Kip, which will probably be whatever distressed you most and doesn't bother her.”

Cliopher sighed, and checked the temperature of the water in the kettle.

“But Kip,” she said cautiously, “I understand how babies happen. I don't understand how my brother comes home from his honeymoon to introduce the Emperor is his *fanoa* and the Moon Lady is his baby's mother. I thought you were some kind of secretary.”

Kip pulled out and unwrapped the bottle from his bilum. The water was just cool enough, but he might see if he could make space in the cold box until Aurelia started fussing.

“I am his Radiancy's personal secretary, and the Secretary in Chief of the Offices of the Lords of State,” said Cliopher, now looking through the cupboards for a funnel.

“What does 'the secretary of the lords of state', or whatever you said, mean?” asked Vinyë, examining the baby bottle.

“It's the title for the head of the Service,” said Kip, opening a third drawer of mixed cooking utensils and finally giving up. “Do you know where the funnels are? I thought Mama had three or four, or I would have brought one with me...”

Without comment, Vinyë pulled a funnel out of the pile of dishes on the draining rack, and handed it to Cliopher.

Blushing, Cliopher said, “Ah, yes, thank you.” Then he set himself up over the sink, carefully holding the funnel in the bottle with one hand, and the kettle with the other.

“Oh, let me,” said Vinyë, grabbing the kettle so Cliopher had two hands for the bottle and funnel. “So you're the head of the Imperial Bureaucratic Service in the Palace?”

“No, that's my second, Kiri. I manage all of Zunidh,” he said, focusing on the water line in the bottle. “Stop, thanks Vinyë,” he said, when the mark had been reached. Then he removed the funnel, capped the bottle, and shook to mix the formula.

“I'm sorry,” said Vinyë, refilling the kettle, “You're the head of the entire Service? The Ministries, the Princes, the Post Office, the Lights, the courts, the Guard?”

“Oh, no,” said Cliopher with a chortle, and Vinyë relaxed a little. “His Radiancy is the Judge of Last Appeal, and chairs the Council of Princes. Commander Omo is the head of the Guard.”

Vinyë rather slammed the kettle onto the stove. “I am going to leave aside the question of when you were going to tell us you are running the world government,” she said, waspishly.

Cliopher balked, and opened his mouth to protest. But she held up a hand.

“Instead, I'm going to ask if *you* inspired the recent run of secretary romance novels, or if *they* inspired you,” she said with a cheeky grin and a raised eyebrow.

Trying to maintain his court dignity with his sister was only going to get him mocked. So he turned instead to the cool box, and put the bottle inside. “I have not read them, so it cannot be the latter,” he said, with as much calm as he could muster.

Both he and Vinyë broke into giggles.

“But we're *fanoa*, not lovers,” said Cliopher, when he got himself back under control. “We will be co-parenting Aurenia.”

Vinyë rolled her eyes and shook her head at him, but didn't press any further as they returned to the living room.
