Cliopher sat blinking, looking at the space where the Moon had been. It took him a few moments for the sound of his own name to break through.

"Cliopher!" came Conju's irritated voice.

He looked around, and noticed that while he had been staring at his Radiancy, at least half the gathering was now staring at him. He looked down, wondering how he could have embarrassed himself this time.

In his arms (how had he not noticed the weight?) was a baby. Swaddled tightly, and asleep despite the noise and light. Near black skin against the pure white wrapping.

"Oh," said Cliopher, and the baby blinked open their eyes. Golden pools he had only seen in one other face. "Don't you just look exactly like your father."

By size and weight the child was surely not a newborn, but while Cliopher had held many babies he had no idea if this one was three months or a year. He held them close, rocking slightly as they began to fuss.

"Cliopher," came Conju's voice again.

Looking up, Cliopher could see in Conju's carefully controlled face all the questions the other man was carefully not asking. Cliopher smiled at him. "I don't know," he said simply.

A few of the women had come to stand beside Conju. One of them handed over the baby she was carrying to a friend. "Give them here, and I'll see if they're hungry, hmm?"

Cliopher blinked. He had a child. The moon had given him a child. He had asked for his Radiancy to be happy, how could that have anything to do with... mutely, he handed the baby over. The woman winked at him, turning the child's face to her breast with practiced hands.

Cliopher looked over at his Radiancy. He was standing behind the chair he had been sitting on. His face was serene, but his knuckles gripping the back of the chair were white.

"How old is she?" his Radiancy asked. His voice neutral, as if he was enquiring after the child of a new acquaintance.

"About six months, I'd say, sir," said the woman who was now happily feeding the baby. "She's probably started on solids, there's a few teeth there."

Suddenly the enormity of what just happened hit Cliopher. "I am sorry, my lord," he said, his voice cracking slightly, "I promise you, I did not ask the moon for a child." *For your child,* he thought, but very carefully did not say. He did not know *how* he knew the child was his Radiancy's daughter. But he did know it was dangerous knowledge.

His Radiancy said, in that same calm voice, "I hardly think you would be so irresponsible, Cliopher." He took a deep, slow breath. "What will you call her?"

"I don't know," said Cliopher, honestly. Since Ghilly had broken off their engagement, he had assumed he would never be a father. He had his work, and his nephew and niece, and all his cousins. 

His work! How was he going to care for a child? How was he going to feed a baby? Could he get a wet nurse? There was probably infant formula in Gorjo City, there would be at the Palace. How could he possibly have time? Would he just carry her on his back, like they did in outer ring?

Would he have to leave his job? Move back in with his mother to raise the girl? But if she was his Radiancy's daughter, they would need to be at the Palace. Cliopher wouldn't want to separate the girl from her father.

His hard-learnt court face crumbled under the weight of all these emotions. Tears started running down his face, even as he closed his eyes against them.

"There, there," said the woman feeding the baby, "You've had a bit of a shock, it's normal." Aya had joined her, and she and the other three women were holding a whispered conversation.

Conju tried again, "Cliopher..."

"I'm keeping her," said Cliopher, defiantly. "I'll bring her back to Solaara. I'll find a way to make it work." He swallowed, and turned to his Radiancy. "That is, my lord, if you will allow me, I would very much be honoured to continue."

His Radiancy responded immediately, "Of course, Cliopher, I could not do without you. And I would not want to separate you from your new daughter."

Cliopher reeled again. His daughter. He was a father. The moon had given him a child, his lord's child. And her child? Cliopher did not want to think when and how his Radiancy had... No. The Moon was a goddess, he would accept what he was given.

"We'll round up some supplies for you, *hani*," said Aya, "Mikiya here will come back with you tonight, in case you need any help."

Mikiya looked up from the baby girl and smiled, "I'll bring my little Nora with me, I've got more milk than she's ever wanted."

Cliopher stood up, and bowed over his hands at the women. "My profound thanks to all of you," he said, aware of the incredible gift they were offering, and the huge inconvenience this would be for them all.

The baby girl had settled in Mikiya's arms. Mikiya passed the child back to Cliopher. At least holding a baby was something he felt confident with.

Again, Cliopher turned back to his Radiancy. His Radiancy gave a small polite smile. "Shall we head back then?" he asked, gesturing to the path.

"Of course, my lord," said Cliopher. The guards formed up, and with his Radiancy in the lead, they walked along the path to the house. 

"Cliopher," said Conju, who had stepped up beside him, "Are you sure? You can't possibly expect to keep a child in your rooms."

Adjusting his hold on his daughter slightly, Cliopher replied, "The Moon lady gave me a daughter, I'm not going to foster her out. She should grow up knowing..." He looked meaningfully at his lord walking in front of him.

"The Ouranatha," whispered Conju, fiercely, "are going to steal her away if they get *any* idea. Even if you mention the Moon was involved. How do you think you're going to hide her from them in the Palace?"

"It only has to be until he retires," Cliopher whispered back just as fiercely, "and it is not a decision I can make alone."

Cliopher slowed down his walk slightly, so he was walking with Mikiya and Nora instead. He said to Mikiya, "Thank you again for coming with me, I have a nephew and niece, and many cousins, but no children of my own until now."

Mikiya smiled, and launched into a lot of helpful advice. Nora, it transpired, was her fifth baby, and Mikiya was something of a midwife and village auntie. Cliopher listened intently, carefully ignoring Conju, who was now whispering to Ser Rhodin.

When they arrived back at the house, Cliopher had a much better idea of what to expect when taking care of a six-month-old. She had slept happily the entire time, despite the discussions going on around her. Cliopher knew he wanted to ask his lord so many things about the baby (his daughter, their daughter), but he was nervous.

His Radiancy took up his customary position on the verandah, against the railing. The others went inside, except for Ser Rhodin and Ato.

Perhaps it was some lingering effect of the Moon lady, or the holiday in general, but Cliopher decided to take a risk. "Do you have any ideas for a name, my lord?" he asked, stepping close, and angling his arms so his Radiancy could see the baby's sleeping face.

"Did you say before that your family has names shared through generations? Surely you would want to name your daughter following that?" said his Radiancy, looking at the tiny face. Cliopher could see his lord was carefully gripping his hands together, as if worried he might reach out accidentally.

"Yes, my lord, they do. But I would value your input..." he took another deep breath, and continued in a whisper, "as a part of her family."

His Radiancy flicked his eyes up to meet Cliopher's. His lord took in a slow, shaky, breath. "Nobody can know," he whispered back, and then his normal voice, continued, "We are much enamoured of the Vangaveye-ve. Honouring a member of your family sounds like a wonderful idea."

Cliopher wished he could offer his lord more. "I will not let anyone take her," he said, firmly, "in Solaara, we can say I found I had a child unexpectedly when her mother passed away. I will do anything I can to keep her in your life." 

"I will never be able to hold her, to hug her. It would be safer for both of you if you stayed here," whispered his Radiancy, now looking out at the ocean.

"Yes," said Cliopher honestly, "it would be safer, my lord. But it would not be good. She needs her family, all her family. We will clean out the Ouranatha, and unwind the taboos. We will change the government so you can retire, my lord."

"You make it sound so simple, Cliopher," whispered his lord, still looking at the beach.

"It won't be easy, my lord, but worthwhile things rarely are," replied Cliopher.

There was a silence, which felt only slightly uneasy.

"What will you name her?" asked his Radiancy.

Cliopher paused, considering. "What do you think of Aurenia, my lord? It was the name of my grandmother."

His lord finally looked back at the baby in Cliopher's arms. "Hello Aurenia Mdang," he said softly, "I look forward to getting to know you."

Cliopher smiled, looking between Aurenia and his lord. Aurenia chose that moment to wake up again and start fussing. Cliopher adjusted his hold so she could see more around her. She turned her head to looked directly at his Radiancy.

"Well," he said to Aurenia, "I suppose I can at least teach you something about magic, little mage."

Aurenia smiled, and closed her eyes again.

"I should probably put her to bed," said Cliopher, looking around, "Hopefully someone has found a bilum."

"A bag?" asked his Radiancy, confused.

"Yes, my lord," answered Cliopher, "Traditionally babies sleep in a bilum, on top of blankets. It's usually hung near the parent's bed for easy feeding. I doubt a house like this has a cot."

His Radiancy looked slightly surprised. "No, I don't suppose they do. Well, good night then, Cliopher."

Cliopher wanted to bow to his lord before leaving, but this was tricky while holding a baby. He compromised with a slightly abbreviated bow, hoping his lord would understand. "Good night, my lord."

Ato opened the door for him, and he went looking to see what they could do about sleeping arrangements.

After much discussion, Cliopher and Mikiya agreed to share the bed in his room. It was plenty big enough; but Cliopher had felt awkward about it and tried to convince Mikiya he would be fine with a mat on the floor. She overruled him. Two bilums were found, and the babies hung inside the mosquito net. 

Cliopher thought he would never sleep, lying there, watching the tiny body of Aurenia, now free from the swaddle. His daughter. His lord's daughter. Their daughter. She was beautiful and perfect in every way. At some point, however, he did fall asleep.

The next morning, Cliopher woke up with the dawn, before Aurenia. He lay there again, watching her, until his body demanded that he go to the toilet. He felt bad leaving her, even though Mikiya was right there, feeding Nora who had woken.

On his way back, he spotted a woman he didn't recognise approaching the kitchen. He waved, she waved back, and he joined her.

"I heard about the unexpected baby," she said, smiling, "so I thought I would drop off some things. My youngest is nearly two now."

Cliopher enquired after her children as they entered the kitchen together. He looked around, and saw his Radiancy there, talking with Loven and Beila. In front of them on the kitchen table was a beautifully woven basket-tray, full of baby supplies.

All three women, and his Radiancy, smiled at the stunned look on Cliopher's face. The new woman opened her bilum, and from it added to the stacks in the basket. There were napkins, blankets, wraps, two bilum, even a rubber teething ring. Nestled in a small box of straw was something Cliopher first assumed was a glass banana, but realised was a baby bottle.

Loven laughed at his amazement, "You're not the first person to have a surprise baby, although you are the first one where the babe arrived six months old."

Cliopher blushed, "I should—"

Loven interrupted him, "Now sir, if you're about to say you should pay for these things, don't be absurd. Most of this has seen five or six babies already. Except that bottle, Nania's *velio* mother-in-law sent her that, but it's not so much use here. The instructions say to freeze the milk or use formula, and this is the only house with a proper cold box."

The visitor waved, and left, while Cliopher was still examining the basket. He could have bought all this in Solaara, or in Gorjo City. But now, on this island, it was an enormous blessing. 

He was more determined than ever to see the universal income be realised. His people saw this gift as natural, but how many mothers on Zunidh had nothing when their baby arrived? How many parents wrapped their children in rags and paper to sleep? How many gave up or sold children they loved, but could not afford to feed?

"I have cleansed the bottle," said his Radiancy, "And put preservation and health charms on it."

Cliopher wanted to bow deeply in thanks, that his lord would do such small magics for him. But it wasn't for Cliopher, was it? It was for their daughter, Aurenia. So instead he said, "An excellent idea, my lord, I know they can be risky otherwise, thank you."

His Radiancy smiled, and Cliopher had to work very hard to suppress the urge to bow. He looked around instead, for a cup of coffee.

Coffee and baby supplies acquired, he headed back to his bedroom and Aurenia. She woke as soon as he opened the door, and started fussing.

"She'll want changing," said Mikiya, "There's a chamberpot and bucket for soiled napkins by the washstand."

This was something Cliopher had only done one or two times before, under careful supervision. But he was determined that he would not be leaving the care of Aurenia to hired staff, so he would need to learn.

He lifted her out of the bilum, and carried her over to the washstand, grabbing a clean napkin and a rag on the way. Setting Aurenia down on the side of the washstand, he checked everything was ready. Then he began to unwrap her napkin. 

To his surprise, the napkin was clean. He lifted her up to check, and she began to pee. Quickly, he lifted her over the chamber pot. This was good timing, as she took the chance to release the other end as well. Thankfully her feet were not in the way, and only a little urine had missed the chamberpot and napkin.

"Is that normal, Mikiya?" he asked, his voice squeaking a little bit.

"Oh yes," she said, chuckling a little, "Some babies can be taught that, and some are just fastidiously clean and hate soiling themselves. Lucky you!"

Cliopher took a deep breath, and Aurenia giggled. "I wonder where you get that from," he whispered to her, remembering the Imperial Baths. Then he set about cleaning her up, and wrapping a new napkin.

Mikiya fed Aurenia as Cliopher dressed and did his own ablutions. Then she handed Aurenia back. "You two should have breakfast together," she said, "if you give her a piece of papaya or banana she'll probably have a good time gnawing on it."

Cliopher returned to the verandah, and found Conju setting out tea for his Radiancy. The latter was standing, watching the ocean.

"Good morning again, Cliopher," said his Radiancy, as he turned around, "and Aurenia, of course."

Conju looked slightly askance at Cliopher, but didn't say anything. Cliopher wondered how long it would take his friend to get used to the idea of seeing a child around. 

"Good morning, my lord," said Cliopher, turning Aurenia in his arms so she had a better view of his Radiancy. He made a slightly different attempt at a bow he could do while holding her, and was glad he managed not to overbalance.

"Would you like to join me for breakfast, Cliopher? Aurenia?" asked his Radiancy, with a small smile for Aurenia.

"Very much, my lord," said Cliopher, and sat in the indicated chair after his Radiancy sat down. Conju bustled off to get breakfast for Cliopher too. Cliopher sat Aurenia on his lap, supporting her with one hand.

"Are you sure you wish to come back to Solaara, Cliopher? Would it not be easier to have the support of your family in Gorjo City?" asked his Radiancy, sipping his tea. His voice was serene, familiar. But Cliopher could see him turning his teacup in precise quarter turns, and missed the light from his lord's face that he had seen these past days.

"In some ways, it might be easier, my lord," replied Cliopher, wincing as Aurenia chewed on his finger, "But as I said last night, I do not want to separate you. She has already been abandoned by her mother, I do not want to separate her from you, my lord."

Cliopher saw his Radiancy relax the tiniest fraction. Good. He didn't want to force his lord into a relationship with his daughter. He also didn't want his Radiancy to be afraid of how Aurenia would be treated in Solaara.

"To that end, my lord," he said, "I was wondering... that is... if something should happen to me, my lord... my next-of-kin is currently my mother."

"You would not trust your mother to raise Aurenia?" asked his Radiancy, somewhat surprised.

"Oh, it's not that, my lord, but my mother _would_ want to raise Aurenia in the Vangaveya-ve," Cliopher paused for a deep breath, "She would not understand, about you. If I told her, it would be all over Gorjo City, and would surely make it to the Ouranatha eventually."

"You are young and fit still, Cliopher, surely this is an unlikely event?" asked his Radiancy.

Cliopher was spared answering immediately by the return of Conju. There were pastries for his Radiancy, fruit for Cliopher, and sweet buns for both of them. Conju smiled at them both, and headed back inside.

Cliopher picked up a piece of papaya from the plate, and handed it to Aurenia. She grabbed it in one little fist, and began mashing it against her mouth. Cliopher sighed, as small bits of papaya fell on him and her.

"Even if I am sick, or injured, or need to go to Nijan to negotiate again," said Cliopher, carefully, "it should be you, my lord, and not my mother, who cares for Aurenia."

The girl in question babbled happily, although Cliopher didn't think she had successfully eaten any of the papaya yet.

"I cannot," said his Radiancy, his voice once again calm and controlled, "risk acknowledging her. I will not put Aurenia in this box after me. Or worse."

Cliopher so wished he could reach out and comfort his lord. "I do not want that either, my lord," he said softly, "but if I could make you my next-of-kin, my lord... if that would not be too forward... she would be safe. Of course, my lord, I know that is a great presumption, and I—"

Thankfully, Aurenia broke Cliopher's increasingly incoherent talk, by blowing a loud raspberry. They both looked at her, and she giggled again, and then resumed playing with the mashed papaya that was all over her hands and face.

His Radiancy gripped his teacup so tightly, Cliopher was slightly worried that the thin, elegant porcelain might shatter. "What are you proposing, Cliopher?" he asked quietly, "I do not think the court would look favourably on a marriage between us."

Cliopher tried to ignore the idea that the main objection his lord had to their marriage was public opinion. "In the Vangaveye-ve, we have a relationship called _fanoa_, my lord," he said, trying to hide how much this word meant to him. But also, he wanted to see his lord's reaction, to know if he had overstepped, if this was too far. Cliopher looked at his Radiancy's face, into his golden eyes, as too few people dared.

"It is something like a marriage," he continued, "It has a similar legal recognition. _Fanoa_ is very old-fashioned, my lord. Archaic even. The word means shell, any small white shell, but particularly a small clamshell. It also means two matched things that go together, like a canoe and outrigger. Between people... trading partners used to be called _fanoa_, or other business partners. The best of friends. Adopted siblings. People who wanted to co-parent, but not marry. It is not used much these days, but it is still there, in the law. And in the stories. My family would understand."

Aurenia took the chance to hit Cliopher's arm with her very sticky hands, as if he were a drum. He sighed, and handed her another piece of papaya. He took the chance to grab a bun for himself.

His Radiancy seemed to have frozen. Cliopher was worried that he had gone too far. What was he thinking, to propose any relationship with the Lord of Rising Stars, the Last Emperor of Astandalas, the Sun-on-Earth? Cliopher was his secretary, sometimes his hands, nothing more.

If he hadn't been holding Aurenia, he would have slid off his chair and to his knees. Even if only days ago his Radiancy had snapped at Conju and asked,  "When have I ever given you cause to think I would punish you for speaking your mind?" It seemed wrong, not to beg forgiveness for his obvious misstep.

Cliopher put the bun back down again, having not eaten any of it. "My lord,—" he began, but his Radiancy held up his hand to forestall him. Cliopher's insides twisted.

"Yes, Cliopher," said his Radiancy, "I will be your _fanoa_."

Aurenia cooed, and Cliopher was so happy he thought he might float up out of his chair.

"But," continued his Radiancy, and Cliopher startled very slightly, "If you, if we, are going to, do as you said. If we are going to 'clean out the Ouranatha, and unwind the taboos' so I can touch, and 'change the government' so I can retire, I will need to ennoble you. Give you real and enforced authority. You would have to step down as my personal secretary, for there will be far too much work—even for you! As Lord Chancellor—Cliopher, you would be required to fulfil many of the courtly, ah, performances you have managed to evade thus far."

Cliopher looked down at Aurenia, and up at his Radiancy again. "Eeeeei," said Aurenia.

"Yes, my lord," said Cliopher, "I will be your Lord Chancellor."

"Eeeeei," said Aurenia again. Then she burst into tears.

"I think she just realised she's covered in sticky fruit, my lord," said Cliopher, "I will go and clean us both."

His lord nodded his dismissal. Cliopher went back to his room, with _fanoa_ singing in his heart.
