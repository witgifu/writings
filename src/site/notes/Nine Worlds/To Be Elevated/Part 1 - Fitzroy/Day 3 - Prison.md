I woke the next morning with the dawn. Not from the dawn, but the chanting that had invaded my dreams. I could still hear it with my waking ears. Looking around in bleary confusion, my first thought was that the years of my adventuring had been a dream. 

But no, I hadn’t dreamed the chickens, or the shadows, or any of the times I’d had to use a shrub as a toilet. I might have dreamed That Party, or of having friends, or kissing the Moon Lady. Dreams, however, are light on toilets.

Then I remembered why I was back in the tower. The chanting must be another change in the spells. I entered a light trance, just to see if I could feel what the change was exactly.

There were two changes. The tower spells had shifted, and had now trapped me in the top two floors. And one of the bundle of ropes that had been attached to the necklace was now attached to me.

What if I’d slept on the bottom floor, I wondered? Would I have woken in the night as the spells compelled me upwards? Would I have escaped the tether line reattaching? The maybes made my head spin. I couldn’t stop worrying that I had made things worse for my own comfort.

Until I remembered why I had chosen to sleep here. Then I ran around the tower again, peering out of every window as far as I could. I couldn’t see anything, not a bird, not a boat, not a friend.

I slumped down next to the bath. They would come, it just hadn’t been enough time yet. They would come. I just needed to be patient and not panic.

I was still panicking a little bit. What spell had re-attached to me? It didn’t feel like any of the compliance spells, or the compulsions. I felt no need to light incense or pray or eat tiny cubes of pressed rice. I wasn’t calm enough for it to be any of the mental effects. Perhaps it was some spell I’d never noticed, never had need to notice.

“Eritanyr is the real terror of Astandalas, and deserves a slow and painful fucking death,” I said loudly. It didn’t seem to be cursing or speaking ill of the Emperor then.

I went back upstairs, and looked over the books. I had read them all so many times. The breakfast arrived while I was thumbing through an incurious volume on fly-fishing (why was that even here?). I didn’t think, I just sat down and picked up the golden eating sticks. I flinched, and tried to put them down again. I could not. My hand shook as I fought for control.

I compromised, picking up a single grain of rice. The magic loosened slightly, and I threw down the sticks. I pushed away from the table, and bolted back down for the safety of the bathroom.

Huddled in the corner, I waited. The magic had strengthened over the night. I had thrown off those Marwn spells, hated spells. I would do everything I could to keep them off.

Eventually I entered another trance to check the magic again. Three more ropes of power had snapped onto me. The necklace looked the same in my magical sight.

Someone was casting new spells. On me. Someone with access to the hair and blood stored away… no, not someone. Many people. I could feel the tangle of the work of a dozen or more strong wizards.

*They are coming here*, I thought, terrified. *Coming for me*. Why? What for? Had a new Marwn been made so they could dispose of this one? Was there some ritual for which they needed my body as well as my soul?

I might find out soon. Would my friends make it here first? I required a plan in case they didn’t. Would I be able to escape a dozen powerful mages on the road? A long-buried memory welled up. Being passed hand-over-hand, catching glimpses of faces and silver and gold masks.

I shuddered in reaction to the memory. But I hadn't known I was a mage back then. I was a child, and fully wrapped in the enchantments. It would be different this time. 

As long as they weren’t coming for an immediate human sacrifice. But even the worst rumours of Ertitanyr didn’t include that. I would not give in to baseless fear.

I shook, huddled in my corner.

So, plan to try to break out on the road. Which would mean I wanted whoever was coming to not be on their guard. And that meant I needed to look like I’d spent the past fourteen years dutifully waiting here and performing all the ceremonies.

But… not the necklace. I couldn’t take those spells back. I wasn’t sure if anyone could, even if I wanted them to, and I very much did not want to. It *would* need to be put it somewhere less obvious.

I slunk back up the stairs, and found a decoratively carved sandalwood box that held incense sticks. I dumped the sticks onto the table, irreverently. It was priceless imperial incense, made from the best of all the worlds of the Empire. Using a silk handkerchief, I pushed the necklace off the edge of the table and into the box. Then, hands shaking slightly, I replaced the incense one stick at a time until I couldn’t fit any more.

I put the box in the shrine, just under the portrait. The shrine was already the anchor of a number of spells that reached out to the heart of the empire. Hopefully it would disguise the necklace.

I crammed the handful of remaining incense sticks into the sand and lit them all with a wave of my hand. Without being able to open the windows it would shortly become difficult to breathe in this room. Hopefully they would burn down in an hour or so.

I grabbed my old clothes from the bathroom where I had shucked them last night. They’d been ignored by the spirits, who must not have recognised them. I held them up to my face. They smelt of sweat and dirt, but also slightly of campfire. And more faintly (or was it my imagination?) of Jullanar’s ginger biscuits, and the oil Pali and Damien used on their swords.

I carefully put them at the back of a chest, under a pile of woollen blankets I had not remembered. Those blankets would have been more useful in my Bag than the slippers. Ah well, Jullanar had the bag.

From the wardrobe I pulled out the silk robes in black and white. As I did, so I felt a ripple of magic across them, and they grew slightly. That was a relief, as I would no doubt look ridiculous in the clothes that had fit me at eighteen. I sighed. No colour at all, in these ugly clothes.

When I got free, and we all found each other, my friends would laugh to see how I was dressed. But the Bag contained plenty of other clothes. This was only temporary. I could play a temporary fool.

I escaped the overpowering smell of incense for the bathroom again. This time I scrubbed myself raw, and then applied all the lotions and potions I could find in the cupboards.

The mirror in the bathroom was full-length. For what purpose, I did not know, it was not as if the Marwn needed to prepare for visitors. I looked over myself, stroking my hair. If I was to be a convincing Marwn, it *would* have to go. I bit my lip, and looked away from my own reflection in shame. It would grow back. My friends would be horrified, but they would understand.

I braced myself against the bench, and pulled a pair of scissors from the drawer. My hand shook, as I held them up above my head. I had to, I had to. The Marwn had a clean shaved head, like any good Astandalan princeling. I started hacking.

The hair fell to the floor, making soft dark clouds. I kept going, but I cried as I did so. When I had done all I could with scissors, I pulled out the jar of depilatory cream (how I hated the smell) and rubbed it over my head. It stung my hands, I’d forgotten to wear gloves. I left it on until it started feeling like my head was burning, then washed it off under the tap.

I couldn’t bear to look at myself in the mirror again.

Two hours after lighting the incense, I returned upstairs with trepidation. The room was thick with the smell, but the incense had burned out. I threw the base of the sticks into the little terracotta pot, where they disappeared. 

Just like they had always done. I wondered what would have happened if I had thrown whole sticks in. Would they have disappeared? Or did it only take used ones? I looked around for something to experiment with, and ended up tearing a tiny corner from a sheet of paper. It didn’t disappear when I dropped it into the pot. Nothing happened. I shrugged. 

Lunch appeared. A white cube, a black pyramid, and a yellow ball flecked with gold. I sat at the table (now without the necklace) and ate it. The black was salty and chewy, the white was sticky but didn’t taste much of anything. The yellow was sweet and mango flavoured.

The magic liked that I ate the food. I could feel its warm approval.

“I didn’t do it for you,” I said, disgruntled.

The magic didn’t notice or didn’t care. I could feel that it now wanted me to do something else. Three bowls appeared on the table to replace my empty plate, and a candle behind. One black bowl with flaky salt, one yellow with peppercorns, and one white with yellow flower petals.

How had fresh flower petals made it so far, unbruised? They looked like they came from Zunidh somewhere, I thought. The pepper was from Ysthar. Was the salt from Alinor, then?

My hands remembered the motions. It made my wild magic itch to do it, this ritual of schooled magic. It needed no magic from me, but my magic disliked it. Lighting the candle with magic felt too uncomfortable, so I did it with the long matches supplied.

Flick a pinch of salt on the candle. Light it, and drop a peppercorn (which popped amusingly) and then a flower petal (that didn’t burn at all well, why did they use fresh when surely dried would be easier to store and use?) into the flame. Wave my left hand over the flame right to left, then my right hand left to right. Then extinguish it with the little silver snuffer. As soon as I finished, the whole thing disappeared again.

Once again the magic felt pleased, and I grumbled to myself. I had no desire to maintain the power of the Empire. In truth, I’d spent years doing all I could to rankle it. I had written many songs about it that had been banned, and to sing or print them was treason.

To spite the magic, I went to the harp and played Donkey Ears, singing all the words. It was very rude, and I relished it. The magic didn’t seem to care, but I felt better. I played the harp some more. Just pieces I had learned as a child, that I could play without thinking about my friends. Who were still not here. 

But I was on an island. Maybe they were on the shore, looking out, trying to work out how to reach me. Did Pali have the carpet her sister made? I wasn’t sure.

No. They would find me, we would find each other, once I was on the road. I just had to be patient until I was let out of this tower (I was not very good at patience).

I wasn’t used to so many hours alone anymore. I imagined Jullanar was there, listening. To my memory of her, I asked for suggestions for what to play. I didn’t ask her where she was, or why she hadn’t found me. I just wanted to pretend, for a little while, that things were normal. Or as normal as life got in the Red Company.

After I finished, I remembered to check on the magic. The walls were spinning faster, and the cords attached to me were even thicker.

I dropped out of the trance, and wondered idly if they would get here tomorrow. Stepping over to the bookcase, I ran my hand along the spines again, trying to pick what to read. I pulled out a thin volume of Ystharian philosophy.

And I froze. I couldn’t move. I tried to call out, but I couldn’t, I couldn’t even breathe deeply. Squashing down rising panic, I breathed slowly as the nun mages in Alinor had taught me. 

In, hold, out, hold. Slowly, gently. Let it fill my mind, just breathing.

I heard new sounds filtering in from outside. Some kind of crashing sound, wood on stone. Voices, yelling, distant. The chanting had returned, even louder. I hadn’t noticed it stopping earlier, but it must have faded out over the day.

I squashed a brief hope that this was my friends. This was surely the Ourunatha. They had come for me.

The door to outside banged open. Someone yelled a word, the exact sounds muffled to be unintelligible by the three floors between us. There was a flash of light, and a smell of bitter oranges.

I passed out.
