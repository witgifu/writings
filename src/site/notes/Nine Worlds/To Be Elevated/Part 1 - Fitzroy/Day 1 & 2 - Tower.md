We had been talking, in the forest. About to set up camp for the night, bantering over chores. I walked down to the stream to fetch water. 

Suddenly there was a roar through the forest, like a wave. It bent all the trees, like a tornado does. I dropped the bucket and ran back towards the camp. But the wind dragged at me, pushed me, refused to let me back to them. I could feel the magic in the wind, the intent. It wanted *me*.

Instinctively, as the wind roared in my ears and pulled at my clothes, I turned from it. I found my horse, perhaps my serendipity or Pali's training. I mounted, and tried to outrun the wind.

It was like being caught in a whirlwind. I was racing at the speed of sound, of wind, of magic, not knowing where or when I was. Night and day seemed to make no difference, if they passed at all. I would spy a building, or a tree, but once glimpsed it would vanish again.

Except that a whirlwind, I might have been able to dissipate or at least free myself from. But this storm was no natural phenomenon, that had grabbed me from my closest friends, and thrust me across three worlds. 

It smelled and tasted, this wind, only of Imperial magic. Not just the Schooled magic, as many citizens of the Empire might make, or buy. But that of the Ourantha, the feel of the chains of my childhood, the smell of ritual incense.

Maybe also the fear and anger that rose in equal portion, knowing the source, blinded me to an escape route. I, who had broken into the Palace of Stars, and out of countless places as well. I, who had escaped the bonds of my tower so many years ago, could now feel them closing back in. And I did not know how to throw them off a second time.

I found myself in a landscape I had only seen once before from this perspective. Grass-covered hills rolling to steep cliffs above a grey ocean. Sheep dotted the hills like clouds did in the sky. Just visible in the distance, an island with a tower sticking up into the blue-grey.

It was nearly low tide. The causeway would only be open for just enough time for me to run across. (I could feel the wind still, pushing me across, I did not want to swim it).

I dismounted, and turned to my horse, my friend. "I am sorry for these past few days, I haven't eaten either," I said, and she snorted and sniffed at me, "Make yourself at home, hopefully I won't be long."

The path down to the sea was a scramble more than anything. I arrived at the edge of the water just as it began to split with the ebb and flow of the tide receding. I ran, my eyes on my footing. Each step a little dryer as I reached half way. Each step a little wetter as I reached the island.

The wind was still behind me.

Then, just as abruptly as it had thrust me onwards, the magic caught me. I opened the door of my tower, and the magic slammed it shut behind me.

I turned around immediately and pulled on the handle. I was now much stronger from years of travel. But it did not budge. I ran through the tower, trying to open every window. None of them moved. I tried force, I tried magic, I even tried leavers. I tried smashing the glass.

From every angle I could feel the old sapphire necklace looking at me, reaching out. I had attached to it a lot of the magic that had ensnared me here. I didn’t want it back. I dodged it, kept as far away as possible. I could feel it reaching out, like a vine growing far too fast for nature, trying to grab me.

*Why now?*, I thought desperately, as I pounded the bathroom window with a chair. What had changed, made the magic strong enough to pull me in and trap me from so far away? My friends would surely follow… but what if I was still stuck inside, with them outside?

Eventually my panic subsided enough that I realised I needed to examine the trap more carefully. Trying to be as far away as possible from the necklace, I went to the empty bottom floor. I lent against the wall, and went into a deep trance.

The walls of my tower blazed in my sight. They were a constantly moving tapestry, a weaving of thousands upon thousands of tiny golden, white, and black threads. There was no door, no windows, in the magic version of the tower. I looked above and below, where it flowed seamlessly into the rock and the sky.

I tried to pull at the threads, cut them, burn them with fire. Every time I removed even the tiniest fragment, the hole closed immediately and the threads moved faster. How many wizards were weaving this spell? For surely this was a spell being actively renewed and worked on, unlike the necklace upstairs.

The necklace — now with so much more experience at magic than last time — I could see it more clearly. The threads running in a straight line, through walls physical and magical. It twitched, like it was dreaming. Maybe it was. Did it dream of all the stories I’d read in this tower?

I’d found no answers in my trance. Had I been tempted to stay there, in the world of magic? I was still trapped, either way, but in the trance my feet didn’t hurt and I didn’t feel so dirty. But if someone came, if my friends made it, I wanted to be able to see and hear them. So I came out of the trance, dazed and sweaty.

There would be food, I knew. But it would be next to the necklace, whatever strange ritual food the spirits of this place had made for me. So I had a bath instead. It was still a lovely bath, although I spent as much time as I could craning my neck to see out the window, hoping for a glimpse of Pali.

When I finally got out, my skin thoroughly wrinkled, twilight was turning to true night. I was exhausted, having used every physical and magical skill, and not knowing how long it had been since I had slept last. I put back on my underthings, too tired to try and rummage up anything else to sleep in.

I could pull all the bedclothes and cushions down from the bed (my bed, once) to the bottom floor. That felt safest, as far away as possible from the necklace. But I also wanted to be near a window, to see the sunlight (if not the sun), and any sign of my friends. Who were surely just hours behind me.

I chose to sleep in hope, and got into the bed. But I turned my back to the necklace, and faced the window. The sky was endless dark grey.

I slept deeply.
