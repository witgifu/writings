I woke up distressed and disoriented. There was a choir singing, and chanting. Some kind of awful hymn to the Empire. I would have preferred the anthem, at least that was rousing. The hymn was plodding, and the words fit awkwardly.

For a moment I thought I might have woken up after sleeping in a corner of a temple of the Sun. But that would have meant waking on hard marble floors, not silk sheets and a soft feather bed.

The curtains around the bed were pulled back, and you were ushered out to join, again, the priest-wizards. A semicircle of imposing grey and silver.

"If, if you would be so kind as to co-copy-my-movements, Glorious One," said a priest-wizard, who must have been more junior, not to be granted the mask to hide behind. They were transparently terrified. Not of me, so-called 'Terror of Astandalas', but of you, who had supposedly been trapped in a web of obedience spells all your life.

You copied their movements carefully. It wasn't hard, except that it was the inverse of the dawn ritual I had done in the tower at the edge of the world. Pulling in, instead of pushing out, gathering instead of flinging. I could feel the magic draining into me, like water. Water that refused to mix with the oil of my own magic, that should have been fire.

Calmed to a still lake by the 'snowfall' drugs. Trapped where I couldn't reach it, couldn't lash out and burn down this horrible place. So-called Palace of Stars. Those who had seen the stars dance in the Divine Realms knew it to be only an imitation made by mortals for their own aggrandisement.

The ritual finished, and you were led into another room. Feeling nauseous, although if the dawn ritual had made it worse was hard to know. These limbs and organs didn't feel like mine, they hardly felt like yours.

The cowering servants stripped you naked again, reflected in half a dozen mirrors. Presumably for your own self-admiration, since nobody was supposed to look the Emperor in the eye.

How much did they have to practise, not to accidentally do so, while dressing you? Was that why they turned their faces and ducked their heads and bowed? So much bowing. The process seemed interminable, just to get you into three layers of pure white linen robes. No cosmetics, no accessories.

Until one pompous man approached, with a large gold ring held in a pair of golden tongs. Baffled, you stretched out all your fingers. He slipped it on to the ring finger of my right hand. You looked down.

The imperial signet. If, no, *when*, I escaped, I would be taking that with me. What a glorious jest, to place it on the hand of Fitzroy Angursell. It was apparently not part of the Imperial Regalia, with which you would be crowned. With which they would *want* to crown you. We would be rescued first.

You nodded, and put your hands behind your back. 

The pompous man led you to a dinning room. A huge table, a dozen servers, one place setting. Your stomach twisted, food seemed inadvisable. Apparently, it was expected anyway.

You sat, and were presented with enough food for the entire Red Company, including the children. Cautiously, you selected a single piece of toast, nibbling it without adding even butter. There were fine cups of tea and coffee, you selected the former as safer on an uncertain stomach.

The number of watchers was unnerving. I was a performer, I knew the feel of a crowd. This one was wary, and neither you nor I had any idea how to turn them around. A joke, or a song, my usual approach, would reveal far too much.

"Would you please introduce yourselves?" you tried.

Everyone in the room froze. That had, obviously, not been the correct thing to say. You applied yourself to the toast again.

The pompous man reappeared, bowing low as he entered the frame of your vision. He waited, bent.

"Yes?" you said, not even knowing a name or title by which to address him.

"If it pleases your Imperial Serenity, I am your Groom of the Chamber, Lord Efrin no Poyon," he said, unbending slightly.

"Thank you," you said, and then awkwardly added, "Rise, please."

Lord Efrin unbent, and as he did so, I wondered if he had served Eritanyr. If he had done so willingly. How did a Groom of the Chamber get appointed? What did they do? Did you dare ask? 

In theory, you were the Emperor, and could do anything you wanted. Presumably the Ouranatha had fetched you hoping for a pliant, biddable, puppet Emperor. Was this Lord Efrin a part of that?

"Would you introduce me?" you said, politely.

There was a definite air of disapproval as Lord Efrin gestured to the first server. You couldn't tell if it was because of your turn of phrase, or that you had asked to know these peoples names. It was not, seemingly, such a horrifying request that he refused. Instead, he named each person, and their role, as they bowed deeply.

All men, all noble, and all *attendants*, not servants. You thought that distinction was probably important to them, and I did so treasure using the correct term. Lord Efrin did not introduce the guards stationed by the door. Possibly because he did not know their names and ranks.

You put down the half-eaten slice of toast, and finished the tea. The more awake you became, the more alert to how your body ached. Not in the good way, of having exerted oneself; in the painful way of being tied up by someone who didn't care one whit for your comfort. Or circulation.

Again came the priest-wizards. Surely the Emperor's life was more than as a ritual object, bent to their schedule. The thought of going back into that ritual chamber with the pillar of stone filled you with fear. It was not a heroic feeling, but you, at least, were no hero.

Your trembles were probably visible as they led you back there. Reformed the surrounding circle, and began chanting and waving again. They dabbed oil on your face and hands, and with each word and gesture you could feel the claws of their magic hooking into your skin. Scraping you, stinging as flesh exposed to air. Bruising your spirit and soul. You, and I, were powerless to stop them.

Time lost all meaning. There were bells, but you couldn't tell if they were time-bells or ritual bells. They clamoured in your head, bouncing like a ball in a bottle. You were glass, green glass like a wine bottle, rippling the light. Wobbling on an uneven base. Or was the floor moving? When the floor moved like waves, that was called an earthquake. If the stone pillar rippled, was that a stone-quake?

Something was tugging on you. A rope, perhaps, anchored in your chest. You moved to follow, but the priest-wizards behind you grabbed you by the shoulders, and held on fast. Was this my friends? Had they found some gap in the magic, some way to pull me through and away? I tried to shake off the priest-wizards, but I was too weak.

"No!" I think I called out, hoping they would hear me.

Then the thing broke. Ripped apart, like a wild beast shaking its prey. Suddenly you, I, were adrift. It wasn't like floating in water, or even flying on Pali's carpet. There was no down, no up, no directions at all. It was as if I was simply no longer held down by my own weight.

Since I had fallen from my tower into a portal between worlds, I had always had a sense of those boundaries. The liminal feeling of world brushing world, of the wood between. Even stronger, I had always known which was Ysthar. The others could be harder to pick, but Ysthar had always smelt of roses and called to me the strongest. It was gone.

You knew you were on Ysthar. The evidence of your eyes was clear, there was nowhere else in the Empire, or outside it, that could be like this. You remained in the ritual room, in the Palace of Stars, in Astandalas the Golden, on Ysthar. Logically, obviously.

That anchor was gone. I had no idea where I was, no sense at all. They had ripped that knowledge from me. It was disconcerting, disturbing, devastating. I had not even known it was a thing that could be lost, or taken.

More than any other time since you had woken on that ship, under their spells, I felt that I was not you. I watched you stumble, and the priest-wizards hold you up. From somewhere slightly above, and to the side. You shook for the rest of the ritual, but that didn't seem to be a problem. Alas, this strange floating did not come with any relief from the physical effects.

The last step of the ritual involved changing shoes. You had been wearing white leather sandals, presumably for the same reason you were dressed in white linen. The priest-wizards removed them with much ceremony, replacing them with gold sandals, studded with gems. Magic sandals, you could tell, as soon as they touched your feet. They weighed you down, pulled me down, like a child wheeling in a kite.

Apparently you needed to be detached, but not floating. No freedom of flight for you. No respite.

As you staggered out of the ritual room you realised that despite the multiple ridiculously ornate rooms, you had yet to see something you needed rather urgently.

"Lord Efrin," you said, hoping that he was one of the blurs in front of you, "The toilet?"

The blurs moved forward, and you followed.

"Through here, Glorious One," came Efron's pompous voice, and the ever-present guards opened the door for you. Hurrying in, it wasn't until you had sat down on the commode that you saw two guards had followed you inside.

I nearly giggled, with the thought that they might grab me, if I tried to flush myself away. It didn't seem worth raising a fuss over, when surely my friends would be here this day or the next, although it was deeply strange and confronting. Somehow more so than being changed and naked before strangers, or having them wash and anoint you.

You tried to act as if they weren't there, and this seemed to cause them no offence. And anyway, you needed to concentrate on not falling over. Or showing how much pain you were in.

It had never occurred to me that perhaps the Emperor was more closely kept than any of his prisoners.
