Again, you woke in a ridiculous bed. They had replaced it, as you requested, and it was now only pointlessly large, and not also clearly an instrument of Eritanyr's crimes.

The choir were the same, and the semi-circle of priest-wizards. You rose, and joined them. The gestures and motions were easier this time, although the rising nausea was not. Far more quickly your body became aware of the abuses it had been subjected to. Twinges of pain and a low-level hum of aching bones.

I tried not to think about how it might impair my ability to run, when Pali and Damian finally broke in.

The attendants, it would not do to think of them as servants, undressed you and redressed you again. They seemed to be very careful never to touch your skin, and you wondered if that was also some stupid part of this custom. This normal daily even turned into a ritual by pomp and ceremony.

Today you were bedazzled. Layer upon layer of silk shot with gold, and on top of it a prince's ransom in jewels. Well, you supposed, they did think you some kind of prince. Still, it seemed a little ridiculous to wear jewellery everywhere it was possible.

You jangled your way through the rooms that seemed to only exist to have to walk through. Breakfast was in the same room, again with far too much food on offer. 

Well, perhaps the leftovers were given to the poor of Astandalas. That was the custom, in some country estates on Ysthar, to make far more than one needed in order to give away the "extra". It seemed inefficient, but what did you know of economics. Only that it seemed to keep the poor where they were, and let the rich feel good about hording. 

You sighed, and sat down. From the corner of your eye, you glimpsed every attendant suddenly still. As if waiting for a blow. One was not, it seemed, allowed to sigh when sitting down. You noted this, and moved carefully and slowly as you reached for the teacup. When you appeared absorbed in the aroma, rich and dark, they all began to relax. You noted they were all the same as the day before, names and roles coming to you as they always had to me. 

As the bells rang the hour, Lord Efrin appeared.

"Glorious One," he said, performing the time-wasting series of bows that had his head nearly on the floor.

"Please rise," you said, when it became clear he was going to stay there until you responded. He did so with the grace of long practice.

"Glorious One," he repeated, "The Commander of your Imperial Guards has prepared them for your inspection."

An image came to my mind of me walking down a line of soldiers, and adjusting everyone's hats to be at a jaunty angle. That was most certainly not what they had in mind. Presumably it would be some kind of parade.

You were not led outside, where perhaps I could vanish into the gardens, but into a hall. A vague childhood memory of a picture of a horse dancing suggested it might have had an equestrian use at some point, as did the large doors. There were no horses today.

Before you were rank upon rank of men in white kilts with oiled chests. At the very back you spotted a few who might be women, granted the addition of some kind of chest covering. Although it appeared the fabric had been removed from their kilts, which were shorter. 

With a thud of hundreds of feet stamping in unison, they stood to attention, and saluted. You nodded, hoping that was the correct response. The Red House had no guards, as far as you knew.

The very first rank of guards were more decorated. Leopard pelts, gold tips to their spears, elaborate feathered headdresses. I remembered from our escapades that these were the inner guard. You stole a glance at the two men following you, who were dressed to match. Something of the drugs might be wearing off, you hoped, that you were able to notice such things now.

The world seemed less of a blur. Perhaps time would start behaving better too. It would help with planning my escape which I really ought to put more effort into.

A man approached, with a dark blue cape, edged in gold. The captain?

“Commander Daitch, Glorious One,” supplied Lord Efrin, with yet another bow.

Commander Daitch saluted.

“Commander,” you said, uncertain what came after that.

The Commander made a hand gesture, and the assembled guards released their salute. You looked out over them again. A thousand? Two thousand? Too many to count easily, few enough that you could see individuals even at the back.

Then it hit me. These were your guards. If I ran, these were the people tasked with stopping me. Hundreds of them, and they now all knew what you looked like.

We had fought regiments before, the Red Company and I. But since I had fallen into the Painted City I had always had allies. From the first night I performed, Damien and Jullanar had been watching out for me. The only place I had escaped alone was the tower of Harbut Zalarin, and that I had only escaped once. The second time the Empire had been far more cunning.

While I silently panicked, the guards marched in formation. Making an isle in the middle, they then marched down and out and around. Spears glinting, skin shinning, faces impassive.

Did they know this was a show of force against me? Did they intuit, with some skill that the guards hadn’t shown before, what I wanted to do?

It would be different, I told myself, when my magic returned. I was only an ordinary swordsman, a terrible spearman. The Emperor’s Dogs hadn’t made an appearance since you had arrived on Ysthar. Magic was my one true skill, and the luck of wild magic as well.

There were footsteps behind you, and hushed whispers. You carefully didn’t turn to look at them. I tried a whisper of magic to bring the conversation to me, and to my delight it worked.

Or possibly they just weren’t speaking as quietly as they should have, or the acoustics of the room were working in my favour. It didn’t *quite* feel like my magic.

“We require him,” said a voice you recognised as usually coming from behind a mask, "For a ritual."

“Your holiness we are very nearly done here,” said someone else, obsequiously.

I tried another whisper of magic, just a test, snaking out to see if I could learn anything of the magic around me. It practically snapped back to me, stinging. 

Suddenly you were struggling to breathe, tight bands around your chest constricting, pulling you to turn around, to face the priest-wizard. Not that he showed you his face.

Hundreds of men saluted behind you, as you were dragged off like a naughty child. This felt much more like the Marwn bindings, and instinctively I shrunk back and let you handle whatever was going to happen.

Through the maze of the Palace of Stars, and back to the Tower, you were dragged. Now, you were more aware of watching eyes. They seemed hungry, staring. Yet as your parade approached, they fell silently to the floor. 

The sick feeling in your stomach was more than just the constant nausea from the schooled magic wrapping you. It was also that hundreds of people felt the need to debase themselves before you. What had you done? Simply walked past them showing off. 

I hated every moment of it.
