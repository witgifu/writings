You drifted in and out of sleep, as exhaustion warred with renewed nausea. It seemed unlikely now that there would be any stops before the Palace of Stars. Others might have to wait at the gate between Zunidh and Ysthar, the soon-to-be Emperor would not.

You were proved correct, when you felt the dislocation of the gate and the coach did not even slow. You held your stomach as a wave of nausea rolled through you, but it seemed that with the drugs wearing off, it was not quite as bad.

I tried to access my magic. Nothing still. It would return with time, but it was hard to know how long. As long as a week, depending on how much they'd given you.

The maid returned at some point with more crackers and fresh water. You thanked her, but she said nothing and refused to look at you. Well, if she had worked for Eritanyr, her fear was justified.

It could have been days, or hours. With no windows and nothing else to mark the time. Eventually, the coach did slow. Presumably even the state coach of the Emperor had to pick its way through the outer slums of Astandalas. The middle-class markets, the hawkers that thronged the streets, even the walled compounds of the wealthiest, were surrounded by traffic.

You could *just* hear the sounds of the crowds outside. A rumble that was different from the rumble of the coach on the road. Was there anyone I knew out there? Would they have any idea who was in here?

I wanted to thump the walls, to yell, to scream for help. You held quiet.

*Your friends will have plans, if they are here,* you thought, *do not spoil them with recklessness. You don't even have your magic.*

I whined in the back, afraid.

The carriage wound its way through the streets, and I felt the textures of different roads through the wheels. Cobbles, stone, beautifully even pavements. On one of these, it came to a stop, and my heart lept. Had my friends held up the carriage, in the centre of Astandalas? Oh, what a story that would be!

No. There were trumpets, playing the Imperial Salute. The tune I had so cunningly mocked in _Donkey Ears_, now played honestly, earnestly, back at me. The priest-wizard came from the back, and two more entered through the front door. Faces turned to me, draped on the couch, still mostly focusing on not being sick.

“Come, my lord,” said one of the two new ones.

You scraped us off the couch, and up to standing. Trying to maintain some semblance of dignity, you stepped forward, and they back. So, weirdly, like a dance I half-remembered from somewhere on Voonra, they exited backwards, and you forward. 

In a smaller scale, it might have been called a coach-gate, this building. An arch of pure white stone, reaching up three or four floors, curving over and behind us. To each end, little glimpses of sky. Before, two black doors, with a gold sun-in-glory.

Four steps up, but you would not, it seemed, be permitted to walk them. No, for a few paces in front of you was a palanquin. Had the illness been reported already? Did they not trust you to walk even a dozen paces? 

To both sides, ranks of silver robes. The highest of the priest-wizards wore masks, and you could see the full count before you. Without magic, without my friends, dizzy with sickness, you complied. You walked the few steps to the golden box with silk curtains, and climbed inside.

Inside, piles of yellow silk cushions. Two gold rings that I hoped were hand-holds. You grasped one as the palanquin was lifted, and you rocked, unsteady with unfamiliar motion. 

Outside the box was silence. Was that some magical treatment to the transport? I couldn't tell, with only ordinary senses. The ride, again, seemed interminable.

When at last the box was set down, and the curtains drawn back, you dragged yourself out. Three wizards, six guards. A white room, with two sets of doors, and nothing else. Did it exist purely for getting in and out of the box? What a waste.

Two of the guards stamped the butts of their spears on the floor, and I flinched at the sudden loud noise. It must have been some signal, for the doors before you opened. Then beyond that, more and more doors, like some parody of a journey to the afterlife.

You took us forward. At the final set of doors I paused. I hadn't noticed the decor in the previous rooms, except that I was now standing in a hall of mirrors. Looking in horror at the next room.

“What is this?” I said, my voice hoarse and cracking.

“The Imperial Study, Glorious One,” said one of the masked priest-wizards, with a bow.

Desperately I tried to recall the descriptions of the Palace of Stars. This was… the Imperial Apartments. Decorated, very clearly, to my late uncle's taste. 

“It's disgusting,” I said. If it had only been a matter of style, of fashion, well I hoped to be rescued soon. But surely nobody wanted to live in rooms like this. It made me hesitant to touch any surface.

“Glorious One,” said one of the priest-wizards, “Your seneschal will—”

“I won't go any further until the pornography is stripped out,” I said, waving my hand at the tapestries. I had no idea if this would work, you had no script for this. You wanted to try to ignore it, to simply walk past the crimes depicted. I held firm.

I waited, which was not my natural inclination. Some silent communication must have gone on that I didn't see, because the doors closed in front of me, and there was suddenly a lot of noise in the room beyond. The efficiency was impressive.

“Glorious One,” (what a ludicrous title) said one of the wizards, indicating a door to the side. It must have been previously concealed behind the mirrors. Whatever they wanted was apparently important enough that they were willing to take their pet through the back passages of the Imperial Apartments.

There were six guards, three wizards, I had no magic and still felt ill. I let you take us through the door. 

We wound our way through narrow halls and tight stairs, surprising not a few staff. All of them pulled themselves into doorways or corners, bowing as low as they could and averting their eyes. I felt like I was a full chamber pot being carried along, the way everyone avoided me.

Eventually we arrived in a room that contained a towering pillar of stone. It disappeared into the floor. Even with my magic disabled, I could feel a tension, something heavy in the air. Like a brewing storm, or—

There was a permanent schooled magic circle laid into the floor, and several more wizards stood around it, holding incense, and gold pots of various powders. It was what I had first run from, first rebelled against. I hated schooled magic, and it had enslaved me for my entire childhood. Mind and body, bent to the cause of the Empire.

You walked over calmly, and stood in the circle.

*They probably think they needed to remove the Marwn bindings*, you reasoned. We both hoped they wouldn't notice they were no longer attached to me.

It was no less awful without access to my magic. Every action, every word, grated against the fibres of my being. Their work also made me feel a bit hazy, disconnected from the world. I only realised that was deliberate, that they were doing something like the Marwn bindings, when they grabbed my arm, and I was unable to pull it back. A ritual silver knife in one hand, the wizard made a thin slice across the palm of my hand. They pulled my arm out of the edge of the circle, and touched my bleeding hand to the stone.

At once, three things happened. A bright, golden light, burst out from the stone where my hand touched it.

There was a shake, like when a heavy door is shut, or a portcullis dropped, but if one had such a thing the size of the Palace itself. No sound came with it, and nothing actually moved, so it wasn't an earthquake. 

I collapsed to the floor. It was agony such as I had never known. Cold, and hot, burning, biting, tearing. Stretching me like a drum-skin, and I thought I would burst. I wanted to pass out, I begged whatever gods might be there that I would pass out.

One of the wizards, not the one who had cut me, pulled me back up. Two of them held me, while the rest finished something. I had no control over my body, it was shaking, my head lolling. Shortly, I lost control of my bladder.

When the ritual concluded, I did, finally, loose consciousness.

You woke, looking up at a white ceiling. The pain had subsided to the point where it reminded me of when I had burnt myself when attempting to help Jullanar in the kitchen. Inside my bones, rather than on my hand. You felt different. You lay there, trying to work out what, apart from the pain, and the background nausea, and the raging anxiety, was different.

Clean, you were clean. I'd last washed in a river, in the Silver Forest. It had been some days, I didn't know how many, since then. Someone had washed your body, while you were unconscious. It was a disconcerting revelation, although I can't say I was entirely disappointed not to have woken up in my own piss.

Struggling to sit up, the room was large enough for a small town hall. Every wall was blank white stone, and the floor was black marble. A small group of people on the left were prostrated on the floor. You were in a bed, silk sheets, bees embroidered on the quilt. Was this…?

“Was this Eritanyr's bed?” you asked, trying to keep any accusation out of your voice.

“Glorious One, it is the Imperial Bed.” Who answered you was hard to tell, given only the guards by the door were standing, and it wasn't them.

Disgusted, I looked over it again. Yes, there were… restraint points. Shuddering, you let me scramble to the edge.

One of the crowd slid forward toward you, keeping their face to the floor, which seemed like an art. They were holding out a pair of slippers, black with gold embroidery. Suddenly I realised why they hadn't simply walked over.

“You may all rise,” you said, and I wanted to throw up. Eritanyr would have enjoyed making people do this kind of performance. A waste of time and labour. I was perfectly capable of putting on slippers!

They did stand, although none of them would look me in the eye. They were all carefully looking at my waist, or possibly my hands. Part of me wanted to check if my fly was undone, but no, I was wearing a silk nightgown. None of them said anything.

Shortly, the priest-wizards arrived. Ignoring the strange tableau we must have made, the men watching my body, and you, leaning on the edge of the bed for support, the pain still thrumming in my bones. You failed to stop me from shivering at the sight of them, anxious that they would take me and do the same again.

“The Glorious One is cold,” said the priest-wizard with the golden mask.

The men in front of me all bowed low, and two of them dashed into the door behind the group. They brought forth a heavy, quilted dressing gown. As if you were an old man who complained at every imagined draft of air. Had Eritanyr? Was this robe his? They approached you slowly, clearly waiting for some action on your part.

I supposed they wanted to put it on you, but you allowed my disinclination. Instead, I said, “Replace the bed.”

It was hard to spot shock, given none of them would meet your eyes anyway. It was probably a shocking thing to say, to want to sweep away everything of the previous Emperor. However, they offered no protest.

You turned to the priest-wizards, and waited for them to speak again.

“Glorious One,” said the golden-masked one, with a deep bow, “The rituals of cleansing must begin immediately.”

Without any protest that you had been washed, you followed them, stumbling. Trying to remember if the rituals of your childhood had included baths. Certainly some had required baths _beforehand_, as had some of the rituals of your tower. 

This was, it turned out, a ritual of bathing. I had never had such an uncomfortable experience in such a beautiful bathing suite. You were perhaps a little distracted from taking it in. The servants stripped you, while you stood in the middle of a circle of robed and masked men (although perhaps some were not men, the garments were shapeless). I had never felt so _naked_ in my life, you held still.

They painted you with oil, from the top of your head to your toes, chanting the entire time, and waving incense. Then the oil was ceremonially scraped off. I was tired, and sore, and wanted to sit, or lie down. You managed to hold yourself in place, though you wobbled a little.

Servants took over again, covering you in depilatory cream and other unguents. Only your eyebrows and eyelashes were spared. From the downy hairs on your forehead, to every pubic hair, and right on to the weird hairs that squiggled out of your big toes. You let them, while I considered what the whole body itch was going to be like when it regrew.

You would learn better, later.

Then came steam, scented with perfumes and incense. The priest-wizards were back with censers and wands of various metals. Perhaps there was some drug in them, because the rest of the process became a blur. Slowly you returned, to find us floating in a pool. Or was it a bath? I looked at the ceiling, trying not to think if the sparkling tiles were actually bejewelled.

Someone coughed, from the side, and you pulled us up to standing. A servant, holding a towel. You were dried, and anointed, and perfumed. Then dressed in fine robes of while, and gold, and black, and… Imperial yellow. I wished for scarlet, and brilliant blue. You didn't let us express that wish, undoubtably wise.

_My friends will come,_ I thought, _We can survive this for a few days, and then what a great story it will make._
