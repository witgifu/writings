When you came to, you were lying on your side. It was dark, and you were on something soft. 

You tried moving. You could wiggle your fingertips, blink, move your head a little, and breathe properly. They must have loosened the spell slightly. 

Whatever you were lying on was rocking. No, it was moving, swaying, as a boat. But it didn’t sound right. I’d been on boats and ships before. You couldn’t hear any of the right sounds. Instead, there was a constant low-level roar. A strange, special ship of some kind. Maybe they were using magic to fill the sails.

*My* magic itched. It didn’t like the bindings you were under. You didn’t like them either. But for now, you had to endure.

You would surely have to disembark at some point to go through at gate between worlds. Jullanar was the one who was good with maps. You tried to remember the official gates. Cholhélhé had a sea-gate, to Zundith. The main gate to Ysthar from Zundith was on land, so presumably you’d get off the ship before that.

You looked around the room. You were lying in a box bed, curled up on your side, like a child. It wasn't like you would have fit stretched out properly anyway, the bed was short. Not unusual on a ship where space was precious.

The bedding felt soft and smooth under your fingertips. Not silk, but fine, dense linen. You were naked, under the sheet, but not cold. You could feel some weight on you, which must be blankets. There was no smell of wool, so you wondered what they could be made of.

Directly in front of your face was a side table, or maybe a small set of drawers. The dark wood was polished to a mirror-like shine. On it was a basin and ewer, golden but presumably brass, on top of a plush towel. Nothing personal, nothing magical, nothing to indicate who used this room when it wasn’t for prisoner transport.

There was a tiny round window, you could just see if you turned your head all the way to your right. It had a gauzy curtain in front of it, but you could at least tell it was daylight outside. The mage light inside blurred all the shadows from the sun. You had no idea what time it was.

Had it been hours? Had it been days? You had no way of knowing. Were you still on Colhélhé? It was hard to tell immediately, I was so tied up in magic, and on a boat. My magic disliked water, and curled away from it. I sent out a tiny tendril, and dipped it into the water, like dipping a toe into a cold pool. It was Colhélhé. 

But… I could also feel another world nearby. A gate, but not my preferred wild gates. A schooled magic gate. Towering with the power of Astandalas, it roared in my magical senses like a waterfall flush with monsoon rains. We would not be making land anywhere on Colhélhé. It would be the gate to Zunidh.

Even the fastest ship, pushed by magical winds, would take at least a day to get from the tower at the edge of the world to the gate to Zunidh. So… I had passed the first milestone. My friends would know something was very wrong (if the terrible magical wind hadn't given it away). I wouldn't make it back to camp in three days. 

Would they wait three weeks at the safe house? It had never been tested before, we had all made it back in less than two. Would they know they needed to come looking for me? That I was trapped? Would they come? How would they know where?

I had never managed to shake off the magic that had prevented me from telling my friends who I had been before I had met them. I had dropped hints, but since I couldn't talk about even the existence of the Marwn, it was hard. I also didn't know my birth name, or rank. It's hard to hint that you *might* be an imperial duke, when you don't even know yourself. I never denied the resemblance, however much it irked me.

You tried to push down the anxiety that was once again rising.

The door across the table, and the wall at your feet, were whitewashed wood. There was a shelf on the wall, painted black, with a good lip to keep things contained. Above it, stuck to the wall, the official portrait of Eritanyr, the bastard. No doubt then who had trapped us, then.

The portrait was not one of the cheap pasteboard versions, that were often copied from memory or coins. Not a trinket quickly made for a pious labourer. This was clearly a fair copy of the original, if small.

Your mind drifted back to a memory. Looking up at your tutor as you looked along the portraits of your illustrious ancestors, lined up down the upper hall of the house where you (we) were raised. Each with its own small mage light, each a kind of shrine.

“But,” you had asked, looking up at your uncle’s golden eyes, “why do they all have gold eyes? Are their eyes really gold?”

“They are covered for our protection. To look on the eyes of the Glorious and Illustrious One, is treason,” your tutor had said softly, and then quickly added, “Glory to Astandalas and the Sun-on-Earth.”

You had softly echoed the ritual words. The lies you had been trained in.

You examined the portrait again. Someone had tucked a white handkerchief around the frame. Your first thought was it was a rather sacrilegious thing for someone to keep their handkerchief wedged into the Emperor’s portrait.

But then you remembered reading a (dry, tedious) account of the coronation of Eritanyr. And how in the week before all the portraits of Empress Anyoë had been draped in white, for mourning. 

Eritanyr was dead.

Part of me relished the thought. I hoped it had been violent and painful, and he’d died feeling some of the suffering he had dolled out to others. That at least in death there had been some justice, some vengeance. But it was probably an aneurysm brought on by too much wine and red meat.

But if Eritanyr was dead… then Shallyr was Emperor of Astandalas. Fuck.

Shallyr wanted, like every Emperor, to outdo those that came before. More conquests, or more buildings or, in the case of Shallyr, more cruelty and debauchery. And he personally hated me.

I supposed the feeling was mutual. But I mostly wanted to shame and mock him. Well, I guess wanted him dead, but I didn’t want to personally torture him to death. I *had* written songs mocking him. And now he had caught me.

No, he hadn't caught me. The priest-wizards had come for the Marwn, not Fitzroy Angersell. What did Shallyr want with the Marwn? Was the Marwn a secret part of the coronation ceremonies? But surely the balance of magic would be better if the Marwn was at the edge, in their prison.

My thoughts on the balancing required for Schooled magic were interrupted by the unexpected turning of the door handle. You closed your eyes.

“You can open your eyes, my lord, I can tell you’re awake,” the voice was old, and tired.

You opened your eyes. Obedient. Ignorant. A true worshipper of the Sun-on-Earth. 

*Ah,* I thought, *this is why you are here.*

*Yes,* you thought back, *quiet.*

The person who came in wore the silver robes, and a silver mask. Their hands were all you could see, small, and thick-fingered, spotted with age.

“Isn’t that better, my lord?” they asked, with a voice like one might use on a small child who was scared to meet a stranger.

They couldn’t possibly think you were stupid. Surely they had the reports from your old tutor. But, you conceded, they would expect you to be ignorant. When I had first escaped the tower I had been confused by my shadow and thought a dozen people a crowd. They would expect you to need to have everything explained, be shown how to do anything they needed.

You couldn’t nod, or say anything in response. You blinked, as if you were confused.

“Don’t worry, my lord, we’ll explain everything soon. This is for your safety, travel can be very dangerous.”

I wanted to laugh in response. I’d surely been in more dangerous situations in the past month than this priest-wizard had their whole life. I’d probably found more gates between worlds than they’d had birthdays. Something of my feelings crept into your breathing, and it stuttered.

The priest-wizard got entirely the wrong idea, thankfully. “Don’t worry, my lord, we are here for your protection. And we are travelling as fast as possible.”

You couldn’t see a smug smile, because of the mask, but you were sure it was there. Conceited prick.

They had at least know how to place a good sleep spell, you hadn’t woken up starving and covered in excrement. I tried to be thankful for small mercies, but it actually made me more worried. Would they continue knocking you out for almost all the time? How could you get away if they only gave you minutes, and you were only able to move your fingertips and your head a little?

As my fear rose and panic worsened, your breath became fast and ragged. The magic didn't like this, and tightened on you. That only made you breathe faster, as a wave of terror at this magic washed over you. It tightened again.

The priest-wizard said something, you couldn't hear what, but suddenly the pressure on your lungs was gone. You coughed and coughed, but you could breathe now. Air, stale from below-decks on a ship, and salty, but as much as you wanted. They stepped closer, examining me through a monocle that shimmered golden. Nodding, they said, “No harm done, my lord.”

You blinked, your breathing still ragged. No harm to you? No harm to the spells trapping you? You didn't dare ask.

“We will be going through a Gate soon, my lord, which will interfere with your protections,” they said, still looking you over.

I wanted to make a snide remark about how spells that choked me were hardly *protections*, but you resisted. The Marwn would believe them. The Marwn would trust them. You nodded.

“It would be safest if I release them now,” they added, quietly.

Hope sprang up in my heart. If I could crawl out the tiny window into the sea, I would. If I couldn't, maybe I could find some other way off this ship, while everyone was being distracted by the Gate. I might be able to escape, if they believed you were docile and biddable.

They pulled a small vial out of a pocket. “This will make sure the passage doesn't make you sick, my lord.”

Oh, they were going to swap magic for some kind of drug. I felt revolted. Trying to pass it off as sea-sickness prevention as well. Typical high-handed Imperial nonsense.

But, my chances of escape would be much better on land. There wasn't much chance of escape at the sea gate. On both the Cholhélhé side and the Zunidh side were vast stretches of empty ocean that I had no idea how to navigate. And I didn't have a boat.

So when the priest-wizard dropped the spells a little so you could drink, you did. I thought to avoid swallowing as much as possible, but the priest-wizard watched you closely. I needed to have my eventual escape be as surprising as possible, and not give any grounds for suspicion. So you swallowed it all.

The priest-wizard watched you while it took effect. The spell had been instant, but the drug was much slower. You could feel your senses fading out, as the world turned quiet, and grey, and soft. Like snowfall. Then finally you couldn't feel anything at all.
