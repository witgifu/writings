In her seat in the cyclo, Sister Lilly chewed her lip, and twisted her robes in her hands, sick with worry about Cindy. After a few moments, her seat-mate cleared their throat.

“Would you happen to know if the house has a back door, Sister?” they asked, politely.

Sister Lilly was slightly taken by surprise. “Oh yes, it does,” she replied, “It goes out into the alley at the back.”

“Could you point it out to me when we get near, Sister? It might be prudent for me to enter via the back door,” they asked again.

“Of course, not a problem at all,” said Sister Lilly, her mind full of fear of what was happening to Cindy, and only half listening.

This late, the streets were empty. Anybody who was anybody was at the ball at the Procurator's residence. And anyone else was taking the chance to have an early night. When the ball finished, the streets would be full of cyclos taking people home, but for now, all the light was sucked out.

It felt like an eternity before they turned near Cindy's street. But of course, they had actually made very good time. Sister Lilly pointed out the back door to the servant, who gave a polite thanks and jumped out of the cyclo.

Approaching the front door, Sister Lilly could see Aiden helping Mathias. He didn't seem to have had any more accidents, but she suspected he smelt awful. The Procurator stepped out of their cyclo, thanked the driver, and left the Bishop to get out on his own.

The Procurator approached Mathias. “Keys?” they said, and held out their hand. Mathias fumbled in his pockets for a few moments, before dragging out a set of keys. Procurator Julius snatched them out of Mathias hand, and strode up the stairs to the front door.

The Bishop followed the Procurator, with a bit less spring in his step. Sister Lilly followed the Bishop. Aiden stayed at the bottom of the stairs, holding up Mathias, encouraging him onto the first step.

The Procurator looked thoughtfully at the lock, and the pile of keys in their hand. They picked up one, jammed it in the hole, and turned. Success. They pushed hard on the heavy door, and it swung open.

Lit by a single oil lamp on the table, the hall made a dramatic tableau. At the back, mostly in shadow, were two men who had clearly been chosen for strength rather than style. In the centre of the hall, kneeling on the rose roundel in the mosaic floor, was Cindy. Tears pouring down her face, still dressed in the beautiful indigo gown, but now her hair had come down in straggling tendrils around her face. She held her hands up and out, as if in prayer before an altar. In front, and to the side, stood Ana, one hand raised and holding a thin rattan cane.

Ana spun round. She was obviously expecting to see her husband, but her look of sweet supplication was rapidly replaced with confused anger. “What are you doing bursting into my house!” she demanded.

Sister Lilly noticed the two men in the back step slowly backwards, and towards a corridor. They had clearly decided that they were not getting paid enough to get *caught*, and were choosing to make a timely exit.

Procurator Julius did not smile, but stepped further into the house, allowing the Bishop and Sister Lilly to enter and flank them. “I am here to take custody of Lucinda Roy from Mathias Roy,” they said sternly, and then pocketing the keys added, “he let me in.”

“I wasn't consulted,” spat Ana.

“You don't need to be,” said the Procurator, coolly.

The Bishop coughed. “Their Excellency is correct, Lucinda is a minor solely in the control of her father.”

At this point, Aiden had finally managed to get Mathias up the stairs, and he came staggering through the door. Everyone turned to him, and he leaned heavily on the hall wall.

“Is this true, my love?” asked Ana, her voice all sweetness, “Are you sending Lucinda away?”

Mathias went to nod, decided that was risky, and just said “Yup.”

The Procurator clapped their hands together, “Then that's settled,” they said, and turned to Sister Lilly, “Would you mind helping Lucinda pack anything she would like to take with her?”

Sister Lilly bowed slightly, and walked over to Cindy. Cindy dropped her hands, and Sister Lilly supported her to her feet. “I… I don't have anything,” whispered Cindy. 

Sister Lilly gave her a small hug, and said, “Well, that makes it easy to pack then,” and gave Cindy a small smile. Cindy tried to smile back, but faltered, and another tear ran down her cheek.

The two burly men returned to the room, with their hands raised. Behind them came the Procurator's servant waving a nasty looking dagger. Sister Lilly mentally upgraded them from *servant* to *bodyguard*.

“Anything you need from these gentlemen, your Excellency?” the bodyguard asked, “I found them trying to sneak out the back door.”

“Not today, thank you,” said the Procurator, “but Aiden, could you take down their names and addresses in case we need to reach them later?”

Aiden rustled a notebook and pen out of his pocket, and went over to stand next to the bodyguard, who did not move. Aiden began politely inquiring after their names and scribbling down the answers.

“Then I think we are done here,” said the Procurator, and turned to the Bishop, “Would you like to return to the party, or I can take you home?” 

The Bishop looked a bit confused, but said, “Home I think, I was getting a bit tired anyway.”

Sister Lilly held Cindy's arm tightly, and steered her around Ana, giving wide berth. They then stepped together down the stairs, and into the night.

“Will you see Lucinda to the cloister?” asked the Procurator, stepping into the cyclo, and sitting down next to the Bishop.

“Of course, your Excellency,” said Sister Lilly, “We will take good care of her for you.”

When they arrived back at the cloister, only Mother Grace was still up. “Is everything all right?” she asked, when they opened the door.

“It was a bit scary,” said Sister Lilly, “and I think Cindy's hands could do with some first aid.”

Mother Grace shooed them into the little medical room, next to the refectory. She motioned Cindy to sit on the bed. Cindy sat down, trying to hide her hands in the folds of her skirt.

“Let me see dear,” said Mother Grace gently, “best to get injuries treated right away.”

Slowly, with Sister Lilly nodding encouragement, Cindy held out her hands flat. To her credit, Mother Grace didn't make a sound, but Sister Lilly noticed the furrows on her brow deepen. “The cuts on this hand have re-opened, and will need iodine,” said Mother Grace gently, “and let's get a clove balm on both of them to help with the pain.”

Cindy bit her lip and nodded. Very, very gently, Mother Grace dabbed iodine on the cuts, but Cindy didn't flinch, holding perfectly still.

“You're very strong,” said Sister Lilly, trying to be encouraging.

“I put the iodine on myself the first time,” said Cindy, in a matter-of-fact way, “it's a bit easier if someone else does it.”

Mother Grace got down the little pot of clove oil balm, and pulled out a generous lump. But then she very gently slid it along Cindy's hands, so only the balm touched her hands, and there was no rubbing.

“Let's cover them up too, so you don't cover your sheets in balm in your sleep,” said Mother Grace, and pulled a pair of thin cotton gloves from a drawer. She helped Cindy get them on, slowly and carefully.

“And now,” announced Sister Lilly, “I think it's bedtime.”

Sister Lilly found Cindy a spare nightgown, and showed her to one of the guest rooms. “This is where the fancy ladies stay,” she said, winking, “and since you're going to be a fancy lady soon, you get one too.” She pointed into the little room, with a small but comfortable looking bed, and its own washstand.

Cindy hesitated.

“Unless you'd rather not sleep alone?” asked Sister Lilly.

“It's not that… it's just...” said Cindy, unsure of what to say. “Thank you. I didn't get to thank anyone, and you've all been so good to me, and I really don't deserve it.” Another tear rolled down her cheek.

Sister Lilly gently held both of Cindy's arms, and looked her deep in the eyes. “It's not about deserving or undeserving,” she said, “I love you like a baby sister. I want you to be free.”
