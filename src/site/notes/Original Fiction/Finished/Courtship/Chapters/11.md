Cindy looked down again at her dress, and the stars sparkled with the yellow light of the cyclo driver's lamp. It had been such a whirlwind, it seemed unreal, like a dream where you go from room to room and everything keeps changing. But as the cool breeze of the cyclo's movement brushed over her, she thought *this is real*.

But then out from a side street came a dark shape, or multiple, Cindy wasn't sure. They rushed at the cyclo driver, who yelled “Oi!” and tried to swerve around them. The cyclo tilted, and came to a shuddering halt. Hands grabbed at Cindy, and she tried to wiggle free.

And then a voice came from behind the cyclo, “I don't think you've been a good girl, have you?” and Cindy froze.

It was Ana. Cindy's mind went completely blank. The two men who had assaulted the cyclo pulled her out of the seat by the arms. She could see the cyclo driver lying on the road. He moved a little, and one of the men holding her kicked him.

Cindy wanted to struggle, wanted to run, but her body wasn't listening. Her heart was pounding, her mouth was dry, but she felt like she was pinned down by Ana's gaze, like a bug on a collector's card. Her body knew, from many years, that struggling would only make things worse.

“Did you think I wouldn't recognise you? Waltzing in with your accomplice? And now trying to sneak back before I got home? Despicable,” said Ana, her voice soft.

Cindy did not look her in the eye, and kept her gaze firmly on the ground. She didn't answer, didn't apologise. Cindy knew Ana's moods instinctively, and while sometimes an immediate apology or confession could blunt Ana's anger, this was not that time.

“Get her into the trailer,” said Ana to the two men holding Cindy. Methodically, like it was a usual thing to tie up another person, they tied her hands behind her back with her own gloves. Then wrapped and tied a long length of rope around her skirts to keep her legs together. One of them grabbed her jaw and pulled her mouth open, shoving an old sock in, before bandaging her mouth closed. 

They picked her up together, and tossed her into the wooden base of a cycle trailer, like she was a very large bag of flour. Then they spread a canvas over her, as if to keep off the rain.

She could hear Ana talking to them, but couldn't quite make out the words. Then she felt the trailer sway slightly, as the driver got into his seat. With a squeak of cheap gears, they were off.

Unable to brace herself against the edge of the cart, she rolled whenever they turned a corner. Cindy tried to think only of here, and now, but her mind kept drifting to what would happen when she got home. She had, a few years ago, fallen asleep while Ana was out late. When Ana returned to find her dozing in the parlour, Ana had woken her father. Ana had held her down on the hall bench, while her father caned Cindy on the legs eight times. And then, since *slothfulness casts a deep sleep*, she was forced to scrub all the downstairs floors through the night.

Eventually, the driver stopped. He waited, and after a few minutes his accomplice came jogging up. “Had to drop her at the front, didn't I?” he said, presumably to an angry look or gesture from the trailer driver. Together the two of them removed the canvas, and looking out, Cindy could see she was in the back alley of her home.

The two men picked her up again, and carried her through the kitchen, the back hall, and into the front hall. They lowered her to the ground, none too gently. Cindy could hear Ana's foot tapping on the polished tile floor.

“You can untie her now,” said Ana, coolly. And the two men did just that, removing the gloves, rope, bandage, and finally the disgusting old sock. They rolled her around to get everything off, and left her on her back, staring up at the ceiling and their faces.

*Tap, tap, tap*. Ana walked over.

Cindy was torn between staying where she had been left, and rolling over to prostrate herself before Ana's wrath. But a fresh wave of the terror that had frozen her before ran through Cindy's body, and stuck her in place.

Ana's face appeared in Cindy's view. “I should wait until your father returns,” she said softly, “so he may whip you, as you are *his* daughter.”

She paused, bending down to look at Cindy's face more closely. “But to delay justice is injustice,” she added.

Sister Lilly and Procurator Julius were enjoying a pleasant silence. They would have to rejoin the party shortly, but it was nice for a moment to be away from the noise and the lights.

Their quiet was interrupted by Aiden, when he opened the door. “The girl you were with, Sister Lilly, has been kidnapped. The cyclo driver was attacked, he's down in the kitchen being patched up.”

Sister Lilly stood up, pushing her chair back so fast it fell. She bolted out the door, pushing past Aiden, and back out into the main hall. Ignoring any confused (or drunk) looks she might have got, she ran to the back hall, and down to the kitchen.

Through the approaching open door she heard, “I think my foot just got caught and twisted, nothing broken.” It was indeed the voice of her cyclo driver. She turned into the room, and there was the driver, his foot up on a chair, another driver hovering behind him, and a medical attendant bending down and prodding the leg in question.

“I'm so sorry,” said the driver, when he saw who had come running in, “It were two against one, and they were big fellas.”

Sister Lilly walked over, and grasped his shoulder gently. “I don't blame you at all,” she said, “I should have gone with you both. Perhaps with a nun there they might have had second thoughts… Did they say anything about why they wanted her?”

The driver nodded, “There was some woman with them, fancy sounding. Maybe her mother, from what she said?” he replied.

Sister Lilly winced. Somehow Ana had recognised Cindy, and thought she was at the ball for her own amusement. Well, at least that narrowed down where Cindy had been taken.

More footsteps running down the hall. Sister Lilly turned, and the Procurator stepped through the door. “Do you know where she is?” they said to the room.

The poor cyclo driver recognised who had come in, and tried to stand up. “No. your honour,” he said. Sister Lilly pushed down on his shoulder, to keep him in the chair. “Yes, your Excellency,” she said.

Everyone looked at Sister Lilly. The Procurator expectant, the cyclo driver confused, the second driver and medical attendant interested. “Ana has taken her home,” she said softly.

Procurator Julius swore. Sister Lilly was surprised, she'd never seen them lose their cool for so much as a second. 

But then they transformed back into cold efficiency. The Procurator pointed at the other cyclo driver, “Secretary Aiden should be at his desk, the two of you find Mathias Roy and bring him round to the front door. I imagine he'll be trying to drink himself blind in the garden.” The man bowed, and slipped out of the room, past the Procurator in the doorway.

“Sister Lilly,” continued the Procurator, “find Marcus and tell them to keep a discreet eye on Ana Fortin's daughters. They must not leave the party. Then meet me at the front door.” 

“Yes, your Excellency”, said Sister Lilly, and slipped into the corridor. Behind her, she heard the Procurator say, “Now get this man up to a bedroom, he's done a hero's job getting back to raise the alarm, he needs a proper rest.”

Marcus was no longer in the front hall, nor could Sister Lilly spot them among the still-full dance floor. She tried to walk quickly, but not too quickly. A determined walk, not a rush. Beyond the ballroom, the card tables had filled out. And Sister Lilly spotted Marcus, perched on the edge of a spindly table, commentating on the game next to them to a large man with an unfortunate moustache.

Sister Lilly walked over slowly, staring at them, willing Marcus to look up and catch her eye. But they were focused on the game. Eventually, she had to interrupt, “Excuse me, your Excellency, would you be able to spare a moment?”

Marcus raised an eyebrow at the honorific, but put down their drink and promised to their companions to be back in just a moment. They walked away just far enough to not be overheard immediately.

Sister Lilly bowed slightly, and Marcus gave her a withering look, “What is going *on*?” they said.

“Their Excellency, the Procurator, requests that you keep an eye on Ana Fortin's daughters. They must not leave the party,” she said, in her best neutral diplomatic voice.

Marcus gave a cheeky grin, “All right,” they said, “my cousin's wish is my command. But you better let me in on why later.” Marcus waved her off, and then left behind their drink and card game. They picked up a flustered looking young gentleman, and headed into the ballroom.

Sister Lilly went back through the ballroom too, again trying to not walk too fast. She bumped into a pair of girls who were trying to waltz but were clearly a bit tipsy. All three apologised, and Sister Lilly managed to untangle herself without either of the girls falling over.

Out the front door, Sister Lilly looked left and right for the Procurator. She spotted Mathias Roy being sick in a bush, Aiden and the cyclo driver keeping a close eye on him. How had he possibly drunk so much between when she'd last seen him and now to be sick? Sister Lilly's already exceedingly low opinion of Mathias dropped even further.

Procurator Julius appeared through the library french doors. They were no longer dressed as a slightly comical historical general, but instead had on their regular official robes. Behind them came their servant, who Sister Lilly hadn't seen since the Procurator's arrival. And then the Bishop, who was dressed as a drummer from a marching band.

“Does it need to be now, your Excellency? Couldn't this wait until morning?” pled the Bishop, adjusting their toy drum as they trotted to keep up with the Procurator.

“If I waited until tomorrow, I would be asking the doctor for an autopsy,” replied the Procurator, not looking back.

Suddenly it hit Sister Lilly just how much danger Cindy was in. If Ana found out what Mathias had agreed to, her rage would know no limits. Cindy sneaking out was one thing, Cindy out of Ana and Andre's control was another.

The Procurator looked at the cyclo driver with Mathias. “Can you find two more drivers?” they asked, and added “Double pay for whoever takes him,” and pointed to Mathias, “we're taking him home.”

The driver bowed, and hurried off to a knot of drivers playing cards on the ground. Two of them hurried to their feet and all three went to get their vehicles. Moments later they returned.

Aiden prodded Mathias into a seat, and sighed before sitting next to him. The Procurator and the Bishop took the nicest looking vehicle, and then Sister Lilly and the servant were left with the last one.

All three rolled out, in a tidy procession.
