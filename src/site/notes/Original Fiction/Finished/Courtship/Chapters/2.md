It had been a miserable winter morning, with the kind of mist that you wish would just give up and turn into proper rain. Sister Lilly had been volunteered to be “spiritual advisor” to the new Procurator, and she, amongst various notables, were waiting for the arrival of the party of dignitaries. 

The meeting place was a pavilion, behind the official residence, that was meant for summer parties. But since the previous Procurator had been recalled in disgrace in the middle of winter, the replacement was arriving in early spring rains. Sister Lilly resisted the urge to shiver, although the cold damp was winding its fingers through every seam in her clothing.

Eventually, three damp people-shapes appeared in the mist. One holding an umbrella over the other two. Not that an umbrella made any difference.

*Oh joy*, thought Sister Lilly, *we're such a backwater they've actually sent nobody this time, just a letter.*

The three walked up the stairs to the pavilion, and the one with the umbrella threw it outside. “Your excellencies, your graces, ladies and gentlemen, Procurator Julius, and their cousin, Marcus,” announced the now umbrella-less person. Everyone bowed, and then queued up in order of importance behind the chief of staff so he could introduce them.

Sister Lilly watched the Procurator carefully as everyone was introduced, and the line in front of her got shorter. She expected a glazed expression at best, and completely ignoring everyone at worst. But it seemed that the new Procurator listened carefully, and said something to each notable.

“Mino, The Secretary of the Board of Education,” announced the chief of staff.

“Is the curriculum review proceeding well?” asked the Procurator.

“Oh, yes your excellency,” said Mino, bowing, and slightly surprised.

The Procurator inclined their head in acknowledgement, and Mino moved away to make space for the next person.

This continued for everyone in line. It seemed as if the Procurator actually knew who many of them were, by reputation if not by sight. Sister Lilly was stunned. Her predecessor had only attended this first gathering, and was then sent a generic letter telling them further attendance would not be required. It seemed likely that this Procurator was going to be different.

Finally, her turn came. “Sister Lilly, appointed as your spiritual advisor, by the Bishop,” said the Chief of Staff, a line they must have memorised since Sister Lilly had never been in the same room before.

She bowed, and the Procurator said, “I look forward to your insights, Sister.” Since that wasn't a question, she decided it didn't need an answer. As she straightened up, she got a good look at this new fancy tax inspector.

Procurator Julius appeared exactly as she expected a government functionary to look like. Close-cropped dark hair, over dark angular features, swathed in their robes of office, hands hidden. But with amber eyes, not the grey or occasional shocking blue that usually came from the capital.

Sister Lilly turned out of the line to give the next person their chance, and wandered over to the edge of the pavilion. There were a few people she knew a little here, but since she wasn't one for idle chatter (or more likely, serious politicking) she looked out into the mist, pulling her robes tighter against the cold.

“Sister Lilly?” said a voice behind her that she didn't immediately recognise. She turned around, hoping that maybe it was someone telling her they could all go inside. It wasn't, it was Marcus, the “cousin”. Previous pseudo-relatives of Procurators had come before, mostly to avoid creditors elsewhere and gain some kind of diplomatic immunity. Sister Lilly hid her assumptions behind a hasty polite smile.

“Yes, your excellency?” she replied, in her best effort at the calm smooth tone her mentor and mother of her cloister was an expert in.

“Oh, no, I'm no excellency. Just plain Marcus. I'm not even a real secretary, just a clerk” they said, smiling. 

Marcus looked so like the Procurator, Sister Lilly thought they might actually be cousins. But since they hadn't actually asked a question, Sister Lilly just inclined her head.

“I was hoping — Julius was hoping — that you might be available to assist us after this,” said Marcus, with a small polite smile.

“Of course, I am at the Procurator's service,” she said, wondering what could be so urgent and requiring her assistance. But it would be rude to ask.

“Thank you, it shouldn't be too long,” said Marcus, bowing in return and wandering off. Sister Lilly doubted this claim, as a gathering like this was a prime chance for all sorts of politics, even if nobody was going to pay much attention to the new Procurator.

But, Marcus hadn't wandered off aimlessly. Sister Lilly watched as they approached various knots, and each of them disbanded, and everyone dispersed through the garden and house, and back to their various domains. In fact, it had been timed perfectly. As the last person in the queue, the new private secretary, was introduced to the Procurator, everyone else had left. 

Marcus approached the Chief of Staff and the private secretary. “Would you be able to introduce us to the household staff tomorrow morning?” said Marcus to the Chief of Staff. The Chief, who had seen three previous Procurators, and wasn't surprised by much anymore, simply bowed in response, and left. The secretary trailed behind.

Sister Lilly suddenly realised that being left alone with the Procurator, the cousin, and their servant, probably didn't have the best history. She tried to relax. So far they had been different, she desperately hoped that would continue.

Marcus watched the Chief disappear into the mist, as this time the Procurator approached her, “Sister Lilly,” they said, their hands appearing from somewhere in their robes, and clasping quite tightly. “Would you be able to show us the cathedral? I was hoping, I understand, that it might be appropriate to offer a prayer, although I do not know the correct forms.”

Despite everything so far going against Sister Lilly's expectations, she was surprised. “Of course, your excellency,” she replied, bowing.

The four of them walked out of the pavilion, with Sister Lilly beside the Procurator, and the other two following. Sister Lilly didn't venture any words, feeling it wasn't her place to start small talk if the Procurator didn't wish to. The Procurator seemed to be happy to walk in silence, looking around at the buildings and the little gardens squeezed in any spare space. She hailed two cyclos at the front of the house, and directed them to the cathedral.

The silent set arrived at the cathedral, just as a canon was tidying up after the morning service. With relief, Sister Lilly recognised him. “Your Excellency, may I introduce the Reverend Tomas?” she said, hoping the canon could read the robes and work out who had joined them. The Procurator inclined their head in acknowledgement.

“How can I be of service, your Excellency, Sister Lilly?” the canon replied, putting down his dust brush and bowing.

“Would you be able to lead us in the Litany of Thanksgiving?” she asked, gesturing to the Procurator, “Their Excellency would like to express thanks for their safe arrival.”

The Reverend Tomas's face lit up. “Of course, I would be delighted!” he replied, and walked briskly up to the altar in the centre. Sister Lilly followed more sedately, and knelt in front of the rail. Without missing a beat, the Procurator knelt beside her, and then Marcus on her other side. The fourth member of the group, who Sister Lilly hadn't yet got a good look at, also knelt beside Marcus, but with a gap between.

“The response is 'with thanks and praise'” announced the Reverend Tomas, lifted up his hands, and began the prayer.

Marcus missed the first response, but the Procurator joined from the first. Sister Lilly was impressed, they must have studied before arriving. This was not on the normal curriculum for capital bureaucrats.

Was this a performance for her? Why would they feel the need to appeal to a mere spiritual advisor? Or was this for the benefit of the canon, who would surely share this story around? Sister Lilly had many questions, but didn't see herself as likely to be getting any answers.

“It is so,” finished the Reverend Tomas, and all four kneeling responded “it is so.” Sister Lilly rose, but the Procurator was faster. “Thank you again, Reverend Tomas,” they said warmly, “I hope to be able to attend services here in the future.”

The Procurator turned to Sister Lilly, “Would you mind showing us the way back to our residence? I'm afraid we seem to have lost everyone else, and would rather not wander the streets asking strangers.”

----

After showing the Procurator home, Sister Lilly fetched her bicycle and returned to her cloister. The round and aged Mother Superior, Grace, met her at the door.

“More of the same?” asked Mother Grace, closing the door behind them.

“Not at all…” said Sister Lilly, trying to work out how to capture just how different her morning had been from what she expected. “I'm really not sure what to make of them. The Procurator seemed to know everyone before being introduced, and then we went to the cathedral,” she said.

“What, everyone paraded over to the cathedral? Whose idea was that?” Mother Grace asked, confused.

“No, no,” replied Sister Lilly, “only four of us. I was the only local. But I think we should find out who this Procurator Julius was before they came here.”

Mother Grace looked at Sister Lilly quizzically. “The bishop might know someone in the capital to write to,” she replied, thoughtfully, “I will ask… we perhaps should have inquired sooner, but I had assumed…”

“As I think had everyone, judging from the expressions I saw this morning,” interjected Sister Lilly. “We may be facing more capital interference than we've seen in decades.”

Mother Grace grimaced. “But visiting the cathedral—”

“—And praying!” interrupted Sister Lilly.

“—is not the kind of thing I would expect of interferers.” finished Mother Grace. “Of course,” she said, “You could always ask the Procurator.”

Sister Lilly opened her mouth to reply that of _course_ she wouldn't see them again in private. But she realised that she shouldn't assume what this Procurator might or might not do. “I'll see,” she said, instead. And Mother Grace nodded.
