Sister Lilly smothered yawns through the dawn service in the little cloister chapel. But despite her tiredness, she was filled with hope and joy at her answered prayers. Afterwards, while she might have been excused to take a nap, she wanted not to waste any daylight.

She cornered Sister Rosemary and begged her to take what should have been Sister Lilly's morning duty of helping in the washroom. She traded it for taking out the night slop, which was disgusting, but much faster. As she worked, Sister Lilly's mind was making plans.

After a thorough handwashing, she checked in with Mother Grace.

“A ball?” Sister Lilly asked.

“It does still seem like our best idea,” replied Mother Grace.

Sister Lilly nodded, “I'll take it to the Procurator then.”

She wasn't technically due for another meeting with the Procurator until late afternoon. But Sister Lilly decided to leave right away, in the hope of being able to grab a five-minute gap of time with Procurator Julius at some point between. She got on her bicycle, and headed over.

---

Once again, Sister Lilly faced the Procurator's private secretary, Aiden, over a very full diary.

“I can't even see where the Procurator will pee today, to be honest,” said Aiden, running his finger down the day's column. “Thankfully they like breakfast early, but it looks like lunch will be combined with dinner. A few meetings from yesterday had to get pushed to today.”

Sister Lilly winced internally. She had already taken a lot of the Procurator's valuable time yesterday, and had thrown today into chaos. And now she was asking for even more time. 

Marcus bounced down the hallway, and into the vestibule. “Hello again, Sister Lilly,” they said, full of their usual bright cheer, “I thought I heard your voice.” They noticed the diary, and rolled their eyes.

“I don't know how Julius does it,” they said, “I need to eat at least three times a day you know.”

Sister Lilly smiled tensely, and Aiden tapped the book slowly. “Maybe next week?” he offered.

“You're in a meeting later today, maybe you can sneak a word in then?” offered Marcus, “For now, come, and I'll get you some lunch.” They turned, and Aiden gave a pleading look at their back. “I'll bring you something too Aiden,” said Marcus, “Since you daren't move from your chair.”

Sister Lilly followed Marcus out into the main hall, and into a back passage she'd not seen before. Most of the public rooms she was familiar with, but Marcus was leading her into the private spaces. She felt slightly awkward, as if Marcus might take her to their bedroom. But it was only a hallway, and they only went into what looked to be a very ordinary kitchen.

“Cook's day off,” said Marcus, as Sister Lilly looked around, “So I'm afraid it's just whatever I can scrounge up today.” Sister Lilly was slightly surprised that the cook had a day off, she was sure previous Procurators would have demanded service all hours of the day and night.

Marcus poked the stove fire hotter, and put a kettle on to boil. Then they disappeared into the pantry, and came back with some jars, cheese, eggs, and green onions. “We're out of bread,” they said, disappointed, “but I can make omelettes for us. And I'll take up soup and tack to Aiden and Julius.”

Sister Lilly took a seat at the warped and worn kitchen table. Marcus began what looked like a dance, but was clearly well practiced. Some kind of powder from a jar — it must have been a dried soup base — went into two large mugs. Green onions were chopped. A tray was pulled out from the sideboard, and two saucers with a very dry looking biscuit a piece, went on it. Cheese was grated, at a speed where Sister Lilly was sure she'd have sliced her fingers. Six eggs were cracked, and viciously whisked into a uniform pale yellow. The kettle boiled, and steaming hot water went into both mugs.

“Would you mind taking this to Aiden?” asked Marcus, waving to the tray with a dripping whisk, “I'll get the eggs on.”

“Not a problem at all,” replied Sister Lilly, a 'your Excellency' hastily swallowed. It did seem very out of place in this kitchen, that Marcus seemed so at home in. She lifted the tray carefully, since the mugs were very full, and carried it sedately back to Aiden.

“Oh, you're a lifesaver,” said Aiden when he saw the tray.

“I'm just carrying it,” said Sister Lilly, “Marcus did all the work.” 

Aiden took a mug and saucer off the tray, looking for a place to put them on his messy desk. “Hmm,” he said, returned both to the tray, pulled an empty saucer and mug from under a file, added them to the tray, and removed the full dishes again and put them on top of the file.

“I'll take in the ones for their Excellency,” said Aiden. He removed the other dishes from the tray and headed into the meeting room.

Sister Lilly returned the empty dishes to the kitchen. Marcus was serving up as she came in. “I'm afraid that unlike my dear cousin I cannot survive on soup, so I've mostly been having lunches by myself. It's nice to have some company,” they said, shuffling omelette slices out of a cast iron pan onto two plates.

“Thank you for the invitation,” replied Sister Lilly.

“Oh no problem. This way I get to grill you on my own for a change,” said Marcus, handing Sister Lilly a plate and some cutlery.

Sister Lilly sat down tentatively, wondering what she was in for. Were they going to ask why she became a nun? Or about her family?

“So what's the plan with your friend?” said Marcus, taking a completely different tack than Sister Lilly was expecting. Somehow in the warm cosy kitchen, Sister Lilly had momentarily forgotten why she had come. Of course, they would ask about that.

“I was hoping…” said Sister Lilly, pushing a piece of omelette around her plate.

“Go on, spit it out,” said Marcus, and then realising added, “the words I mean, not the omelette, although if I used soda instead of salt again feel free to spit it out.”

“That the Procurator might host a fancy dress ball,” she said finally, taking a delicate bite. It was actually quite tasty, and did not seem to contain soda instead of salt.

Marcus, on the other hand, almost choked. “A fancy… dress… ball?” they got out, in between coughs.

Sister Lilly wanted to get Marcus a drink, but since this wasn't her kitchen she had no idea where to find cups or water. 

“Yes. It would give my friend's parents a reason to leave the house all night, Lucinda could then sneak out and come in disguise to the ball. Nobody would notice one extra young lady, and the Procurator could meet her in a side room somewhere. Suitably escorted of course,” she said.

Marcus got up, and found themselves a cup and filled it with a jug from the cool box. After a few gulps they managed to regain their composure, and resumed their seat. “It's not a bad idea,” they said, “It's just the thought of Julius dressed up as some old famous general, dancing all night with every young person in town, oh it would be hilarious.”

“Oh, hmm,” said Sister Lilly, “I hadn't thought, but you're right if it's their ball then they'll have to spend all night on show. It might be hard to get them away for a clandestine meeting.”

Marcus stabbed a piece of omelette, and ate it without incident. “I could host it,” they said, cautiously, “I'm much more of a party person. And then I can be the centre of attention, with all my adoring fans, and Julius can hide in a broom cupboard.”

Marcus giggled a little, “Although I may still make them dress up as a general. I think they'd look gloriously silly in old-fashioned armour.”

Sister Lilly tried to picture Procurator Julius's head on the statue of a general by the wharf, with not much covering it except a breastplate and a very short tunic, and her brain absolutely refused. 

“That would be excellent,” she said, and then added, “you hosting, I mean, not their Excellency in old-fashioned armour, although I'm sure they would look very, um, elegant.”

Marcus giggled again, “They'll be so awkward, it'll be amazing. I will cherish the memory forever, and bring it up any time they annoy me.”

“Who will you go as?” Sister Lilly asked, nearly finished her omelette.

“I will go as a great ancient philosopher!” said Marcus, dramatically, “Only I will sew my toga on because I do want to be able to dance.”

Sister Lilly wasn't sure what was a more confusing idea, the Procurator as a famous general, or Marcus doing a waltz in a toga.

“And what will the young lady wear?” asked Marcus, finishing off their last piece.

“I suppose she'll probably have to come as a nun, as that's the only spare clothes we have at the convent,” replied Sister Lilly.

“No, no,” said Marcus, obviously now really into this idea, “That's far too boring. The attics here are absolutely stuffed with random things, I will send you baskets full, and surely we can find something more exciting than a nun. Um, no offence Sister Lilly”

Sister Lilly smiled, “None taken, a nun is not usually the life of the party. Thank you for the offer of a costume.”

“Okay, great,” said Marcus, clearing up the plates, “I *will* need to get Julius's permission, but let's pencil it in for the full moon next month. I think that's end of the second week?”

“Thank you ever so much,” said Sister Lilly, slightly overawed, “Please let me know if there is anything I can do at all to help, now, or in the future.”

Marcus winked, “Oh this will be so much fun, no need to thank me. I've been trying to get Julius to do something like this since we arrived, and now they can't turn me down! Ha!”
