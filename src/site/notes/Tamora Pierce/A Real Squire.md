Owen of Jesslaw was trying to do his best. Sir Myles had asked him to write a report on the recent history of the Scanran clans. He supposed this was useful, but it felt like make-work. He sighed, and turned the page of the family tree book and tried not to grimace at yet another 'Rosto' on the Council of Ten.

The door knob turned, and he looked up from his notes. Sir Myles walked in and swept a glance quickly around the room. Finally lighting on Owen in the corner, behind a pile of books.

"Owen!" said Sir Myles, "Lord Wyldon asked to see you."

"Sir?" said Owen, dropping his quill and causing an ink blot on his latest page.

Sir Myles smiled, wryly. "You're not in trouble, don't worry. When you're done there, tidy up your notes for me and return any materials to the archives."

"Yessir," said Owen, and managed to free himself from the desk without further mishap. He hoped that maybe a knight out in the field had written to Lord Wyldon, offering to take on a squire. That would make sense, the knight might not know the pages and squires, and might be looking for Wyldon for advice.

Not that Owen expected to get a good report from Wyldon. The training master had made it perfectly clear that he didn't approve of Owen. Well, Owen thought Wyldon was unfair to Kel, so that was fine.

Owen bowed belatedly to Sir Myles before he left the office, and Myles waved him out, amused.

He jogged through the corridors that were now as familiar to him as his home in Jesslaw. It was cold in midwinter, and most people were huddled around fires and braziers with their families, or packing for the resumption of the Progress.

Owen wondered if he would stay with the Progress. Not, he hoped, with the Master of Ceremonies. But he did enjoy watching the jousts and other competitions. He even admired the fan dance the Yamani ladies did.

No, it was better if he kept his mind here and now, or he'd end up slipping down a staircase or walking into someone. Well, the turn into the corridor with Lord Wyldon's rooms solved that problem. He saw a man-servant carrying out a crate, and bags set up outside the door. Were Wylden and the pages joining the Progress?

The door was open, when Owen reached it. Wyldon was checking the doors of his unusually empty desk. Owen knocked on the door frame.

Wyldon looked up, with that face of iron that always made Owen want to babble.

"You sent for me, sir?" he managed to get out correctly, and bowed.

"Jesslaw, good," said Wyldon, and paused. He didn't sit in the chair, as Owen was used to seeing him, whenever he'd been called in to be assigned punishment duty for his behaviour. In fact, he looked almost awkward.

"Would you like to be my squire?" asked Wyldon, finally.

"Sir? What about the pages?" said Owen, confused. Wyldon could hardly have a squire *and* teach all the pages. Unless...

Wyldon gave Owen a withering look. "Obviously someone else will be taking them, Jesslaw. I've resigned."

Owen could hardly think what that meant. Wyldon had taught the pages for so long, had been the axis around which Owen's time as a page had moved. What would he do if he wasn't teaching them? Go home? It was hard to imagine Lord Wyldon administering his estates.

"What will you do, sir?" said Owen. As soon as he heard it come out of his mouth, he regretted it. "Sorry, I mean, will you be joining the Progress?"

"No," said Wyldon, "Or at least only if we happen to cross paths. I'm going home to annoy my wife for a bit, and then in the spring I'll be going up to the Scanran border."

"Oh wow! Is it heating up there? Is that why Sir Myles has me digging through old records and writing reports on the Clans?" asked Owen eagerly.

Lord Wyldon raised a single eyebrow. "You still haven't answered the question, Jesslaw."

Owen's mind had filled with pictures of Wyldon on a charger, leading an army unit, and facing off against a raiding party. It was the kind of thing he'd always imagined a knight doing, unlike the seemingly endless paperwork Sir Myles did.

"Um, sorry, sir," he said, trying to reign back his thoughts to the present moment. Lord Wyldon had asked him a question. He should answer... the question seemed to have slipped his mind.

It returned to him in a moment of shock. He flinched so hard he almost jumped. "*Your* squire, sir?" he said, and gasped, "Of course! I would be honoured!" He was so excited he was shaking. He had to grip his hands behind his back to stop himself waving them in the air.

"Good. I'm going to work you like a horse, mind," said Wyldon, "The Scanran border is no place for larks, Jesslaw. But if we have time I'll teach you something about dog breeding."

"Thank you, sir!" said Owen, and bowed again. A real knight! He was going to squire for a real knight!

"Meet me outside the stables at dawn tomorrow," said Wyldon.

"Yessir," said Owen, understanding the dismissal. He bowed, and bolted for Lord Raoul's rooms, and Kel.
