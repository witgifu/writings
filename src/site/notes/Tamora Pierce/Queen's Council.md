Sir Alanna, King's Champion, and theoretically Lady of Pirate's Swoop, rested her head on the stack of papers in front of her on the table. It was all bad news, and she was tired. The famine that had started on Jon's coronation was sweeping through the country in waves. Alanna didn't want to think about it. Famine was not something she could challenge in single combat.

Next to her, Myles and George were discussing the likelihood of bread riots in Whitethorn. “A few of the worst are already hikin' barley prices,” said George, “Bread'll follow quick.”

The door opened, and she raised her head up just enough to peek at who was joining the meeting. Garry came in, followed by a page with yet another stack of documents, and the Lord Provost. The page dumped the documents next to Gareth, and trotted back out.

“Closed meeting?” asked Alanna, face still resting on a report.

“Yes,” said the Lord Provost, but didn't elaborate further, just settled himself in his usual chair.

The door opened again, and Jon and Thayete came in. Garry, George, Sir Myles, and the Lord Provost all stood, Alanna a beat behind them. Jon nodded, and they all resumed their seats. Alanna tried to give Thayete a smile, but either it was very unconvincing, or Thayete was feeling about as well as Alanna. Alanna only received the tiniest of nods back.

Jon looked surprisingly nervous. He looked around the table, but didn't stop to make eye contact. Nor did he announce why he had called them all here so close to dawn.

Eventually it was Thayete that broke the awkward silence. “This morning I found a note in the pocket of my dressing gown,” she said, and slowly pulled a slip of parchment out and slid it across the table.

The Provost took it first, examining both sides before reading it. From two seats down, Alanna could see that the writing was erratic, possibly someone trying to disguise their usual hand.

“I assume you've checked it, sire?” asked the Lord Provost, not looking up, but examining the cut edge carefully.

Jon nodded. “I couldn't detect any magic on it, or on the dressing gown.”

The Provost handed the note to George next, saying, “Looks like standard palace supplies, sire. Anyone allowed through the gates could get access to scribal parchment and ink.”

George read it, and passed it across to Alana with only a raised eyebrow.

> the bitch's half-breed whelp should be ripped from the womb

Alanna looked up in astonishment at Thayete. “Are you…? There's been no announcement?”

Thayete didn't look at Alanna directly, but at the centre of the table. “Only Jon, Buri, and our healer, and now you all, know,” she said softly, “it's only two months along; I don't want to raise anyone's hopes with an announcement until we're more sure. At least until the quickening.”

Myles took the note from Alanna's limp grip. “It could just be speculation, your majesties,” he said as his eyes flicked over it.

“Odds in the city favour a babe before the end of the year,” said George, not unkindly.

“But still,” said Alanna, “this could be a rebel who escaped your net, George.”

“Or someone entirely new,” added the Provost, “there's always people suspicious of anyone from outside Tortall.”

*Or event a different part of it,* added Alanna mentally, remembering how the Bazhir were stared at in Corus.

Garry rubbed his chin, and looked from Miles, to the Provost, to George. “Which of you will investigate?”

There was a brief pause, while they all considered. Having the three of them was a bad idea, this was only one threat among many.

“I'll check with my contacts, sire, in case this is something out of Sarain,” said Sir Myles, “But a local threat sounds more likely. There has to at least be an agent or contact in the Palace.”

“Within' the Palace should be the guard's business? Or the city, my lords?” asked George, catching on quickly that this was likely to be a sticking point. The palace guard wouldn't want the Provost's men sticking their low-born noses in Palace business; and the Provost's men would be infuriated by the palace guard bumbling around the city.

“So I guess that's me that can poke around in both, then?” said George. Alanna could see a brief look of relief cross Jon's face. There was a reason George had led the cleanup of the rebels.

The Lord Provost inclined his head, and said “I'll also keep a discrete eye out.”

“Thank you all,” said Jon. Then he turned carefully to look at Alanna.

“Yes, sire?” she asked, seeing more of the king in his manner than her friend.

“I would like to increase Thayete's security—” he began, but Thayete quickly interrupted him.

“I am perfectly capable of looking after myself, and I have Buri,” she said, with the air of an argument that was well-worn. Presumably from even before this note had turned up.

“—Buri is an excellent guard, and I know you can fight as well my dear. Alanna is—”

“—Yes, I have seen her fight as well, I have no doubt of her skills, but—”

“—well versed in court manners, at least as capable with her Gift as I, and not even the most uptight of the oldest nobles can object—”

“—I will not be locked up or sent away because a letter frightens *you*. I have lived through years of constant threats, before I met you, I will not give in now—”

Neither of them, Alanna noticed, were actually listening to what the other was saying. She would have laughed, if this weren't so serious, and if she wasn't slightly worried by this chink in their relationship.

“Thayete,” said Alanna, interrupting both of them. They both looked away from each other, as if just realising where they were. Alanna knew they wouldn't have begun (or resumed) the argument if they hadn't felt so comfortable with all the people here. It was still, however, embarrassing to be caught arguing by their friends.

“Thayete, have you chosen an inner circle of ladies yet?” asked Alanna, gently.

“No,” said Thayete, with a small sigh, “There have been many more important things on my list, I have simply been rotating all the young women presented as candidates.”

Alanna smiled, knowing Thayete had been deep in planning education reforms. “Then I would ask, my lady, if it might be possible that I could be appointed there temporarily? To help you choose the permanent members.”

Thayete, wise to the game Alanna was playing, raised one elegant eyebrow. “To vet them? To assess their dancing and embroidery? You?”

“To make sure every one of them is able to roll out of bed and into a saddle,” said Alanna, trying to appeal to Thayete's desire for freedom, “and be of some use in a fight. Check for any Gift. And to advise you on any political implications, if you like.”

“And I _do_ know how to dance,” Alanna added, quietly.

Jon grinned. Alanna was sure he was remembering how he had made her dance with every court lady when she had been his squire. She had yet to perfect all the dances as the woman's part, but she could dance them all as the man.

“An' I'll do a check on 'em too,” added George, smiling at Alanna and Thayete, “don't want anyone up to their ears in gamblin' debts, or drunks.”

Sir Myles blushed very slightly, and pushed the note back into the middle of the table. *He* had been known as the court drunk not that long ago.

“Beggin' yer pardon, sir,” added George, with a nod to Myles.

“Will you let Alanna help you find some more friends?” asked Jon, quietly.

Thayete looked around the table. “Oh fine!” she snapped, “But you better tell me which ones Jon slept with, Alanna.”

Jon went a little pale, “Uh, well, I…” he stammered.

“I don't want anyone offering unsolicited advice,” said Thayete, with a small smile.
