Faleron came to Cliopher after a breakfast. This had probably been discussed earlier, he wasn't sure. There had been fittings for clothes a few days ago, and he did remember a lot of talk about whether full mourning was appropriate when the Sun had incinerated the person in question for blasphemy. Now Faleron was here to help Cliopher into the new clothes.

Dutiful, Cliopher stood in the dressing room and closed his eyes. Layers of fabric, jewels, shoes. A dusting of cosmetics. They must be going to a formal court function, for all of this. Perhaps a memorial for Shallyr, before his name was removed from the lists of emperors, and marked as a usurper. There was no body for a funeral. The ashes had blown away in the wind.

Following Faleron's pleasant chatter, Cliopher left the dressing room and bedroom, and arrived at the front entrance. Princess Melissa, Lady Jivane at her side. Four guards, two maids. Cliopher wondered vaguely why he had been selected. He didn't ask anyone though; the Princess had asked, so he was here.

Out and up they went this time, but not so far. At a splendid pair of gold doors, they left behind the guards. More doors followed, with more guards. They were watched by nobles, some who bowed with smiles, and some with carefully neutral faces. Did they know what he had done? Did any of them suspect?

Finally, the Princess paused, and looked them over. “Wait here,” she told the maids, who bowed and stepped aside. Cliopher also bowed, and moved to step aside. Lady Jivane shook her head very slightly. So he stayed with them.

Spears stamped, and the doors were opened. “Princess Melissa Damara, Lady Jivane of Zunidh, Cliopher sayo Mdang,” announced the guard. Looking into the room, for a fraction of a second, Cliopher wondered if Shallyr had come back.

But no, the man on the couch looked frightened and tired. He was also more slender than Shallyr had been. Most noticeably, he didn't glow. On his left was a priest-wizard, hiding behind a mask. On his right, a high lord.

Then it hit Cliopher. He'd seen this man before, and had convinced himself it had been a dream or hallucination. How was he here? Had he not been the man he claimed? Where were his friends?

In his confusion, Cliopher missed immediately noticing that Princess Melissa and Lady Jivane had dropped into the obeisance. Belatedly, and no doubt red with embarrassment, he followed them.

Where had they found him? How had they found him? Had he agreed to this?

The new Emperor gave the signal to rise, less fluidly than his cousin had. He looked at the Princess, who kept her eyes lowered, and Lady Jivane who did the same. Cliopher, shaken out of his training, met the new Emperor's eyes. Both the Emperor and Cliopher were shocked.

“We would speak to our sister alone,” said the Emperor, his voice clear and calm. His eyes, still locked on Cliopher's, betrayed his fear. Fear of what, exactly, Cliopher wasn't sure.

They all backed out, except the guards and the Princess. Outside the doors again, they waited to hear what the Emperor wanted. Who was he? How was he the Princess's brother? And how could an Imperial prince be gallivanting the nine worlds as an infamous outlaw poet?

What had he wanted to talk about? Did he know the role Princess Melissa played in his cousin's death? Or perhaps he wanted to ask after their mother?

When they were summoned to return, the Emperor announced, “We will respect the Grand Duchess Melissa's mourning for her betrothed and our mother.”

The Princess made a deep bow of thanks.

“We will not ask her to break it to marry us,” he continued.

Cliopher had thought he had become inured to the horrors of Palace. Had Princess Melissa come into this meeting expecting a marriage proposal? From her own brother? Again, shock caused Cliopher to look from the Princess to the Emperor. His eyes were glistening, and he was trying to blink back tears.

“My great thanks for your unfailing kindness, Glorious One,” said the Princess, still maintaining a posture of submission. “I offer to you Sayo Mdang, who has been my consolation, in the hope that he may also be yours.”

Cliopher froze, unsure what to do. The Princess must have noticed the way they had caught each other's gaze. Perhaps she thought the Emperor wanted him. Cliopher had no right to refuse the Emperor, who was possibly Fitzroy Angursell. Who had offered to rescue Cliopher? Emotions spinning inside him, he did the only thing he could think of, and dropped again into the obeisance. 

Cliopher saw the Emperor gesture to rise, somewhat quickly, as if he was annoyed. Looking up has he rose, Cliopher could see a hint of disgust or horror on that face. Was that the disgust of the Emperor, unhappy with the gift? Or was it that of the outlaw bard, disgusted at the idea that people could be given as gifts? Horrified that if he rejected Cliopher, he might be executed?

“We thank you, and wish you well,” said the Emperor, in clear dismissal. And so the Princess and Lady Jivane withdrew. 

“When is our next appointment?” asked the Emperor.

“There is a ritual in twenty minutes, Glorious One,” said the priest-wizard, “Are you sure, your Radiancy, that you do not wish to talk further with her highness? The list of candidates—”

The Emperor interrupted him, “I am aware. You may go prepare for the ritual, lord wizard.” 

Then he turned to the high lord, “A suite will need to be prepared for Sayo Mdang. Scour it down to the stone first.”

The two men bowed deeply, and left without further complaint.

Cliopher stood frozen, waiting for the Emperor to dismiss him. Instead, the Emperor waved his hand in that gesture he had seen Saya Auletes do once. The sounds of the palace faded away. Then the Sun-on-Earth whispered, “Kip?”

“Y-yes… Fitzroy?” whispered back Cliopher, his back to the guards and hoping they couldn't read lips.

“Oh, thank the gods, it is you,” said Fitzroy softly, “What happened?”

“Emperor Shallyr gave me to Princess Melissa, Glorious One,” said Cliopher.

Fitzroy closed his eyes tightly, as if he was in pain.

“Are you well, your Radiancy? Should I call for someone?” asked Cliopher, gently.

Fitzroy looked at him sharply, and Cliopher automatically took a step back. “You don't think it would hurt to be called the same names as my uncle and cousin? Who called *me* a terror, while they scourged five worlds for their own amusement?”

Cliopher dropped into the full formal prostration. He couldn't reconcile Fitzroy Angursell with Emperor. He didn't know what he was supposed to do. “Please forgive your humble servant, my… your… most gracious lord,” said Cliopher, slightly frantic, “What would you have me call you?”

Fitzroy looked at Cliopher, “I don't suppose it would be safe to use my name,” he said, sadly, “Not that it's my *birth* name. Nobody has even told me what that is yet.”

“I am sorry,” said Cliopher, still face down on the ground.

“Come sit up here, Kip,” said Fitzroy, with a sigh.

Obediently, cautiously, Cliopher got up and sat next to Fitzroy.

“I had wondered if you might have escaped, but now it looks like we're both trapped here,” said Fitzroy, looking around. “They tell me that once I'm crowned I can't even touch anyone, or they'll burn to death. They're trying to teach me how to stand still while they dress me. I feel like a fucking _doll_.”

“You will be able to touch people who have been purified,” said Cliopher softly, looking at his lap.

“Like my uncle's collection of child concubines? Like my cousin raping someone from every province of the empire to prove his dominance?” said Fitzroy, dropping his face into his hands. “This place is _sick_.”

“Sorry,” said Cliopher, unsure if he was trying to apologise for the palace or the way he had given in to Shallyr's decree, “You can send me away. Or you should probably just ask the guards to kill me. It's my fault you're here anyway.” He winced internally at that last bit, but it was true.

“I don't know how it could be your fault, unless you think it was *your* prayer that woke the Sun to finally take notice of the crimes being done in his name,” said Fitzroy, bitterly.

Cliopher looked up, looked at those beautiful golden eyes. That shortly would blind almost anyone who dared to cross them. “It was me,” he whispered, barely moving his lips, “I was purified, I touched the spell to Shallyr.”

Fitzroy didn't say anything for a while, and Cliopher looked back down and away. Closing his eyes, he waited to hear the sound of guards walking across the room. What was the punishment for a commoner who had committed regicide? They would probably torture him to death, in some inventive way. Would they come after his family as well?

“My lord, if you are at all inclined to mercy, please spare my family,” he begged, unable to stop his eyes welling with tears. He was such an idiot, why had he said anything?

“Kip?!” said Fitzroy, alarmed and confused, “If that's true, what you did, you're a hero!” He reached out a hand to Cliopher's shoulder, this time not flinching away at the schooled magic.

Cliopher shook his head. “I'm a murderer,” he said softly, “And I'm still purified. I'm a danger to you if I stay, and if you send me away I know too much.”

“I'm fully willing to believe you'd be in danger if I sent you away,” said Fitzroy, with a sigh, “But what you did was surely self-defence.”

There was no time for Cliopher to reply, as spears stamped, announcing someone else. Fitzroy waved to dismiss the silence, and to admit whoever it was. The great lord had returned. He must be the Emperor's seneschal or similar, Cliopher realised.

“A suite has been prepared, Glorious One,” he said, in a deep bow.

The Emperor, Fitzroy, stood. So then, did Cliopher. “We will see it,” said the Emperor.

If this surprised the seneschal, he did not show it. Instead, he led them to a suite of rooms that were even more opulent than those Cliopher had used in the Princess's apartment. A sitting room, study, bedroom, washroom, dressing room. All in stark white, with nearly no decoration apart from the movable furniture. Cliopher wondered if this was what 'scoured to the stone' meant.

When they had seen each room, the Emperor said, “It is adequate. Make sure to get Sayo Mdang's things from the Grand Duchess.” The seneschal seemed to relax minutely.

The Emperor walked out through the door to the suite, and since Cliopher hadn't been dismissed, he followed him. Up and up and up, to the very top room of the Tower. The guards opened the door on a room that seemed to be bare, except for the pillar of stone rising through the floor, and ending just below the ceiling. Four priest-wizards bowed as the Emperor entered, and walked into the prepared circle.

Cliopher stood at the edge of the room. There were no corners, it was perfectly round. He watched as the wizards anointed with oil, and waved incense. They chanted and did various things with salt. It was strange, to see something that looked so much like his own purification rituals, from the outside.

The chanting wound down, and Cliopher very nearly relaxed. But then the lead wizard drew a very fine silver stiletto knife. Alarmed, Cliopher pressed himself back into the wall. The wizard used the very tip to prick the Emperor's finger, and squeezed it to make a bead of blood. He dripped it onto the stone pillar. Even Cliopher could feel the wave of power than ran out from the pillar.

All the priest-wizards bowed low, and walked backwards out of the room. The Emperor swayed, his eyes closed, sweat running down his head. Nobody moved forward to help him, and as Cliopher looked around the room, he realised there was no one there who would. An acolyte was cleaning up, the guards were looking stern, the seneschal had left.

Indignant, Cliopher walked over to the Emperor, who was clearly struggling not to collapse. He was breathing hard, and shaking slightly, biting his lips. Cliopher wrapped a gentle arm around him, and said softly, “You can lean on me, my lord.”

The Emperor flinched, and opened his eyes. But when he saw Cliopher was the one holding him, he took a shuddering breath and then did, indeed, lean on Cliopher. In turn, Cliopher held onto Fitzroy tighter.

“Let's find you somewhere to rest and recover,” said Cliopher, looking around. The room was bare, with not so much as a stool or even a box. He didn't think he was strong enough to carry the taller man. With an internal sight, he decided to try the stairs.

“Come, my lord, this way,” he said softly, gently encouraging Fitzroy over to the door. The guards formed up, one in front and one behind, but made no move to help. “Here are the stairs, my lord, lean on me and the wall, that's it, one foot,” Cliopher continued a soft patter as they made their way very slowly down the stairs.

At the first landing, Cliopher moved to make for the door. He had no idea what lay behind it, but he hoped there would be at least one chair in whatever room was there. Possibly a preparation room for the ritual room above. 

But Fitzroy shook his head once. “No,” he said, his voice creaking, “Not. Clean.”

It wasn't clear to Cliopher if he meant ritually or physically, but he said, “Of course, my lord, we can keep going down.”

They went all the way down to the main level. Cliopher only knew the audience room and his own rooms, and wasn't about to ask the unhelpful guards. So he led the Emperor, Fitzroy, to his own sitting room. There he helped him onto a divan, removing his shoes so he could pull his legs up.

An attendant entered, and was startled to see them. Cliopher wasn't sure if they were surprised to find the Emperor, or surprised to see him curled, with Cliopher kneeling at his side.

“He needs cold water, fresh tea, and plain bread with nothing on it, not even butter, please,” said Cliopher. He had no idea who the attendant was, if they were meant to serve him or the Emperor or both. Regardless, they bowed deeply and withdrew.

The seneschal found them before the attendant returned. Ignoring Cliopher entirely, he bowed deeply and said, “Glorious One, the Admirals will be in the Lilly Room shortly.”

Fitzroy moved to push himself up, but Cliopher held onto his arm. “Please, my lord, you need to rest. Surely the meeting can wait half an hour,” said Cliopher, with a bravery that surprised him.

The seneschal seemed to pretend that Cliopher hadn't said anything. He continued to watch only Fitzroy's hands. “In half… an hour,” croaked Fitzroy.

Disapproval clear on his face, the seneschal bowed and withdrew. At the same time the attendant returned with an overflowing tray, which they nervously deposited on the nearest table.

“Thank you,” said Cliopher, and the attendant bowed low and withdrew to the edge of the room. Cliopher handed the glass of water to Fitzroy first, before setting out the tea things. Once the tea was brewing, he looked up at Fitzroy. 

The Emperor was holding the glass of water, but hadn't drunk any of it. He was just staring at the gold-rimmed glass, seemingly watching the condensation beading out the outside. “Will they come, Kip?” he whispered.

Cliopher almost asked who Fitzroy meant, but there was really only one possible answer. The Red Company had broken each other out of many terrible predicaments in the past. “I don't know, my lord,” said Cliopher softly. Thinking of how they had come, so unexpectedly for him, and how he had turned them down.

And what would happen if the Red Company did come? Fitzroy would leave with them, surely. Would the Empire then fall into fighting? Could they perhaps arrange something so it was clear that Princess Melissa was the heir? Cliopher had no idea.

With one hand, Cliopher gently lifted the glass towards Fitzroy. He took a small sip, and a long slow breath. On the tray was a fine linen serviette. Cliopher took it, and gently dabbed the sweat from Fitzroy's face, trying not to smudge the cosmetics. Fitzroy sighed, and took a long drink of water.

“You don't have to do this, Kip,” said Fitzroy, his voice clearly improved.

Putting down the serviette, Cliopher poured the tea, humming agreement. “I had seven weeks of purification rituals, my lord, multiple times per day,” he said softly, “Nobody should have to do that without a friend to hold them. And I am sure whatever they are doing to you is worse that what happened to me.”

“A… friend?” said Fitzroy.

The cup of tea in Cliopher's hand clattered against the saucer as he put it down. In his head, he could hear Lady Mairel's tutting, and he clenched his hands reflexively. “My apologies for the presumption, my lord,” he whispered.

“I—” said Fitzroy, and then took another sip of water, “I would like to be your friend, Kip.”

Cliopher looked up from the fine china tea service, once again into those glorious golden eyes. They sparkled, with an inner light. Had he caused that? Holding perfectly still, Cliopher said, “I also, my… friend?”

Fitzroy took a hand off the glass, reaching for Cliopher, who gave him his left hand, as he steadied the glass with his right. “Thank you,” said Fitzroy, his eyes welling with tears.
