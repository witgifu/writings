Faleron arrived with the coffee. Cliopher thanked him, and then stood by the windows to drink it. The view of the gardens was beautiful, with the distant city looking as golden as its name. Rows of trees and plants were artfully arranged to make patterns from the high windows, like an embroidery.

What use could Cliopher possibly be to an Imperial Princess? He hadn't even finished his university degree. Everything he had seen so far had shown him how unimportant he was, how powerless. He had a few weeks training in the skills valued here. It was painfully clear that an Emperor like Shallyr wouldn't want to listen to anyone, but particularly not a funny-looking provincial.

Putting down his empty coffee cup, Cliopher went over to the shelf with the oboe. _His_ oboe, he supposed. For lack of anything else to do, he opened the case and fitted the pieces together. Perhaps now it would be okay if he played music just for himself, rather than the court music.

Starting with the pieces he remembered best, he flowed into the Islander tunes he knew. Closing his eyes, he imagined, remembered, playing them with his family. How perhaps at a hundred-day feast, they might pull out their various instruments. Moving from song to song, people joining or dropping out as they felt like it.

It wasn't until he heard the knock at the door that he realised he had been playing _Aurora_. Panicking slightly, he put the oboe down on the table, and hurried over to the door. Technically, only the words were banned. That was not an argument he really wanted to be making to anyone in the Palace of Stars.

It was Princess Melissa, and Saya Auletes. Swallowing, Cliopher dropped into the second-degree obeisance. “Your… grace?” he said, nervously, suddenly remembering nobody in her household had called her 'highness'.

“You play very beautifully,” said Princess Melissa, with the tiniest smile and the gesture to rise.

“Thank you, your grace,” said Cliopher, his heart still beating far too fast, “I am sure there are many better here.”

“Technically, probably. But hearing someone who clearly loves what they are playing is a rare treat,” said Princess Melissa.

Cliopher tried to stop himself from shaking. He was sure she had recognised what he was playing. Was she mocking him? Testing him? He had been foolish to play anything but the music he was told was safe and approved. He waited, with no idea what he should say.

“May I come in?” asked the Princess, gently.

“Of course, your grace,” said Cliopher. It was, after all, _her_ room. He moved over to the table, and picked up the oboe again, pulling it apart and putting it back in its case.

“How did you come to be here, Sayo Mdang?” she said, carefully pronouncing the 'ang' nearly correctly.

“When the Navy came to collect the tithe, your grace, I volunteered,” said Cliopher, simply. Then he added, “The proclamation said that if nobody volunteered who met the criteria, then they would take all the sixteen-year-olds.”

“Yes,” said Princess Melissa, “Much of Colhélhé is in rebellion after the Navy attempted to do so. The border in East Oriole has fallen back.”

Cliopher stared at her in disbelief. How could the border of the Empire move in any direction other than outwards? And how could any rebellion hope to last? But then, what would the Vangaveye-ve have done if they'd had no candidates to volunteer, nobody who met the criteria? Would they have just let the Navy take whoever they wanted?

“Will you let Auletes examine you, Sayo Mdang? She has a small skill at magic,” said the Princess, seemingly changing topic.

“Of course, your grace,” said Cliopher, “Anything.” He went to remove his over-robe.

“You can stay as you are, Sayo Mdang,” said Saya Auletes, “I only need to hold your hand.”

Immediately, Cliopher held out his hand to her. She stepped forward and took it, closing her eyes. To his small relief, Cliopher could feel nothing at all of her working. 

She opened her eyes, and nodded at Princess Melissa. Then she released Cliopher's hand, and made a sweeping gesture he didn't recognise. The background sound of the palace, of the apartment, disappeared.

Princess Melissa noticeably relaxed. “You have been checked for any magic other than the purifications, Sayo Mdang. Your arrival in my household really did seem too convenient.”

“Your grace?” asked Cliopher, timidly. He wanted to protest that he had no skills or talents, but it seemed like boasting to even suggest that might be why he was useful. Suddenly he realised even the gentle query could be taken as contradicting her. Blushing, he dropped into a bow of second degree apology.

“Come, let us sit down,” said Princess Melissa, “You have nothing to apologise for, Sayo Mdang, and I have much to explain.”

Cliopher waited for the Princess and Saya Auletes to choose seats and sit down, before he did so. He made his best efforts at a formal correct posture, back straight hand hands loosely clasped in his lap.

“Shallyr is not fit to be Emperor,” said Princess Melissa, baldly.

Cliopher's heart pounded, and he looked immediately to the door and windows. That wasn't just treason it was…

“I, with others, intend to remove him before he kills millions,” she said, moving from probable high treason to the textbook definition. “I regret that we did not act immediately on Eritanyr's death. If we had done so… Already he has tortured and executed many for his own amusement, not to mention those lost on Alinor and Colhélhé. I believe he was also behind the death of my mother.”

She fell silent, as if waiting for Cliopher to ask a question.

“I will do anything I can to help you, your grace,” said Cliopher, unable to stop his hands from shaking, “But I am no soldier, assassin, or spy. I'm not even a good liar.”

“You are purified to touch the Emperor,” said Saya Auletes, “Although the spells require careful maintenance.”

“Yes, your grace,” said Cliopher, looking at his own hands. He didn't ask if she was also purified. She would have to be for the wedding, but it might not yet be complete. The wedding would also presumably end with a consummation, and Cliopher could understand why the Princess might not want that to happen.

“We have a plan, but for the safety of everyone involved it is best if you know as little as possible,” said Saya Auletes, with a meaningful look at the Princess.

“I understand, your grace,” said Cliopher. Clasping his hands together tightly, he added, “I only ask, if it is possible, to be killed quickly after. I would not want my family to know of the trial and execution, or be tarnished by it.”

“If we fail, we will try to protect you. If we cannot, we will do our best to make sure you do not face a traitor's death,” said Saya Auletes.

“If we succeed, only the two of us will even know you were involved,” added Princess Melissa. 

“Thank you, your grace,” said Cliopher, wondering what their chances of success were. Probably very low, but perhaps higher than of the Vangaveye-ve succeeding from the Empire.

The bells rang, and Princess Melissa sighed. “I must be off,” she said, “Auletes will instruct you.”

She stood, and so did Cliopher. Making the second-degree obeisance as she left the room. When he rose, he turned to Saya Auletes and waited.

After a long silence, she spoke, “You could have asked to be made a duke if we succeed.”

It hadn't occurred to Cliopher to ask for anything for himself. Not that he wanted to be a *duke*. He hadn't thought about what he wanted, beyond sleep and to not be in pain, in a long time. Not since the hallucination of Fitzroy Angursell. 

“I will do whatever her grace asks of me,” said Cliopher, honestly.

When Faleron opened the door to the parlour and waved Cliopher in, he stumbled into the door frame. Princess Melissa was expected, and Saya Auletes was not a surprise. But sitting next to the Princess was a man, a mage, Cliopher had hoped to never see again.

It took all of Cliopher's control not to shake has he went to his knees for the second-degree obeisance. He had been asked to attend the parlour, he had not been told why. Stomach rolling he tried to convince himself that it didn't matter why. If Princess Melissa send him back to Exa, he would go. He *would*.

Princess Melissa gestured for Cliopher to rise.

“Wizard Iyro, this is Cliopher sayo Mdang. Sayo Mdang, Wizard Iyro has graciously agreed to maintain your purification,” said Princess Melissa, her voice firm and clear.

Cliopher bowed deeply to the wizard, who was apparently not Mage Exa. Looking more carefully, he could see it now. Iyro was older, his features softer.

“You know my brother,” said Iyro, sadly. His voice sounded nothing like Exa's, it had none of the high court accent. Instead, it sounded slightly hollow, reminding him of someone he'd met at university.

“My apologies for staring, Wizard Iyro,” said Cliopher, with a bow of second-degree apology, “It was unconscionably rude.”

Iyro looked to Saya Auletes. Her hands moved quickly as she translated into sign language.

“I understand,” said Iyro, “My brother Exa owes apologies to many people. Including me. He did your purification?”

“Yes, sir,” said Cliopher, with a nod.

“Your grace, I will need to do one spell today to repair and stabilise the magic. After that, Sayo Mdang can maintain it himself with the right equipment,” said Iyro.

“Thank you, Iyro,” said Princess Melissa warmly, and Saya Auletes translated, “Would you like to use my workroom? Or I can send him to you.”

“Or I can do it in your room, Sayo Mdang,” he sighed and added, “You can remain clothed, and have anyone else present you would like.”

Something inside Cliopher sparked, some anger he had buried deep. It was hard to explain to himself even, but his dislike of Exa turned hard and sharp.

“Whatever would be most convenient for you, Wizard Iyro,” he said, mostly managing to contain his feelings. They weren't important.

“Then your workroom would be best, your grace. We can do it now to avoid any… questions,” said Iyro. Then he turned again to Cliopher, “And I can teach you the rituals to maintain it.”

Princess Melissa nodded, “Very good.”

Iyro and Saya Auletes stood, and bowed to Princess Melissa. A beat behind them Cliopher bowed as well. They left the parlour together, and Saya Auletes led them to a small room off the library.

This was not a repurposed cupboard, but a purpose-built room. It seemed a little empty, lacking the personality of a regular user. Saya Auletes flashed a question at Iyro, who shook his head.

Cliopher went and stood in the centre of the circle on the floor. Iyro opened the cupboards, pulling out a few containers and placing them on the bench.

“Schooled magic depends on creating a pattern the magic wants to flow into,” said Iyro, “Once the pattern is set, each subsequent time becomes easier. After sufficient repetition, the caster requires no magic themselves, which is how the aristocratic rituals are effective.”

Cliopher nodded, that fit with his vague understanding of magic from school.

“The downside is that once the pattern is established, it is very hard to change, sometimes impossible,” Iyro said, as he opened one of the containers, which held salt. “This purification not often used, because when it has been tried on anyone with even the slightest magical talent, it has sent them mad. It burns all the magic out of you. It does not strictly need to be _painful_, but if my brother has made it so, I may not be able to prevent that effect. If nothing else I can give you pain medication afterwards.”

“I understand, sir,” said Cliopher quietly, and closed his eyes.

There were the sounds of salt and oil being added to the circle. Then Iyro began to chant. It was not melodious, which Cliopher realised now could be tricky if you were deaf. But it was very rhythmical. Cliopher braced himself, as the magic rose. It stung a little, but not as bad as he had expected.

When Iyro stopped, he said, “All done. How are you feeling, Sayo Mdang?”

“Fine, sir,” said Cliopher automatically.

Iyro raised one eyebrow at him. Then he gave Saya Auletes a flurry of signs.

“You will need to do a ritual every sunrise and moonrise,” said Iyro. He then proceeded to carefully and gently show Cliopher what to do. There was also a special bath every day, and restrictions on what he could eat. The last one Saya Auletes told him not to worry about, they would make sure he was given appropriate foods.

“I will make sure everything you need is available in your room, Sayo Mdang,” said Saya Auletes, as they left the library.

“Thank you, ma'am,” said Cliopher with a bow. Then he went back to the room. He was surprised that he only felt a little tired and stiff. Absently, he did the dancing stretches, and then went to the bookshelf for something to read.

Cliopher hadn't managed more than a chapter of the rather vapid novel he had picked at random, when there was a knock at the door. He went to answer it, not bothering to mark his place in the book when he set it down.

“Hello, sir,” said Faleron, when he answered the door, “I have a few things for you.” It turned out to be more than a few. 

A table-top alter appeared first, with incense and silk flowers. It should have had a picture of the current Emperor, but perhaps the official one had not yet been made. Instead, it had an icon of the Sun.

Bath salts and oils went into the washroom. Faleron also added a small box of medicinals, explaining the labels and effects of each. Cliopher had never much liked taking medicine, he found most of them had side effects that made it not worthwhile.

Finally, Faleron showed Cliopher an ingenious device. Something like a clock, but instead of striking even hours based on the solar day, it ran by the hours of the moon. A little knob allowed an alarm to be set, and so Cliopher could be woken at moonrise. The palace bells rang dawn, noon, sunset, and midnight, loud enough to wake even the oldest noble to do their rituals. So there was no need for anything else to track dawn.

When Faleron left, Cliopher decided to have the bath, and get it done for the day. In the bathroom he noticed the razor was missing from its usual location. Instead, there was a jar there. He picked it up, confused why someone would have taken the razor. Did they want him to let his hair grow again? That would be…

Looking at the jar more closely, he realised it was labelled 'depilatory cream'. He shouldn't have let himself hope. Not even a tiny bit. Carefully, he read the directions, and followed them.
