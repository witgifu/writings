Cliopher walked in the entourage following Princess Melissa. He was dressed again in full court costume, or something that looked like it. This outfit referencing Damara rather than the Wide Seas. For the first time since he had arrived, he was outside in the sunshine.

They paraded in the direction of the Imperial Military College, through artful gardens. Courtiers and servants stopped to bow deeply to the Princess as she passed, carried on a sedan chair by four guards in the lesser panoply. Cliopher watched the back of Saya Auletes in front of him.

It took them half an hour to reach the edge of the gardens. There, a temporary Sun Temple had been constructed, and although they faced the back of it, it was clearly impressive. Easily a hundred steps rose up to the building at the top, which was covered in gold leaf. On the other side where it faced the sunlight, it would probably be impossible to look at directly.

As planned, Cliopher and Saya Auletes peeled off from the group, as if they had been sent on an errand to the College campus itself. At some point along the path that looked exactly the same as any other, Saya Auletes whispered, “Now.”

Cliopher turned sharply off the path, climbing over a herbaceous border and trampling a bed of violets. He pushed on, between rosebushes that snagged at his clothes, and something with razor sharp edges to its leaves. No doubt the court costume was ruined already. Finally, he reached the edge of the temple. Where, as promised, a small tunnel had been dug under the bottom step. Watching carefully for patrolling guards, he lay down on his stomach and wormed his way through.

Once inside the construction, he waited for his eyes to adjust. What he saw astonished him. It wasn't as if he knew much about construction, but it was clear that this had been built hastily. From the outside, everything had appeared uniform, but inside it seemed like every spare piece of wood in all the city of Astandalas had been slapped together. By many people, who had different ideas on how to construct it, and hadn't bothered to discuss the process. Bolts, nails, pegs… one strut even seemed to be just tied into place with string.

The poor construction actually made it easier to see, since from underneath it was obvious that where something would not be visible, it had simply been left off. So many of the tops of the back stairs were open to the sky. Cliopher couldn't decide if he was impressed with the thriftiness, or horrified.

Climbing, however, was much more interesting. He scrambled through and up, feeling more like he was in jungle trees than a human-made building. Much of the wood was covered in hasty cuts. If he hadn't been wearing gloves to cover the spell Iyro had given him, he would probably have collected many splinters. As it was, they tore at the fine leather, and his clothes.

Halfway up, he stopped to rest. There should be time, he thought, rubbing his hands, and adjusting his shoes. He could hear a rumble that must be a distant crowd, and the wood creaked as various people climbed up and down the front steps, finalising things. When Cliopher could hear the distant sound of marching feet, he forced himself to continue upwards.

At the top, under the temple itself, it was much darker. There were four great pillars, visible on the outside at the four corners. From here he could see they were plaster over a canvas frame. The fraying edges of the fabric were visible underneath. He very carefully did not laugh. Instead, he pushed himself as close as he could, up under the frame in the centre. Examining the underside of the floorboards for chinks of daylight.

Eventually he saw it, a thin line of light around a square of wood. Just large enough to reach his hand through. Shimmying around, he adjusted himself so that his left arm was under it, and his body arranged as comfortably as possible. This left him lying flat under the floor. For some reason this triggered a memory of Bertie telling him about how some cultures buried their ancestors under the doorways of their houses. They'd had a robust discussion over if it was meant to protect against evil spirits or was a part of ancestor worship.

Cliopher waited patiently. The sound of marching got louder and louder, and he could imagine the square in front of the temple was now full of the Imperial Army Officer students. More and more feet climbed the stairs, and somewhere there was Princess Melissa. Probably one step below the temple itself.

Finally, the Imperial Salute rang out, to indicate the Emperor's arrival. There was a booming sound, which must have been the students and their teachers saluting. Cliopher realised he could see the shadow of the Emperor's palanquin rising up the stairs. With it, the courtiers and priest-wizards ranked down the stairs would be falling into the obeisance. It reached the top, eventually. Cliopher heard the Emperor exit with a most unimperial groan.

The final step he took alone, and the shadow of the palanquin was rapidly moved to the side, so the audience could get a good view of their Emperor. Kip could hear his sandals on the wood only hand-spans from his face. With his teeth, Cliopher pulled off the glove on his left hand. Then slowly, slowly, he moved it to under the square of light. There was the soft swishing sound of the Emperor spinning around to face outward.

Cliopher gently pushed the square of wood up and away with his knuckles, and then slipped his hand up and out. He felt slowly forwards, until the tip of his finger found the edge of the Emperor's robe. Taking one final deep breath, he slipped his hand under the robe, and snatched at Shallyr's ankle. It was, as they had planned, bare.

There was a loud crack as his palm met the Lord Emperor's bare skin. Even through the floorboards, Cliopher could see the usual glow of the Emperor had been replaced with a brightness more like looking at the sun directly. As fast as he could, Cliopher pulled back his hand. The screaming had begun.

By the time he had wrangled the glove back on, the screaming had spread all the way down the stairs. But he could also hear Princess Melissa's clear voice, demanding something. Cliopher's role was complete, Princess Melissa's had just begun.

The climb down the frame was tricker, not because it was harder to find footholds and handholds, but because Cliopher kept stopping to listen to what was going on above. It was dangerous, he needed to get out as soon as possible.

“Lord Wizard, if you do not intercede for us _now_, I do not see why the Sun should not strike us all down for blasphemy!” Princess Melissa cried. Following this, there was less screaming, and much more chanting.

She had it under control, and Cliopher needed to hurry. The gap under the edge was far easier to spot from the dark inside. He scrambled up to it, wiggled through it, and looked around carefully before raising himself up off the ground. There was no evidence of guards. Only the best trained would have stuck to their posts after that kind of display.

Sneaking back through the gardens, Cliopher spotted one of the ornamental ponds. Seeing nobody about, he dropped himself in. Once he was thoroughly soaked, or as much as he could be in what amounted to just enough water to kneel under, he got out loudly.

Shaking himself, he dripped his way through the gardens, towards one of the tea pavilions. There, as planned, he spotted Saya Auletes, talking in hushed tones with a number of Palace staff.

It said something about how anxious they were, that nobody commented that he was dripping wet and covered in mud. “The Sun struck the Emperor down for blasphemy,” whispered one of the staff, “Never in the history of the Empire has something like that happened.”

“Never in the history of the Empire has the Emperor stood in the place of the Sun statue in a temple, and demanded to be worshipped,” muttered someone else.

Someone new ran up to the group and announced breathlessly, “Princess Melissa has taken charge.”

There was a general sigh of relief, and a relaxing of tension. Saya Auletes stood up straight, and announced, “I better attend immediately.” Then she headed our in the direction Cliopher had come from.

Finally, someone noticed Cliopher, and sniggered. “Get thrown in a pond?” they asked.

Cliopher nodded. There was a general mix of good humour and sympathy at this. Someone handed him a rag so he could wipe off enough mud that he'd be let back into the Palace via the side doors.

It was done, he was done. Getting back up to the Princess' apartment seemed to occur in unconnected moments. A statue, a huddle of whispering courtiers, a pair of guards marching down the hall. He made it all the way back to the blue and green bedroom without anyone seemingly noticing him.

In the washroom, he stripped off all his clothes. Then more out of habit than anything else, he washed the rest of the mud off while he ran a hot bath, measuring out the salts and oils. 

When Cliopher finished the bath, he reflexively checked the moon clock. It was hours yet. He looked at the books, the oboe, the bed. There was nothing he wanted to do. He had murdered the Emperor, and blamed it on a god. Would the Sun strike *him* down? It would, Cliopher thought, only be fair.

The gods of the Vangaveye-ve accepted prayers, of course. But the general belief was that if you wronged a person you had to fix it with them or their family first, before seeking any gods. Shallyr's closest family had been Melissa, as far as Cliopher was aware.

He tried to turn to the Lays instead. They had always offered him comfort and advice, placed him in the long path of his history. There was a murder in the lays, and Cliopher hummed it as he tried to recall if it could be helpful.

---

The first flame pearl had been found by a young man. His older brother, the village chief, had claimed he should have it. The younger brother wanted to give it to their mother. Pretending to have accepted this, the elder brother asked the younger to show him where the pearl was found. But when they got to the reef, the elder brother struck the younger, and drowned him, throwing his body into the sea. 

He returned to the village distraught, claiming his younger brother had been swept away trying to find a larger flame pearl for their mother, after giving the chief the first. But in the night, Ani cried until the shaman became disturbed. They went out and listened to Ani, who told them the truth. The chief was exiled from the Vangaveye-ve, with his pearl he had valued above his community and family.

---

Cliopher could not seem himself in the chief. There was nothing he had gained, or wanted to gain, except perhaps a hope that nobody else would be tithed as he had been. Also, he was in no way the equal or superior to the Emperor. The Emperor wouldn't refuse a request he made, because it wasn't even possible for Cliopher to make a request. And Cliopher was already an exile. 

The Lays had always been his guide, his answer. He could remember all those days with Buru Tovo calling out questions and him responding in quotes. What would his Buru Tovo ask him now?

*Is this where you stop?*

Perhaps it was. There was nothing else he was needed for. Could he just go to sleep and not wake up? That sounded like such a relief.

Cliopher had only dressed in a simple long tunic. So he shucked off his sandals, and climbed into bed. As he closed his eyes, he thought of nothing at all.

---

Some time later, he woke to the moon clock chiming. He got up, and did the ritual. There was a tray of food on the table, but the idea of eating made him feel sick. He ignored it, instead curling up on the couch, hugging a cushion. From there, he could look out the window at the sky.

He watched the clouds and the stars. There was chatter in the hallway outside, and a distant part of him wondered what Princess Melissa would do next. It didn't seem real, somehow, the idea that the Emperor was gone. Even though he'd heard him scream as the magic burned him hot and fast.

Perhaps the Princess would be made Empress. Or maybe someone else. He could go and look in the book of family trees, it was just over on the shelf. Yet, it seemed like too far.

The dawn bells woke him again, and he did the dawn ritual. It wasn't clear to him why he was continuing. Perhaps because being purified had been the only way he was useful. If he kept waking up, it was important to do the one thing that mattered.

Cliopher bathed, and slept. He nibbled on a piece of flatbread, and watched the clouds. He curled up on the couch and cried.

Faleron left a letter on the desk, presumably from his mother. Cliopher couldn't bare to open it, to think about anyone at home. He was broken, lost, they should forget about him.

Saya Auletes knocked on the door, and asked him if he would come to lunch. He went and opened it, and politely accepted the invitation, asking what to wear. It felt like he was pulling the strings on a puppet of himself.

At lunch, he was seated next to Lady Jivane, and opposite Princess Melissa. Wizard Iyro joined them. There was talk of court factions, of what the Ouranatha were doing, and if the Army and Navy were likely to do this or that. Cliopher knew he should join in, that was correct behaviour. But he knew nothing of the machinations that were going on.

It became clear that the Princess was not the next in line to the throne. There was some other relative, who had been hidden away. Nobody knew anything about them, except it seemed that perhaps Princess Melissa had once read something they had written as a child.

There were fears of civil war if that person could not be found. Princess Melissa had support in the Palace, but there were other relatives better known and liked on other worlds. Cliopher hoped, desperately, that the Vangaveye-ve would be far enough away from any troubles. There was little else he could do.

Cliopher felt woefully out of place. He wished he'd been able to refuse the invitation, or had slept through the knock on the door. By the end of the meal, he wondered why the Princess was keeping him. Surely it would be safer for her to have him quietly killed? Already, he knew too much, had seen too much, done too much.

Passing Saya Auletes on the way back to his room, he almost asked her when it would be. She looked at him, curiously. But the words felt too heavy in his mouth, and he quietly slipped past.
