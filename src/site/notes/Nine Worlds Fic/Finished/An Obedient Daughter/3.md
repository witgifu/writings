When he finally put me aside, it was a relief.  

Part way through the second dance of an evening like any other, he stood up from his throne. We were in full court, and I was standing dressed in the Consort regalia on the lower dais, alone. The music stopped, and every knee in the room dropped to the floor, including mine. He had chastised me in public before, and that was what I was expecting. To have him, and a few of his cronies, hurl insults at me. Then I would crawl to the bottom step, and beg elegantly for his forgiveness.

Instead, he announced, "Our Consort is barren."

The silence was an ice that had formed on everyone, and frozen them in place.

"She is cursed by the gods, for what misdeed we neither know nor care. We are sick of her whining pleas and will hear her no longer." He sat down.

I had been given clear instructions not to speak, and so all I did was fall into the deepest formal prostration. At the time, I thought it only a new twist in the games he played, that he would demand I denounce one of my few remaining supporters, or perhaps be subject to further 'investigation' by the priest-wizards.

Someone in the room must have requested leave to speak. I could just see the gesture granting it. The Guard Knight Commander, ever a bootlicker, began to loudly declaim my many supposed sins. Greed, pride, acting unfitting to my position. He was followed by another, and another. I mentally ticked off the most loyal of his lackeys. But it didn't stop there. Voices I knew less well, ministers of government and high lords and ladies alike.

When some I had once counted as friends joined, I could hardly blame them. It was clear that only acceptable path was to denounce me. If I hadn't been ordered to silence already, I might have denounced myself. 

Perhaps they were even right. Maybe it wasn't generations of inbreeding, but my own selfishness, my lack of true devotion to my Emperor and the gods. Over five years, not a single pregnancy. More than sixty times, I had failed in my greatest duty. I lay my head on the floor, and waited for the guards to take me to the execution block.

Eventually, the repetition of the insults became too boring even for him. He must have made some gesture, for I heard the stamp of feet and spears behind me. Roughly, they pulled me back, twisting my arms. I let them, I knew how to move with them, to cause myself the least injury. They were more business-like, less vengeful, than he.

Down the steps, and across the jewelled floor. Everyone was trying to look at me, while carefully making sure they looked like they were looking at him. Except for the Lord of Ysthar, who looked me full in the face. His expression, perfectly court-neutral. He had not denounced me, and I wondered why. Perhaps he had simply not asked in time.

The moment passed, and I was taken through the guard's door, and into parts of the Palace I didn't remember. There were no doubt routes and doors known only to the guard, perhaps even the Inner Guard. I had been a child in the Palace, but I had never been free to explore such things. Always, duty. Always, watched.

I didn't particularly recognise the guards who held me, but then they were largely interchangeable. He had a tendency to suspicion that led to any that were notable being quickly removed from their posts. Once we were out of sight they let me walk properly, although they held me tightly.

I hoped, as we walked, that it would be quick. If he had given me leave to beg, that was all I had left to ask for. I was entitled to a master swordsman, but he had been known to strip titles prior to execution so that he could enjoy the entertainment of their slow and lingering deaths. Bears or tigers, goaded to rip people apart. Burning alive. Torture.

The guards stopped outside a door. We weren't in the dungeons, I could tell that by the way their footsteps echoed. We were seemingly up somewhere high. Perhaps I would be pushed off the roof. They opened the door, but it didn't lead to another flight of stairs, or even a balcony. Just a small bedroom, such as a pair of maids might share. A high window, a heavy and out-of-fashion bed, a chamber pot.

They shoved me inside, and locked the door behind me. Leaving me in the dark, except for the crack of light that seeped in around the door frame, and the moonlight through the window. Released from observation, from duty, with nobody to undress me.

Carefully, I removed what I could. Rings, pins in my hair, bracelets and bangles. The great gold collar I slid around carefully until I could reach the clasp. I assembled the hoard of a small dragon on the floor before me.

Thankfully, on this night, I had not been sewn into my costume. With patience, and a lot of wriggling, I managed to get down to my undershirt and stockings. Having laid out the priceless garments as best I knew how, I stepped over to the bed and climbed on it.

The mattress was lumpy, the one blanket thin and worn. In the past five years I had slept on the floor of the Imperial Bedroom, on a rattling coach, on a rug by the library fire. Whenever I was not needed, like an unloved lapdog. 

I curled up, and fell asleep immediately. The worst had happened, there was nothing more I could do.

When the door creaked as it moved, I woke instantly. I scrambled up from the bed to stand beside it. Three guards came in, but in the dark I couldn't see their expressions. I asked no questions, offered no challenge, I assumed they were here to take me to my execution.

"Melissa Damara," said the left-hand one, "We are here to reclaim the jewels."

"I am in all things but an obedient daughter of the Empire," I said, softly, demurely, and gestured with my hand to where I had laid them out on the floor. Had they been expecting I would desperately hold on to them? That they would have to rip them off me?

I had no desire to go to my execution wearing the chains of my office. What would it prove? That I had been falsely accused? When had truth mattered? Would it get me any mercy? No.

Quietly, they collected everything I had discarded, and left. I returned to the bed.

The second time I woke was to a pigeon cooing at the dawn. This was so incongruous that I simply lay there listening. Nobody came to poke me with medical probes, or to perform rituals, or to send me to him. I was alone, although I couldn't assume that nobody was listening.

Inside I laughed, that I would be relishing my imprisonment. Five years of walking on eggshells had ended. It had ended in failure, but somehow on this side it didn't seem to matter. Upon reflection, I knew I would feel different if he chose to starve or torture me.

I sat up, and the pigeon flew away from the window. By the weak daylight I could see the room was very plain. Whitewashed walls, scrubbed timber floor, high ceiling. There was only the one door, and not even a stool. 

The only other object in the room was the chamber-pot. I considered its plain, ceramic form for a while. I thought it likely wheel-thrown; unlike fine slip-cast wares, it had a very slight asymmetry. Perhaps the person who made it did that deliberately, to make it different from the mass-produced and identical versions. Or maybe they were in a rush, or weren't highly skilled. The pot didn't tell me, and I wasn't so interested in it to look for a maker's mark.

Eventually, I used it. Some such vessels are magical, spiriting away their contents immediately. This one was the more prosaic variety, alas. I left it beside the door, and sat on the bed.

I waited. I was an expert at waiting. With nobody to watch me, it was easy. Letting go of my body, I could float and watch myself instead. Or disappear into childhood memories of storybooks.

The bells rang noon when the door opened next, and I stood again to face it. A guard put down a bowl and jug on the floor, looking at the chamber pot with a wrinkled nose. From behind him darted a servant boy who swapped it for another.

"My thanks," I said softly, with a small bow. I had no idea which of my titles had been formally stripped, but I could assume at best I now ranked at the bottom of the aristocracy.

The guard snorted, and the servant ignored me. With long practice, I noted but did not react.

When the door closed again, I examined the delivery. A jug of water, and a bowl of gruel. Enough food and water not to starve, just. No spoon or cup. I ate slowly over the afternoon, with my hand, licking my fingers clean, wondering when he would set the date for my execution. Perhaps the priest-wizards had convinced him to wait for a propitious conjunction.

That evening the guards came, four this time. The captain pointed to where I should stand, and obedient as always, I did so.

He slapped my face, and kneed me in the stomach. Familiar with this pattern, I folded over and down, turning to try and put my head between my body and the wall.

Expecting them to grab my hair and pull me back up, I covered my neck with my hands. Instead, he kicked me. They took turns doing so, not particularly enthusiastically. They must have been ordered to beat me, and were making sure they could all swear truthfully they had.

After a kick each, they all filed out again. None of them had said a word to me. They could have, they could have called me all the names he used when he was drunk and angry. Or high and angry. Or just taking out his frustration on an easy target. The guards had been kind. A few bruises, not even any skin broken.

I wondered why.

When the bells rang midnight, a nervous priest-wizard came. Bowing respectfully, I couldn't see any of their symbols of rank, although I didn't think I saw any glint of mask.

They chalked out the octagon and star-points on the floor, and I very nearly laughed aloud. But, instead, calm and decorous, I stepped into place. As I had every month since my wedding, I performed the High Full Moon Blessing. 

I had just enough magical sensitivity to feel it doing something. The priest seemed to relax, they must have been able to sense the effectiveness. 

When I finished, I stepped out of the markings, and they said, "Thank you, your highness." They must have realised that was wrong, because they rushed through the cleanup without another glance my way. 

On the third day I do not remember much, except for lying on the bed and shaking. The physicians had given me so many different medications I had no idea what it might be. Perhaps it was only psychological. Every part of my body felt like it was burning. I cried out, no doubt begging him incoherently, an automatic response to the pain.

I must have managed to drag myself to the chamber pot and the water jug. The memories are hazy. The days blurred, and I tried to sleep as much as possible.

When the door opened, I woke, and stood, and did as I was bid. Rituals and beatings. Nobody needed to question me, I would get no trial.

I turned my underclothes inside out to stop them sticking to my skin. With nothing to wash with I became increasingly filthy, but I didn't resort to wasting my little drinking water. I combed my hair with my fingers, and tied the braids with bits of ribbon I picked from the hem of my shirt.

My period, the rhythm of my life for five years, never came. For a split second, I had hope. Had I, at the final step, succeeded in getting pregnant? I was uncertain how many days had passed since I was imprisoned. But no, I knew *he* had not had procreative sex with me since my last period. Nor had anyone else. They were drugging the food, to stop my cycles. An ironic relief, when I had no way to wash and no pads or sponges.

It became clear that, for whatever reason, he was not going to execute me. He would not bring me back now, and perhaps in the dark, cold, lonely nights, I finally found my courage. I stopped eating. What was the point? To keep this body, that had failed in its only purpose, barely alive?

The guards paid no notice, even when I no longer got out of the bed when they entered. For the beatings, they struck me with a cane rod where I lay. The bruises took even longer to heal. Perhaps they finally took pity on the weeping I could not hold back, for I thought they came less often, and shorter.

It must have been the priest who reported my state. When they came to do a ritual, and I stumbled to the chalk marks, struggling to stand upright. All my mind was filled with the desire for food, but I was used to ignoring the needs of my body. I had performed rituals with still-healing bones, swollen bruises, so full of medications I had no idea where I was. Slurring slightly, I did my duty.

The next day, the guards force-fed me. Held me to the bed, shoving a rubber tube down my throat. I choked, but I was weak. They poured thin gruel, probably with drugs and sugar added, down into me.

As soon as they removed the tube, I vomited it up on the floor. The servant boy came to clean it up. When he was done, I saw the guard captain look between the boy and me.

"If you don't eat, we'll force feed you and beat the boy," he said, kicking him for emphasis.

My throat was too raw for me to speak properly. It wasn't a strategy *he* had ever tried. Possibly it had never occurred to him that anyone might see a reason to protect anyone above themselves.

"I am in all things but an obedient daughter of the Empire," I croaked, forcing my words out past the pain, "You could have ordered me. You need not beat or force."

The captain looked at me in surprise. Did he think I was a rebellious political prisoner? That I had stopped eating as some kind of protest? If I had wanted that end, I should have killed myself on my wedding day, when everyone was watching. Not forgotten in a tower, a slow lingering death of starvation all that was left to me.

He grunted, and gestured to the guard with the new bowl of gruel to bring it forward. I pushed myself up against the headboard, panting with the effort.

"Eat it," said the captain, as I was handed the bowl. I held it to my face with shaking arms, and lapped it like a cat drinking milk. I could barely hold it for more than a few moments.

"Please," I whispered, "Might I have a spoon?"

The captain waved at one of the other guards, who disappeared, and then reappeared shortly with what looked like the sugar spoon from their tearoom. Whatever the origin, I took it with a quiet, "My thanks," and was able to eat more readily.

Having not eaten at all for many days, my stomach was not much enamoured with this, and I tried to go as slowly as I could. The captain watched me impatiently, the servant boy nervously, and the rest of the guards with the calm faces of those who had to do a lot of standing around.

"The priest-wizards say they need you alive for the magic," said the Captain, finally giving up on watching me, "If I see any food in the bowls coming out of this room, it's back to force-feeding, understood?"

I nodded, and added belatedly, "Yes, captain." I had no idea if he had any other rank by birth or gift, but he seemed not to be offended by the address.

When they had all left, and the door was once again locked, I put the bowl down on the floor. It wasn't finished yet, but I knew if I continued now I would only vomit again, and didn't wish to get in trouble for that. Instead, I curled around my aching stomach, and tried to take a nap.

The days returned to their previous rhythm, except that I was no longer beaten. Dirty, lonely, kept just far enough from starving that I could stand in a ritual circle: I was the happiest I had been since my wedding day. One morning, I realised I had not a single bruise or injury from anyone else. I cried, silently, into the mattress, at the ridiculousness of the situation.

I was, in all things, but an obedient daughter of the Empire.
