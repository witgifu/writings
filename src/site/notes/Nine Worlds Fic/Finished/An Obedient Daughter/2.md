Lady Persephone picked up the golden wand from the golden tray, and after polishing it with the silk cloth, she handed it to Lady Enderva. She then turned to me, and lifted the hem of my silk nightgown, where it rested against my shins.

"Moon bless you, Illustrious Consort," she said.

"So we all pray," I replied, and forced myself to relax. As I had for five mornings out of every month for nearly five years. We both meant it, but after so long it seemed unlikely our prayers would be answered.

Enderva inserted the wand, and swiped the tip three times around my cervix. Then she removed it, and handed it carefully back to Persephone, who put it on the tray and turned the little golden sand timer. Enderva fussed with my nightgown, and I looked at the ceiling.

For the first year I had prayed by rote. In the second year I had prayed fervently. In the third, desperately. Now, it was back to rote; I knew my prayers would go unanswered, but I did my duty.

When the timer finished, I knew what the answer would be. No matter how hard I tried I couldn't stop that tiny bubble of hope. Beautiful, shining, that I dared no longer give any voice or conscious thought to. Once, I had imagined presenting it to _him_, and that in return I might receive a single scrap of kindness. I knew better now; the hope was pointless, and he was incapable of kindness. So I tried to keep my dreams to the possible, desperately fought to push that hope down, and thought I had. 

Until the timer stopped, and the hope shattered. Both ladies dropped into the obeisance, and Enderva said to the floor, "Next time, Glorious Consort."

I waved them up, hand brushing through the shards of my hope, falling like snow through the room. It wouldn't be next time, we all knew. Yet every time it hurt. A kind of grief, not blunted by time, no matter how long. Quiet, which I could not share, or even express, for fear of someone else blaming themselves. For fear of him blaming anyone but me.

I had once heard that being waited on hand and foot was to 'provide for my every happiness'. Thankfully this was a notion my mother disbursed me of well before I was wed. It was so I could provide an heir, nothing more. I was kept in the best health, a shinning polished ornament, but my happiness was immaterial.

My mother had grieved years of stillbirths, miscarriages, and infanticides. I had seen the last few pregnancies and births first-hand. My own grief was nothing in comparison, could be nothing in comparison. I was nothing, in comparison. She had presented her lord two healthy children, I had not even managed to provide a positive test.

We proceeded to the bathroom, where I was washed. The dressing room, where I was dressed and painted and anointed with perfume. The breakfast room, where I was fed.

This first meal I always had alone, since *he* wouldn't rise for some hours yet. He slept after the dawn ceremony, until one of his appetites woke him. I took tea, and plain toast. No matter how the attendants tried to push the jam or honey towards me. There were so few choices in my life, I cherished every one. 

Every quarter-hour of my days was scheduled. Every public event *he* didn't want, he sent me to. I gave speeches not of my writing, and offered pleasant greetings to dignitaries, scholars, and vicious warlords. This morning was for visiting the army officer hospital.

I was redressed for this next occasion, and then chivvied into the palanquin. Down to the front court, where I took three steps in the sunlight. No Consort had been stolen by the Sun. Then into the carriage, and the city.

Visiting the sick and injured was one of the few activities I did that I enjoyed. It was exhausting, and sometimes harrowing, but always I felt like I had made them feel valued. I could do nothing for their wounds; not convince *him* not to send them to war, or treat them better on their return. But I could listen to their stories, and thank them for their sacrifice. 

They did blur together after a while. A few that day did not. A woman who had lost all her toes to frostbite, saving her little band of scouts when they got lost in a storm. A captain who hugged tight to the toy bear their little child had brought them. An ambulance officer that couldn't believe he had come down with meningitis the day he arrived at the front.

Those who said too much, and those who said nothing at all. 

On the way back, I did allow myself to cry. It was considered acceptable for me to mourn the loss to the Empire, and if I cried also for myself, nobody would know.

That afternoon, as I worked on my correspondence, I felt my period start. Always it would be a particular ache, and the first large clot. I made the gesture to my ladies to indicate I needed the toilet. Then as they attached the pad to the garter belt around my waist, I asked them where *he* was.

Together, my ladies and I processed to his bedroom.

"I humbly beg my exalted Emperor for the gift of an audience," I told the door guards. 

One looked bored, the other sneered. They knew what this was. Word passed through, and back.

"In fifteen minutes," said the bored guard.

"The Glorious One is with his favourite," said the other.

"I am in all things but an obedient daughter of the Empire," I said in reply.

We waited more than a quarter-hour, but that was his prerogative. I had no pressing engagements until Evening Court; I did not want to request this audience there. He had made me do so once, and I would if I had to. But I preferred this performance to be with as much privacy as I was ever given.

This time, he relented, and granted me admission. I stepped softly into the room, head bowed, not making eye contact with anyone. The dirty looks I received from everyone didn't need my witness. At the prescribed distance I dropped into the full formal prostration, and waited to be acknowledged.

"Get on with it," he said, sounding tired.

Turning on my high, sweet, breathy voice, I begged. "Oh most Merciful and Gentle Lord, I have failed you again. Please, I beg of you, most Generous and Illustrious One, have mercy on those who have served me, and done their best to prepare this vessel for your holy seed. If there is blame to apportion, it is mine alone, Serenity, and I accept willingly whatever punishment or penance you assign in your justice."

He groaned, and there was the sound of him shuffling on the bed. Feet pressed into slippers. Sliding across the floor. Pulling me up by my hair, he growled, "Useless bitch." With his other hand he slapped me across the face, and kneed me in the stomach. 

I rolled with the blows as much as I could, whimpering slightly. He liked to know the impact of his words and actions, and he had trained me well to be obedient to how he liked everything done. I offered no more pleas, apologies, or protests.

Throwing me down to the floor again, he continued to kick, aiming at my head. I covered my face with my arms, letting the tears run free. Tiring early, he smashed his heel into my wrist, and then turned away.

"Get out," he said, bored.

I scrambled to my feet, and exited backwards as quickly as I could. My ladies and guards formed up around me in the hallway, hurrying me away.

"Thank you, my lady," said Persephone.

"My lady, you are so generous to protect us," echoed Enderva.

Trying to smile against the swelling of my jaw, I said softly, "You all do your best for me."

Back in my chambers, the physician examined me. She set the bones of my wrist, and applied the magic that knit them back together. Eased the swelling, faded the bruises, set days of healing against the scratches and burned away any infection. 

_He_ wouldn't want me to appear damaged at Court, even though it was his own work. It would be over a week before he was close enough to me to even tell that I had been healed, and by then he would have forgotten exactly what he had done. Would be thinking of what he could do to me next.

It was nothing less than what I deserved. I was, objectively, a failure as his Consort. If he had been a different Emperor, perhaps he would have set me aside, cloistered me in an abbey at the edge of the Empire. But he was who he was, and he would no doubt execute me for my failure. Until he did, I would do what I could to protect those around me from my failures.
