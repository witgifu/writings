I don't think I experienced a winter in that room, but it was possible that they simply heated the room with magic. There was no snow visible out the window, but perhaps I never looked at the right time. Only three people ever came into the room. The guard captain, who opened the door, the servant boy, and the priest.

Until, one day, there was a knock. I waited, thinking it must have been an accident in the hallway outside, or maybe even someone in the room above or below.

It came again, more insistent.

"Come in?" I called out as I stood up from the bed, my voice cracking for disuse. Had the captain fallen foul of  *him* and been replaced? Perhaps the priest or servant had.

I placed myself against the wall, looking down. If this was something new, perhaps it was finally my execution. They had found some other woman in our family to perform the rituals, and could be rid of me. Perhaps he had pried Princess Anastasiya from her home on Zunidh.

"Melissa?" said a wholly unexpected voice, "Is that you?"

Looking up slightly, I could see the robes of two lord magi, the colours of Ysthar and Zunidh. I swallowed, and having lost the automatic skill of suppressing it, began to shake. I didn't know what to say, what to do.

"Oh, Melissa," said Lady Jivane, stepping into the room. I pressed myself back into the wall. Why was she here? When had she been made magus of Zunidh? I didn't understand.

"Has the Glorious One ordered my execution, my lady?" I asked. It was still the only conclusion I could believe. That he might recall me to his side, I neither believed possible nor wanted.

"Emperor Shallyr is dead, your highness," said Lord Thalassior, "Come, you need not stay here."

"Who is his heir?" I asked, trying to force the shakes down, trying to remember the mental tricks I used to calm myself.

There was a pause, that felt like a yawning chasm opening up before me. Was it not known? Would there be civil war between the decedents of Emperor Lodyr's siblings? Surely Princess Anastasia or my unknown brother had a better claim?

"You, Melissa," said Jivane eventually.

Finally, I looked up. I mouthed 'no', unable to say it. My hands pressed against the walls, and I tried to focus of the texture of the walls, the sturdiness. I must be dreaming, hallucinating. I shook my head, continuing the denial.

"Emperor Shallyr executed every other near relation," said Lord Thalassior, "Every decedent of Emperor Lodyr; many more distant relations fled the Empire. If you refuse the throne..." He shrugged, the most informal gesture I had ever seen from the imposing and powerful wizard.

I looked from him, to Jivane, and bowed my head. "I am in all things but an obedient daughter of the Empire," I said, trying to drag up the court-formal voice I had once perfected. Trying to believe the words that had once fallen so often from my lips. Dragging out memories of how to behave, what to say, how to stand. I didn't want to, but what I had wanted never once mattered.

"Come, Glorious One," said Lord Thalassior, stepping back into the hallway.

I flinched at the address. My whole life it had belonged to the men who had ruled me; first my uncle and then my cousin and husband. Taking a deep breath, I said, "Yes, my lord and lady."

Stumbling a little with the movement I wasn't used to, I followed them out into the hallway. The guards forming up behind me. Through back ways I didn't bother to remember, we wound our way through the Palace of Stars. Eventually, a door opened in front of me to a hall I recognised.

The main hall of the Imperial Apartments. It was, as I had never seen it, completely empty. Not a courtier, or servant, or a painting or stick of furniture. Yet, I could recognise it by the stained-glass windows.

"What..." I whispered, staggering to a stop.

"In his last month, Emperor Shallyr became paranoid, Glorious One. He was convinced that anything moveable could be an assassin in disguise and ordered everything removed," said Lord Thalassior, as if he was reporting on the weather or a minor bit of gossip.

"Oh," I said, uncertain what to do with this information. Shallyr had been exceedingly cruel, violent, selfish, but I had not known him to be quite so irrational.

They led me through the empty rooms, to the Heart Stone. Three nervous-looking priest-wizards awaited us. How many had Shallyr executed, that those left now didn't have masks? Or had Shallyr banned the masks? If the priest-wizards were used to hiding behind masks, perhaps they had never had to learn to control their faces.

Nodding to them, as I would have as Consort, I stepped forward into the room. Participating in Schooled Magic rituals was at least something I had continued to do in my imprisonment, and was still used to.

"What do you need of me?" I asked, and then, closing my eyes, corrected myself, "What do you need of us?" I wondered how much time I could give myself to get used to the new ways I was expected to perform. Possibly none.

"If you would be so kind as to stand in the ritual signal we have made for your Illustrious Self, Glorious One," said the central wizard, "And if your Radiancy would be so generous as to please hold out your right hand towards me."

I stood as instructed. They chanted, and waved incense, and threw powders and salts around. A ritual knife appeared in the hand of the one who had spoken, and I braced myself against what I expected to happen next.

Indeed, he sliced a thin cut across my palm, and then taking my bleeding hand, pressed it to the Heart Stone. There was a sound like a crash of thunder, and there was a sigh much like relief from the three wizards and the lords magi. A few more gestures and words completed the ritual, and healed my hand.

"Most Serene and Holy One," said the wizard who seemed to do all the speaking, "That is the first ritual to bind your Glorious Self to the Throne of Astandalas that will culminate in ten days with your Radiancy's coronation."

I nodded. "Thank you....?" I said, turning the end into a question. His skin nearly as dark as mine, it didn't show a blush, but the priest-wizard bowed low. "Idamar, High Priest of the Sun, Glorious One," he said, and indicating his colleagues identified them as the High Priest of the Moon, and the Evening Star.

"Mel—Glorious One," said Jivane, and I tried to resign myself to never hearing my name from her again, "Would you like a bath?"

Doing my best to suppress the urge to cry, I said, "That would be most appreciated, Lady Jivane." Clean. I could be clean.

She gave me a sympathetic smile, and I carefully didn't look at what I could see of my own disgusting body.

"Afterwards," I said to them all, "We should address our household."

Jivane and Lord Thalassior bowed, accepting the order.

With the halls and rooms empty, and my body shaking with the exertion, I walked slowly. I needed to go, not to my old suite of rooms, but to the Imperial Baths. Thinking carefully, I found the door to the Emperor's—no, the Empresses's—dressing room. Like all the doors since the ritual, it opened before me. Two attendants waited there, and both dropped into the deepest obeisance.

I gave the gesture I had so often received, to stand. This room was not quite as bare as the others had been. Two racks of clothes, a gold-framed mirror, and the dressing pedestal. Carefully, I stepped up onto it, and held my arms out. As Consort, I had not been permitted to dress or undress myself, nor had any say in what I wore. It would be easiest to continue not to say anything.

Not looking at my face, the two young women efficiently removed the filthy shirt, drawers, and stockings. Wrapping me in a fine linen robe that would no doubt be burnt after contact with my body in this state. I tried not to think why these women had this position, nor about the guards behind me seeing my naked body. For all the years I had been Consort I had born witness to the abuses of *him*, and accepted my total lack of privacy. I wouldn't have any privacy now, but I was determined that my attendants not live in fear.

"You may wear the protective garments, or not, as you prefer. We have no preference but that of your safety and comfort," I said, as I stepped down, carefully looking neither in the face. My touch wouldn't burn yet, not until after the coronation. But I knew well the power of psychological safety a layer of fabric could give.

They both genuflected, and one of them whispered, "Your condescension to your poor servants is most generous, Illustrious One; we do not deserve even a moment of your thought, Lady of Rising Stars."

Flicking my hand in both acknowledgement, and to release them, I walked out and into the Imperial Bedchamber. I had not looked once in the mirror. The bed was the only piece of furniture remaining. Trying to ignore it, I forced myself to keep going. In the baths, I could collapse.

When I arrived, I made the same offer to the bath attendants. Their leader in turn made the same response. They removed the robe, and finally I was able to sit on a stool as they washed me. This I _had_ been able to do for myself as Consort, and the sponges-on-sticks were a strange experience. I responded promptly to their quiet directions, and closed my eyes against the water and soap. There was a lot to remove.

Behind me, I could just hear the edge of a whispered conversation between Jivane and one of the attendants. Finally, Jivane walked around in front of me, and said softly, "Glorious One?"

"Yes, Lady Jivane? What request have you of me—of us?" I replied, opening my eyes and attempting a polite smile. 

"There has not yet been time to train a hairdresser," she said, not meeting my eyes. My heart twisted inside that she already felt this space between us. And then worry, for Persephone had done my hair, before I was imprisoned. What had happened to her?

"Our hair is no doubt mostly matted beyond recovery," I said, hating every word, "Cut it short, and we will wear wigs and head-coverings until it grows back and someone is available to care for it." I knew a skilled and patient person, could, with enough time, probably have repaired it. Neither time nor skill were available, and it wasn't the fault of anyone here.

The attendants cannot have been entirely clueless, for when they took me to another chair, they doused my hair in oil before approaching it with scissors. I tried to ignore what was happening, and just relax for a little while. Soon, I would need to think about politics, and the armies and navy, and whatever terrible things had happened in the final weeks of my cousin's rule.

Eventually, they stopped filing nails and removing hair and whatever else they felt was required for the Empress. My body had never been my own, it had always belonged to the Empire, and now was no different. The jewels would be different, but the chains were familiar. Finally clean, walking slowly, I made my way to the magnificent main bathing pool.

Carefully, I walked down the steps and into the water. The gentle warmth was a blessing on my aching body, as was the respite from gravity. I had been here before, but carefully pushed those memories to the side. Slowly, not wanting to spend energy I would need later, I wafted myself around the bath as I floated.

After less time than I had hoped, there was a polite cough. I pushed myself back to the stairs, and then forced myself up them and out of the water. The attendants dried me, and then wrapped me in another robe. 

Back through the bedroom, we returned to the dressing room. Presumably *he* had died earlier in the day (what time was it? I had ignored the bells), not enough time to make anything new. They could have found some of my Consort clothes in storage, but I wondered how they would look on my emaciated frame.

Ties, wraps, and belts seemed to be the solution. Including a black silk turban, dripping with tiny diamonds. A pair of white silk slippers, that had perhaps been hastily decorated with bees. My face was painted, perfume applied, nails lacquered.

Finally, I looked in the mirror. I didn't see myself. The family nose was the only recognisable feature. Hollow cheeks, eyes that seemed too large. They had carefully covered with fabric or cosmetics the lasting scars *he* had given me, and had done what they could with optical illusions to make me look hale.

"Thank you," I said, recalling myself to a lifetime of court manners, "You have all done excellent work in unforgiving circumstances." I hoped they would believe me.

Jivane led me onward to the official study, and the attendants and guards followed behind. I would need to make my first grand performance in a long time. Half-way down the hall, I asked, "Is it—what is the date? We rather lost track of time." As if to emphasise the point, the bells rang nine o'clock. 

"It is Silverheart, Glorious One," said Jivane, with a small bow, "In the seventh and last year of Emperor Shallyr."

A year and a half. Both more and less time than I had feared. I inclined my head in thanks, and proceeded. It was Silverheart, time of family, when I had no family left. Except, perhaps, in a tower at the edge of the Empire, I had a lost brother. Desperately, I hoped he was happy there. Possibly nobody but I, now, even knew he existed. It seemed impossible to drag him back here as my heir, a man who hadn't seen another person since he turned sixteen. To the rest of the family I would issue pardons, and pleas, and try to find someone in the extended family tree who was... competent, and willing. It might be a long search.

In the study, after I gave the gesture for them all to rise, I recognised a very few faces. The guard captain, trying to hide at the back, and the servant boy. One of the kitchen staff seemed familiar, one of the clerks, and the ancient Master of the Lower Wardrobe. I smiled politely and benevolently at them all. Enderva was not there, and I hoped that was because she was safe somewhere else.

"Some few of you may remember us from our time as Consort," I began, trying to break my habit of making eye contact with any familiar and supportive face by looking out the far windows, "To those who gave us succour then, by deed or word or omission, we are most grateful. Shallyr's rule over his household was cruel; we do not intend to continue in his ways."

I took a steadying breath, and continued. "If any of you would wish to retire, please do so with our blessing and thanks, we will see to it that you are supported. Nobody will be purified for our service, but all are welcome to wear the protective garments. No gambling, or drinking, or drugs, or sex, will occur within the Imperial Apartments. Our family has squandered the gifts of our forbearers, and we will return in humble supplication to our ancestors the Sun and the Moon... and to the One Above."

Even amongst the practiced Imperial Household, this proclamation received some astonished looks. It was a dramatic change in what was, effectively, state policy. There were some who believed that the decadence of the Emperor was proof of the security and prosperity of the Empire. To me, it had been the sounds, sights, and smells that swum around my time as Consort. That now, in my own head, I could name.

Five years of brutalisation and rape at the hands of my cousin.

If I must take these rooms (I must) and this throne (my duty) and these chains (my burden), I did not have to inhabit them as he had. I had to believe it was possible to be a good Empress.

"Hopefully the current state of the apartments makes that easier," I added, trying to insert a moment of levity. There was only tension, and I mentally stepped back. I had months of relief from *him*, they had possibly seen him this morning, depending on how he died. It wasn't really necessary for me to know how he died, I could guess that some combination of powers had finally decided to risk treason. With good reason, they might be expecting retribution from me; either for the regicide or the treatment I had at *his* hands.

"We will not hold against any of you what you did in service to Shallyr, or Eritanyr. For we would be heaping coals on our own head. But know that we intend to enforce the laws that they flagrantly flouted," I tried to offer a reassuring smile, "We know that some may have already run, or be in hiding, for fear of us. The amnesty extends to them as well."

I moved to more mundane and dry matters; an outline of a schedule, requests for appointments to certain positions, decoration preferences. Finally, I came to the three most crucial members of the Imperial Household. I had not seen the ones I remembered, and so I asked, "Is there a Seneschal, a Groom or Matron of the Chamber, and a Private Secretary?"

Lady Jivane bowed low, and said, "The Private Secretary was executed yesterday, Glorious One, and a new one has not been appointed. Emperor Shallyr dismissed the last Seneschal and refused any appointment of a replacement. The Groom of the Chamber, oh most Merciful One, was found dead in the Imperial Bed beside the late Emperor."

That was... unfortunate. Or possibly fortunate. If the men who had held those positions two years ago had still been there, I would have struggled to work with them. Who that I had known and could trust was left? I could hardly appoint the Lady Magus of Zunidh to be my Matron of the Chamber. She was no doubt busy with her duties already.

"We expect the Service will be able to appoint a new secretary. Are either of the sisters of the Prince of Katharmoon in the Palace? Or the Princesses of Rhondé?"

None of them would be trained, and prepared, but I couldn't wait for that. Whoever I appointed would have to divide their duties between what they could do now and what they would have to delegate.

"Lady Felicia is in residence with her husband, Glorious One," answered Jivane, "The family of the King of Rhondé are mostly in attendance at his court," (safer there, no doubt) "Although Lady Honoria keeps their apartments here. Only her uncle, Prince Benneret, has visited much."

This was going to take more time than a few minutes, and I would need to interview those people I didn't already know. Or rather, they would be granted audiences. And I still needed to find where *he* had abandoned the reigns of government.

"If the head clerk and the Groom of the Chamber's second could remain, everyone else may return to their duties. If you would like to take up the offer of retirement, please talk to them later today," I tried my best to sound warm and thankful, rather than tired. I could feel a years-long headache coming on, and I was _not_ trained for this job.

Everyone else filed out, with deep bows. Except for the guard captain, who had prostrated himself on the floor. Why had he not gone to his Commander, if he had a petition? Or was the Commander of the Imperial Guard _also_ a vacant position? Had he suddenly felt guilty for the way he had treated me as a prisoner?

"Captain?" I asked, "What keeps you here?" It was perhaps a little harsher than I should have been, and I corrected myself. "What would you bring to our attention?"

"Most of the Inner Guard have fled, Glorious One," he said to the floor, "It has often been the custom when the Emperor dies in suspicious circumstances, that all are executed."

Looking at the two guards who had been following me since my release, I couldn't recall if I had seen them before. There had also been another two, who were presumably posted now on one of the outer doors. Why had Jivane and Lord Thalassior chosen them?

"The pardon extends to the guard," I said, levelly. I didn't really want it to, many of the guards had joined in enthusiastically in Shallyr's depredations, but I also didn't want to begin with executions. "As does the offer of retirement, and the promise to enforce the law for all."

"So merciful and gracious you are to us, Glorious One, completely undeserving as we are. In recognition of the suffering I personally afflicted on you, most majestic and sweet defender of your people, my own life is forfeit, and would gladly end it if you but say the word, Glorious One. But there are so few inner guards left, Shield of the Empire, I would not do so without your permission."

This, I saw, was the heart of the matter. "Including yourself, captain, how many remain, and what ranks?"

"Ten, Glorious One... And I find myself the only one of rank," he said, not moving from his position on the floor.

Just enough to _appear_ like I had a full rota of guards, and not enough if anybody got sick or injured. Not only was the Commander position vacant, but all the shift Lieutenants, and presumably many other positions that I didn't even know existed. For that matter, why had a Guard Captain been seemingly the only person guarding me of late? Another question I wouldn't ask.

A vague memory from my childhood stirred. "The recruits are trained by semi-retired guards," I said, desperately wishing the room came with a chair, or the dignity (or costume) of the Empress allowed me to sit on the floor. "Have any of those remained, and came from the Inner Guard?"

"Yes, Glorious One," said the captain, "Two of the three."

"Very good," I said, "You and they are to attend us... with whatever most senior army and navy officers we have to hand," I looked to Jivane, "At three o'clock?"

She nodded, and I smiled, and something of our old relationship was there. I treasured it, one glimpse that something other than the carapace of duty remained.

"We are sorry to say that you are correct, we will need you to postpone your suicide, captain." I gave the gesture to dismiss him, and he got up. It was, I noticed somewhere in my stomach, what he had done to me. He had forced me to live, and now I was forcing him to. Hoping that he had not been so genuine in his offer to kill himself, I watched him leave.

Finally, I turned to the senior clerk and the man who was possibly the Master of the Bath.

"We are sure you have many pressing things to attend to, so we shall try to be brief. We prefer competency to rank; if you need work done, get someone to do it. Better Palace staff who know what they are doing, than the scions of great houses who don't know how to care for silk or file a receipt."

They both bowed. The clerk I remembered as quietly competent, which was possible in the lower levels of the household. She would have never needed to see *him*. The Master of the Bath had the blank look of those who had become inured to the horrors of *his* life, and simply moved through their existence trying not to be killed. It was probably what I had often looked like.

"We will take lunch at noon, in whichever room has sufficient furnishings," I said.

The Master of the Bath bowed deeply, and said in a horse voice I would surely have remembered if I had heard it before, "The breakfast room, Glorious One."

When I gave them the gesture to release them they both departed as quickly as they could. Now it was once again just me, Jivane, and the guards. "_I_," I said determinedly, "Am so glad you are here, Jivane."

She smiled, and bowed, but only stood as close as was permitted for those not attending to the body of the Empress. Soon, I would not be able to touch anyone. Desperate for the last time someone held my hand not to be the priest holding it to the Heart Stone, I held it out.

"Glorious One," she said, casting her eyes down again, "I did not defend you. I pretended you didn't exist, and went along with Emperor Shallyr."

"You came when you could," I said, my heart twisting within me, "I would rather have you alive now than executed for trying to protect me; I don't hold it against you. If *he* had given me a chance, I would have denounced myself."

She looked up, finally making eye contact. I could see unshed tears in her eyes, and I wondered if there were the same in my own. She took a shuddering breath, and held out her hand as well. Gently, the fingertips bumped together, and then I stepped forward and grasped her hand in both of mine.

"I have no family, and nearly no friends left alive," I said, "Please don't leave me, Jivane, I need you."

"Of course," she said, and then with a flash of the wit I had always loved added, "Who else would tell you to put down a new novel and go to sleep?"

She then looked at me in shock and fear, dropping to her knees with me still holding her hand.

I pulled her back up, and into an embrace. "I may never get to do this again, Jivane, but please remember it any time I look at you fondly. You are my heart-sister, and I need you. Including needing you to tell me to go to bed. Or that the outfit I've chosen in horrible, or that I can't subsist entirely on peppermint tea, or that I should delegate better, or that I'm moping."

Carefully, Jivane hugged me back.

"Let's find a room with chairs," I said, "There must be at least one couch left."
