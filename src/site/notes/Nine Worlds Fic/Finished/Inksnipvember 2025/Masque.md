It was a masque ball tonight. You were not in costume, could not be in disguise, it was far too dangerous. Someone might touch you, might look in your eyes. The whole point of court was for everyone to know. To see, from a safe distance. The golden emperor on the golden chair in the golden hall. They wore fanciful costumes, you admired them, they worshipped you.

There was a theme tonight. It appeared to be based on the play _Aurelius Magnus and the Seafarer King_. You quite liked the play, although you hadn't seen it in many years. Your eyes wandered over them, mentally scoring their costumes. 

You could see quite a few aristocrats dressing as Aurelius, it was by far the most popular choice. Then there were some soldiers, or his generals, or his (rumoured) lovers. 

The Seafarer King was the more daring costume, and the more varied. A few had attempted a conglomeration of the 'Islander' types from the Atlas of Imperial Peoples. Some had gone for an 'ocean god' look. One had taken the laziest approach of assuming the Seafarer King dressed as a modern sailor, but with a crown made of tropical flowers.

There was a special dance, in honour of the Fire Dance that the Seafarer King had supposedly danced for Aurelius. It was a simple variation on one of the standard court dances, but everyone was holding candles. You hoped nobody dripped wax on the floor.

While the dance wound on (and on), your eyes drifted to the back. A spec of burnt umber was winding through those hanging at the edge of the room. There must have been some kind of emergency, and some aristo was being called out of court to come and deal with it. You would, no doubt, get a report on it tomorrow.

The spot eventually made their way along the side wall, far enough that you could see some of their face. They must have needed one of the dancers. The Minister for Health was currently cavorting with a Duke from Alinor, the Service member seemed to have eyes on them.

Something about the secretary's face changed, as he watched the dancers (as you watched him). Even at this distance, you could see the way he stiffened with anger. His walk changed, he looked taller. Was he annoyed that whoever he needed was ignoring him? Or was it something in the dance, or the costumes?

There was no way you could ask, of course.

Eventually, (finally) the dance ended. The secretary finally reached the Minister for Health, who waved him off. You wondered what it could be that the bureaucrats thought important enough to interrupt court, but that the minister dismissed. You hoped there was not some outbreak of plague somewhere, which did seem the most likely reason for the Minister for Health to be called upon urgently.

The secretary disappeared out the doors. Presumably back to tell whoever had sent him that the Minister did not think the situation so urgent.

It was the most interesting thing that happened all evening.

---

There was nothing unusual in the reports the next morning, nor in the meetings with the other ministers. It was two days later that you finally saw the Minister for Health.

They presented you with a long introduction to developments in the treatment of tropical diseases. It was word for word what had been sent in a report the previous month. You said nothing, maintaining an attitude of polite interest. At the very end, you were able to ask if there were any current outbreaks.

There was, as always. Crowded cities, little sanitation, and an unwillingness to invest in the mosquito repelling charms to protect whole communities. None of the members of _your_ court would be at risk, of course, with bespelled houses and carriages and boats.

You asked, cautiously, carefully, if any of the recent work might be applicable to the outbreak of yellow fever. Reluctantly, they admitted it was not. The Minister offered to send a report on what could be done, and you accepted graciously, and moved on with your day.

You wondered where, within the bowels of the Health Department, the angry secretary worked.

---

The report arrived the next day. They must have had it prepared, but just not to hand when you had asked. As you expected, the primary recommendation was to stop the spread with anti-mosquito charms. Knowing already that your princes and lords would refuse, you sighed internally, and turned over the page.

There was a small note that Morrowlea was trialing a kind of mosquito trap that seemed to be more effective. A footnote included the full reference. You tapped your finger on the page, wondering if it would ever make it out of the university.

The very next sentence included a suggestion for a manufacturer. You happened to recall that they made the sort of cheap toys that were often found at festivals, and so would have a wide distribution network, even if they were an unusual choice.
