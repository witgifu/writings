Cliopher hadn't expected the Moon Lady to turn up to the eclipse party, he hadn't expected her to practically assault His Radiancy, and he certainly didn't expect her to curse him when he refused her.

Well, the curse was probably expected. That kind of thing _did_ happen if you got caught up with the gods. The lays and stories of his people told him that very thoroughly.

She spoke unsmilingly. "The poems are locked in your heart. The dream is of what is left when hope is gone. And to remember, Best-beloved—"

She touched one, beautiful, pure-white finger at His Radiancy's neck, sliding it down as far as she could against the fabric of his robes.

She laughed, like the chiming of bells, and shone radiant for one glorious moment. Then the last sliver of the earth's shadow passed from the moon, and she vanished.

His Radiancy hiccoughed so hard, he almost fell off the chair.

They walked briskly back, pausing regularly as His Radiancy twitched violently. The guards held everyone at a slightly further distance, in case their lord stumbled.

Cliopher was confused. He couldn't think of a similar precedent, and as a reasonably fine legal mind *and* a student of the lore of the Ring, this frustrated him. A curse like that, from a god, should come with a clear task to remove it. But the moon had only made some confusingly vague comments about poetry and dreams.

Conju was trying to be practical. Cliopher wasn't sure if any of the usual cures would work, but he understood why Conju was going to try every one he could find just in case.

"What about laughing?" asked Cliopher, after Conju went through every cure he knew of, "My friend Bertie used to take the chance to tell all the dirtiest jokes he knew."

"I am not," said Conju very quietly, "Going to spend half a bell making nob jokes for His Radiancy."

It ended up taking two bells, of course.
