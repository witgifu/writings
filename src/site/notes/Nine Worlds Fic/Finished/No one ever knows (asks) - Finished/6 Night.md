Cliopher came back to the world, lying once again on the divan. He could see Domina Audrey looking at him through a monocle, and Conju on the other side.

For a moment he thought he was back in his sickbed with bonebreak fever. “Don’t you dare get up,” said Conju, looming over him. That was exactly the kind of thing Conju had said then.

“He will be fine with some rest and food, your Radiancy,” said the physician, packing away her probes, “There should be no more lasting effects than from exercising too much, or a few sleepless nights.”

Cliopher tried to sit up, but Conju pushed a thin finger into his chest.

“And if he is unlikely to follow that advice?” asked Conju, giving Cliopher a dirty look.

“I could prescribe a sleeping tonic, if he has trouble falling or staying asleep? Or a painkiller, if that is what will keep him awake?” Domina Audrey answered, but addressed her response to his Radiancy.

“Please send to sayu Kalikiri, and advise her sayo Mdang is ill and We have ordered him to bed rest for three days,” said his Radiancy. Ludvic saluted before opening the door to pass on the message.

Cliopher huddled down into the cushions. Yes, he was prone to forgetting to eat lunch. And he did often stay up late reading reports or writing memos. But he enjoyed it! It wasn’t as if he asked anyone else to do the same.

“Thank you, Domina,” said Conju, “A painkiller would probably be a good idea.” He looked at Cliopher for confirmation. Ruefully, Cliopher nodded. It would be nice if everything didn’t hurt so much.

Domina Audrey made a few notes, and left, with a promise to send up the medicine immediately. Her soft footsteps receded down the hall.

“Now Cliopher, you are not in any state to be looking after yourself,” said Conju, “And I am not asking anyone to hike up to the end of the Alinorel wing—yes, I know you like the walk Cliopher—we will find you somewhere closer to stay.”

Both Conju and his Radiancy were silent. Cliopher realised they were both thinking through the optics. If anyone heard that Cliopher had spent tonight in the Tower, and if the Ouranatha told anyone the Cliopher had been purified, the rumour mill would spin at fever pitch. 

But if they sent him out to the infirmary, the conclusion could be similarly awkward, that somehow his Radiancy had injured Cliopher. No, Cliopher would not let them do that; he did not mind if he seemed to be in disgrace, but he did not want anyone to look at his Radiancy and remember Eritanyr.

There were plenty of empty suites near the Tower, but if Cliopher was in one, and then returned to his rooms in the Alioneral wing, that would definitely be seen as a disgrace, possibly that the Emperor had misjudged Cliopher. If he stayed, that would seem to be confirmation of a change in status for their relationship.

Eventually, they all seemed to come to the same conclusion. “Cliopher can stay with me, your Radiancy,” said Conju, with a bow, “It won’t fuel any rumours that weren’t there already, and it will dissuade any new ones about any disgrace.”

”I don’t want to be a bother…” said Cliopher quietly.

His Radiancy and Conju both studiously ignored this statement. “Thank you, for your care of Our Hands, Cavalier an Vilius,” said his Radiancy. 

The palace bells rang the second hour of the evening. Court would start in half a bell. His Radiancy and Conju both made for the doors.

“I will send someone to see that you do not move, Cliopher,” said Conju, in the acerbic tone that Cliopher knew ment Conju was worried.

His Radiancy said nothing. The guards left with the Emperor.

Cliopher lay where he was, alone. His lord had said nothing to him since he woke up. Was he angry with Cliopher? Had he imagined his lord asking him to stay? Had his Radiancy changed his mind while Cliopher was unconscious?

Cliopher tried to imagine how he would feel if the Ouranatha sent one of his staff to him, falling over with exhaustion. And then to find that he was expected to take them to bed. He felt sick at the idea.

Of course, he tried to instil independence and a willingness to debate in his staff. But even for them, it could be hard to refuse. And for the Lord of Rising Stars, every whim was an order, any disobedience was treason. His Radiancy could not expect anyone to say ‘no’, and so he could not trust anyone who said ‘yes’.

Not that Cliopher… his mind shied away automatically from the question of if he wanted to say ‘yes’. It didn’t matter. His Radiancy had made it clear that he wouldn’t ask.

Cliopher understood why his Radiancy offered an escape. If his Radiancy insisted, if he couldn’t face looking at Cliopher and wondering if Cliopher was afraid, Cliopher would leave.

Suddenly he was sure that his lord would send him away. The Ouranatha would not keep what they had done a secret. How could Cliopher continue as the head of the Service, as Hands of the Emperor, with everyone wondering if he was sleeping with the Emperor? What would he tell Kiri, or his family?

Even if he wanted to stay, and his lord wanted him to stay, Zunidh had to come first. Zunidh needed a stable government. It was too new, too fragile, had already been rocked by too many scandals, had seen so much change. The princes saw their authority as coming from the Sun-on-Earth; continued peace depended on that stability.

Oh, but it hurt.

He cried. Alone, his body aching, as his dreams crumbled in his heart. Big racking, ugly sobs. There was nobody here to see him. Nobody to judge him for not hiding his feelings. He turned to hide his face in the cushions anyway, in case anyone came in.

After a quarter bell, or maybe a little more, Cliopher's tears ran dry. He curled up on himself, hugging one of the cushions. He needed a rock to grab onto, as this rip pulled him deeper and deeper. There was nothing, just endless dark inside his mind. Pressure, darkness, cold.

The door opened, and shut, but Cliopher did not look up. He had been told to stay where he was. Someone sent in to tidy would have to work around him.

“Conju is making arrangements,” said the warm voice of Ludvic, “He will be back to collect you shortly.”

Cliopher rolled over to face Ludvic. He was sure his eyes were red; they felt puffy, sore, and a bit itchy. “Weren’t you with his Radiancy?” he asked, confused, his throat sore from crying.

“Yes,” said Ludvic, calmly, “he shuffled the guard shift for this evening.”

“Oh,” said Cliopher, his mind working slowly.

Ludvic didn’t offer any further explanation. He simply stood, watching. Not at attention. Not with his spear, Cliopher noticed belatedly. Not on duty. 

Conju could have sent anyone in the household to watch Cliopher. But his Radiancy had chosen to send Ludvic. A friendly, familiar face. Who was not an aristo, but was an outsider like Cliopher.

“Did he say anything?” asked Cliopher, his voice cracking a little.

Ludvic didn’t answer immediately. “Conju said to make sure you didn’t do anything stupid.”

Cliopher swallowed. From Conju, that could include anything from standing up to self-harm. Although, with his current condition, there was every chance standing up would be self-harm.

"And he said to take one of these," added Ludvic, and passed over a glass bottle. The paper label said something in a language from Ysthar that Cliopher couldn't read. It contained round, white pills. "Domina Audrey said it shouldn't give you any nausea, like something stronger might."

Cliopher nodded, and looked around again for the water. It was just out of reach, on a spindly side table, with the ewer. Ludvic took the option to move the little table closer, rather than just hand Cliopher the glass.

"Thank you," said Cliopher, and burst into tears again.

Ludvic took back the bottle of pills, and shook one out. He handed it to Cliopher, with the glass of water.

Cliopher dutifully took the pill, tears continuing to track down his face. He finished off the glass of water, which felt wonderful against his sore throat. 

His mother would have said something about him always crying when he got too tired as a toddler. His sister would have said something about how emotional pain fed off physical pain. Ludvic said nothing, but in a way that gave space for Cliopher to just be, rather than feeling the need to school his feelings to make his friend comfortable.

Ludvic sat in a chair opposite Cliopher's divan. He didn't look at Cliopher, but sort of generally looked at the room, a classic guard pose. Cliopher eventually cried himself out again.

Cliopher hugged one of the cushions tightly, as if he could shield himself with it. He rubbed his sore face on it, trying to ground himself in the different textures of the tapestry cover.

"He won't send you away," said Ludvic, quietly.

Cliopher almost jumped. Thankfully he had put the glass back on the table, so there was nothing to drop or knock. Cliopher knew exactly which 'he' Ludvic was talking about.

"He should," whispered Cliopher, "it's—"

Unusually for Ludvic, he interrupted Cliopher. "The government needs you, and he needs you."

Cliopher kneaded the cushion, twisting it around. "There's plenty of secretary candidates in the Bureaucratic Service," said Cliopher, not looking at Ludvic, "I'm sure they would do admirably—"

Ludvic interrupted again, "No, he needs you. He asked you to stay."

There was no non-treason rebuttal to this statement. Of course, his Radiancy *could* change his mind. But that was not to be said out loud, and certainly not acted upon.

Cliopher spent some more time taking out his anxiety on the cushion. It would probably never be the proper shape again. Cliopher wondered if Conju considered 'abuse of a cushion' to fall under 'doing something stupid'.

Before Cliopher actually tore the cushion cover, there was a knock at the door. Ludvic got up and went to answer. There was a hushed conversation through a crack in the door that Cliopher couldn't quite make out. Ludvic came back over to Cliopher.

"Conju's staff are ready," said Ludvic, "We'll take you out the back way."

Cliopher swung his legs around and put his feet on the floor. The world didn't spin, so he pushed himself up to standing. He wobbled quite a bit. Cliopher looked at Ludvic with a grimace, "I'm not sure I can walk by myself."

Ludvic looked Cliopher over, appraising him. "Do you think you could walk holding on to me? Or I can carry you."

Cliopher sighed, "I'd rather not be seen being carried out of the Imperial Appartments. That would probably give the wrong impression."

Ludvic nodded. He moved to stand next to Cliopher, and put his arm around Cliopher's shoulders. Cliopher took a deep breath, and grabbed Ludvic around the waist. Together, slowly, they left the room.
