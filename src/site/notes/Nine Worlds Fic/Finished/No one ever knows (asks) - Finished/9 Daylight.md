You awoke, and I remembered. I wanted to hide behind these sheer curtains. It was true that I wanted that every day, but today it was stronger, almost overwhelming.

Last night I had overwhelmed you. You had melted, shattered, fallen, as you had not done in decades. Centuries, maybe. An audience of four had seen me. Had seen *me*. Not through you, like Cliopher and Conju glimpsed. Naked, frozen, terrified. 

Now, in the light of dawn, you had to face the cause. The sun would not stop for the Sun-on-Earth. So you got up, let your attendants wrap you in your robe.

In theory, behind those anonymous masks could be any wizard. Today it was not. The high priest of the Sun had come. 

Neither you not he acted like today was different from any other day. Salt, fire, chanting. The same as yesterday, the same as tomorrow.

Did he know what had happened yesterday? Had he come to see how many had been in the Imperial bed? Was he complicit in the assault?

Or was it coincidence that he was here today? 

You waved your hands in the concluding gesture. You turned. He said nothing, made no move to follow. You left for your dressing room.

“Any news in the night, an Vilius?” you asked your ever so capable Groom.

“Sayo Mdang is still sleeping, your Radiancy. I have heard that he was seen yesterday afternoon looking ill and out of uniform. It was assumed he had been seeking medical attention, possibly for stomach flu.”

Conju’s hands and wands were as deft as always. He gave away nothing of the tension I was sure he was feeling. But then neither did you.

"Very well. This afternoon We will be reviewing the state of magic in Western Dair, please rouse Us to attend Evening Court."

Conju bowed, and you moved through your day.

It was disappointing to have sayu Kalikiri appear instead of Cliopher. She was competent, and if she had been sent to you before Cliopher you probably would have given her the job. But she was clearly overawed, and lacked any of Cliopher's willingness to challenge you.

If Cliopher decided not to come back, it was going to be hard for you (for me). We would not think about that now, there was easily a morning of correspondence to go through. Focus on the immediate problem, one that you know how to fix.

When the noon bell ran, sayu Kalikiri was clearly relieved to be dismissed. You ate lunch, and settled onto the terrace. It would be nice, someday, to use my Private Office for a trance. But since you needed Conju to wake you to make sure you made it to Court, it would need to be where he could reach you.

We disappeared into the magic. There was something in the hills, in the mountains. A little slub in the fabric, or a tangle in the thread. Carefully, gently, we teased it out. We wandered down the slopes and valleys, finding other little twists and nudging them into more harmonious paths. A kind of music, if not the kind I loved best.

Conju pulled us out, as requested. Once you were all the way back, having been plied with tea and sweet biscuits, I noticed Conju seemed a little distracted.

I let it go. He would tell you, if it was important. It could just be he was worried about Cliopher (like I was). Or that he felt you hadn't had enough biscuits. 

You had a your bath.

Conju did everything perfectly, as always. Dressed you for full court with a sparkling ahalo cloth outer robe, shot with gold, held fast with two bee brooches, joined by an impossibly tiny chain. Gold lip paint, black eye liner, white stars painted on your face and studded with tiny diamonds.

It was when he finished, that he finally decided. You could see it in his eyes, as he tidied away the cosmetics. He picked up the mahogany box of diamond chips, and in its place on the table placed a folded piece of paper. With just the gesture he would use when passing something to you (ever careful, always mindful of the taboos).

He didn't say anything, but turned to return more items to their proper homes.

I picked up the paper. It was a note. I recognised the hand immediately. How many hours had I watched those hands, spinning your words, our words, into beautiful writing. I had a brief flash of panic (you did not even twitch) that Cliopher was writing to accept the position of—

But no, the words filtered through to me. He was asking after me, and apologising. Oh, I wanted to run down to him and beg his forgiveness again. But you could not, we could not. You did not even have time to write a reply. I folded the note as small as I could.

Your outer robe had a pocket. Not a proper pocket, more of a patch to cover the back of the brooch-clasp to make sure it didn't snag on the silk under it. Conju saw you slip the note into it. His face did not betray the disapproval you were sure he felt.

---

You sat on your throne, and watched the eddies of the court. Groups forming, and parting. I wanted to pull the note out of my pocket and re-read it. But I could not, so you did not.

I had often wished I could learn the the trick some soldiers once told me of, sleeping with eyes open. Surely you could have gotten away with it, if we had ever learnt how. I had also dreamed of replacing you (us) with a statue, or a magical automaton. Except for walking to and from the throne, nobody would ever know.

And I could have done something useful instead.

I drew shapes in my mind over the patterns your courtiers made. A cat, sitting proud. A bell. A bow and arrow. It was like seeing faces in clouds, or constellations. Both things you could look at now, but hadn't always been able to. It was a way to pass the time.

A better way than fretting (I was fretting).

When the hour came that you had appointed, we departed.

The palanquin arrived back at the imperial appartments, and you swept through the doors. You made a direct line for your desk. Beautiful soft sandalwood, hardly ever used. You sat at the chair, and opened the drawer for paper, pen, and ink.

Nobody told you not to. It was late, you should be going to bed. Conju opened the ivory doors to see why you had not come in further. He saw you at the desk, saw you pull out the note from the pocket. He said nothing, and you ignored him.

I re-read the note. It was awkwardly not-quite-formal, exactly like Cliopher. 

He claimed to be feeling better. Nobody ever said the truth about such things to you, but then Cliopher seemed to have a reputation for not saying the truth about his health to anyone. You were hopeful anyway. Conju said he had slept long, which was something.

He begged your forgiveness — as if it was *his* fault. I hated how the crown did this. That the victim should feel the need to plead forgivness to the criminal (oh, how I had failed him). If any of your lords had trapped their secretary into concubinage, you would have at least sent them from court in disgrace with a large fine.

The Emperor could have no faults, and so the fault wasn't yours (it was mine, oh and it burned me, and I deserved it). And so it must be Cliopher's fault. His fault for what? For not knowing enough about Schooled Magic and the purification rituals? He had no reason to know those things. His fault for not complaining? I wished he had (oh, how I wished), but you could not (I could not) *blame* him for that.

The Ouranatha. Now, there was someone you could blame. If they had been selling their supplies, or cooking the books, that would be a much easier charge for you to lay. And maybe a chance to wrest back a little more control.

We scraped three attempts to reply where you fell into the words and turns of phrase you habitually used. That Cliopher habitually scribed. A spark of magic turned each to dust.

Finally, I got up and slipped into my private office. You did not come here, so your unhelpful words would be left outside too. 

I sat at the desk, covered in books and papers and length of scarlet linen. I found a bit of paper, and pen, and ink, again. I wrote out my anger and my grief. My fear that I had hurt the first person to *see* me in decades (centuries?).

He had asked me to stay. Not you, me.

And he had touched my hand. I could feel it tingle at the memory. So soft, so careful. If he stayed—

But no, I could not plan for that. It was his choice, and I would, you *would*, hold the space for him to make that choice. Every day.

I finished the note. I dithered over how to sign it, and ended up just writing “A”. I folded it.

Looking around the study, I tried to find a safe place in the chaos to put Cliopher’s note. I found a decorative lacquered box that held only an old cork. I tossed the cork on the floor, and replaced it.

Back in the official study, Conju was patiently waiting for you. At the desk, you put the note into the space where Cliopher would usually place documents for your seal. You turned to Conju with the court gesture for a favour request.

Conju bowed his acceptance. There was no hint of the reproach I knew he was feeling. You were up late (far too late, the bells had stopped), and had been writing a personal note to your secretary.

He took the note from the desk, and you submitted to his ministrations. Careful and caring, he put you to bed.

---

The next morning the high priest did not join you. I wondered again what they knew and what they suspected. Had the acolyte who held the box of salt now, held salt when Cliopher had returned time and time again? Did they know what was happening?

You stood still while you were washed and lotioned and dressed. I wondered how we might bring justice to the Ouranatha. We needed more information. You would need to speak to your guard captain about it. Discretely.

At breakfast you turned through the pages of report summaries. Between a crop productivity report for Solaara (good news) and a guard recruitment update, was a folded slip of paper. Something misplaced? I wondered, as you nibbled on the flatbread.

You opened it.

A second note from Cliopher. You put down the bread, and smoothed the note flat with your clean hand.

He wanted to stay. He wanted to stay!

Your hands didn’t shake as you took a sip of tea. Your breath didn’t falter, as you (I) reread the note again and again.

You put the note to one side. Attentive, always, to your duty, you finished the reports.

Before sayu Kalikiri arrived, I took the note into my private study. I added it to the box with the previous one.

I wanted to reply, but all I could think of to say was “thank you”. And I did not now have another half hour to write. So I consoled myself by rereading both notes again.

Then you returned to the official study, to greet sayu Kalikiri. 

Two more days, today and tomorrow, and then Cliopher would be back. You could work out how to reign in the Ouranatha together. Cliopher could hand you the documents to sign directly. 

Maybe your (our) hands might touch, briefly. I allowed myself to hope.
