Cliopher woke, and looked around the room. He still could only see blurred colours. He held out his hand, moving it closer and closer until he could see it clearly. It became visible at the same distance as yesterday, and every day he had been staying with Conju. In frustration, he dropped his hand to the blankets with a slap.

It had been weeks, surely. Time had run more consistently since his Radiancy had woken, but it was still hard to track months and years. But Cliopher was reasonably sure it had been multiple weeks. With no improvement.

Would he be stuck like this forever? He still didn't want to plan for that future, but it seemed increasingly naive not to. At some point Conju, who had been so incredibly generous, would want his spare bedroom back. Cliopher would need to find a way to navigate the outer wings of the Palace safely.

The stress and disappointment spiralled inside him. He had a reached for his greatest dream, working directly for the Lord of Rising Stars. But at such cost. Would he ever be worthy? Did his lord keep him around out of guilt? It was Cliopher's mistake, his crime, his treason.

Not for the first time, Cliopher wept silently in his bed. Guilt, stress, anger, sadness. The headache that never went away. It spilled out into sobs that he smothered with the blankets. He didn't want Conju or Zala to hear and coming to check on him. They had both been so kind.

It wasn't as if he could go home like this. How would he pay whatever wild fee a merchant charged to go through the wall of storms? He couldn't pay with helping them navigate. Not when he couldn't see the moon, much less the stars.

If by some miracle or charity he did make it home, there was even less for him to do there. Would he sit in his mother's house, in his old bedroom, feeling sorry for himself? Would he ask one of his old friends to take him to Loaloa and join the village? 

No, he was more use here. He could still be a secretary. Dictation wasn't a problem, and he could still write his elegant chancery hand. He had even managed various meetings where he tried to wrangle extra information from the departments.

Perhaps he could propose an audit team. His Radiancy seemed amenable to his work in that direction. Having more analysts would make the work go faster.

Cliopher rolled out of bed, and scuffed his feet along the floor, hunting for his slippers. Somehow they were never where he thought they should be. He would have claimed the cat had been playing with them, but Conju didn't have a cat.

Washing his face and cleaning his teeth could be done with eyes closed. He did wish he could see his reflection to know if he needed a haircut. The hair by his ears was starting to curl and tickle them, so he suspected it was due. He ran his fingers through it, and yes, it was definitely past regulation length. And he didn't need anything else someone could use to reprimand him for.

With his mind on his appointments for the day, Cliopher paid less attention than usual making his way to the breakfast table. He bumped his elbow on the way into the room, before more carefully and slowly finding his usual chair. 

It wasn't until Conju said “Good morning, Cliopher,” that he realised anyone else was in the room.

“Oh!” said Cliopher, startled, “Good morning Conju. I'm sorry I didn't notice you, I'm afraid I was distracted.”

Conju sipped his tea, “Well, I think this is the first time we've had breakfast together? His Radiancy had a midnight ritual, and insisted I take the morning off.”

Cliopher was unable to tell from Conju's tone of voice if this was good or bad. He suspected that Conju disapproved of resting while their lord was working. Even if the other Tower staff were perfectly capable. 

“Do you have any appointments before the third bell, Cliopher?” asked Conju.

“Not today, no. I was going to review the Health budget proposal,” said Cliopher, his mind drifting to the niggling feeling that something has been skimmed off somewhere.

“Really, Cliopher, that's hardly relaxing,” said Conju with fond admonition, “Why don't we go for a walk in the gardens. There's a new section that will be an arboretum that they have just opened.”

“Oh, no, thank you Conju. I really do need to read that proposal again. Maybe another time,” said Cliopher, feeling awkward.

“You can't hide away forever, Cliopher. I'm sure some sunshine would do you good, you're looking very pale,” said Conju, clearly not willing to let the idea rest.

Cliopher fiddled with his spoon. Conju wasn't wrong, he had been keeping to himself. But, it had been Conju's idea. Had something changed?

“I don't want to appear before his Radiancy injured again,” he said quietly.

“Of course not,” said Conju, “But it would be daylight, with me. You've been getting a lot better about not bumping into things.”

Cliopher couldn't suppress a wince. “Only because I've gone to places I know well.”

In a much more sympathetic tone, Conju said, “Your eyes haven't been improving? What did the Domina say?”

“No,” said Cliopher, trying to keep the bitterness from his voice, “they've been the same for a few weeks. Nobody's seen an injury like this before, so it's just wait and hope.” 

Then Cliopher guessed what Conju might be hinting at, “I should probably move back to my own rooms. I'm sorry for imposing on you for so long Conju. I'll try to organise—”

Conju interrupted, “Not at all, Cliopher. It's lovely having you, and I won't hear of you moving back until Domina Cirle recommends it. She didn't give you any exercises or tinctures?”

“Thank you, Conju,” said Cliopher quietly. He still wasn't sure why Conju had been so generous, and he felt awkward that it was unlikely he would ever be able to return such a favour. “I haven't seen the Domina in a while, but she didn't have any suggestions then.”

“When you say 'a while', Cliopher, do you mean to say that you haven't seen the Domina since you came to stay here?” asked Conju. 

Cliopher didn't like how perceptive that question was. “Um, well, no.” He took a slow sip of coffee to hide his emotions.

“Cliopher! Really?” exclaimed Conju, “Well then, I am spending my free morning on taking you to see her.”

Cliopher sighed, “She will have other appointments now. She's a busy healer, and those people probably need her help more urgently than me.”

“Then I shall take you to _make_ an appointment,” said Conju.

Cliopher took a deep breath, “I'll be fine, Conju, I'll wait. It's just going slower than I'd like, that's all.”

“If you are not healing at the expected rate, Cliopher, you need to go back to the doctor. I don't understand why you're so resistant, she seems nice enough,” said Conju, trying to insert a little levity.

Usually, Cliopher thought he was reasonably patient. He had learnt not to talk back to all the comments about how he was a barbarian, a primitive. But his own fears that he wouldn't get better had worn down his defences.

“It's too expensive,” he said, through gritted teeth, “They'll just give me a script for painkillers that I don't want and send me away, and I…” He couldn't finish the thought. The memory swam up before him. His mother leaving Navalia's hospital bed, to beg the healers to let her stay another night. He and Vinyë, huddled together in the kitchen of an evening, drinking coffee before they went off to night jobs. Not able to visit Navalia, as they worked as much as they could, trying to make the payments.

Tears sprung unbidden from his eyes. Embarrassed, scared, unable to explain, Cliopher stood up. “Excuse me, Conju,” he said quietly, and left the table.

There was still a bell before he was due at work. He would wash his face, and read that budget proposal. Then he would feel more himself. He was good at putting himself back together after a storm and sailing on.

Conju, unlike a typhoon, could follow him.

“Cliopher,” said Conju softly, from the doorway to the bathroom, “You do know you don't have to pay anything, don't you? The Tower covers all the medical expenses for the staff. And I know his Radiancy specifically ordered you to receive all the care you need.”

Looking into the sink, holding a wash cloth, Cliopher tried to hear what Conju was saying. It didn't fit. “That was just the initial injury, the reaction, not—”

Conju interrupted, “Cliopher, I manage all the Tower staff _except_ you. I know what the benefits are, I explain them to every new hire. I don't want anyone coming in with even a sniffle. Now please, why will you not see Domina Cirle? Or another healer, any healer.”

It was hard, resisting the urge to turn and hit Conju. The knowledge that he would probably miss, and punch the unforgiving tiled wall instead, did help.

In a very small whisper, Cliopher said, “What if she says I'm not going to get any better?”

“Then you learn how to use a cane, or you get a guide dog, or some kind of magical spectacles,” said Conju, his voice gentle, “But you won't know unless you talk to her.”

“All right,” said Cliopher, gripping the sink so hard his hands were slipping, “I'll go.” It was clear Conju wasn't going to give up. Cliopher's stomach rolled.

“Good,” said Conju, more warmly, “I'm sure you'd look very fetching in glasses.”

Cliopher laughed weakly, but then his headache spiked and he groaned instead. He was unable to hold back the grimace in time.

Conju placed a hand on Cliopher's shoulder. “Let's get you a headache powder first, and then go to the Infirmary.”

Bitting his lip, Cliopher nodded slowly. Conju pulled him along to the hallway. At some signal Cliopher didn't notice, Zala appeared.

“A headache powder and a glass of water for Cliopher, please Zala,” said Conju, and steered Cliopher into the drawing room. 

Conju mixed the power in for Cliopher, and Cliopher didn't even bother complaining that he could have done it himself. He couldn't work out what had worried Conju so much, but it was nice to sit down again. 

As soon as Cliopher finished the glass, Zala whipped it away and replaced it with another with plain water. Conju coughed quietly when Cliopher didn't immediately pick it up. So, feeling a little like he was being managed, Cliopher sighed deeply, and drank the extra water.

On the walk to the Infirmary, Conju plied Cliopher with gossip. It was pleasant, both because the headache powder was starting to work, and because Conju was happy to keep the conversation going on his own.

Once they arrived, and talked to the receptionist. It turned out Domina Cirle was able to see him that afternoon, when he had a gap between meetings.

“Cliopher sayo Mdang is the Private Secretary,” said Conju, smiling at the receptionist.

He nodded, and made a note on Cliopher's file. “Thank you, Lord Conju,” he said, looking back up, “There will be no bill, Sayo Mdang.”

Conju was standing so close that Cliopher could just see his artfully arched eyebrow. “I did believe you,” said Cliopher dryly.

Infuriatingly, Conju continued to be correct. When Domina Cirle saw Cliopher that afternoon, the healer provided him with two exercises, a tonic, and instructions to make sure he spent at least twenty minutes out in the sunlight every day. She also reassured him that he would improve. Cliopher worked very hard to make sure he did _not_ cry on his way back to Conju's rooms.
