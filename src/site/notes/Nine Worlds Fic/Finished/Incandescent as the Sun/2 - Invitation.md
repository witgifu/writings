Cleaning his brushes was a task Conju trusted to no-one else. Not only because he liked them to be dried just so to maintain their points, but also because doing so was a ritual in itself. It felt right that it should be done by the Groom of the Chamber. 

It was, thankfully, a ritual tolerant of interruptions.

“Excuse me, Lord Conju, would you be able to spare a moment?” asked Ser Ergaz.

“Only if it truly *is* a moment, Ser Ergaz,” replied Conju, quietly wishing that some day there would be no minor hiccups he needed to smooth over.

“It's about the new secretary, Sayo Mdang,” began Ser Ergaz.

Conju interrupted him. “You are aware he is not a member of my staff, yes? If you have a problem with him, your commander needs to raise it with his Radiancy.”

“Yes, Lord Conju, and the commander asked me to speak with you,” said Ser Ergaz, professional and calm.

“Very well, how can I help the commander?” said Conju, as he examined the tip of one of his liner brushes.

Ser Ergaz shifted slightly, the only giveaway so far of how he was feeling. “Sayo Mdang has been injuring himself, repeatedly.”

Conju looked up, one eyebrow raised. “So?” he said, tartly, “That sounds like a problem for the healers.”

“They aren't willing to keep him in the Infirmary. The only thing wrong with him is his sight, which they say is improving.”

“His sight, which he damaged when he _broke one of the taboos_,” interrupted Conju, with a hiss.

“Quite, Lord Conju,” replied Ser Ergaz, undeterred, “But his Radiancy has forgiven Sayo Mdang. The inner guards have reported that his Radiancy is clearly… distressed… by Sayo Mdang's obvious injuries.”

Conju carefully hung the brush point-down from the drying rack. 

He should have noticed that his Radiancy was distressed. It was his job to notice things like this, tiny changes in his lord's mood. Had Conju let his disdain for the impudent secretary get in the way of his professional judgement?

“Please thank your commander for bringing this to my attention, Ser Ergaz,” said Conju, his voice calm.

Ser Ergaz saluted, accepting the dismissal. “Thank you for your time, Lord Conju.”

Alone again, Conju considered the problem. His Radiancy clearly liked the man, which eliminated all the easiest solutions. Previous secretaries had simply been tolerated, until the Emperor became too frustrated and dismissed them. They had not been _liked_.

First, Conju would need to find out *why* the secretary was injuring himself. And then what he could possibly do to stop him.

---

The staff of the Tower readily solved the first of Conju's tasks. The rumour mill was full of stories of things Sayo Mdang had apologised to, after colliding with them. Additionally, some of the staff had seen Mdang injure himself first-hand. 

It seemed one of the primary culprits was a sculpture in the Alinorial Wing. Sayo Mdang had walked into the upraised hand so often, he had chipped the sculpted stone fingernails. This artwork had a tendency to bruise his face, which was very obvious. But Sayo Mdang also regularly banged his arms and hands on doors and doorways.

Sayo Mdang's eyesight was damaged. If he refused to stay in the Infirmary until it healed further, or learn how to use some kind of assistive aid, he would continue blundering into things. There was nothing Conju could easily identify that he could do.

In desperation, Conju wrote a polite note to the Infirmary. He asked after Sayo Mdang's health, and inquired if there were any accommodations the Tower could make to aid in his recovery. 

In reply, Conju got a visit from Domina Cirle.

“Please understand, Lord Conju,” said Domina Cirle, looking rather exasperated, “That I would not normally discuss a patient's case without the patient's permission, not even with a family member. But at the moment my only alternative seems to be to confine Sayo Mdang to the Infirmary, as a danger to himself.”

Conju pursed his lips. While he could quite understand the sentiment, he did not think his Radiancy's mood would be improved if he heard his new favourite member of staff had been locked up as mentally ill. Or even if Sayo Mdang was unavailable for an indefinite period of time. They needed another solution, where he was still able to work.

“And it would be unreasonable to pad every doorway and sculpture in the Palace for Sayo Mdang's protection,” added Conju, only a little sarcastically.

“I did actually ask the maintenance staff, who have been repairing chipped paint and so on. They said he would really only have to be all the Alinorial Wing. But still.”

Conju clutched at that piece of information. “Only the Alinorial Wing?” he asked, curious.

“One of the many magics that used to be in place in the central, and especially public, sections of the Palace. It subtly discourages people bumping in to each other and the walls. Much of the magic was shredded by the Fall of course, but that one is still working,” she said, rubbing her eyes. “I'm reasonably sure that's the only reason he hasn't broken a leg—or worse!—by falling down the central stairs.”

Conju smiled politely. “Then perhaps all we need to do is move him into rooms nearer the centre?”

Domina Cirle shook her head, “He's also injuring himself in his rooms. The spells don't extend into any of the private spaces, so he would still be in danger as long as he was living by himself. If he had family in Solaara, or even within visiting distance, I'd tell him to write to them and ask them to come and help him. But I understand they all live very far.”

“And he cannot possibly stay in the Tower,” said Conju, positively appalled that he'd even thought of the idea. Staff did sometimes stay overnight; Conju himself had a daybed in his workroom for when his service would requite both a late night and an early morning. Sayo Mdang, however, was not a member of the inner staff taking a double-shift. Nor was he dealing with an emergency.

Conju *could* see a solution that neatly tied away every variable. His Radiancy had not asked, but Conju was proud that he did his work without his Radiancy _needing_ to ask. And so, if there was a solution, however personally distasteful and inconvenient, Conju would take it.

“Sayo Mdang can stay with me,” said Conju, “My rooms are in the Collian Wing, quite near the Palace centre, and my staff can monitor him.”

Domina Cirle looked like a weight had been lifted off her. “I would have asked you directly, had I known you were such good friends!”

“We haven't even been introduced,” said Conju, with a slight smile.

“Oh,” said Domina Cirle, startled, “Um, I suppose I could introduce you?”

---

Conju felt that engaging the Domina for an introduction, when he suspected that Sayo Mdang resented her, would make the situation harder. Instead, he took the morning refreshments in to his Radiancy himself. Tea for his Radiancy, water for Sayo Mdang, and the cook's latest attempt at making ginger biscuits to his Radiancy's exacting tastes.

“Thank you, an Vilius,” said his Radiancy as Conju poured the tea. 

Then, when Conju took the glass of water to the secretarial desk, his Radiancy said “We should introduce you. An Vilius, our secretary, Cliopher sayo Mdang. Sayo Mdang, our Groom of the Chamber, The Caviller Conju an Vilius.”

It was immediately obvious what had distressed his Radiancy. The secretary's face and neck were mottled with bruises in various stages of healing. As were his hands and wrists. Conju could only assume the damage continued under the man's uniform.

Conju offered a small, polite bow. Less out of an acknowledgement of rank (for he need not bow to a commoner), but from respect for the other man's position. Cliopher bowed back, seemingly randomly. Conju carefully did not sigh with exasperation.

“Thank you, my lord,” said Conju to his Radiancy. Not because it was necessary, but entirely so that Sayo Mdang could hear his voice, and have some chance at recognising it in future. Then he collected the tea things and left. 

Move one was complete.

---

Conju interrupted Sayo Mdang's departure by the simple method of arriving in the third antechamber shortly before the other man left for the day. Knowing that the secretary would not see someone standing to the side, much less an arched eyebrow, Conju stood in front of the next set of doors.

As soon as Sayo Mdang passed the previous set, Conju said, “Excuse me, Sayo Mdang, may I have a moment of your time?” The irony of repeating Ser Ergaz's words was not lost on Conju.

“Yes?” said Sayo Mdang, still clearly trying to place who it could be approaching him.

“I need to discuss a matter of some delicacy regarding our service to his Radiancy. I was hoping we could meet later today,” said Conju, trying to hint to Sayo Mdang who he was without naming himself. He did not want to sound like Mdang was an elderly relative who had to be reminded of everyone's names.

Thankfully, Ser Ergaz was standing right behind Mdang. He whispered something Conju carefully did not hear.

“Of course, Lord Conju. Would the second bell be acceptable? I have to see someone from Health at the first,” said Sayo Mdang, now looking confident and business-like.

“Thank you, Sayo Mdang. I will meet you in the official study.”

Sayo Mdang bowed, Conju inclined his head, everything was very polite and correct. Conju removed himself from Mdang's path out of the Apartments. Not rushing, but moving quickly to be in place for his Radiancy's lunch.

The second move was complete. The third, he was sure, would be the much more difficult one. But he was determined, for his Radiancy's sake (clearly not for his own), that the matter be settled promptly. And with a minimum of fuss.

---

Sayo Mdang arrived in the official study just in time for he and Conju to stand and wait for the bells to ring.

“Good afternoon, Sayo Mdang,” said Conju, “Please, come with me to my workroom.”

Sayo Mdang bowed slightly, and indicated for Conju to lead.

Conju's preference would have been to walk in silence, through what he felt were the slightly hallowed rooms of the Imperial Apartments proper. However, he was concerned about Mdang's ability to walk behind. So he launched into small talk, to make sure Mdang could follow his voice.

“Have you ventured much into the gardens of late, Sayo Mdang? No, well I am afraid I also have not had the time I would like for such things. I have heard there is a newly blooming palm tree that has the horticulturists much interested for they are unable to place it with any certainty from the records in the library. Those with such talents have confirmed that if of magical origin it has no more magic in it now than any of the others near it. Of course…”

They arrived at Conju's workroom. Like every other room in the Imperial Apartments, it was mostly stark white. He had made sure to drape the two comfortable chairs in throws of blue brocade, to make them easy targets. Gritting his teeth, Conju patted one of the chairs audibly, and gestured for Sayo Mdang to sit.

“You understand, Sayo Mdang, that my role in his Radiancy's household is to see to it that everything be done for his Radiancy's comfort and happiness?”

Sayo Mdang nodded, “Much as mine is to smooth communication with his government.”

“Indeed,” said Conju, and paused. “It has come to my attention, that there is something that is causing his Radiancy distress, which I will need your assistance to rectify.”

Sayo Mdang held a practiced polite smile, which Conju was sure was hiding curiosity. All the gossip he could gather on the man suggested that he thrived on being irritating, and solving thorny problems. 

“Of course, anything that is causing his Radiancy distress must be addressed. But it seems unlikely that I can be of much assistance, Lord Conju?”

That was a more astute response than Conju had expected. Neither dismissing Conju's concern, nor actually offering to do anything. Well, Conju knew how to fence with words.

“Indeed, all of Zunidh prays for the happiness and long life of his Radiancy. But we, who are given the honour of serving his Radiancy directly, have also the burden of doing all we can to fulfil those prayers.”

Sayo Mdang inclined his head in acknowledgement. “Yes, Lord Conju. But what help can I provide? I am only new to his Radiancy's service.”

Internally, Conju smiled. “You know that his Radiancy has found your services invaluable? You have already spent longer in his service than the past two secretaries combined.”

Neither Conju nor Sayo Mdang mentioned that one of those secretaries was dismissed before they rose from their obeisance. Conju continued, “Indeed, on the one day you were incapacitated, his Radiancy dismissed your substitute an hour early.”

Left out by Conju, was that his Radiancy had then shut himself in the private study, and refused to come to lunch. The impasse was only resolved when Conju was able to confirm that Sayo Mdang had been released from the Infirmary.

“Thus,” said Conju, now reaching the first lemma of his argument, “His Radiancy's comfort and happiness is dependent on your good health.”

This was apparently too much for Sayo Mdang to entirely contain behind his court face. “I am flattered to hear that anyone thinks so of me, and I do not wish to impinge on your knowledge of our lord, but surely there must be some mistake. I only wish to provide his Radiancy with the best service I am able.”

“And yet,” said Conju, allowing himself to tap one finger on the arm of his chair, “You are arriving stiff, sometimes limping, and mottled with bruises. Don't mistake me, Sayo Mdang, I am neither saying your work has been less than exemplary, nor that his Radiancy would prefer that you stayed away; far from it. However, his Radiancy is distressed that you are being injured in the course of your duties, and that you have not sought any accommodations.”

Offence, confusion, and then resignation all flitted over Sayo Mdang's face. Conju never did anything so gauche as to crow in triumph, he did not even allow himself a smile, even though he was sure Sayo Mdang would not have been able to see it.

“Very well, Lord Conju, what do you propose?”

“I understand it may be some months until your vision is healed?” asked Conju, sympathetically.

“Yes,” said Sayo Mdang, stiffly, “The healers aren't able to give me a specific timeline, but thought I might be able to get glasses in six months.”

“Have you noticed that it is harder to bump into things in the centre of the Palace? There is some magic, I assume to ensure nobody gets crushed by crowds, that surprisingly persisted through the Fall. I live just past the entrance to the Collian Wing, four floors down. I would like to offer, Sayo Mdang, that you come and live with me until you are able to be fitted with glasses. Furthermore, I have plenty of space; the apartment was designed for an extended family, but, well.”

Conju offered this last statement as an admission of shared vulnerability. That whoever they might have turned to in the Palace before the Fall, was now gone. Also, that this might be a chance to at least make an alliance among those who served his Radiancy so closely.

For a moment, Conju was worried Sayo Mdang was going to refuse. He seemed to be holding his breath. But then Conju realised that Sayo Mdang was doing his best to hold back tears. “Thank you, very much, Lord Conju. Your offer is incredibly generous. I—” Sayo Mdang took a deep breath, “I would very much appreciate that.”

Conju nodded. “I will send some people with you to help you pack and so on,” he said, and seeing Sayo Mdang was about to protest, added, “Don't be ridiculous and try to refuse help. We want to have no reason to inconvenience his Radiancy, and that means we need to get you moved and settled in as quickly as possible.”

Apparently while Mdang was unwilling to accept help himself, phrased so that it was for his Radiancy, he gave in. “Thank you again, Lord Conju. I have meetings until the fifth bell of the afternoon…”

“Naturally,” said Conju, “I will have someone meet you at the entrance to the Alinoral Wing at the fifth bell. Don't worry about finding them in the end-of-day crowd, they will find you. I'm afraid I will be busy with his Radiancy until court starts, but I will see you after then.”

“That sounds excellent, thank you again, Lord Conju,” said Sayo Mdang, and moved to get up.

“Not at all,” said Conju, standing up as well, “Let us go together. Have you had time to read much of late?”

And so the two of them walked back through the Imperial Apartments, and out the front doors. Sayo Mdang hurried off to his meeting. Conju asked one of the pages to find Domina Cirle and inform her that their solution had been put into motion.
