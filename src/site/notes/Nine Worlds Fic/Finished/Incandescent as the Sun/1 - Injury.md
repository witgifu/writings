“Oh, very good, my lord,” said Cliopher, and unconsciously looked up into the eyes of the Sun-on-Earth. He had known, everyone knew, that the Lord Emperor's gaze could blind. He had broken one of the great taboos.

Cliopher fell, ungainly, into the obeisance. It was immaterial. Stumbling, trying to remember the way out of the Imperial Apartments by sound and feel, he left. His eyes felt like they were on fire, like they were stabbing into his brain. It was agony, and every step, every breath, seemed to send another jolt of pain through his head.

One hand on the wall, he headed down the stairs, in the general direction of his rooms. What he would do when he got there he didn't know. He should write to his mother, and apologise for his impending execution. Except… of course he couldn't write. He wasn't even sure he would make it back to his rooms. Either the Ouranatha would catch up to him, or he would collapse from the pain.

It was the Imperial Guard who caught up with him. He hadn't actually made it down more than one flight of stairs.

“Sayo Mdang?” came a voice from behind him, as the distinctive sound of guard footsteps became louder, more painful.

“Yes, Guard?” asked Cliopher, trying to keep a light polite tone in his voice. It probably didn't matter that he was trembling slightly and wasn't sure he'd got the correct form of address. He had already committed treason in front of them, no doubt they were here to take him away.

He hoped they would be able to hold him up, and he wouldn't have to walk far.

“Are you well, Sayo Mdang?”

Cliopher was not sure how to respond to that question. His usual answer would be to brush off the concern. But if they had come to arrest him it didn't matter how he was feeling. He would very shortly not be feeling anything much at all.

“Ah, no, not really,” he said, somewhat hesitantly.

“Can I help you down to the infirmary?” the guard asked.

“I should probably just go back to my room and lie down,” replied Cliopher, not wanting to incur the cost of a visit to the healers. It would add insult on top of injury to have the bill sent to his mother when he was no longer here to pay it.

There was a pause. Cliopher's eyes hurt too much to attempt to open them and try to look at the guard.

“His Radiancy has asked that you receive all the medical and other assistance you need,” said the guard, in a softer tone.

“I—”

Cliopher gave up. He had vaguely wanted to go on his own terms, but his head hurt too much to protest further. Not when his lord—no, not *his* lord, not now, but—the Lord Emperor had ordered help. Who was he to refuse the Lord of Rising Stars anything?

“Yes, please,” he said in a small voice.

The guard gave him an arm to lean on. He was slightly taller than Cliopher, and of course incredibly well muscled. Cliopher held on tightly, and winced with every step.

They led him a different way, not down more stairs, but into a lift. Well, of course, the sick couldn't always walk, so there would need to be some alternative. He couldn't recall ever seeing a lift before, but perhaps the doors were disguised, or he had assumed they were doors to another room or apartment. There were thousands of rooms in the Palace of Stars, there was probably nobody who knew all of them.

It felt like an age to Cliopher, before the familiar scents of the core of the Palace gave way to the astringent smell of antiseptic. 

The guard steered him gently, until the back of his legs bumped into the edge of a chair. Cliopher sank down gratefully, letting go of the guard's arm so he could bury his head in his hands, rubbing his face. The initial intense fire was dulling into a more steady blaze. 

He could feel tears on his face, but couldn't remember when he had started crying. Cliopher knew it was considered shameful to cry at court, but he had already shamed himself so thoroughly today it probably didn't matter. On the scale of 'minor social faux pas' to 'capital offence' he had already swung right to the end.

Two pairs of footsteps came towards him.

“Sayo Mdang?” asked a new voice, brusque and feminine, “I'm Domina Cirle, I understand you've had some kind of magical accident and injured your eyes?”

“Yes, Domina” said Cliopher, uncovering his face just enough not to muffle his response.

“Do you have any magic of your own?” she asked, kindly.

“No, Domina.”

There was a pause, and Cliopher imagined that the physician was probably checking some kind of diagnostic device. “Don't try to open your eyes yet, but what can you see now, Sayo Mdang?”

“Black, with two white-gold circles,” he replied, dully. Would the Lord Emperor's eyes, his beautiful golden eyes, be burned into Cliopher forever? That was surely what was stuck in his vision, like he had looked at a fire in the dark, or the sun. But the pain was so very much worse.

“How bad is the pain? Out of ten.”

Cliopher hated that kind of question, he had no idea what the scale was supposed to mean. Scales needed reference values to be useful. “More than when I broke my arm,” he said instead, “but not as much as when a cut on my leg got badly infected.”

“Let's get you into a dark room and check your eyes then, Sayo Mdang” said Cirle, “and I'll get you something for the headache as well.”

Cliopher didn't ask how she knew about the headache. Instead, he struggled to leaver himself up. The guard quickly jumped to help him again, and Cliopher leant heavily on the solid arm offered.

He did notice the change in the way their steps echoed, as they entered what he first assumed was some kind of treatment room. The guard helped him up onto a soft bed, and he lay down. Not a treatment room then, which would have had a bench or chair. A private room. In the Palace infirmary. If he did survive, and was sent the bill, he would be in debt for the rest of his life. Cliopher desperately hoped that the guards hadn't tricked him into coming.

Domina Cirle closed the door, and walked over to the bed. Then she did something Cliopher couldn't identify, and it was suddenly, blessedly, dark. Cliopher could feel the difference, feel a slight easing in the pain in his eyes and head.

“Can you open your eyes, Sayo Mdang?” asked the physician, from somewhere near his head. 

Opening his eyes felt like peeling a bandage off. Sticky, painful. But when he had finished, his eyes hurt no more than they had closed. “Yes, Domina,” he said quietly.

“Please keep looking at the ceiling. I'm going to bring out a small light, I want you to tell me if you can see any difference,” she said softly.

There was a difference. Cliopher couldn't see the ceiling, and the circles were still there, but he could see a general lightening of the room. Indeed, as if a small mage light or candle had been uncovered. “Yes,” he said, “it's lighter.” The idea that he was not totally blind was too wonderful, too fragile.

“Please turn your head towards me, and tell me what you see.”

Cliopher blinked, his eyes itchy, and did as he was bid. His headache pulsed with the movement. But, but… he could see a blob. Very blurry, wavering slightly. He squinted, but that didn't seem to help.

“I see you, Domina. Not with any detail, just a vague lighter shape. Wherever I move my eyes, the white circles stay in place.”

He started crying again. He wasn't entirely blind. Or not yet, anyway.

“And now?” asked Domina Cirle, and Cliopher could see (he could see!) that she had uncovered more of the light.

“I see more light. It's still—” he stopped abruptly and hissed as the pain got stronger. He squeezed his eyes shut against the white-hot stab.

“Let me give you something for the pain,” said the physician.

“No opiates,” hissed Cliopher, raising his hands again to rub his head.

“Do you have an allergy?”

“I don't like them.”

Domina Cirle paused, before asking “Has you or anyone in your immediate family had an addiction?”

Cliopher did not have the patience to be polite, when he was in this much pain. “Not to my knowledge. I just don't like not being in control of myself.”

“I understand,” said the physician, patiently, “I will give you some eye drops, which should help stop the reaction. And a sedative inhaler, which you can use to self-dose pain relief as you need.”

That was new, and surprising. When he had broken his arm they had offered only laudanum.

“Thank you,” he said in a small voice, aware he was being difficult.

She did something to turn the main light back on, Cliopher could feel the light increase painfully even with his eyes shut and covered by his hands. He heard her opening drawers and pulling things out. 

She pressed a clarinet mouthpiece into his hand. Or at least, that was the only reference point Cliopher had. Although it felt like it was missing the screws to attach the reed, so maybe it was for a recorder.

“Hold it in your mouth like a whistle, and breathe in through it,” said Domina Cirle, “I will need to peel back your eyelids to get the drops in and the light is going to hurt.”

This turned out to be harder to do than Cliopher expected. He had plenty of practice blowing out through his oboe and in through his nose. Now he had to do the reverse. It required concentration, which was good, because having something else to focus on also helped with the pain.

The physician pulled up each eyelid, causing fresh stabs of pain from the light. She dropped in something cold and soothing. Between the drops and the sedative, Cliopher finally felt his chest and back relax.

“Your pupils are reacting to the light already, Sayo Mdang. That's good, you should be able to cope with a dimly lit room once the initial reaction headache wears off.”

Cliopher took the whistle out of his mouth, to ask what he needed to know the answer to most. “Will I be able to see again, Domina? How long…” He couldn't finish the statement. There were too many horrifying thoughts.

Even if the Ouranatha did not come for him in the night (and they could), he had run out of options in the Service. He would have to go home in disgrace again. He had known that this morning, he had thought he had accepted it. The thought still made him feel sick.

Domina Cirle interrupted his spiralling thoughts. “That's hard to say. You will recover more vision, but it may take a while. We should be able to release you back to work once the reaction has stabilised and the pain is manageable. I'm sorry I cannot be more concrete.”

Perhaps the sedative had lowered his carefully learned inhibitions somewhat, because he surprised himself by replying. “I doubt I will be returning to work, Domina. If I am not executed, I will surely be dismissed.”

There was a moment of silence. The physician started putting things away. Cliopher guessed she had been at the Palace from before the fall. Possibly, probably, she had patched up people only to watch as they were taken from her hands to the executioners block or the torture chambers. He felt a little guilty for speaking so casually about it, they must be unpleasant memories for her.

It was the guard who spoke first. He had been so quiet, Cliopher had assumed he had left. “If you are able, Sayo Mdang, our orders are to confirm you in your position.”

Cliopher took a sudden gasp, through the inhaler. It made a whistling noise, which seemed too light-hearted for the cross-currents of emotion Cliopher was feeling. Thankfully the guard and the physician were both professional enough not to laugh.

He didn't know what to say.

“There you go, Sayo Mdang, your job will be waiting for you when you are well,” said Domina Cirle, the cheerfulness returned to her voice. “I will come back and check on you in four hours. In the meantime, you should rest.”

She turned off the lights, and left.

Cliopher was shocked again. Nobody got seen more than once a day by the physician unless they started getting a lot worse. Even out of the general ward he could only expect an assistant once every six hours. What had the guard said to them?

Realising the guard was still there with him, Cliopher said, “Thank you very much for your assistance, I'm not sure that I caught your name…?”

“Ser Ergaz, Imperial Guard,” he said, but he sounded less stuffy than Cliopher would expect from the nobility. Well, the Fall had changed many things, perhaps Ser Ergaz had been forced to deal with commoners more.

“Thank you, Ser Ergaz. Would you be able to inform the Master of Offices? And… I should let someone from the Imperial Household know I suppose? I will need at least a replacement for tomorrow, if they will not let me leave before then.”

Cliopher was entirely unsure what the correct protocol was to report sick on your first official day as Private Secretary to the Emperor. Was he supposed to find his own replacement? The staff were supplied by the Master of Offices, but reported to the Emperor himself.

“Sayo Mdang, please rest. Everything else will be handled.”

---

After three hours of reciting the Lays in his head, Cliopher was thoroughly bored. Lying in the dark, with nothing to do. While Ser Ergaz had been very helpful and polite, he was shortly replaced by another guard, who was even more silent.

Thus, the arrival of Domina Cirle was at least a distraction. She turned on a light, but kept the room still quite dim. Cliopher was pleased to find that the increase did not immediately re-trigger the pain.

She held up various objects in front of the light. Cliopher was distressed to find he could identify none of them. She came closer, and repeated the test, and then closer again. By the fourth time, when she was holding them so close they almost touched his nose, Cliopher was about to ask her to stop.

But then he realised she was holding up a pen. “A pen, brass nib, black wooden holder. Standard Palace issue.”

She moved to the next item. “Telescoping pointing device, brass.” And another, “Ring, silver.”

Finally, she held up a small piece of card. “Saya Mngeno's Used Goods Emporium,” he read off it, excitement rising in his voice, “all metals and glass, bought and sold.”

She moved it back a finger length, and it disappeared into a blurry rectangle. “Nothing now,” he said with a sigh.

“Already better than it was, Sayo Mdang, I'm very pleased with your progress. I'll give you some more of the eye drops.”

Cliopher let her peel back his eyelids, and drop the cold liquid in. When she had finished with his eyes, and moved on to making other checks, Cliopher decided to be brave and ask.

“Domina, might I be allowed to read a book? Or a pamphlet?”

There was an extended pause. “When you can see a hands-width from your face, and are comfortable with normal light levels,” she said eventually.

Cliopher sighed, and nodded. He went back to reciting the Lays.

---

At the third bell the next morning, Cliopher was released. Perhaps slightly reluctantly on the part of Domina Cirle, but Cliopher was glad to be out. She had told him to come back every afternoon to be checked, Cliopher quietly decided that he would only do so if the pain became unmanageable again.

They had let him read just four hours after he had first realised he could see. At the first bell of the evening, he had convinced the new guard to send a page for the morning reports. At the midnight bell he convinced the next one to send back the reports with his cover sheet and notes.

He had slept. By the next morning he could see a hand span in front of him. The morning guard was Ser Ergaz again. Cliopher did not understand why he rated a guard, presumably it was something to do with his lord's order to make sure Cliopher got appropriate medical care.

Standing at the entrance to the Alinorial Wing, he was now trying to convince Ser Ergaz that he did not need to be escorted back to his rooms.

“Thank you for your kind offer, Ser Ergaz. Bit I've had the same rooms since before the Fall. If I could find my way back then I'm sure I can now. You must have more pressing duties than making sure a secretary goes to bed, surely?” said Cliopher, smiling politely at the long blob that was all he could see of the guard.

“Very well, Sayo Mdang, I will see you tomorrow then,” said Ser Ergaz.

Finally free of people fussing at him, Cliopher walked back to his rooms. He only walked into four door frames, two statues, one stranger, and a column. He was fine.

Hunched over his desk, he completed his work from the day before. It would be late, but that seemed unavoidable when the healers wouldn't let him use a proper desk. His chancery hand was good, but not even he could do it in bed.

Finished, he returned to the centre of the palace (walking into three pillars, two of which he apologised to, and two door frames, but no people). There he distributed the documents to the pages, and started trying to get ahead for tomorrow.

---

The next morning, Cliopher presented himself at the doors to the Imperial Apartments. He did not yet have enough vision to identify the guards any more than he had the day before. But at one of the challenges he heard Ser Ergaz's voice, and bid him good morning.

Arriving once again in the official study, Cliopher's only impression this time was how very bright the room was. He dropped into his obeisance, and rose when the Emperor told him to.

Having only been in the room once before, Cliopher was a little nervous about finding his desk. Thankfully the dark wood of the desk and the mostly empty white room, meant he had no mishaps. He thanked the Emperor profusely for his kindness and generosity, and apologised for his inability to attend the previous day. Then they got to work.

The Emperor forbear to comment on Cliopher's hunched posture. Cliopher was relieved, as he knew he should be sitting up straight, but he couldn't yet see well enough at that distance to write legible shorthand.

The session went as smoothly as the first. Cliopher appreciated his lord's musical voice even more today, now that he was unable to admire the artworks or his Radiancy's costume.

He did stumble very slightly into one of the guards as he left, but nobody said anything. Cliopher hoped the Emperor hadn't noticed.
