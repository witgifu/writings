You dismissed him. It was all you could do, to stop me breaking out, to stop me confessing who I was and what I had done. Confess, to the man who had most been my support and hope through all these centuries. The man who hummed my greatest work, and wove it into everything he did. The man who I—but no, we could not say that, could not ask it of him. To say is to ask, and to ask is to command, and you cannot be denied.

You dismissed him. And he went, as he must at your word. Perhaps out of the room, perhaps out of the Palace, you did not specify. And then, holding onto the fragments of your serenity like a robe made of ribbons, you swept into the Private Study.

The cupboard. _My_ cupboard, where you did not go. Where I could rage, and cry, and have all the emotions that you were not allowed. The emotions that people had suffered because of, had died for. You had no desires, no dreams, apart from the good order of the Empire (gone) and Zunidh (thriving, thanks to Cliopher). 

I threw things on the floor, I tore up paper, I yelled at you. My destruction was muffled by the spells, but probably not silent. I didn't care (I did), I wanted to tear everything down (or maybe jut the taboos), I wanted _so badly_ to climb out of this tower like I had my last (but I could not, I could not, I could not).

The window called to me, like the window had all those years (centuries?) ago. Would there be a branch and a key? There never had been before out this window. (What tree grew so high?) But perhaps if _she_ had come, disguised as in a tale I might have spun, there would be now a key. I climbed the table and the chair and I looked out. 

Sky, sky, sky. Distant mountains. Nothing more.

There was a knock at the door. I nearly fell off the chair, balanced precariously as it was, and had to scramble down. I stood frozen, waiting. Who could it be? Who would dare? Ludvic, who had first suggested retirement? Conju, who had finally given in and _asked_ you directly what we wanted for breakfast? Rhodin, hoping he could reach me with silent communication?

Another knock.

You had dismissed Cliopher. Had he come back? Nobody had ever done that, nobody had dared. Yet Cliopher was the greatest supplier of petty treasons. But this would be no petty treason, no minor faux pas. In the days of Astandalas you would have had people lining up to execute him for you. Even now, perhaps, the right person might.

Another knock. "Tor."

I knew that voice, better than I knew my own. Silently, I stepped over to the door, and opened it a few inches.

"You were dismissed," you said, and I shuddered at letting you in here, even with the door open.

"Yes," said Cliopher, unquenchable Cliopher.

We stood still, at an impasse. You, holding me back, barely. From doing what, I do not know. Me unwilling to let you say anything more, do anything more, on the border of my sanctum. Caught between worlds, caught in the gaze of one Cliopher sayo Mdang. Who had been dismissed by the Last Emperor, the Lord of Rising Stars, the Lord Magus of his world, and chosen to return.

"Tor," he said again so softly, "Please."

I could not dismiss him again, and I would not let you. We stepped back, back until I lent up against the desk, and let him in. As we, as you, as I, had let nobody else in.
