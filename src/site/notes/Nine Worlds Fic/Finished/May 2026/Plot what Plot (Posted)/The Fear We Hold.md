Kip wakes, to total darkness. Whatever dream he's been having is forced abruptly from his mind. He closes and opens his eyes a few times, but it doesn't help.

Everything is falling, or dead, or gone, he can't tell. Up seems to be suddenly relative, and when he moves his head slightly it becomes disconnected. Almost like deep underwater, but he can move normally.

His heart rate rises, and it's the only thing he can hear. The silence is uncanny, his ears are stuffed, or maybe they're full of water. Maybe he is diving then.

No, he remembers this. The bells will ring, and he will fumble in the dark to the door. The hallway will be just as dark, and he will call out and all he will hear back are the echoes of his own voice. Then he will walk forward, and trip on something. He will reach down, slowly, to see if he can step over it or on it, and he will realise it is a body. There will be so many more.

He can't do it again. If the gods have sent him back as some kind of test he has failed. Some roads are only possible when you don't have a map.

All he can hear now is his own heartbeat, and his breathing. Are they too fast? They hurt, they don't seem to be working right. There are bright dots in his eyes, finally something other than the darkness. He starts choking.

“Kip!” calls a voice, but it's the wrong voice, he knows that voice and it's asleep. Will be for a year, or a hundred. “Kip!” it calls again, strange and echoing like his own voice was, or will be, in the hallway.

Suddenly warm arms wrap around him, and it's only against their stability that Kip can feel he is shaking. “I'm here, beloved, I'm here, it's just a dream.”

Kip shakes his head, it's not a dream.

But then a bright, warm, mage light appears in front of him. It's floating in the air. How is the magic working, all the magic broke, the lights went out, that's how—

“Shhh,” says the voice. Fitzroy, it's Fitzroy, so quietly in his ear. “I'm here. Can you feel me breathing? Breath with me.”

Kip tries. It's hard, but sometimes hard is good, sometimes hard takes focus. And then you can't think about other things like the dark. But it's not dark, not anymore, there's a light. He can't see anything, it's all wavy and blurry. That's because he's crying. It's okay, Fitzroy doesn't mind if he cries. Breathing. In, out, in, out. Slowly, the shaking is not so bad, and he can relax into Fitzroy's arms. *Fanoa*, his fanoa's arms. He is safe.

“Do you want to talk about it?” asks Fitzroy, still holding him tight.

“The dark,” says Kip, feeling a bit like he's five and complaining about monsters in the wardrobe, “I thought...” he hiccups, “the Fall.”

“Oh, beloved, I'm so sorry,” says Fitzroy, tucking his head into Kip's neck, “I turned off the light.”

“Not usually a problem,” sniffles Kip, rubbing Fitzroy's arms, “There's stars, or city lights… Just *so* dark.” He shudders again, as memories of the Fall come crowding back.

“Oh, I didn't think, this room has no windows. I'll make sure there's always a light, always. I'll make you one you can keep with you,” says Fitzroy. He starts to pull away. 

Kip grabs Fitzroy's arms, and pulls them around him tighter. “In the morning?” he says in a small voice.

“Of course, beloved,” says Fitzroy, and gives Kip a good squeeze.

Fitzroy is rubbing Kip's arms, slowly. Gently, like he's patting a timid cat. Kip can feel the warmth, the security, slowly trickling through him. He feels a bit silly, now. It's been so long since the Fall, surely it shouldn't be the first thing that comes to mind when he wakes in the dark.

“Sorry,” he says, awkwardly.

“Whatever for, Kip?” asks Fitzroy, rubbing his face on Kip's shoulder.

“Being stupid,” says Kip, trying to bury his words in the pillow, “Panicking.”

Fitzroy hums a few bars, nothing in particular, just something gentle. “Whenever someone mentions the Fall, you can always tell who remembers it,” he says, “It's a different reaction in each place. But the darkest shadows haunt those who survived in the Palace.”

It's true, Kip noticed years ago. There were other places where it was worse, probably, but time was so shattered not many of them are still alive. Perhaps some of their friends on Alinor are similar. And it's clear that however the Lord of Ysthar survived, was painful.

“It's not stupid,” said Fitzroy, “Do you say it's stupid when I wake from nightmares of being crowned?”

“No!” says Kip, rolling over and tucking his face into Fitzroy's chest, “Never. But those are _dreams_.”

“Last time I caught a whiff of Imperial incense when we walked past a temple, did you notice?” asks Fitzroy.

Kip nods, rubbing his hair on Fitzroy's nightshirt, “You went serene, and flinched away when I reached for your hand.”

“Because I can't—I couldn't—panic,” says Fitzroy, rubbing the back of Kip's neck now. “But it's the same thing.”

It's not possible for Kip to hug Fitzroy any tighter without restricting his breathing. He doesn't know what to say, he still feels silly. Finally, he just mumbles, “I love you.”

Fitzroy kisses Kip on the top of his head, “I love you too.” And then he begins to hum Aurora.
