It was so much, and not enough. Oh, yes, it satisfied the magic and the legal niceties. It didn't satisfy me.

A thousand years untouched, and I had held Cliopher's hands in mine. It was good I had spent those same thousand years learning to hold my composure while looking him in the eye as he committed treason. It helped prepare me for the way my heart stopped, the way I had to go on, even though my entire being was vibrating like the string on a harp. 'Beloved', it sang, my heart sang, I sang, deep in my soul.

Cliopher's eyes, his smile, echoed it back. And yes, somehow his heart, I this moment of magic, of connection like none  I had ever known before. Zunidh herself bridging our hearts so we could speak that deepest truth. It felt like a drug, like the wine of the fey. It filled every corner of me, my love for Cliopher, and overflowed to wash him. As wave after wave of his love crashed against me.

Then the moment passed. Food, and drink, and music. Important conversations with various people to reassure them, of what I was not entirely sure. I felt adrift. Untouched again, untouchable still for some more weeks. Somehow, I suspect at Rhodin or Ludvic's arrangement, I found myself with the Mdangs. 

The harp from our holiday appeared, and I played the music of my childhood with whoever would join. Many did, they were well-loved tunes, and many who would play a little. I tried to lose myself in the music, to forget about that touch for now. This was Cliopher's triumph, and I would not ruin it by being maudlin because I still had to wait.

Lost in the music somewhere, we segued into another tune. From the first note I knew what it was, what Cliopher always subconsciously picked. I couldn't stop myself. My hands were desperate to play that which I had always thought best. When they all stopped, afraid, you made some light joke about an amnesty. So I could go on, to join Cliopher, as I had wanted to for hundreds of years. It was not among my best performances, but it was heartfelt. I could see Cliopher enjoyed it too.

When it was over, I gave the excuse of needing to relieve myself, and slipped away. Out to the garden, with my guards and my harp.

I had done two things in one day that I had thought I might never do again. Touched another without fear, and played my own music. And yet, it wasn't enough. In both I had felt my heart catch fire with longing. More, more, more. I could not, I knew. Not now, maybe not ever.

Still, I had to hold back my tears, my desires, my love. The dam was shaking, I had to hold it tight. It was easier here, in the garden under a tui tree. The guards behind, the stars above. I could imagine I was alone, that my heart was as calm as the pond with water lilies before me, gilded by the lamplights. To breathe, to rest, to fit myself back into the box.

“My lord?” came the voice. Conju, or Ludvic, or Rhodin, would not have called.

*Beloved,* sang my heart. “Cliopher,” said your voice. I cringed, at letting you do it.

“Too crowded?” he asked, and without waiting for a reply, continued, “My family can be a lot, my lord, I understand.”

“It's nice out here,” I said inanely.

He looked at me, with shinning eyes and a slightly lopsided grin.

“Ah,” I said, “The rum punch?”

Cliopher grimaced, “It's a lot stronger than it tastes, my lord.”

“Sit down then,” I said, “We must not have you injuring yourself tonight.”

“I'll try to save that for tomorrow, my lord,” he said, looking around for another bench or chair. I hadn't noticed there were none. We both looked at the bench next to me.

“It would be safe,” I said, not brave enough to look up at his expression.

“Yes, my lord,” said Cliopher in his best neutral court voice.

Ah, well. So far and no further. I could hardly fault him. It wasn't as if I wasn't also afraid and uncertain of how to move, how to act in the world. I—

Cliopher plonked himself down on the bench next to me. There really was no more appropriate word for the way his whole body seemed to slump into the bench.

“Hello,” I said, into the silence that followed. I very nearly said it as I once would have, if a beautiful stranger, a little tipsy, had sat next to me. That might well have given me away. So it came out a little oddly.

“Hello,” said Cliopher back. He was one hundred percent himself. Not his court mask, not the self-deprecation he hid behind with his family.

*Beloved,* sang my heart.

I lent forward, admiring the pond, leaning my hands on the bench for balance. Not looking at Cliopher, not admiring his shoulders or his legs in his wonderful finery. Firmly keeping myself in control. Letting you in, just a fraction, to stop me doing anything stupid.

Cliopher copied me. “Can you see any fish?” he asked.

“No?” I said, confused, “I thought it was just lilies.” I sent out a tendril of power to brush the pond, but I could only feel plants and insects. Oh, and a snail.

“If you see the golden fish in the pond, it's supposed to mean you'll meet your love before the next moonrise,” he said, nonchalantly.

“Is that in the Lays?” I asked, wondering what had happened to the fish that had presumably lived there once. Perhaps they had died in the Fall.

Cliopher shook his head, and one of the feathers of his magnificent headdress tickled my ear. “No, my lord,” he said, “It's a court story, not an Islander one.”

He twitched forward, as if he had seen something. As he did so, his shoulder briefly touched mine, although mine was covered in several layers of silk. “Still,” he said, “Lots of teenagers tried to sneak in here to try and see it.”

“Including you?” I couldn't resist asking.

“Me, my lord?” he said sounding slightly surprised, “No, I was never… I never wanted love the way other people did."

My heart caught. Had he not heard this evening? When our hearts had been joined? Or had he heard and not wanted? Had I done want I had always promised I would never do and forced my love on someone unwilling, someone who couldn't say no, who had to—

“I never wanted love at first sight,” Cliopher continued, unaware of the torrent of emotions that had been raging through me. That stopped at his simple clarification.

Containing my shakes, I let you say, “Oh?”

Cliopher shook his head. “It seemed… Dangerous. To love so strongly someone you had only just met.”

“You didn't want… passion?” I managed.

He licked his lips, and while he was still watching the pond I was definitely watching him. “You mean sex, my lord?” he said, casually, “Sex is nice, but I don't like to start there. I know, I know, what a weirdo, are you sure you're a man, ha ha.”

This last part was delivered in faint mocking tones. I could hear the hurt behind it, as he preempted anyone else mocking him. Me, mocking him. “Oh, Cliopher, no,” I said softly, and then trying to lighten the mood myself said, “You don't have to want to shag everything that moves.”

Finally, he looked at me, and sighed. “No,” he said, “I know that.” He seemed unable to say anything more.

“But, love?” I asked, tentatively.

“Yes,” he whispered, as if that answered my question. I froze for a moment, lost and confused.

Then I realised what I had said. “Beloved,” I whispered back.

“Beloved,” he echoed, and slipped his hand along the bench. Until the sides of our fingers touched. 

I crept mine over his, cupping it much as I had when he held the shell. “Yes,” I whispered, and a shiver went up my whole body. I was touching someone, they were letting met. It was safe, I was safe, and loved.

Cliopher lifted his hand, carrying mine with it. He bent down to look at it, examining it with a care I associated more with charts in budget proposals. “Beautiful,” he breathed. His face was so close I could feel his breath on the back of my hand.

You had been complemented in every possible way as Emperor. Never had I heard such sincerity. I probably smiled. “Conju—” I said, desperately.

“Did not make your hands, my lord. Did not teach them how to move, or play the harp so beautifully,” he said. And then, he lent down the last few inches, and feather-light, touched his lips to the back of my hand.

I gasped. So warm, so soft, I had forgotten. My whole body writhed, wanting more. You clamped down on it. We could not take advantage. He was drunk, high on magic, and the relief of the completed rituals. He was not himself, and even if he was, you were still his lord. You could not.

Cliopher, not privy to the battle inside me, said, “Beloved.” Then he turned my hand over, and kissed my palm.

I bit my lip so hard I nearly cut it. Hard enough that I was sure Conju would notice and wonder. My breathing had become heavy, and erratic, my pulse climbed. *Domina Audrey would not approve,* I thought. I waited for you to stamp down, to take control and return order.

But you were gone. As if Cliopher had kissed me better, kissed you away. (You were not really gone, I knew, just… absent, for now.)

Cliopher nuzzled his face into my hand. I felt the soft smoothness of his cheeks, the bounce of his nose, the tickle of his eyelashes. It was nothing like touching my own face, and yet from so long looking (longing) it wasn’t unknown.

He turned my hand back over, and held it in both of his. “Always,” he said, with the sincerity he had made his oaths earlier.

“Beloved, always,” I echoed back, eyes flicking from our hands to his face and back.

“For as long as you will have me,” he said.

I brought my other hand over, to join. Twisting on the bench to face him fully, as he faced me. So close, so unbelievably close. 

“Forever, then,” I said, “Beloved, always and forever.”

“Beloved, always and forever,” he echoed back.

His face was even more beautiful close up. I could see the texture of his skin, the soft downy hair I had felt before.

Cliopher lent in too, and rested his forehead against mine. As I had seen him do with his family and friends. Our breath mingled, and I could smell the rum punch, as well as that which was uniquely him. The musk of hard work, of dance, of laughter and music. I drank it in like the best wine.

“Kip, beloved, always and forever,” I whispered, and felt him shiver against me.

“My—Tor—,” he said, uncertain.

You were gone. I had nowhere to retreat to, and I realised I didn’t want to. If we were to be this, he had to know. 

“Fitz—,” I breathed, but I flinched from my own speaking, my own naming of myself, when I wasn’t even sure it was true anymore. Perhaps I was only Tor, only Artorin, could only hope to be Artorin the Good and not, anymore, a Bard.

“Fitzroy?” he said, tenderly. His hands grasping tighter on mine.

I nodded, not trusting my voice. Lost, adrift, terrified of what he might say or do. Our foreheads still touching, he nodded too.

“Fitzroy, beloved, always and forever,” he said, and I could hear love and wonder in every word. It seemed impossible, unfathomable.

I tilted my head back a fraction, so our noses fitted next to each other’s. Burying mine in the softness of the fold between his cheek and nostril. Cliopher matched me, nuzzling my face as he had my hand.

Our heads tilted back a little further, and I couldn't say who pressed in first, because we were so close already. Lips touched lips, and oh the feeling crashed over me like a wave. Was Zunidh back, bridging between our hearts again? As I kissed my beloved, as he kissed me?

Lightning coursed through me, and I couldn't tell if it was my magic reacting to my own fear or if it was the taboos. You caught me, stopped me from moving so far, or making a sound that would startle the guards. I scanned his face. Had we, had I, hurt him? He might not say, he might try to hide it, you weren't allowed to be concerned for others, not even my beloved Kip.

Cliopher looked at you, blinking and smiling. He raised one of his hands up to his lips, feeling them. “Are they gold?” he whispered.

“No-o,” I said, shaking my head. They looked much as they always had, except that now I knew what it felt like to kiss them.

Looking down at our hands, he blushed and smiled awkwardly. “Well,” he said softly.

“Are, are you disappointed, Kip?” I asked, mirth bubbling up to replace the panic.

“Mmm,” he said non-committal. Still not quite looking at me, so I could see the blush on the top of his ears.

“I'm sure Conju would let you have some of whatever he paints me with,” I said, smiling.

“S'not the same,” mumbled Cliopher.

“Well, I'm glad I don't have to worry about priests attempting to assassinate you because I—” somehow I couldn't finish it. I couldn't say 'kissed', it seemed to small a word to cover what we were. What I dared to hope we could be. “Because I love you,” I said instead.

“Rhodin would catch them,” said Cliopher, looking back up, and touching the tip of his nose to mine.

“Mmm,” I said, not wanting to think about Rhodin, about anyone other than the magnificent man in front of me. I didn't want to think about how soon I would have to leave him, when I felt like I had just found him. Just found us.

I slipped the tip of my nose down the side of his, tilting my head for another kiss. Once, perhaps, I would have tried to show off, would have licked his lips and fluttered my eyelashes against his cheek. But it was so much already, just here, just touching.

“Fitzroy,” he mouthed, into my mouth. Practically undoing me right then and there. Whatever he wanted, he could have, I would be. My name on his lips, brushing mine, I was indeed lost in him. Kip wasn't surprised, or angry, or embarrassed. It was as if he had always known, and perhaps he had. Or maybe he had only just learnt, and didn't care.

It was the greatest gift I had ever been given, to be named by Kip, my true name. To be called beloved still, to be touched so gently.

“That!” said Dora, and I heard the door to the garden bang behind her. “That!” she said again, and we both twisted around to look at her. She stood behind the bench, hands on hips, next to the guards. “That was *definitely* a marriage.”
