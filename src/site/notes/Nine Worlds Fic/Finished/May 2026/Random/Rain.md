London, Raphael reflected, had always been wet. Now much of the water travelled secretly underground, but it was still the veins of the city. It wasn't always clean, in fact some decades the waterways had been less than half water, and the clouds almost entirely smoke.

It hadn't always been quite so _locally_ wet.

“Robin,” said Raphael, turning back from the view out the window, “I don't believe trees can usually swim.”

Robin, who had been fiddling with the settings on his new stereo, looked up. “Not usually, no. Unless you consider dryads to be trees. Why, have you met one that can?”

“No,” said Raphael, “Or at least not yet. We shall see, I suppose.”

Putting down the remote, Robin said sagely, “He plants the trees to serve another age.”

“Oh, it should be far sooner than that,” said Raphael, looking back out the window, “Although if one of your neighbours opens a downstairs window there might be a delay.”

Robin sighed, and joined Raphael at the window. “Oh,” he said softly, “Um. Do you know how to stop it?”

Raphael let his magic run over the courtyard again. “Yes,” he said, “Are you saying you didn't do this deliberately?”

“You know, I don't _always_ cause chaos deliberately,” said Robin, looking a little put out, “Although… do you think it would be possible to get it to follow Will around next time he's writing a tragedy? It might help him get in the mood.”

“It might hinder his ability to put pen to paper, if they're all soaked,” said Raphael.

Robin sighed, “And then he'll just be grumpy.” He gave the courtyard a look as it if had personally offended him, and stamped his foot three times. The small grey cloud, that had been making a very localised torrential downpour, dissipated and blew away.
