Cousin Zemius and Toucan had sat her down and tried to explain the difference between what had gone on between Cousin Kip and Lord Artorin and a wedding. But by virtue of being six, and having a large family, Dora had seen enough weddings to know just how different they could be. She had seen indoor weddings, and outdoor weddings, ones between islanders and velioi, ones with lots of gods called upon, and none. It _had_ been a wedding, and if everyone else couldn't see that Cousin Kip and Lord Artorin were looking at each other just like a newly married couple, well they were stupid.

What Dora did get tired of was trying to explain this to everyone. They were kind, but most of her cousins insisted on talking to her like she was four, and it was tiresome. (And if tiresome just happened to be her new favourite word, well, it was a good word.) Toucan did at least actually try to explain properly, citing laws and so on. But even he admitted that weddings had happened before the Astandalan Law Code was written down, before the Lays. So even if it might not be whatever kind of marriage Toucan said counted, it might still be one. She counted this as a victory.

There weren't a lot of other kids at this party, which meant that at some point when Dora was feeling tired of talking to adults, she wandered off on her own to explore. There palace was big; not as big as Gorjo City, but bigger than her school. And all of it was new, which was very fun. She found a room that seemed to just be for storing fabric, and another just for chairs. A room that seemed to contain just empty large vases, but she looked in each one to see if they had anything interesting. Most held only dried leaves or dust or cobwebs, but one contained a small box of coloured pencils. Dora liberated them, thinking she might put them with others if she found a room entirely full of pencils.

Instead, what happened was that a crowd of important velioi came up the hallway, bickering loudly. Not wanting to get trampled, or yelled at for touching their fancy clothes, she squished herself into a corner next to the table that was there. Irritatingly, they all stopped right at the table, so that Dora could neither go back nor forward. And they all ignored her. 

With a sigh, Dora ducked under the table. The underside was a much lighter wood than the top, and Dora wondered how that might have happened. Had someone spilled paint or wine or something that stained on the top? If so, this probably wasn't a very valuable table. Maybe it was just waiting here to be put somewhere less important, like outside. Well, if it had already been damaged, a little more wouldn't matter. She pulled out the coloured pencils, and drew the first thing that came to her. Cousin Kip and Lord Artorin, holding the big okana shell, and with a big love heart between them, so nobody could claim they were 'just friends'.

Satisfied with her work, she crawled out the other end, and went on exploring.

Earl Baljan's cousin Linus suppressed a snicker as Baljan dropped into the hot spring next to him. 

"I didn't know you were into secretary romances," said Linus, stretching.

"I'm not," said Baljan, confused. Then he blushed furiously. "I got the tattoo when I lost a bet, okay?"

"Sure," said Linus, not at all believing him, "What did you bet on?"

"That upstart Mdang loosing his head of course," said Baljan, beginning to get angry, but sliding further into the water anyway.

Linus snorted, "You lost that one completely."

Baljan didn't answer, just slid further down until only his nose was out of the water.
