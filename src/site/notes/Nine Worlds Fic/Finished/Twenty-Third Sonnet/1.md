I was in my private study, lying sprawled over the divan. There was a book of poetry from one of the Lord of Ysthar's friends open in my lap. It was very beautiful, but I had been struck by the first stanza of the twenty-third sonnet, and couldn't move past it.

> As an unperfect actor on the stage,  
> Who with his fear is put besides his part,  
> Or some fierce thing replete with too much rage,  
> Whose strength’s abundance weakens his own heart;

I had often considered you to be an actor. Or me to be an actor playing the part of you. How often had I, had you, missed a line or made a movement too suddenly. But the audience of the Emperor had not laughed or jeered at the poor actor, they had flinched in fear.
å
Until Cliopher had come. From the first day, he had seen me behind the performance. And over the years, as he had teased a more peaceful, more prosperous society from the ruins of Astandalas, he had teased out glimpses of me from behind you.

You had not let the rage out, except once, at Navikiani.

The fear, you thought, he had probably seen more often. Perhaps first when you had sent him to Littleridge. How he had managed that task was still a mystery that you (that I) treasured.

Then the magic around Kip flared. He was below me, somewhere in the Offices of the Lords of State. Something was very, very wrong.

I staggered up. The book tumbled to the floor, ignored. Two steps, and I pulled open the door. 

“Lord Mdang, in his office, get Domina Audry, Commander Omo, and Ser Rhodin. Run.”

I was gasping between phrases. Something in the spells was causing some kind of rebound back to me. I didn't have time to deal with it. Kip was the priority.

The guards, Pikabe and Ato, saluted. The message went out. It was slow, it was so slow. Kip needed help now. You, of course, could not run through the Palace. That would start a panic.

Instinctively, I shoved more magic into the spells I had wrapped around Kip. That would probably cause problems for Domina Audry when she arrived, but it might keep him alive until then. And I wasn't even sure if the issue was physical. If it was magical, if he had been cursed—

I had to go. You, with my fear, put on your part. Through seven anterooms, walking as fast as you dared. Down the hall, down the stairs, down to the flickering feeling of Kip.

Your hands flicked in acknowledgements automatically, your face was serene. Nobody panicked, you could have been walking to a regular meeting. I grated inside at the delay. 

There was an increase in volume, as you approached the Offices of the Lords of State. That was often true, even on days when my heart wasn't trying to break out of my body. The tone today was slightly different, anxious.

Everyone made way for you. They knew the sounds of your guards footsteps, the sound of people behind them falling to obeisance. You walked on, through the offices, into Kip's own.

He was lying across the floor, in front of Kiri's desk. Kiri and Eldo were holding Gaudy, who was weeping freely (as I wanted to). Zaoul was doing chest compressions, Tully was kneeling by Kip's head.

For the first time you had known her, Kiri didn't notice you. Everyone's eyes were on Kip. You knelt down carefully beside him, opposite Zaoul, giving particular care to keep out of the way of Zaoul and Tully.

It was possibly the fastest I had ever dropped into a trance. I reached out magical hands, safe hands. Every ward seemed fine, none of them had noticed any magical interference, all of them were present and working. The only spells activated were the ones for physical danger.

I meticulously went through each layer, polishing them to perfection and making sure Domina Audry would not be impacted by them when she arrived. I was almost done when I felt her. She felt like a needle of fire, incredibly fine and intense, compared with my bonfire. I withdrew.

When you opened your eyes, Kip was breathing on his own. Zaoul and Tully had stepped back to join Gaudy. Domina Audry was kneeling beside Kip, opposite you.

You remembered to breathe. Calm, serene, safe. If you were calm, everyone else could be calm. Your mind was still fizzy from working so much magic so quickly.

Kip's eyes flickered open, and looked directly at you. Before you could react, move back, move out of reach, he lifted his hand and grasped yours, where it rested on your knee.

You could not contain my fear, it burst out in a look of terror and a small gasp. Kip mirrored my reaction, but he did not release your hand. You tried to restore the serene mask, to shove my fear away, to bury it. He had not burst into flame, or been struck by lightning.

“Tor,” he whispered, his voice hoarse, “the Grandmother was calling me. It's too soon… I said it was too soon...”

“Don't talk, Kip, just rest,” you said, and looked over to Domina Audry.

She nodded, “You have had a heart attack, Lord Mdang.”

You held me in, held me tight. You needed to be strong, for Kip. Seeing you scared would make everyone else scared, and the government still needed to function.

Kip squeezed your hand, and his eyes fluttered closed. You counted your breaths, and he matched them perfectly. He held firm to your hand, too tight to let you move away. Now that he was touching you, I didn't really want to make him let go.

It was wonderfully comforting to touch someone, anyone. Which was why he had reach out, of course. You, the fool, had been there. Were there still. His slightly sweaty hand on your dry one, proof he was alive.

Domina Audry continued her examination. There were various delicate instruments, and she read his pulse from his neck. You did not interrupt, although I very much wanted to know the prognosis.

You tore your eyes away from Kip to check the room. To assess your audience. Ludvic and Rhodin had sent Pikabe and Ato outside, possibly to try to keep more people from coming in. Both guards stood formally by the office door. In front of Kip's desk was the huddle of junior secretaries, including Gaudy. Between the desk and the door hovered Conju.

Calm, serious, confident, was the feeling you tried to project. You turned back to Domina Audry. “I can detect no magical component,” you said, accidentally dropping into the informal. Well, you were kneeling on the floor, the taboos still actively being broken. Formality had probably been lost awhile back.

“Thank you, my lord,” she said, not looking up, “I'm afraid he's so wrapped up in your magic I would find it hard to tell.”

Thankfully, a blush was hard to detect on my dark skin. I *had* put a lot of protective magic on Kip, but I felt it was justified. He had no magic of his own, and a charming disregard for his own safety.

Including his health. A wave of guilt ran through me. Had I caused this? Kip would never tell me if he was overworked, he would just push on. He had done so through illness and injury before. I had been desperate to push forward to start on my quest. In my haste, had I nearly killed the man who would set me free?

Tears threatened. Years of practice kept them to a dampness in your eyes, and stopped them spilling. You would have to wait for Domina Audry's verdict. Waiting and watching while others worked was a large part of your job, you could do so now for this little while.

A stretch of time that felt like hours, although no bells rang. Then Domina Audry looked up at you. “He has taken less damage than I would have expected,” she said, “but he is very weak. We should move him as little as possible, but he would be better somewhere he can sleep.”

Thankfully, he no longer lived at the end of the Alinorel wing, but nearby in the Lord of Zunidh's apartments. You looked up at Conju. He bowed, and said, “I will prepare his household, my lord. I will return when they are ready for you to move him.”

Next, you turned to Kiri. Now that she could see her boss was alive, if very unwell, she had recovered some of her composure. 

“You may let the Office know what has happened, and send people home for the rest of the day. We will notify the Court this evening.” She performed her obeisance, and left.

The junior secretaries were now in a huddle without their boss. You tried to think of a useful errand to send them on, people always felt better if they could be helpful. 

“Sayo Vawen, would you please to write to your family, and let them know what has happened?” you asked. “If they would like to visit, they may make use of the skyships.”

He bowed, watching your hands, held tight by Kip. “Of course, Glorious One. Thank,” his voice cracked, “thank you.” He took a deep breath, and he and his young colleagues gathered themselves together.

"Saya nai Vasiaan, Ser Tkinele, thank you. Your service will not be forgotten,” you said. Since Conju wasn't there to look shocked, you added, “If there is anything I can offer you in return, please let me know.”

All four looked slightly stunned, a look you were very familiar with. But they managed to perform their obeisances and exit with decorum, and without any obsequious blather.

Domina Audrey got up from the floor, and began pulling vials and powders from her bag onto Kiri's desk. “I will make something to strengthen his heart, my lord,” she said, her focus intent, “but the most important thing will be rest. At least two weeks of bed rest.”

You nodded, and looked back down at Kip. Now that only Ludvic and Rhodin were watching, your mask cracked a little. I frowned, watching his breaths that were to shallow and too uneven. Like my own, I realised.

Unable to hold in the shakes as I did so, I turned my hand under Kip's to hold his as well. I squeezed gently. We both breathed more evenly, more deeply. I would fall apart later, now Kip needed me to be strong.

His eyes fluttered open again. He looked worried, until his eyes landed on you again, on me. “You're here,” he breathed.

“I'm here, Kip,” you said, wondering if he'd been dreaming, what he'd seen, “Everything will be fine.”

He smiled, such a sweet, trusting smile. How had I done this to him? How could I?

You pulled me back again. Recriminations would not help him now. You smiled back, tentatively. “How do you feel?”

“Fine, my—” he began, and then blushed. “Bad,” he corrected.

Your smile broadened, more genuine now. He must be feeling truly awful to admit that.

Domina Audry finished making her medication, and poured it into a half-full glass of water that was sitting on Kip's desk. She came back over, and knelt down again. Gently, she lifted his head and helped him drink it.

The medicine must have tasted horrid, or perhaps Kip's throat was still sore. He grimaced, but drank it all down. Then he closed his eyes tightly.

You squeezed his hand again, wishing you dared reach out further, hold him close. The man who seemed able to achieve anything you put before him, felled by his own body (by my recklessness). He had seemed to have an endless supply of optimism, of belief that it was possible to make the world a better place.

Looking down at your entwined hands, you could see shimmers of gold on the edge of his skin. Tilting his hand gently, it was golden wherever you had touched. You *had* done damage to him. 

The priest-wizards were working to unwind the taboos, so that you could go out into the world. You had known it wasn't safe yet. The memory of that poor woman was scarred into your mind. She had vanished in fire so intense it consumed her before she could scream. How had you not moved away?
