I returned to the rose-covered box where I was kept. After a little while, a knock came at the door, and the junior attendant answered it. Then she turned to me.

“The Glorious One has invited you to lunch, your highness,” she said, with a small bow.

I did not hesitate, “His Radiancy is most kind and generous, I will of course attend him.” Standing, I waited a fraction of a second to see if the attendants thought I required changing again.

The senior one swooped in to swap the mantle. She must have overheard the original message. And of course there was no possibility of refusing such an invitation. If I had been in the throes of labour, or passed out drunk, they would have worked out how to send me to him.

I arrived at the dinning room not two minutes later. The Glorious One was standing by the fretted windows, looking out on the gardens. I briefly wondered if he could remember the gardens of his childhood, at the red house. Stuffing such insolent thoughts aside, I made my obeisance.

The gesture to rise was given to me almost indecently quickly, as if he had been asking me to not lower myself at all. Nervous, I took it as a request for reduced formality. So when he said, “Good afternoon, Melissa,” I replied (heart in my mouth), “Good afternoon, my lord.” I could not yet bring myself to use his name.

“Please,” he said, and gestured to the table. I had expected he would be dining with some of his ministers or courtiers, but the table was set for two. Two, and unusually close. Not as equals, no it was clear which place was mine and which his. Yet, still, closer than I expected.

Much closer than I deserved, I thought. A consort who had failed on her wedding night duty. Yet it seemed he was giving me all the respect as if I had pleased him greatly. Nervously, I waited for him to sit so I could as well.

“How was your morning, Melissa?” he asked, gently. Soup dumplings were served to both of us.

“Very well, my lord. I spoke with a few who had requested,” I said, and tried to take a dainty bite of the slippery morsels.

He nodded, “And do you have any plans for the afternoon?”

“I had some thought to my correspondence, my lord. But of course if you have any request or suggestion, my lord, I would be delighted,” I said. High, soft voice; eyes demurely down; cheeks gently flushed. Suggesting, providing every enticement I knew how.

He smiled at me, and I flicked my eyes up to his face and down again. But he didn't so much as reach out to touch the hand I had left on the table. (If I had done that to Shallyr he would have had a hand down my shirt at least)

“We thought it might be well to give some thought to your household,” he said, “Where have you been placed?”

“The Rose Room, if it pleases you, my lord,” I said, putting down my eating sticks. A flicker of displeasure crossed his face. Was he upset I had been given that room? Was it too far away?

“That is hardly adequate,” he said, quietly, looking at his plate, “It doesn't even have its own dressing room.” Then he looked up at me, adding, “It is no matter, Melissa, it must have been a mistake. We will show you your suite after lunch. Now, attendants?”

“Two ladies have been waiting on me, my lord,” I said, still anxious at his displeasure, “I do not yet know their names.”

He paused again, and I wanted to fidget nervously with my eating sticks. Instead, I ate another dumpling.

“You have our permission and encouragement to appoint anyone to your household who was or is a part of your own or the Princess Lamissa's household,” he said, in his formal voice. It had the air of something carefully composed. “You may also dismiss anyone from your household without consulting us,” he added.

“My lord is most kind,” I said, and meant it quite sincerely. To have even one of the ladies that I knew and trusted, oh it would be a relief.

He accepted this with a very small smile. “According to what we were able to extract from the records, the Consort should be provided with at least a dozen daytime attendants, and half as many night attendants. A wardrobe mistress, a household manager, a secretary, and for some reason either a musician or a fool.”

I had to employ all of my years of training to stop my mouth dropping open. What would I do with so many people? There would be enough for an assistant to every maid!

“The Tower works in shifts,” said his Radiancy, obviously aware of the decadence, “So you would have four attendants during the day, and two at night. We are not sure what hours the musician-or-fool would work. We don't recall seeing a fool in the court?”

“His Radiancy, the Emperor Eritanyr, banned them, my lord,” I said, surprising myself with my boldness.

“Of course,” said his Radiancy, pushing a dumpling around his plate, “Well, you are free to employ either. Or both, if you like. And we will talk to the guard commander about assigning you a detail, should you wish visit anyone, or perhaps walk in the gardens.”

I very nearly choked. Had his Radiancy realised what he had just given me? Permission to leave when I wanted to? Carefully, I took a sip of water. It might not be wise, yet, to take up such a gift, but it would also be incredibly rude not to.

“My lord is so very generous to his newly-made Consort,” I said, “Such a thing has rarely been known.” I hoped he might pick up on what I was hinting at.

Our eyes met, and I could see in them a sad acceptance. Well, perhaps he could afford to show emotion in his eyes, since they would blind anyone else.

“We wish to give you every comfort and consolation,” he said, slightly carefully, “But we know that so much change may be overwhelming, and so ask you to take all the time you need.”

“My lord is most wise,” I replied. It seemed we had both successfully conveyed our meaning.

“Please also,” he added, “Take care to the advice of Princess Lamissa for the appointing of an appropriate secretary. We are not sure if the Master of Offices is knowledgeable to appoint one for your needs.”

Since his Radiancy had managed to dismiss three secretaries in six months on the throne, I could understand why he might think it better I try to find one through another channel. And indeed, I would likely mostly require a social secretary, managing my appointments and correspondence. No proclamations would be sealed by the _Consort_.

“Thank you, my lord,” I said, applying myself to the steamed vegetables.

He kindly turned the conversation to music, a topic where I could gently express opinions and maintain a flow of chatter without such close concentration on every word. It was almost relaxing, except that I was aware with every turn that I needed to entrance him, delight him. I tried gentle flattery, soft looks, I even touched a finger to my lips. He seemed unmoved, and I hoped it was just because his mind was on other things.

I was aware he had not taken a concubine of any kind. I had assumed that was because any available had been prepared to Eritanyr's taste. Like their preferences in decor, I had assumed they were simply very different. But there was a chance, perhaps, that his Radiancy was simply not so interested in physical intimacy. Perhaps he also saw it as a duty, and so only wished for a Consort. More likely I was not appealing to him correctly.

We finished eating lunch at exactly the same time. I was uncertain if he had been trying to keep an even pace with me, I had certainly been attempting to follow him. To finish before the Emperor was to be stuffing oneself, to finish after risked having your unfinished food whisked away.

My lord stood, and perforce so did I. Then he, and his guards, showed me along the hall to the suite he had intended for me. Of course, it wasn't on the scale of the Imperial Bedroom and the baths commissioned by our grandmother. Yet it was indeed sumptuous. And decorated more tastefully than the rose room. A sitting room, private dinning room, dressing room, washroom, and a bedroom with a bed that could fit three comfortably. 

Then a final empty room. Neither of us commented on it, although I knew immediately it would see no use as a nursery. A Consort, as my mother had explained, may not raise or even suckle her own child. If I managed to have a live child, if they were deemed acceptable to raise, it would not be me who did so. After all, breastfeeding, as every farm-wife knows, can pause your cycles. Surely he knew? His Radiancy had told me himself that he knew the risks of our union.

Perhaps I would put a small library in that room, or a harpsichord.

My two attendants found me when his Radiancy and I returned to the sitting room. They frankly stared in astonishment at the honour I was being given. I carefully tucked my own reaction behind a polite smile.

“My very great thanks, my lord,” I said with a deep curtsy that was yet just shy of actually kneeling.

“I will see you later, Melissa,” he said, and departed.

“I must send a note to my mother,” I said to nobody in particular, and walked over to the desk in the corner. It was surely intended for a secretary, but someone had stocked it with basic supplies. I bent over, and wrote quickly and neatly.

There weren't many cyphers or codes I could use without a reference text. Most of them, it was obvious that the message was encoded, even if it couldn't easily be decoded. I need to be above reproach. And, I reminded myself, I had been given permission by _the Emperor_ from his own lips, witnessed by his attendants.

> Dearest Mother,
> 
> My most gracious lord had given me a boon that I did not expect, and had not even thought to long for. His Radiancy wishes to appoint me a household of my own, and expressed a desire that it be primarily composed of those whom I already know and trust. I would be pleased if you could send me those women of good character and experience, who would do well in the Imperial Household. The Glorious One has particularly recommended I have both a household manager, and a secretary, as well as a dozen attendants.
> 
> I hope to be receiving guests again tomorrow morning.
> 
> Much love,
> 
> Melissa

I folded the note, and for want of a signet ring or wax to seal it with, applied a simple letter-lock. Handing it to the lower-ranked attendant, I said, “Have a page deliver it.” The urge to add 'please' was strong, but I knew I must not.

I had claimed I would work on my correspondence, which rather assumed I had been given any. There was one attendant left to me now in these vast echoing rooms.

“What is your name?” I said, trying to sound as neutral as possible. Neither angry she has not introduced herself, nor embarrassed that I needed to ask.

She bowed low. “Lady Panyoe Ardroon, if it pleases you, your highness," she said, her voice court-perfect.

“A relation of the Duchess of Ardroon?” I said, keeping the curiosity at bay.

“She is my second cousin, once removed, your highness,” she said, with another bow.

So, was the choosing of the Duchess of Ardroon a move in the Game of Courts, or simply a coincidence?

“Well, Panyoe, I assume some poor souls have been given the task of responding to thousands of letters and cards? And have put some aside for my particular attention?” I said, tilting my head slightly, but not smiling.

“Yes, your highness,” she said, but made no move to do anything. Was she obstinate, or just stupid?

“Is there a particular reason why you think I should not address them now, Panyoe?” I asked, letting a little firmness enter my voice.

Her eyes widened slightly, and she bowed deeply. “My apologies, your highness, not at all. Bonny will fetch them as soon as she returns.” There was a slight flush to her cheeks, and I saw the truth. Someone had told her I must not be left alone, and despite the clear favour show by the Glorious One, Panyoe thought those instructions paramount. Curious.

“Very well,” I said, pulling forward more paper from the desk. “How long have you worked in the Tower, Panyoe, and what positions have you held?”

“Five years, your highness,” said Panyoe, “I was initially in the Lower Wardrobe, I move to the Upper Wardrobe at the ascension of the Glorious One,” (after he fired all the Upper Wardrobe staff for incompetence, I assumed) “And was appointed as your attendant yesterday.”

I scribbled a note about this, and I could see that made Panyoe nervous. Where had she come from, that she hadn't perfected a court mask? Or perhaps she felt she didn't need to hide her feelings from me.

“And Bonny?” I asked, my pen poised.

“The same, your highness,” she said, quietly.

So, interesting. I knew that under Eritanyr the Household had been a microcosm of his love of setting people against each other. And his roaming hands. The Lower Wardrobe was probably a safer place to be. But who had decided to pluck these two from the Upper Wardrobe to serve me? Was it just that these were the only women in the Tower?

“And you were appointed to me by…?” I said, with a bland a face as I could.

“The Glorious One's Seneschal, Lord Neirth, your highness,” she said, more confidently.

Of course, it would be the Seneschal, but it was good to have it confirmed. That was also likely the person who wanted me monitored. Fascinating that Lord Neirth already saw me as a potential threat. What would happen if I dismissed these two women? Probably nothing good. They had not actually done anything wrong, so it seemed a little cruel to dismiss them for simply not being people I already knew and trusted. Yet, I could not trust them. Not yet.
