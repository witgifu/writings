It was a beautiful evening in Gorjo City. I had gone for a late-afternoon snorkel, and come back to the bathouse to wash off the salt. It still reminds me of that first taste of freedom on Lesuia, snorkeling, and so despite having to treat my hair every time, I will persist. Clean again, I ambled inside to find Kip.

Rhodin and Conju were bickering in a friendly way in the kitchen, so I only waved at them and went upstairs. The tai always made me feel warm inside, and so when I walked passed it and saw Kip purusing the bookshelf, I stopped and admired him for a moment. Broad, so full of life, so confident in his skills and knowledge.

I tiptoed over to my beautiful fanoa. Wrapping my right hand over his right shoulder, I kissed the top of his left shoulder that poked out of his sleeveless tunic.

"What are you looking for?" I said, popping my head over his left shoulder to try and see what he was looking at.

I expected him to sigh, and say he must have lent it to someone. Or perhaps joke that he was looking for me and turn around. Instead, he went rigid, and then relaxed.

"Oh, sorry Kip, did I surprise you?" I said, realising that of course on bare feet and dressed only in a bathrobe I was probably silent. Belatedly, I noticed another reason, and pulled my hips back a little. Clearly my moment of admiration had made me half-hard.

We had moved, over the months, towards more physical intimacy. But as neither of us willing to revisit the disaster of a conversation on the vaha, it had been very slow. Kisses, touches. I always let Kip lead, and followed joyously. 

Clearly, I had accidentally gone too far. "I'm so sorry," I said, and stepped back further, "I didn't realise, I didn't mean..."

Kip didn't move. Just stood with both hands on the bookshelf. Bracing himself?

"Kip?" I asked softly, dithering between reaching out and stepping further away.

He lent forward slightly, resting his head on the books.

"Are you unwell?" I said, anxiously, uncertain.

"No sir," he whispered, eyes closed tightly.

Despite my flinch, he had never called me 'sir'. Where had he gone, what had happened to him? Quickly I checked his protections, but they were fine, so it was nothing magical. Not even a whisper of the divine.

Slowly, I reached out towards him, saying, "Kip? Are you there? Come back to me beloved. Wherever or whenever you are, it's not real." I touched him gently, but he didn't react. "Beloved, I'm here, you're home, in Gorjo City, in your house, with your family."

His breaths were slow and controlled, and he didn't move or open his eyes.

Now seriously worried, I sent a tendril of magic to the kitchen to tap Rhodin on the shoulder. The discussion downstairs stopped immediately, and in mere heartbeats Rhoding was pounding up the stairs. As he opened the door, I made the old gestures. Not life-threatening, medical, Cliopher.

Rhodin nodded once, and walked over. I turned back to Kip. The only question he'd responded was a direct and simple one that could be answered with yes or no.

"Are you home?" I asked, trying to simplify the memory test questions Domina Audrey had asked me.

"No sir," he said, quietly.

Rhodin looked confused, I sighed.

"Are you in the Palace of Stars?" I tried.

"No sir," was his reply.

"Is Artorin the Emperor?" tried Rhodin, with a wince of apology to me.

"Yes sir."

That was perhaps not so useful an answer. Kip let out a tiny sob, and instinctively I pulled him away from the bookshelf and into my arms.

'Pliant' was the only word I could think of to describe his behaviour. What past memory was he stuck in, what horror of Astandalas, or the Fall? My great statesman, who had taken on the Sun in negotiation and come away with everything he wanted. Trembling slightly in my arms, and trying to suppress it.

I lead him over to the couch, his eyes were still closed. Rhodin hovered by the door, eventually deciding to go. Presumably to get something or someone, but I ignored him, negotiating not bumping Kip on anything.

When the back of his legs were against the edge, I tried to push him gently to sit down. Instead, he knelt, his feet under the chair. Horrified, I stepped back, and then again when he opened his mouth, his eyes still tightly closed.

My eyes fixed on Kip, unable to look away from my own nightmare, I scrambled backwards over the coffee table, kicking off books. Back and back until I bumped into the wall.

Always able to be silent when he wanted, Conju made the door click shut behind him. I looked from Kip, to Conju, and the lessons of fourteen years gripped me firmly. Instinctively, you forced yourself to relax, and step away from the wall. Looking slightly to Conju's left, you said, "Cliopher appears to be trapped in a memory."

Conju's eyes flicked to Kip, kneeling in front of the couch. But your demeanour had effected him as well, and he stayed focused on you.

"We have not been able to ascertain which one, and we appear to be making the problem worse," you said, cool and calm. As if I was not screaming inside that we had broken Kip. I had hurt him as I had sworn never to hurt anyone, the person who I treasured most in all the nine worlds.

You retreated upstairs, to your tower, and shut the door. I collapsed onto the nest of cushions, that had once been ours, and cried silently. For what I had done, for what I couldn't fix, for a dream of love. Until my tears ran dry, and my head hurt.

There were no bells here, to tell me when my hour was up. But the dimming light gave me some clue that it had been a while. I washed my face, and opened my awareness to the house. It liked it when everyone was here, but particularly Kip. It told me we were all home.

Slowly, I crept down the stairs from my tower. Still horrified at what I had done, but additionally embarrassed that I had retreated into you. That I had forced you on Conju, who deserved his own retirement.

Peering around the corner, I could see people had gathered around the light Kip had brought back from the Sun. I tiptoed further, spotting Jullanar first, and then Conju. Finally, wrapped in the purple angora, Kip. 

I was torn between rushing to his side and running away again. But he looked at me at the exact moment I had decided to retreat, and reached out a hand toward me.

Oblivious to anyone else, I rushed over to hold it, and sank down by his feet. "I am so, so sorry, Kip," I said, my eyes blurry with tears. Apologies were still hard, I suppose I hadn't had much more practice.

He squeezed my hands back, but didn't say anything. I buried my face in the corner of the chair, unable to look at anyone.

"Fitzroy," said Jullanar, gently.

"Mmm," I mumbled, into the couch.

"Have you been to see a mental health doctor, or a counsellor? Since you retired?"

I made a negative mumble, assuming that Domina Audrey's most recent physical wasn't what she meant. The Domina was the only regular medical care I had ever had. In the Palace of Stars, I had always been very careful to contain my feelings. When being seen to be sad could have meant one of my staff committing suicide, or someone drugging or spelling me.

"I think you both accidentally triggered each other today," she continued, gently, "And it's something you can get help for."

"You need to talk," added Conju, "At least to each other."

"Okay," I said, not knowing how to do any of it. But I didn't want to see the Emperor appear in our home ever again. And I would do anything not to trap Kip where he was before.

"Come up here, beloved," said Kip, patting the seat next to him with his free hand.

I scrambled to do so, releasing his hands. Not sure where the boundaries were now, I sort of hovered awkwardly next to him, close but not touching. Thankfully he resolved the issue by leaning into me.

"I told you, when I traveled through the Grey Mountains, I was captured as a spy," he said. 

I smoothed the wrinkles in the blanket and nodded.

"And I told you they nearly starved me, and made me run across the old rope bridges," he continued.

"That's why you don't like them now," I said softly. We hadn't encountered many, mostly the sky ship ones, but I had seen how he disliked them. Rhodin had also told me, back when the fears of the Viceroy were something that could be exploited by his enemies.

Kip nodded, and swallowed. "One of them started offering me extra food… They weren't allowed to visit the village, see their girlfriends or boyfriends… I didn't *want* to… I said no, and he hit me, and strangled me… I… I was so hungry…" He stopped, and closed his eyes.

I wrapped my arm around the blanket, and whispered, "It's *not* your fault. You were trying to survive, and he hurt you."

"I know, but…" said Kip, and I hugged him. Many times I had seen that the things our heads understand are not always what our hearts believe.

It was my turn to take a sharp breath. "He wasn't at the new bridge opening? Was he?" Had I made Kip sit beside his rapist? Made them work together on the new agreements? Did Kip sip tea next to me while the man who violated him sat opposite and fawned at me?

"No," said Kip softly, pulling my mind back to the present. "Thankfully, or who knows how I would have inconvenienced the negotiations. I've never reacted like I did today before."

Of course, I was about to tell Kip that pausing the bridge opening to see he got justice wouldn't have been an _inconvenience_. But Conju spoke instead.

"You did, once," he said, in the gentle voice he had used when I was sick. "I wasn't sure what had happened at the time. It was... oh, not long after the Littleridge Treaty was signed. We'd gone to a bar in the city with Rhodin, and he'd abandoned us chasing some wine merchant. The bar had a band with an obnoxious horn player."

Kip looked up, thinking. "I remember you had just met someone new," he said, "Did he own a glue factory?"

Conju snorted, "Something like that."

"You said later you'd decided not to see him again, because he..." Kip's eyes went wide, "He didn't know how to keep his hands to himself?"

"Yes," said Conju, quietly, "He'd been flirting with both of us, no I don't expect you to remember that, you didn't notice at the time. I think he was just playing around, and he came up behind you as you were standing at the bar. Grabbed your hip."

"I don't remember anything else from that night," said Kip, and I squeezed him again.

"Yes, well. I did wonder if he'd put something in your drink at the time. The symptoms were not entirely dissimilar. As soon as I realised what a state you were in, I took you back to my apartment."

Kip looked at me, and I could see the fear clear in his face. "You're not," he said, "It's not... I love you, Fitzroy."

"I know, beloved," I said, "I love you too, I love what we are now. I didn't mean to—I don't ever want to—Go back."

At his look of confusion, I tried to clarify, "When you were trapped, in the memory, I tried to get you to sit on the couch. But you..." I swallowed. Kip had been so brave, so articulate in explaining what had happened to him. I didn't know why I was struggling to explain what I had done. And I didn't want *you* to do it for me.

"You knelt... It was exactly like in one of my nightmares," I couldn't describe it, the actual dream. That vision that had haunted my nights for years. I was shaking just thinking about it, so I tried a different tack. "When I was Emperor, nobody could say no to me. I did my best to not ask questions, to not let anyone be purified, so nobody could be put in a position where they were forced." Still, I was wandering around the point. 

A single tear escaped my left eye, and Kip brushed it away. His face was soft, and understanding. "You never wanted to be worshipped," he said.

I nodded and then shook my head, and gave him an anguished look. Finally, I decided if he couldn't cope with the whole truth, I would just have to live with that. Hiding who I was afraid I was, deep inside, was not going to help. It wasn't as if the nightmares had stopped when I left the Palace.

"Kip," I whispered, letting my hands go limp, and unable to look him in the eye. "Kip, for at least the past five hundred years I have had a recurring nightmare where you are brought into the Imperial Bed, and I rape you, and you lie there crying but don't try and stop me or get away."

That was it. I had confessed my crime, that I was no better than that man in the Gray Mountains who had tormented him. Like my uncle and my cousin, fundamentally broken. Twisted inside, malformed, inbred, irreparable.

Nobody moved. I wasn't sure if Ludvic or Rhodin were there. Perhaps they thought they wouldn't be able to arrest a planetary mage. I was still untouchable, still above the law.

"You can't control your dreams, much less your nightmares, Fitzroy," said Jullanar, gently. "You call them nightmares, they make you recoil in horror, you did everything you could to make them impossible."

"It's _not_ your fault," said Kip, in such a clear imitation of me earlier that I laughed and sobbed at the same time. "I will get help if you do," he said, and reached an arm around to hug me. I melted into him.

"Of course," I said, "Anything for my _fanoa_." Perhaps I cried, but that was okay, now.
