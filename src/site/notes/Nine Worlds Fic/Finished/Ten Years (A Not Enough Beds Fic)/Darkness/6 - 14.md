Lord Conju returned that evening. When he knocked, Kip remembered to answer the door himself. He bowed Conju into his rooms, trying for a slightly shallower bow this time. It must have been closer, because he only received a brief eyebrow quirk in response.

There was a box in Lord Conju's arms, and a basket hanging from his elbow. “May I take that for you, Lord Conju?” asked Kip.

Shaking his head, Conju put both on the small dining table. “Lady Ylette has kindly agreed to help us. She should be here shortly. I have pulled a few favours and gotten access to some accoutrements used for previous concubine candidates.”

From the box he drew out smaller cases, opening them to reveal three rings, a toe ring, a brooch, a necklace of pearls, a bangle, and two thin gold bracelets. All in the same colours: red, black, and copper. Gems (or were they glass?) winked from satin and velvet pillows. Kip was stunned.

“Surely… I cannot...” he stammered, as the final case was drawn out, containing a matching necklace, bracelet, brooch, and hairpin.

“They're all purified, so they've just been mouldering in the stores,” said Conju, picking up a box with a ring. “And none of them are suitable colours for his Radiancy, so there has been nobody else to wear them.”

Kip briefly imagined that he was being filed away in the Treasury, next to bolts of silk and strings of flame pearls. Put away until the Emperor had need of him.

Lord Conju held out the first ring. It was a coppery metal, two thin vines curling around a red cabochon. It could have been copper and garnet, or a gold alloy and ruby. Kip decided to pretend for now that it was all costume jewellery, to make himself feel less nervous.

Kip took a deep breath, and tried it on. It fit on his index finger only. Conju nodded, and held out his hand to pass it back. They all went back into some organisation system Lord Conju saw and Kip couldn't quite grasp. The ring that didn't fit at all went back in the box, but that was all he could work out.

From the basket, Lord Conju revealed a selection of cosmetics. “Have you worn much before?” said Conju, pulling out tiny containers from a nest of wood shavings.

“No, Lord Conju, not — not like those,” Kip replied, remembering the ochre paints that had been used for some festivals.

“We shall keep it simple for now then,” said Lord Conju, pulling forward three containers. “Tinted lip balm, hopefully self-explanatory,” Conju looked at Kip, and Kip nodded. He had learnt the importance of lip balm his first autumn in the Palace of Stars.

Conju pointed at the next container, “Blush, which I will show you how to apply. Less is more.”

The next box opened to show a small jar, a selection of tools, and what looked like foil. “Nail lacquer, and copper leaf. We will only do your toes for now. You have to inspect it twice per day and if you notice any flaking, repaint that nail.”

It turned out that a calligraphy brush was not entirely unlike the tiny brush used to paint the sticky base that held the copper leaf down. It was then burnished to a shine with a wooden stick. Kip quite liked the effect, when he imagined it on someone else. On himself, it felt strange.

Before Lord Conju had time to show Kip how to use the blush powder (for which Kip was silently thankful), Lady Ylette arrived. Kip bowed her in, and got the same mildly disapproving look as he had before from Lord Conju.

This time, she had brought her work supplies. Lord Conju directed Kip to stand on a footstool. The two others walked around Kip and had a discussion where he was sure he only understood one word in three. Eventually pins began to be added, and Lady Ylette drew on his clothes with a thin stick of chalk.

Lord Conju carefully removed all the layers of Kip's uniform, not disturbing the chalk or pins. Leaving him only in his undershirt and trews. He felt rather exposed.

“I'll have this one done first, and it should be through the laundry buy tomorrow morning,” said Lady Ylette, smiling slightly, “Then tomorrow I can do the next one.”

“Thank you very much, Lady Ylette,” said Kip, feeling a little chilly. He wanted to go and grab his dressing gown, but he thought that would probably be rude.

She carefully folded all the fabric. “Now,” she said, “payment.”

Kip looked worried. He had, in theory, a salary still being deposited into his Imperial Bank account. But unable to leave the Tower, he had no way to access it. Knowing the rates that nobles usually bribed at, it would probably not be a good thing for his balance, even if he could access it.

However, Lord Conju smiled. From the basket of cosmetics, he removed a tiny scent vial. With a flourishing bow, he handed it to Lady Ylette. She laughed, and took it from him, “I will tolerate your behaviour, Lord Conju, only because I know you mean nothing by it.” She tucked the vial into her workbasket, and left with Kip's uniform.

Deciding he was cold enough that he didn't care if it was rude, Kip ducked into his bedroom to fetch his dressing gown. When he returned, Lord Conju had packed away most of the items he had brought with him.

Remaining on the table was one ring, the lip balm, and the nail lacquering set. The ring, Kip was relieved to notice, was the most subdued of all the jewellery Conju had found. It was a simple coppery band, with a stripe of crimson enamel the whole way around. It fit perfectly on his middle finger.

“On the left hand please, we don't want to disturb your writing,” said Lord Conju, and Kip dutifully moved it to the other hand. “You can sleep in it to get used to the feeling. Tomorrow, wear that and the lip balm. And remember to pay attention more!”

“Yes, Lord Conju,” said Kip. He hoped it would make a difference. He could not get the image of wooden eating sticks being thrown into a ritual fire out of his mind.

---

It was hard to go to sleep when you were hoping for a knock at the door.

---

Kip woke before the dawn, and finished the morning reports. He ate sparingly of the fried rice the kitchen staff gave him for breakfast, but requested and received a second cup of coffee.

The adjusted uniform felt different. It was certainly closer fitting over his chest, and he thought it flared more around his knees. He practiced the movements Conju had taught him again, and found they did indeed work better with the adjustments to his clothes.

At quarter to the hour, he arrived at the study doors, and let himself in. He tried to focus on making the movements flow naturally, and not on the Presence that had turned his full regard on him.

Lift, swish, sink, wait, rise, smile… there was something of a smile in the Emperor's eyes. Kip held onto that, as he dropped his eyes back to the Glorious One's hands.

“Good morning, Sayo Mdang,” said the Emperor in a warm voice.

Kip felt his heart sing, and replied, “Good morning, Glorious One.” He dared to break Conju's careful instructions and took another quick look at the Emperor's face. His eyes were still smiling!

It would not do to stand here blushing. Kip continued; pull out the chair, smooth his robes, sit. He arranged his tools, and looked up again at the Emperor.

The pacing had begun. Kip held his brush ready to take dictation.

---

Lord Conju arrived promptly after lunch. Kip gave him a full debrief; the number of times he had made eye contact, how he thought the Emperor looked each time, anything Kip did that caused the Emperor to pause his pacing or look at him.

The elegant man did not interrupt, but smiled politely. “Good,” said Lord Conju, when Kip had finished, “Let me check your obeisance again.”

Kip dutifully demonstrated, including pulling out the desk chair and sitting down. Conju nodded, and made no corrections. This felt like another significant achievement.

“Now,” said Lord Conju, “polite conversation.”

Kip swallowed. He knew he tended to either get too involved if the topic interested him, or drift off.

“With the Emperor, you must never start the conversation, as you already know,” Conju looked at Kip, and Kip nodded.

“In some ways that is harder because you need to be able to converse on any topic the Glorious One might raise.”

So far the only topic the Emperor had raised was his training, which Kip had quite clearly failed to handle appropriately.

“Your goal, always, is to draw out the Emperor's opinions and preferences, to make him feel intelligent, witty, and appreciated. Let's try some examples. I will start in the place of his Radiancy.”

Kip nodded, and sat up straighter.

“Ithian has published a new book.” said Conju, giving Kip a piercing look.

Kip tried to remember if he had heard of the author before. “Have they? Has it been reviewed well?” he tried.

Lord Conju sighed. “Too informal, Sayo Mdang, but right approach if you don't know the author. Try instead 'I had not heard, Glorious One, is it well regarded?'”

They continued in this vein for some time. Music, philosophy, poetry, magic, literature, natural sciences, geography, history. Conju showed Kip how to reword opinions as questions, and when it was and wasn't appropriate to reveal Kip's life and experiences.

“With nearly any other noble, I would hesitate to say any more than you are from Zunidth, and your family is well. Since the Emperor specifically picked you as the Wide Seas Islander, you may talk about your home if specifically asked.”

“Yes, Lord Conju.”

“The portrait of the Wide Sea Islander in the Atlas of Imperial Peoples is very striking.”

“Thank you, Glorious One, I believe it is a portrait of my great-uncle as a young man. Have you… seen much of Zunidh? Made much study of the Atlas? Drat, what if the answer is no.”

“Is it really your great-uncle?” asked Lord Conju, slightly sceptically.

Kip shrugged. “He said it was, and it looks like him, and that's his *efela*, so I don't see how it could be anybody else.”

Conju nodded, slightly surprised. “Do you know if he met Prince Eritanyr? The paintings were done as part of his progress.”

Blushing furiously, Kip said, “Yes, he said he did, Lord Conju.”

“Fascinating!” said Lord Conju, ignoring Kip's blush, “He probably met my father as well then.”

Kip did not know what to say to that, so he said nothing. Baru Tovo had been both explicit and scathing about Emperor Eritanyr as a youth. It was not a comfortable thought that Lord Conju came from a family close to Eritanyr. He tried to get Lord Conju back on topic.

“What could I have asked? About what the Glorious One saw on his progress through Zunidh?”

“Yes, that would be acceptable. You could also ask broad questions about the Atlas, such as which other figures he likes.”

Kip nodded, and to his relief they moved on to other topics. By the end of the afternoon Kip's head was starting to feel fuzzy. It was a different kind of thinking than his work, it felt more like playing chess. Trying to plan ahead likely steps in the conversation.

Part of Kip wished it was possible to just have a normal conversation. To laugh, and tease, and tell long rambling anecdotes. He would have to save that for his letters to Basil. Letter to Basil. In his writing supplies were just enough stamps for one letter home.

Perhaps he could get some more when he was permitted to leave the Tower.

---

In the evening, Conju instructed Kip to work downstairs while the Emperor was at evening court. He prepared what he could for the morning reports, and reviewed the work the undersecretary had done.

Just after midnight, Kip heard the sound of the Emperor returning. He quickly packed away his things, and left the room. As he had hoped, he and the Emperor entered the main hallway at the same time. Kip dropped (lift, swish, down) into his obeisance. 

The Emperor waved him up, but did not pause. Kip rose, and looked into the golden eyes. They were so tired, so sad. Kip's polite smile faltered, and as he looked down to the Emperor's hands, he wondered what had happened at court that evening.

There was no gesture or request for Kip, so he returned quietly to his rooms. He wished he could do something to ease the Emperor's heart, but unless he was summoned, he could do little.

---

The next few days passed in much the same manner, with the only event of note being Lord Conju teaching Kip how to apply the blush. In the morning, the Emperor would greet Kip warmly. In the night, the Emperor would be sad and tired.

Perhaps it was not so surprising, thought Kip, that the Emperor had no desire for company when he was so clearly exhausted by court. Why should he take any time from his precious few hours of sleep?

Was there any other time that Kip could free up in the Emperor's day? There were so many ceremonies that required the presence of the Glorious One. He could do little about the rituals, or court events.

He *could* include potential draft replies with the morning reports. But would that be too forward? Would it seem like Kip wanted control? He didn't, really. Perhaps he could include one draft, for the most obvious case.

For the petitions from the nobles, Kip was already ensuring the Emperor had appropriate briefing notes. It wasn't exactly possible for Kip to preempt *them*. At least half the time they only wanted an audience as part of complex power games. They didn't want an answer, just wanted to tell their friends (or enemies) about their quarter-hour with the Sun-on-Earth.

Kip took notes when the government ministers came to meet with the Glorious One. Perhaps he could start by requesting their departments supply agendas. It might help reduce their tendency to waffle. Or parrot what had already been sent in reports.

It was unlikely Lord Igel would let Kip help dress or disrobe the Emperor. That was Lord Igel's domain, and in true court style Lord Igel's power was in how much time he spent in proximity to the Lord of Rising Stars. He would not want Kip, who already had a large part of every morning, taking any of that.

All Kip felt he could do was hope for an invitation from his Emperor. Perhaps if a meeting was cancelled, or a dinner guest was unwell.
