Kip's entire household walked with him down to the throne room. They had all disappeared, and come back dressed in their own elaborate court costumes. It made Kip feel less alone to have them all sparkling around him. Conju, to Kip's relief, walked beside him.

They saw other great nobles parading down similarly, surrounded by attendants and family. All making for the same entrance to the throne room. A wave of colour, glitter, and chatter. Part of Kip couldn't help wondering how many families could be fed for a year on the jewels on show tonight.

Kip's escort led him through the door, and into the throne room. It took a few steps before Kip really looked at his surroundings. Then he stopped, blinking in surprise. The throne room was truly magnificent, from the jewel covered floor, to the impossibly elegant columns, to the banner above the throne, and the ceiling. But it was all a stage, to show off the Emperor.

From the back, where Kip was, the Emperor should have been nothing more than a golden speck. But somehow, by magic or optical illusion, the lines and shapes of the room made him seem so much larger. And as if he was watching Kip specifically.

Kip assumed that was just a trick, and anyone standing anywhere in the room would feel the same. It probably served the Emperors well to have all their courtiers reminded that every move was observed. Especially in the room most designed and used to show the Emperor's power.

To the left side was a wind quartet, placed on a small stage. Even from this distance, where Kip could only assume the instruments rather than see them (except for the bassoon, which rather hid the player) he could hear the music. It was beautifully performed, if somewhat pedestrian. Kip tried to identify the piece or the composer, for when he wrote to Vinyë and his mother, but it had nothing distinctive. Possibly it would just be _school of the court of the Emperor Artorin_ rather than any named composer.

Hearing the oboe soar through its reflection of the main theme, Kip sighed. He missed his oboe dearly. It was one of those things where, until he'd had to send it back home, he hadn't realised how much he'd wanted to play. He had barely practiced these past few years, but somehow it was worse knowing he couldn't.

Turning from those painful thoughts, he cast his gaze through the audience. The room had little clusters of chairs scattered around, of course none with their back to the Emperor. Some people he vaguely recognised from their colours or bearing as government ministers.

Servants in livery slid between the groups offering glasses of chilled wine, and small bites of savouries. Kip noted that he would have to decline anything he might be offered by them, he would only partake of that served to the Emperor.

Conju and Rhodin carefully directed their group towards the front right-hand side, where the Master of Ceremonies stood with a pair of nervous teenagers behind her. Each teen had a serious looking adult beside them. As they walked over, Kip's escort peeled off until it was just him and Conju.

Conju bowed to the Master of Ceremonies, and Kip carefully did not. The Master of Ceremonies acknowledged Conju's bow, and then bowed to Kip. It was incredibly weird.

“Welcome, your highness. As you hopefully know, you will be the last to be presented today. We are just waiting for one more…” said the Master of Ceremonies, in a surprisingly weedy voice. She looked out over the crowd, not attempting to hide her annoyance with the missing person.

Kip and Conju left her to her worries, and stood at the end of the queue. Trying to appear more calm and comfortable than the teenagers, Kip admired the room. He could see Rhodin (who had decided to wear fuchsia) chatting with a knot of similarly loudly-dressed swordsmen. The others had faded into the mass of people.

Finally, a gangly teen was escorted over by a very annoyed elder, and their little group was complete. The Master of Ceremonies chivvied them into the correct order, and took a deep breath. Then she stepped up to the bottom of the stairs, and stamped her staff three times.

The music continued, the chatter continued. The general gaze of the audience shifted slightly to look at the Master of Ceremonies, but it was clear to Kip that this was a mundane part of the evening that nobody found particularly interesting.

Shaking a little, the first teenager stepped forward. The Master of Ceremonies announced their name and family, and they stepped up to the lower daïs. In the centre there they performed their obeisance. The Emperor gestured for them to rise, but remaining kneeling, they raised up their hands and recited their oath. It was audible clearly where Kip stood, by some magic or cleaver acoustic design.

“We accept,” intoned the Emperor.

The teenager performed another obeisance, and then walked carefully backwards off the lower daïs. The pattern repeated for the other two teenagers. After escaping the daïs and the direct gaze of the Emperor, each one was led off by their escort with congratulations.

Then it was Kip's turn.

“Cliopher Madon, Prince of the Wide Seas of Zunidh” announced the Master of Ceremonies, and Kip carefully didn't wince at the mangling of his name, or at the title. No family, no 'presented by'. Apparently a lot of the crowd were listening with one ear, because more of them turned their eyes to the front for Kip.

Trying to pretend nobody else was there, Kip carefully climbed the stairs. As he had practiced, he swept his robes around him, and dropped into the obeisance. Head just high enough that he could see the Emperor's hands through his lashes. At the signal to rise, Kip lifted himself to his knees.

The Emperor signed again, before Kip could do anything else. *Up*, and then quickly, *approach*. A wave of whispers ran through the crowd. This was not in any part of the script Kip had been sent or practiced.

Taking a deep breath, Kip did as he had been told. He stood, and walked forward to the base of the stairs to the upper daïs. The Emperor had still not dropped the _approach_ gesture, and so Kip continued slowly, waiting for the hand to drop. It did not come at the top of the stairs, or the distance Kip would usually perform his obeisance. 

Finally, as Kip stepped directly in front of the Emperor, where surely Kip was blocking the view of the Emperor for many in the room, the hand dropped. Kip immediately knelt, less than a hand-span between him and the Emperor's knees. Slightly uncertain, now that he had deviated from their careful plans, Kip lifted his hands into the position of supplication.

The Emperor, his Radiancy, Lord of Ten Thousand Titles, reached out his own hands. On the Throne of Glory, he took Kip's hands into his own. Kip's breath hitched, and the throne room fell silent.

Kip looked up into the Emperor's eyes. Joy and sorrow seemed to war there. Kip smiled back, tentatively. Then he recited his oath. 

At the end, the Emperor squeezed Kip's hands, their eyes still locked, and said “We accept.” Then he looked up to the silent audience, “We present to you, Our chosen First Concubine, Prince Cliopher Mdang.” Kip smiled widely at the correct pronunciation of his name.

His Radiancy dropped Kip's hands. Before he had a chance to stand and exit the daïs, his Radiancy signed for Kip to sit beside him. Almost shaking, Kip looked to the side and saw indeed there was a footstool beside the throne. Set so that it angled towards the throne, but also had a view of most of the room, it was cloth of gold over blackwood. Kip stood, and walked around, carefully facing the Emperor, and knelt on the stool.

Turning his eyes only, Kip saw a perspective of the throne room that hardly anyone ever saw. The swirling groups of courtiers, all visible from this position. Conju and the Master of Ceremonies, standing next to each other at the bottom of the stairs, looking uncertain as to what they should do next. Eventually they walked together back into the crowd.

“Well done, Cliopher, and thank you,” whispered his Radiancy, out of the corner of his mouth.

Kip very nearly fell off the stool, being so addressed. “Thank you, my lord,” he whispered back. Was this a conversation? Was he supposed to continue by asking a question? It seemed an impossible task to do with the entire Court watching them. 

Slightly panicking, he wondered if the rumours passed around about Eritanyr being sucked off by his concubines while sitting on the throne were true. Being watched by so many people, he couldn't imagine being asked to give a performance like that. But the Emperor's court robes were very voluminous. Perhaps it was possible to hide an entire concubine under there.

It did not seem like his Radiancy was likely to ask Kip to try. He sat perfectly still, only his eyes moving gently over the crowds. It was as if he was inhabiting one of the temple statues of the Sun. His Radiancy watched the court, Kip watched his lord, and the court watched the pair of them in fascination.

A small table appeared at Kip's elbow, with two glasses of wine, and a single plate with a small selection of savouries. “Would my lord like a drink?” he whispered, unsure if he should have simply passed a glass without asking.

“Yes, thank you,” said his Radiancy quietly, turning slightly to see the table, “And one of those cheese triangles, if you wouldn't mind, Cliopher.”

Kip handed his lord one of the identical glasses, unsure if they were both meant for his lord. Then he picked up one of the tiny flaky pastries. As he reached over, his lord bit it in half out of his hand.

“Do try it, Cliopher,” his Radiancy said warmly, after swallowing.

Nervously, keeping his eyes on his lord to watch for the slightest hint that this wasn't what he intended, Kip ate the second half of the pastry. It had some kind of goat cheese inside, with lemon to cut the fat. 

His lord took a sip of the wine. “Yes, a good pairing,” he said. Then he held the glass up to Kip's face. Dutifully, Kip took a sip. It seemed wildly inappropriate to be sharing a glass of wine with the Lord Emperor in the throne room, with everyone watching. And yet, the Emperor could not be wrong, so it must be fine. 

Kip tried very hard to be fine with it, and ignore what felt like a thousand angry glares. He fed his lord another pastry, eating the second half. The Emperor didn't say anything more, and so Kip did not attempt any further conversation. In silence, he fed them both, and his Radiancy managed the glass of wine.

Before they finished the pastries or the wine, a gong was struck. The Emperor rose, and everyone in the throne room turned to the throne, and dropped elegantly to their knees.

Kip went to slide off the stool to copy them, but to his incredible surprise (it seemed to be a night for them), his Radiancy reached out a hand towards him. Uncertain, Kip reached up his own, the one that had not been holding pastries. His Radiancy gripped it firmly, tugging slightly, and Kip was forced to stand with his lord.

His Radiancy led him to a side door, into the dinning room. Coming through this door, they were already on a high daïs. There was a table with two chairs, one throne facing the hall, and one lower around the corner of the table. Servants waited to pull them out. On the side opposite the throne there was a railing, and then a precipitous drop to the main floor.

Kip was very glad, in this space, for the taboos. They meant that he would never need to negotiate the complex hierarchy represented by the many ranks of tables. Even if he had not just been escorted into the room like the spouse of a great noble, his place here could only be at the Emperor's table.

They sat, Kip and his lord, and then the rest of the court filed in. Most of Kip's household moved towards the back, although Conju came towards the front. When Conju took a seat surprisingly close to him, Kip wondered if that was Conju's own rank, or if his role as Kip's companion had boosted him. It would probably not be polite to ask.

Dinner was much the same as it had been in the Imperial Apartments, except that Kip was being watched by several hundred people. Thankfully, his Radiancy did not seem to feel like conversation, so Kip could concentrate entirely on his table manners. He supposed that with practice this would become less of a harrowing ordeal. For tonight, all the food tasted like sand in his mouth, and felt like a lead weight in his stomach.

At the end, the Emperor stood, and Kip followed him back into the throne room. The chairs had been moved to the edges, and the wind quartet replaced with a chamber orchestra. Afraid that if he did not ask now, he would be unable to under the full glare of the court, Kip said, “Would you like to dance this evening, Glorious One?”

The Emperor turned to look at Kip in what on anyone else would have been called shock. Kip immediately regretted the question. He thought it probably not a good idea to go down on his knees not even halfway to the throne, and so only bowed his head. “Please forget my impertinent question, gracious lord,” he said, sure he was blushing bright enough to be seen by those few fast courtiers who had already entered the throne room.

His Radiancy whispered, “Perhaps another night, Cliopher,” not stopping his stately walk to the throne.

“Of course, whenever it would please your Radiancy,” Kip whispered back.

The Emperor resumed his seat on the throne, his posture perfect, his face serene. Kip knelt on the stool beside him, sure he was still blushing. He tried to keep his polite smile steady, his eyes demurely on his Radiancy's hands. 

As the orchestra struck up a gentle waltz to start the dancing, Kip fretted. He seemed doomed to be unable to offer his Radiancy anything he actually wanted. All he had been able to supply was poor conversation, little musical skill, almost no knowledge of Shaian literature.

Well, the only way to improve was to practice. Practice conversation skills with Conju. Try to remember the poets his Radiancy mentioned and read their works. It was part of his job, as much as taking dictation and condensing reports was. He should treat it with as much care and effort.

As for music, it seemed a little late in life to start learning a new instrument, but for his lord he would try. Perhaps there was a spinet or a lute somewhere in the Treasury he could learn to play? Where would he find a music teacher? Something else to ask Conju.

The dancing went on and on. Kip kept watch out of the corner of his eye, counting the dances he knew, and the ones he did not. If his lord decided to dance, Kip would also need a dancing teacher. Less than half the dancers were ones he was confident with.

“Are you quite well, Cliopher?” whispered his Radiancy.

Kip realised he must have been frowning or looking worried. He quickly resumed his polite smile, looking up at his lord to reassure him. “Thank you, my lord, for your consideration,” he whispered back, “I am felling perfectly well. I was only concerned because I have not known this dance to be done with this tune before.”

“It is a recent fashion,” replied his lord, “begun by some of the younger women who were bored with the usual dance. They also felt that not enough time was given to the more _lively_ dances, and they worked out how to fit them together.”

“What young person doesn't want more dances where they can show off their legs or be lifted into the air?” whispered Kip, before biting his lips shut. He was talking to his lord, in the throne room of the Palace of Stars. Not his cousins walking along the beach at home.

“Well, yes, quite,” replied his Radiancy. Kip was sure he saw just the smallest smile, the smallest twinkle in his lord's eye, before it was smoothed over.

Kip was sure he was blushing again, but he didn't care. Another smile! Kip stored it away in his heart with the others. A collection of pearls, treasures beyond price.
