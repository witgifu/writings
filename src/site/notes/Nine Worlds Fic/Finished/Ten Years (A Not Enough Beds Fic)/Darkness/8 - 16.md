The Emperor was awake and looking at him, when Kip woke. His stomach sinking, Kip scrambled inelegantly into the obeisance. The Imperial Bed was his workplace, he should not have been sleeping on duty. Even if he thought, he hoped, that the Glorious One had intended him to sleep.

A flick of the gesture to rise. Kip looked into the face of the Emperor, as he remembered his polite smile. The Glorious One looked better today, the night's rest had clearly been beneficial. Sill, he looked worried or sad. Kip's smile faltered slightly.

Then the curtains were pulled back, and the choir began to sing. Like a candle smothered, all emotion vanished from the Emperor's face. He slid out from the covers, and into the slipper's proffered by an attendant. Serene, majestic, he walked past the gawking nobles to join the priests.

The nobles, Kip noticed, were stealing glances at him. He put on his best polite smile, and kept his gaze fixed on the back of the Glorious One. He watched the Sun-on-Earth welcome the Sun, and tried not to think about what the audience saw.

Kip needed to finish adding anything from overnight into the morning reports. It would also be good to wash his face and change, his robes were incredibly rumpled from sleeping in them. And he felt more comfortable sitting in a chair with trews on.

The hymn finished, the priest-wizards bowed to the Emperor. The Glorious One turned to enter his dressing room, but then kept turning and looked at Kip, kneeling on the bed. Kip blushed, and bowed his head, feeling the heavy weight of the imperial regard. The Emperor made the sign for dismissal.

Kip performed his obeisance on the bed. By the time he looked up, the Emperor had left. He climbed down, retied his sandals, and went to check with the pages.

After depositing the morning reports in the breakfast room, Kip was glad to be able to retreat upstairs. All the tower staff had been giving him appraising looks, and Kip didn't enjoy the feeling.

As he washed his face and brushed his teeth, Kip wondered what in particular the staff had noticed. Was it that Kip had still been in the bed by morning? Was it disapproving? Did they think he somehow had caused his lord's problems? Were they jealous? He wished he had someone he could ask.

Clean, in a fresh uniform, without the slightly tickling anklet, Kip felt much better. He popped into the kitchen to grab something to eat. At his entry, all conversation stopped. There were just the sounds of cooking.

“If sir would like to wait in the small dining room, I will bring breakfast shortly,” said the head cook with a small bow.

“Of course, thank you,” said Kip automatically. Not wanting to prolong the suddenly awkward event, he left as directed.

He stood in the corner of the dinning room, wondering why the kitchen staff were suddenly so cold. Had he offended them? Were they disappointed that Kip hadn't convinced the Emperor to eat all the supper they had prepared? Nothing about his position or appointment had changed, he had not received extra responsibilities or any titles.

After only a few minutes, one of the attendants bustled in with a tray. Lady Enoié, who was often sent to serve the Emperor's guests. She, at least, gave him a small polite smile. Kip gave her a polite smile back, and a small bow.

She put the tray down, and gave a much deeper curtsy than any of the tower staff had ever given Kip before. He swallowed hard. Something *had* changed.

“Does sir have a preferred time for taking breakfast?” asked Lady Enoié.

Kip was briefly distracted by how _much_ food was on the tray she had put on the table. And then he was not entirely sure how to respond. Was being served breakfast going to be the new normal?

“Would quarter past eight be okay?” he asked. That was in a few minutes, and should give him enough time to be finished and ready in the official study ready to start work for the day.

“Of course, sir,” said Lady Enoié, and set out the breakfast on the table. Fresh bread and jam, scrambled eggs, fried mushrooms, grilled fish, steamed rice, barley porridge laced with cream and honey, beans in a tomato sauce, three different pastries, coffee, and tea.

Kip sat down, and started with the coffee. He didn't usually each much for breakfast. Back home it had often been whatever fruit had been handy. In the Tower he typically had a bit of leftovers or a small bowl of what was spare from the Glorious One's breakfast. He might have _one_ of the things laid out in front of him. Certainly not all of them.

When he had finished his coffee, Enoié refilled it. “If sir would prefer something else…?” she asked tentatively.

“Sorry, Lady Enoié,” said Kip, looking up at her, “I'm just a bit overwhelmed.”

“Of course sir,” she said, and gave him a little knowing smile.

Kip wondered what she thought she knew. He chose the bread, feeling that the sweet jam would probably help give him energy. He couldn't quite face the oily fish or the creamy porridge after a night of interrupted sleep.

The morning's work was much as usual. Kip took mental notes in any changes in the Emperor's behaviour. He seemed a little more tired than usual, but otherwise was focused on the tasks at hand.

At the end of the morning's work, the Emperor watched Kip sorting his notes. Usually, the Emperor would leave before Kip, and so Kip looked up to see if the Glorious One had any further instructions for him.

“When you are finished, Sayo Mdang, would you like to join us for lunch?” asked the Emperor. His countenance and voice were serene, but there was something anxious in his eyes.

“Of course, your Radiancy, I would be honoured,” said Kip, surprised by the invitation. This was not Lord Igel shoving him into the path of the Emperor, this was his own request. Kip blushed.

He rushed through the handover to the undersecretary, keen not to keep his Emperor waiting. Then he hurried back into the inner apartments. The guards were stationed outside the small dining room, and so Kip presented himself there.

Rising from his obeisance, Kip smiled politely at the Emperor, and then looked down to his hands. The anxiety was still there in the golden eyes. Kip took his seat at the table set for two.

The Glorious one offered no immediate conversation, and so Kip focused on making sure his table manners were immaculate. He suspected the Emperor had invited him for some reason, but he could not work out why. It might have something to do with the changed behaviour of the rest of the tower staff.

“I always see you in your uniform, Sayo Mdang,” said the Emperor, calmly.

This was far from any of the topics of conversation Kip had carefully practiced with Conju. He had a brief moment of panic, which he hoped his face did not betray. Then, credibly calmly, he replied, “I do not know much of fashion, Glorious One. You are always arrayed most splendidly, your Radiancy. If you have any suggestions I would be honoured to hear them.”

That, apparently, was the correct response. Shortly, Kip was wishing he had a pen and notebook to record everything. He was surely going to miss some important detail when he relayed this to Conju. Kip did his best to use the memory tricks he knew for memorisation, without the ability to write it down.

Finally, tentatively, the Emperor asked, “Sayo Mdang, what kind of wardrobe have you been provided with so far?”

“Lord Igel has kindly supplied me with uniforms and sleep clothes, Glorious One,” replied Kip, hoping this didn't sound like he thought Lord Igel had done anything wrong. He was sure the staff in the room would report back anything Kip said today. 

The Emperor said, “The ring is from before you came to the Palace?”

Kip looked at his left hand, and swallowed. He did not directly say 'no', that was not done. “The Cavalier Conju an Vilius, who you so graciously provided as my companion Glorious One, found it. He was able to acquire a few pieces of jewellery, including this ring, from storage. From candidates previously presented to your Radiancy.”

Kip looked up from the ring to the Emperor's eyes. There was something there, some uncertainty… 

Quickly, Kip added, “I apologise unreservedly, Glorious One, if that was in any way improper.”

Abruptly, the Emperor pushed back his chair and stood up. Nervous, Kip scrambled to stand as well, and then dropped into a deep obeisance. Not knowing what to say, he said nothing.

“We will inform Lord Igel that he is to provide the wardrobe, staff, and access to the treasury, that are due to your position, Cliopher. We will see you at dinner.”

In a swirl of Imperial Yellow robes, the Emperor stalked out of the room.

Kip stayed where he was, on the floor, for a few moments. Trying to get his racing heart under control, taking long deep breaths. He wasn't in trouble, and neither was Lord Conju. It seemed like Lord Igel was. Staff? Treasury? Dinner? Kip didn't understand.

Once he had himself under control, Kip rose. He tried to walk as calmly as he could past the staff that seemed to be lingering in the room. The polite smile he presented them was perhaps a bit more forced than usual. As if this was a perfectly normal end to a perfectly normal lunch.

Arriving in his rooms, Kip found Lord Conju already waiting. He closed the door behind himself, and then sank down to sit on the floor.

“Sayo Mdang, are you hurt? Ill?” asked Lord Conju, rushing over and looking worried.

“It… I don't… I'm fine… I just...” said Kip, unable to get his thoughts in order. They were whirling around him, tossing him like storm waves, tumbling him until he didn't know which way was up.

Lord Conju passed him a glass of water. “Why don't you start at the beginning,” he said gently, “What happened in the small drawing room?”

Between small sips of water, Kip attempted to recount the evening, the night, the morning, and then lunch. He was sure he had forgotten things, and a couple of times had to go back and add details. Lord Conju didn't interrupt him, for which Kip was thankful.

Finally, Kip got to the end of the lunch. “He asked where the ring was from, and I told him that you had found it for me,” said Kip, and a tiny crease appeared in Conju's brow.

“He didn't say anything for a bit, and I got nervous and tried to apologise in case I wasn't supposed to do that, and then he stood up, and then I was _sure_ I was in trouble. But the Emperor was mad at Lord Igel, not at me. He said he would make sure I got a wardrobe, staff, and access to the treasury. And then he said he would see me at dinner, so I guess I'm having dinner with him as well.”

Kip finished the rest of the water, and looked pleadingly at Conju. “What just happened, Lord Conju?”

Normally reserved, Lord Conju had a clear smile on his face. “Excellent work, Lord Cliopher, First Concubine of his Glorious Radiancy, the Lord Emperor Artorin.”

Unfortunately, this pronouncement induced a fit of coughing in Kip. “Please,” said Kip, trying to get himself back under control, “Please explain, Lord Conju?”

Conju took a deep breath. “The Emperor spent all evening with you, and then kept you in his bed all night, which was astonishing enough. Then he invites you to lunch, and dinner, _and_ is going to tell off his Groom of the Chamber and make him provide you with more...”

Seemingly half-way through a thought, Conju stopped, and stared off into the middle distance. “We need to get ahead of Lord Igel,” said Conju, which did not seem to Kip to be finishing his previous statement. “Or rather, I will. You, Lord Cliopher, will have a nap and a bath, in that order.”

Kip looked pleadingly at Lord Conju, who shook his head. “I'm sorry, my dear, I'll explain more when I get back,” he said, and then rushed out the door.

Pulling himself up with the help of a chair, Kip wasn't sure if he would be able to sleep. Or if he would be able to make it to the bed to sleep. He felt both exhausted and jittery, like he'd been trying to live of coffee for a few days. Stumbling slightly, he made his way to the bedroom.

Arms and legs didn't seem to work properly, and it took a strangely long amount of time to get his uniform off. But eventually, he managed to at least get down to his underwear. Changing into sleep clothes seemed like too much effort. He flopped onto the bed, and twisted the blanket over and around so it mostly covered him.

Some time later, Kip woke to the sound of Lord Conju's voice. It seemed to be irritated. Kip sat up, rubbing his eyes. He wasn't sure if he felt less tired for the nap, but at least his body had stopped buzzing.

“He is taking a nap,” came Lord Conju's voice from the outer room, “I can send for you later, or you may ask Lady Ylette, who already has his measurements.”

That sounded like a conversation that Kip should not make difficult by joining. He was inordinately glad he had Lord Conju's help to deal with whoever it was. Instead of returning to the main room, Kip stumbled into the bathroom.

The bath had already been filled, and his dressing gown was placed on a chair. Kip stripped off his clothes, and began to wash. Deciding prudence was probably warranted, he did the full ritual cleanse as well. Then he carefully lowered himself into the bath.

The water was slightly milky, and had a fragrance of oats. It wasn't bad, but it did make Kip feel a bit like he was one of his own array of breakfast dishes. He hummed to himself, as the hot water helped him to relax.

His Radiancy's pronouncement kept circling through his head. 

Kip didn't really mind wearing the uniform, but it had become clear over lunch that his lord _did_ care. Hopefully, Lord Conju would be able to help him dress to please the Emperor. Kip had passed on, he thought, most of the instructions his lord had given him. None of them seemed much like the imitation finery, and that had been Kip's main worry.

The promise of staff was more confusing. Did that mean more undersecretaries? It might be useful to have someone dedicated to research. Someone who didn't have to send the pages down to the archives with detailed instructions, but could just go down themselves. Maybe someone with some more specialised skills, Kip was reasonably good at finance, but he was weaker on law.

While Kip was sure Lord Conju was thrilled about _access to the Treasury_, Kip was not. He hoped he was not going to be forced into the quantity of jewels the Emperor typically wore. It looked wonderful on his lord, sparkling in his glow, twinkling as he moved. Kip was sure _he_ would look like a child who had gotten access to the theatre costume room.

By the third time around the same set of thoughts, Kip decided it was probably best to get out before he started wrinkling too much. He towelled himself dry, applied the various lotions Lord Conju had insisted on, and then wrapped himself in the dressing gown.

Kip peeked past the edge of the bedroom door, to see if anyone was still in the main room. Lord Conju was doing embroidery, deep in a conversation with Lady Ylette, who was sewing a seam so fast that her fingers seemed to be a blur. Conju looked up, and saw the bedroom door had opened.

“You can come out now, Lord Cliopher,” said Lord Conju, “It will be a little while before Lady Ylette has anything for you to try on.”

Sighing, Kip opened the door properly, and stepped out. He bowed to them both, and then moved over to his desk. If he had some time, it would be good to get a bit of work done.

Lord Conju sighed behind him. Kip turned around, and said, “I'm sorry, Lord Conju?”

“You don't bow to the Tower staff,” said Conju, threading a new colour onto his needle.

“Not even to you, Lord Conju? Or Lord Igel?” asked Kip, confused by this sudden change.

Conju raised one eyebrow. “No,” he said firmly, “nobody below the rank of Imperial Duke.”

Lady Ylette hummed uncertainly.

“Alright, Lady Ylette. We should wait for the formal announcement — which I assume you'll draft Lord Cliopher, so do let me know what it says — so nobody without a title in their own right at least.” Conju looked a little smug.

This was one of those complex rules of Astandalan etiquette. Kip had thought, as a commoner, that he'd never have to worry that much about the various degrees of nobility. They were all above him, all he had to do was make sure he got the forms of address correct.

Kip pursed his lips. “Is that anyone in the Tower?” he asked, and pulled out his chair.

“In theory,” said Lady Ylette, “The Master of the Horse, and the Huntsman both hold titles in their own right. But since the Emperor hasn't ridden a horse since Aurelius Magnus was stolen by the Sun and the Moon, those positions aren't really functional.”

“Of course, his Radiancy might invite anyone to dinner tonight,” said Conju.

Kip took a deep breath, with the feeling that Lord Conju was possibly winding him up. “Well, if I bow to someone I shouldn't, hopefully they'll just think I'm being careful rather than stupid.”

Lady Ylette laughed. Kip smiled a little, and then applied himself to the work before him.
