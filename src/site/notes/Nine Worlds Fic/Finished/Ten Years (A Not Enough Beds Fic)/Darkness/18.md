Kip was seated next to his Radiancy, facing the scrutiny of six Imperial dukes, five Imperial duchesses, three elderly Princesses, and their spouses. 

He missed the anonymizing distance of the court dining room. In the formal dining room of the Imperial Apartments, the space between the Emperor's table and everyone else was as small as possible to allow for conversation. Which meant conversation was to be directed by the Emperor. For all twenty-five guests, and Kip.

Once Rhodin had found out the guest list, Conju and Kip had practiced how the flow of conversation was supposed to go. According to the strictest rules of etiquette, ones contribution should be in proportion to one's rank. Which meant that Kip wasn't supposed to just stare at his plate.

To Kip's relief, nobody else present seemed overly concerned with this strict interpretation. He had nodded along to an extensive monologue from an elderly princess on the health benefits of milk of magnesia, without needing to add any comment. Then one of the dukes decided to give a detailed accounting of his son's recent marriage, which a number of the others had attended.

The Emperor and Kip continued to share any food that wasn't served from a communal dish. Kip tried to discern if any of them had an unexpected flavour, but given that most of them were very strongly spiced, if not spicy, it was hard to know.

They were all dressed in full court, glittering in the sparkling lights. Kip in a midnight blue and light red confection. His Radiancy as always in black, white, yellow, and gold. The others combined seemed to be in every other colour in existence. Tiaras and coronets glinting.

In the end, the only thing Kip said the entire evening was at the very end.

The Emperor stood, and perforce so did everyone else in the room, chatter falling silent. He nodded to the guests, and then turned to Kip. “Shall we, Cliopher?”

“Of course, Glorious One,” said Kip, automatically.

His Radiancy led Kip out of the dinning room, his guards in front and behind. His Radiancy reached back a hand to Kip, who was following a step behind and to the left. Uncertain, Kip reached forward and loosely rested his in his lord's. 

His Radiancy clasped firmly, pulling Kip forward and beside him. Aware that they were being followed by all the dinner guests, Kip slid decorously up to walk alongside his lord. Nervous, he looked up into the golden eyes. The pupils were normal, and there was no sweat visible. He appeared to be aware of himself.

That was a distinct relief. They soon arrived in the antechamber where the Emperor's palanquin waited. Kip expected the Emperor to drop his hand before sitting inside. He did not. Instead, he pulled Kip's hand closer, tugging Kip against the side of the conveyance.

Taking a deep breath, Kip poked his head between the curtains. “Would the Glorious One desire I walk alongside, or join his illustrious self?”

His Radiancy did not answer directly, but he did not release Kip's hand. Glad that for once the Emperor seemed willing to indicate, if not say, a preference, Kip followed his lord. He moved to sit opposite his Radiancy, but there was no way to do so without his lord releasing their hands. So he knelt on the floor, between his lord's knees. Doing his best not to crush his lord's robes.

Nervous at this new desire for closeness, he whispered very quietly, “Are you well, my lord? I did not detect any drugs, but...”

There was a tiny jerk as the palanquin was raised, and began making its way out of apartments. It could have been carried by magic, but that was not as impressive as eight muscled and oiled imperial guards in full panoply. And so anything they said louder than a tiny whisper could be overheard.

His Radiancy just gave Kip a small, polite smile, and stared out the curtains. They paraded down to the Throne Room, this statue of an Emperor, and his adoring concubine. Everyone they approached gave way and sunk into the obeisance. Murmurs followed them.

Kip's hand was held firm the entire way. His Radiancy did not release it when they arrived in the side room that existed just for the Emperor to leave the conveyance. Nor when Kip slid out backwards, or when he himself stepped out.

Since his court introduction, Kip had attended every evening. Always escorted by Conju, and sometimes also by Rhodin. But he had entered through the doors he had used the first time, and spent his hours being introduced to various people Conju thought it would be useful to know. It had been exhausting, and Kip had remembered with longing the quiet of sitting next to his Radiancy and watching instead.

It seemed that tonight, his Radiancy was determined to have Kip's company. He led Kip through the door that was only used by the Emperor, across the dais, to the throne. Even when he sat, his lord kept hold of Kip's hand. Kip was forced to kneel on the provided footstool, while somewhat awkwardly crushing his robes under his knees. He hoped Conju and Ylette would understand.

“Thank you, my lord, for the honour you are paying your servant,” said Kip quietly, with a bowed head.

“I'm afraid it is entirely selfish, Cliopher. I… well, you will soon see,” said his Radiancy cryptically.

Just as on his first evening, Kip could see a small group of young people assembling at the edge of the lower dais. It wasn't long before the Master of Ceremonies stepped forward and began the introductions. When there was only one left, the Emperor's hand clenched around his.

The young person walked up to the stairs carefully. Kip wasn't sure if they were particularly young, or just short. They had dark skin, as dark as the Emperor. Kip's question as to if they were related was answered by the announcement.

“The Lady Lanzuia Yra, presented by her mother, the Grand Duchess Anazuia Yra,” intoned the Master of Ceremonies.

As she approached, Kip could see Lanzuia wore an Imperial Yellow trim on her robes. Which meant they had to have asked for his Radiancy's permission for her to wear it. She performed her obeisance and oath perfectly. Kip though she was perhaps nine or ten, awfully young to be presented at court.

Suddenly awareness came crashing down on Kip. He fought hard to maintain his polite smile. This child was being prepared, groomed, to marry the Emperor. The moment she came of age. He felt sick.

The Emperor's hand did not relax. But he said, “We accept, cousin,” with his usual even tone.

The girl stepped away, and the woman who had escorted her, presumably her mother, wrapped an arm around her. She was absorbed into a group of very senior nobles, many of whom had been at dinner.

His Radiancy stared out at the back of the hall, face serene. This close, however, Kip could see the smallest wrinkle between his eyebrows, and the way his eyes were shadowed.

Kip wondered if the girl's training would be like his. She would not need to be purified for many years. He hoped, desperately, that she had something of a normal childhood. She was only expected to bear heirs, her position was not directly dependent on any other service. Nor would she be expected to raise them. Or perhaps even be allowed to raise them.

When the dancing began in earnest, his Radiancy finally gentled his grip on Kip's hand. He took a long slow breath in and out.

Kip noticed that the small table had returned, with some sweetmeats and chilled wine. “My lord?” whispered Kip, “Would you like a drink?” He held up one of the glasses.

His Radiancy said nothing, but inclined his head. Kip passed the glass over. His lord sipped from it, and then passed it back. Kip took a sip as well, under the gaze of his lord, awkwardly holding the glass in his off-hand.

Kip wished he could tell his Radiancy he would not have to marry his cousin. That he would not be expected to get children on someone half his age. Even if he did, his lord knew better than anyone it would be a lie. He had already caused confusion and distress for refusing to marry his *sister*.

Instead, Kip squeezed the Emperor's hand back, and proffered the wine again. His lord looked at him with those sad eyes, but did not take the wine.

His Radiancy ended court unusually early. Kip knew why, his Radiancy had not released Kip's hand the entire evening. He could only hope for the girl's sake that nobody would blame her, or consider it an insult. Perhaps he could ask Conju and Rhodin to put around the word that the Emperor had done so in deference to her tender age.

Kip was led by the Emperor out of the Throne Room, and back into the palanquin. The entire slow ride back up, Kip watched his lord, rubbing small circles on the back of his hand with his thumb. He wished he dared offer more physical comfort. A hug, a stroke along his lord's too-thin arms.

Even in the dressing room, his Radiancy did not release Kip's hand until it became impossible to remove one of the layers of court robes without doing so. It was not yet midnight by the time Kip and the attendants had his Radiancy prepared for bed.

Kip went to lead his lord to the bed, but he did not move. “Will you wear all of that to bed, Cliopher?” he asked.

“No, Glorious One,” said Kip. He added a small bow of apology, unsure if his lord intended the question as censure or a joke. With the help of his own and his lord's attendants, Kip divested himself of the many layers and gems.

Kip had been taught how to undress provocatively. He did not think after anxiously considering his child bride, his lord would be likely to want the intimacy he had so far rejected. Yet, why had he not gone ahead of Kip to bed? 

Trying to balance the two, he allowed the attendants to completely strip him, before cleaning off the cosmetics. Lord Hari's hand hovered over what Kip recognised as an assignation outfit, looking at Kip. Kip flicked his hand sideways, trying to indicate to pick something else. They put him in a normal sleep tunic.

As soon as Kip was dressed, his Radiancy reached out his hand again. Kip took it, and squeezed gently. He followed his lord across the bedroom, and into the bed.

“I hope that was not too dull, Cliopher. I could not face it alone...” said his Radiancy, his face serene, but his hand still clutching Cliopher's.

“I find the chatter at court somewhat stressful, my lord,” said Cliopher, honestly, “I would be pleased to be by your side any time.”

“You may regret saying that, Cliopher,” said his Radiancy, slightly wryly. “I assure you, after ten years of watching, a little chatter would be welcome.”

Kip blushed at the implied criticism. “My sincere apologies, my lord, I am still uncertain when conversation is expected.”

A fleeting look of distress crossed his lord's face. “I meant no censure, Cliopher. Only an idle comment that court can be very repetitive.”

“Oh,” said Kip, awkwardly, “Of course, my lord.” Since he had already put his foot in it, he thought he may as well continue. 

“I wanted to tell you, my lord, that I believe Lord Igel and the head chef are planning to attempt to drug you again, either with a sedative or the erotic stimulant. I do not yet know why they are trying to do this, or when exactly they will try again. None of the rest of your household appears to be involved, my lord.”

He took a breath to look up and try to judge his Radiancy's reaction. His lord let out a slow breath.

“I believe they are trying to find something I dislike, to ensure I do not eat any. If they cannot find anything, I worry they will resort to drugging us both. I would rather know what the food is, and the drug. Then I can let my lord know what to avoid.”

Kip squeezed his lord's hand. “If there is any food you would like to avoid anyway, it is likely that strongly flavoured sweets would be easiest. I will try to get the word of *my* dislike passed on.”

“Marzipan?” suggested his Radiancy, softly.

“The little marzipan fruits, my lord? Perfect. Those are so cruel,” said Kip, remembering the first time he had tried one of the cherries, only to get the rose-scented almond paste stuck in his teeth. They didn't even have the dignity to be fruit flavoured.

“Exactly,” said his Radiancy, with a small smile.

Kip bowed from where he was kneeling on the covers. “I will try to tell you as soon as I know anything more. I...” Kip swallowed hard, “It may be needful for you to insist I eat some, my lord, to allay suspicions that either of us know.”

“I cannot ask you to take an unknown drug intended for me,” hissed his Radiancy.

“Please, my lord,” said Kip, “it will not hurt me, I have some ability to resist the stimulant. You can send me away, and my household will manage it. Just until we know Lord Igel's purpose, my lord, please. Let me do what I can to protect you. You have done so much to protect me, my lord.”

His Radiancy closed his eyes. One tear leaked out and down the side of his face. He nodded once, a tiny movement.

“Lord Igel has only a few staff loyal to him, my lord,” said Kip, trying to offer some reassurance, “From the priest-wizard who handles daily purification, to the bath attendants, most everyone is devoted entirely to yourself, Glorious One.”

Kip waited and watched. Did his Radiancy have any questions? Any hopes for what else he might learn? His lord lay there, staring up at the canopy. Kip wished dearly he could offer any further consolidation.

He squeezed his lord's hand again. “Please, my lord, may I help you to sleep? It has been a stressful night.”

The Emperor released Kip's hand, and he shuffled up the bed. As he rubbed his lord's scalp with gentle, circular motions, Kip asked, “If my lord has nightmares tonight, would you want me to hold your hand?”

“Please,” his Radiancy whispered back.

“Of course, my lord,” said Kip, and began softly humming the Lays.
