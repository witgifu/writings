Taking a deep breath, Kip asked, “Would you like to retire for the evening, Glorious One? Or would you be pleased to have me read to you again?” He immediately regretted the second option, he should have offered to withdraw, to send for a doctor.

“Yes,” said his Radiancy, without further clarification.

Kip struggled to parse what he should do. Both? “Shall I fetch the book I was reading before, Glorious One?”

“No,” said the Emperor, and Kip hoped that his lord meant there was some other book in his bedroom he would prefer. Perhaps the book of the Lays he had given his lord was still there. 

His Radiancy stood, and a beat behind him so did Kip. Following behind, and to the left. The dinning room doors opened, and there was Conju, standing to the side. Kip had to hold back a gasp of relief.

Kip turned to Conju, as he walked through the doors. The best way to make this seem not at all suspicious, was to act like he was collecting something he had prepared for his Radiancy. “Lord Igel is absent,” said Kip quietly to Conju. They both pretended he had said nothing, and Conju passed the beautiful box of sweets to Kip.

It was rock crystal, with a gold rim and hinge, enamelled with a geometric design in black and white. It fit snuggly in the palm of his hand, and was full of golden sweets. Kip carried it in front of himself, as if he had been told to take a gift for his Radiancy. Two quick steps, and he had caught up with his lord.

Still, the hallway was full of eyes. He didn't dare eat any.

Into the bedroom, across the wide floor. Yes, there was the book on the side table. Kip picked it up with his other hand. 

His Radiancy climbed into the bed. Kip followed behind, placing the sweets and book beside him. The attendant closed the curtains, and their footsteps crossed the room.

Kip opened the sweets, and proffered them to his lord. Eyes betraying confusion, his Radiancy took one. Kip took three, and swallowed them whole. Then he looked again, and took a fourth, chewing it.

They were *intensely* grapefruit flavoured. Kip wondered where they had been found, and who usually bought them. Someone who had almost no ability to taste anymore, perhaps. They were bitter, sweet, and acidic, all at once. He hoped he wouldn't throw up. He hadn't considered purging the drug, and he wasn't sure if it would work.

The confection was proving a challenge to the Emperor's serenity. His eyes were tightly shut, and twitching.

“My apologies, my lord, I did not know this antidote would be so… strong,” whispered Kip, weakly.

His Radiancy swallowed, “Antidote?” he asked, softly.

“It won't entirely prevent the effects, my lord, but it should help to make them manageable,” answered Kip, looking back at the jar. “I should probably have some more.” He sighed, picked out another two, and chewed them as quickly as possible.

His Radiancy looked at the jar, and also selected a few more.

“Shall I read to you, my lord?” said Kip, when he had swallowed the sweets, “Or, I suppose, recite? I could sing them, but I'm afraid my voice is not very good.”

“Cliopher...” said his Radiancy, his voice gentle.

When he added nothing more, Kip's anxiety returned. “... Yes, my lord?” he asked.

“Aren't you afraid?” asked his Radiancy, in a voice so quiet, Kip might have thought he had been imagining it, if he hadn't seen his lord's lips move.

“That I might hurt you? Yes, my lord,” said Kip, blushing furiously. “I am also afraid that… that if… if I am not here. And you are effected my lord, that… you might forget...”

His Radiancy's usually serene countenance fell away, to a look of pure terror.

Kip fell on his face on the sheets. “I do not believe you would ever knowingly or intentionally harm any of your household, most kind and sweet lord. Only that I have been prepared for this privilege, and a tool should be put to its right use.”

“A tool,” echoed his Radiancy, dully.

“A poor choice of metaphor, my lord,” said Kip, “I am sure you received a much smaller dose this time. If you would prefer, I will leave the sweets with you and depart. I am surely a greater risk to you, my lord.”

 His Radiancy whispered back, “Please sit up, Cliopher.”

His lord's wish was his command, so Kip moved back to kneeling. As he did so, he could feel the drug starting to take effect. His heart rate was rising.

His Radiancy said nothing, watching him. Had he missed a cue, a gesture? It felt like he had been asked a question by a teacher when he hadn't been listening. Finally, he gave in and asked, “What can I do for you, my lord?”

Eyes flicking to the book, his Radiancy said softly, “Would you tell me of the Wide Seas?”

Kip smiled, and began reciting the Lays.

When the next quarter bell rang, Kip tried to subtly rearrange his robes to try to hide his erection. The sweets seemed to have stopped the 'inhibition release' part of the drug, but not the physical arousal. It seemed his Radiancy was faring better, he wasn't sweating noticeably, and his pupils looked normal.

At the end of the section, his lord raised a finger very slightly, and Kip did not continue. “Were these originally in another language? Only the rhythm...” said his Radiancy.

“Yes, my lord,” answered Kip, surprised, “They were originally written in the language of the Wide Seas Islanders. These days it is not so much used in Gorjo city, and the Lays may be better known in Shaian.”

His Radiancy nodded, “Whoever translated them did a fine job, it is a difficult task to translate anything with a rhythm.”

Both of them looked up suddenly, when the bedroom door clicked open. What could have had happened, that needed the Emperor's immediate attention? A natural disaster, or a critical military loss?

Footsteps shuffled over to the bed. Kip took a deep, slow breath. He watched the tiny glimmer of openness fall from his Radiancy's face, as the Serenity returned. Nobody opened the curtains, but there was the sound of fabric against the floor as someone dropped into the obeisance.

“Glorious One,” they finally spoke, and to Kip's shock he realised it was Conju, “Please forgive the disturbance, but we thought it best to tell your Serenity immediately. Lord Igel has been found dead in his apartments. The Guard are investigating, your Radiancy, but it appears at the moment to have been an accident.”

“An accident?” repeated his Radiancy, sounding no more curious or concerned that if Conju were reporting on the weather.

“Yes, Glorious One,” said Conju, and Kip admired how calm he seemed, “It appears he mistakenly took far too much of a sleeping draught.”

“I will see the Commander of the Guard tomorrow then,” said his Radiancy, “I assume word has been sent to his family? What else needs to be done tonight?”

“That is all, Glorious One, please be assured we have everything in hand,” said Conju.

“Good night then, Cavalier,” said his Radiancy. There was the sound of Conju rising, and then silence until the door snicked shut.

Kip desperately wanted to go out and ask Conju what was happening. But that would be wildly inappropriate. His place was by his Radiancy's side. 

His lord had just lost one of the closest people in his life. True, that person had been drugging his lord for his own convenience, and without anything like consent. Still, his Radiancy had known Lord Igel far longer than Kip, or even most other people in the Tower.

Automatically, Kip reached out to place a reassuring hand on his lord's shoulder. He stopped himself, a finger width away, and dropped his hand slowly to the sheet. Instead, he offered a look of sympathy.

“My first Groom of the Chamber committed suicide when he heard of the writ of arrest for treason,” whispered his Radiancy, staring at his own hands. “My second lost a duel when he was drunk. I didn't like or trust Lord Igel, but he was sometimes competent.”

He looked up at Kip, and there were unshed tears in his eyes.

“You deserved better, my lord,” said Kip, softly, “You deserve better now.”

“Do I?” whispered his Radiancy, and a single tear sparkled down his cheek.

“Oh, my lord, yes,” said Kip, and reached out his hand to put it as close to his lord's as he could without touching. “The Tower staff would do anything they can for you. *I* would do anything for you.”

His Radiancy took a deep breath, and sighed, slightly shakily. “Did you note they sent Lord Conju in to tell me? Are they so afraid? When have I given them cause?”

“Lord Conju may have been the one to decide to tell you now, my lord,” said Kip, timidly, “The others may have assumed your Radiancy would not want to be… interrupted.”

“Don't disturb the god while he rapes his slave,” said his Radiancy, bitterly.

Kip took a deep breath, and slid his hand, so the side touched his lord's. “Perhaps, my lord, they thought not to disturb a good man, who works long hours every day, on the rare evening he can relax.”

His Radiancy didn't say anything, but he did wriggle his hand under Kip's. Stroking the soft, beautiful fingers, Kip offered his lord a small smile.

“Would you like a massage, my lord?” Kip offered.

His Radiancy gave a small nod, and Kip shuffled into position. But when Kip reached out his hands, his lord gasped once, and rolled over to burry his face in Kip's chest. Instinctively, Kip wrapped his arms around his lord.

Kip could feel his lord's shaking breathing, the sweat on his neck, the tension in all his muscles. Slowly, Kip squeezed him, and rubbed along his neck and head. Finally, finally, his Radiancy let himself cry.
