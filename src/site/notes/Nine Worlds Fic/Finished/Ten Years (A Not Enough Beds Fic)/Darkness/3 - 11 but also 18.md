After finally falling asleep, sometime after midnight, Kip was woken by a gentle but persistent tapping at his bedroom door. Blearily, he pulled himself out from under the blankets, and shuffled over to the door.

He opened it to find a very young woman, who bobbed a curtsy. “Sorry for waking you, Sayo Mdang, sir,” she said, anxiously, “Lord Igel said if we couldn't get his Radiancy to take his medicine we should fetch you.”

Kip grabbed his dressing gown from the chair where he had discarded it, and shoved his feet into his slippers. “I'll come,” he said gently, “…my lady? I'm afraid I'm still learning names.”

“I'm Ylette,” she said softly, as they walked together through his rooms and into the hall, “I'm a journeyman dressmaker; I often get the night shift. I was volunteered to find you.”

Kip wondered why the attendants had insisted that the dressmaker go and wake him up, rather than doing it themselves. Perhaps they thought it beneath them, or maybe they were afraid. They descended the stairs quickly, but carefully, and approached the Imperial Bedchamber.

The guards outside nodded to them, and opened one door to let them slip through.

Two attendants hovered by the bed, whispering to each other. Kip and Lady Ylette joined them. Kip peeked through the partially-open bed-curtains, and saw a glowing ball, surrounded by a mess of blankets and pillows.

“We've not been able to wake his Radiancy to take the medicine,” whispered one of the attendants. Kip wondered why they were whispering, if their goal had been to wake the Emperor up, but he didn't question them.

“Has the Glorious One been thrashing around? Or screaming?” asked Kip, softly. He seemed safe in his nest, even if it might make him a little stiff in the morning.

Then a low keen came from the bed. There were no words that Kip could discern, just a quiet note of pain, shifting higher and higher in pitch. When Kip thought it would turn into a scream, it faded away, turning into sobs.

“No, Sayo Mdang,” said the attendant, the sound having frozen the four of them, “just as you heard now. His Radiancy is clearly distressed.”

Kip looked between the three of them. None of them seemed prepared to order him to drug the Emperor as Lord Igel had. Was that just because they were junior night attendants, or because they had decided his rank had changed, and he was no longer able to be ordered around? He decided he would see how far he could push them.

“Since the Glorious One is in no immediate danger, I would like to see if I can calm his Radiancy instead,” he said, trying to put authority into his whisper. 

Then without waiting for anyone to tell him otherwise, Kip climbed up onto the giant bed, shedding his slippers as he went. He crawled over to the luminous ball. 

The Emperor was curled as tight as could be, his face bent into his chest, and his arms covering his head, his knees tucked up to his elbows. Rather than looking like a sleeping god, or even a sleeping man, he looked like a child expecting to be kicked. Kip's chest tightened in grief.

“Savelin, I'm here,” he whispered. The Emperor whimpered in response. Kip wondered what had happened to this man that his nightmares could trap him like this. He reached out, hand hovering over the Emperor's shoulder.

Carefully, slowly, he rested his hand onto the Emperor's arm. The Emperor twitched very slightly, but Kip continued stroking gently down the Emperor's arm.

Kip moved back closer, gently holding the Emperor's hand in both of his. “I will stay, my lord, if you would like,” he whispered. The Emperor reached up, out of Kip's grasp, onto his shoulder. Gently, he pushed at it.

Kip woke, he was not sure how long after. Not accustomed to sleeping in the same bed, much less in the arms of, someone else. He had woken when the Emperor had rolled onto his back, and released Kip.

Should he leave, now? He had not been dismissed, to wait in the bedroom or to his own bed. Should he go back to sleep? Should he get up to kneel before his lord?

He rolled over to check on the Emperor, and saw he was awake, staring up at the canopy. Quickly, Kip pulled himself up to the obeisance, with a view only of the Emperor's hands. The Emperor flicked the gesture to rise, and Kip rose to kneeling, keeping his head bowed.

“We had nightmares again,” whispered the Emperor.

“Yes, Glorious One,” said Kip, just as quietly.

“And they sent for you to give us the sedative.”

“Yes, Glorious One.”

“But you did not give it to us.”

“No, Glorious One. It did not seem right, when you showed no signs of injuring yourself, to force a medication on you. I thought to try calming your imperial self with a gentle massage instead.”

The Emperor stiffened, and Kip flinched. He tried to explain further, “After you had calmed down, Glorious One, I was preparing to leave. But you reached out to me, and so I stayed. You bid me lay down, and held me close.”

Nothing in the Emperor's posture or hands indicated he was placated by this explanation.

Kip was once again facing the Emperor he had wronged, with no understanding as to how. He fell into the full formal prostration, his nose brushing the pure white sheets. “I beg of you, forgive your humble servant for his ignorance, Glorious One. Please tell me how I have failed you, Merciful One, or dismiss me from your service, so I may not fail so again.”

The Emperor shuffled to sit up, and gestured for Kip to rise.

Once again, obedient to his lord, Kip rose to kneeling. The Emperor's hands were relaxed in his lap. By the glow of his skin, the stain on his nightgown was obvious.

“It is I who should be begging your forgiveness, Sayo Mdang,” said the Emperor. His voice was hardly audible, even as close as Kip was. “For assaulting you; for not protecting you from myself.”

Kip was shocked enough to forget his resolution not to look in the Emperor's face. The Emperor did not apologise to anyone. He was, by definition, always correct. But Kip read, in those golden eyes, such distress and shame that he had to look away again.

“Glorious One, you took no liberties you were not entitled to, and did nothing your servant did not encourage. I beg you not to blame yourself, Illustrious One, the fault is surely mine.” Had the Emperor not been aware of what he was doing? But he had not taken the medicine. Had he still been trapped in the dream? 

The Emperor let out a tiny gasp, almost a sob. Once again, Kip was drawn to look into his eyes. Tears pooled in them, but the Emperor was not looking at Kip, as if he could not bear to.

“Sayo Mdang, if I had found any of my lords had done to their secretary what I have done to you, I would not hesitate to have them prosecuted for rape.”

“That… that is not what… you did not… I encouraged you, Glorious One.” Kip did not know how to express what he was feeling. “If… if you were not awake, most merciful lord, then it is I who should be prosecuted for that crime.” He shuddered, in fear and horror at what he had done. Then added as an afterthought, “And for treason, of course, Glorious One.”

“To refuse the Emperor is also treason,” the Emperor said, “and since nobody could say no, we have been careful never to ask. So we send everyone who has been purified from our reach.”

“That is why you wanted to send me home, Glorious One?” asked Kip, and the Emperor nodded his head, in one sharp movement. 

“But I begged you to stay, and you gave me your word, Glorious One, you would release me if ever I asked,” said Kip.

The Emperor nodded again, more firmly this time, and it shook loose the tears in his eyes. They rolled unchecked down his face. Kip desperately wanted to wipe them away, but he was acutely aware that now might not be the right time.

“Then, Glorious One, while it might be treason for me to say _no_, I could always ask to be released instead.” This logic felt tidy and pleasing to Kip. He had chosen to stay, knowing all that the position was likely to entail. He did not begrudge the Emperor physical comfort, any more than he begrudged him receiving accurate and timely reports.

The Emperor looked at Kip, astonished, “You still do not want to be released?”

“No, Glorious One, unless it is to be charged for the crime of having assaulted you,” he bowed his head in shame again. “If you will not charge me so, then most merciful lord, please provide me with remedial instruction.”

“_Remedial instruction?_” hissed the Emperor, and twitched.

“I want to be able to make you happy, Glorious One,” Kip said quietly, “and instead twice I have hurt you.”

“You have not hurt me, Sayo Mdang. I am just trying to be a better emperor than my uncle was.”

Kip could not think of a single way in which the current Emperor was *not* better than the previous. Not, to be fair, that it was a particularly hard standard. Eritanyr was a zenith in the corruption, decadence, and cruelty of Astandalas.

“Do you not eat meat, Glorious One, because he at too much? Do you abstain from all wine, because he drank too much? If you do not *like* these things, of course you should not force yourself to partake, Illustrious One...”

“I once returned a dish untouched, and the chef committed suicide in shame.”

There was an awkward silence. 

Kip could not at first imagine why the chef would do such a thing. Court gossip could be extremely vicious. Perhaps this was the only way they saw of protecting their family? Sometimes court culture baffled Kip. But just because Kip would not consider such a thing, did not mean the Emperor's fears were unfounded.

Then he realised that might be exactly what the Emperor feared, and blanched. “Glorious One, I have no intention of following that example. Not now, not if you tell me not to touch you, not if you replace me as your secretary, not if you send me home. You have my word, Illustrious One.”

The Emperor nodded. “Thank you,” he breathed, and relaxed very slightly. “I will think about what you have said.”

Kip bowed again. Still, the Emperor did not dismiss him, so he stayed kneeling where he was. Should he ask about the sedative? Should he propose a new arrangement between them? The Emperor seemed reluctant to ask for anything.

“Would you like a scalp massage, Glorious One, to help you sleep? And that I leave after?”

“You don't have to do—” whispered the Emperor, and to his surprise and Kip's, Kip interrupted him. “I want to. But I only want to with your permission, Glorious One.”

The Emperor closed his eyes, and took a deep breath. Then he nodded slowly. Kip scooted up so he could reach the Emperor's head. He began with small, gentle circles at his temples.

When the Emperor finally looked relaxed, some quarter-bell later, Kip gently removed his hands. He shuffled back, to give himself space to do his obeisance. The Emperor gave him the signal to rise, rather than the signal for dismissal.

“Please do not wait up for us, Sayo Mdang,” the Emperor said, sleepily, “You also need to sleep.”

Kip bowed again, and then slid out of the bed feet-first. He ducked under the curtains, and returned upstairs to his own bed.
