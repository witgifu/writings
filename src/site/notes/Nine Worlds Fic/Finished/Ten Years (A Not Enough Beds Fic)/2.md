The next evening, you processed (there really wasn't a better word for it) out of the apartments and to the palanquin that would take you down to court. In the last antechamber, outside the official study, the Wide Sea Islander sat at the desk that had always previously been empty.

As you swept through the doors to the study, he rose from his chair and fell gracefully (if not naturally) into the appropriate obeisance. You were cheered to see him dressed in the uniform of the Secretariat, although the horrid colours did nothing for his complexion.

He rose at your signal, and although you walked on past, you thought you caught him looking at your face. You almost flinched, but passed on and out.

Behind the curtains of your conveyance, you let a little of your emotions loose, and twisted your hands together. Of course, he had presumably been fully purified. Your gaze would not blind him, and your touch would not burn him. After ten years of fear of your own body, had you grown accustomed?

Court was as dull as always, as you sat as a living idol. A reference point for your courtiers, a focus for their tittering, an artwork for their appreciation.

You returned, shortly before midnight. The Wide Sea Islander was still at his desk. Again, he rose and made the obeisance. This time after you made the gesture for him to rise, you stopped in front of him.

As he rose, the guard to your left said quietly, “Cliopher Sayo Mdang, my lord.”

You inclined your head slightly, acknowledging the favour. Sayo Mdang, from the Vangaveya-ve, wherever that was on Zunidh.

“Good evening, Sayo Mdang,” you said.

“Good evening, Glorious One,” said Sayo Mdang. This time, there was no uncertainty. He looked at you full in the face. As nobody had done in ten years.

He must have seen something in your eyes, where you had never needed to learn to hide your emotions. His face softened, and a small, sad smile crept into his eyes.

You waved a dismissal, and he sank back down as you strode past. Focusing all your attention on stopping yourself from shaking with reaction. He had only looked at you. He had known it was safe, you knew it was safe. You couldn't escape the feeling that he had looked at you and seen a man, rather than the Emperor.

It took you a long time to get to sleep. You had repeated nightmares that you woke from sweaty and shivering.

---

You had a meeting at the seventh bell the next morning. Thankfully it was close enough that you were permitted to walk the entire way. You swept through the official study and into the anti chamber, and there was Sayo Mdang again. Already at work? You wondered, and chose to ignore him for now.

It was true that you nearly always went to bed after midnight, and rose with the dawn. Most of your inner household kept similar hours. You did try to insist that nobody was required for both ends of the day, although it was hard when you knew their power and prestige was derived from the time they spent in close proximity to you.

You hoped that Sayo Mdang was just settling in, just finding a rhythm. He would hopefully find it wasn't needed to work quite so many hours.

After all, hadn't your secretaries managed on their own for years? Although maybe they had worked long hours in their own rooms.

You continued in these thoughts as you strode down the hallways, ringed by your guards.

He was there, still, when you returned. When you left again for evening court. When you returned to go to bed. When you left for a ceremony immediately after dawn. When you returned at the second bell of the morning after an excruciatingly extended night ritual.

After a week, at breakfast (for once without guests), you inquired of one of your guards. "Do you know what time Sayo Mdang usually finishes work?" You had spent time carefully crafting this question to neither accuse Sayo Mdang or your guards.

He saluted (of course), and answered without hesitation, “Sayo Mdang has been at his desk since he was appointed, my lord.”

The floor dropped out of your stomach. You froze completely, face carefully neutral, posture perfect. “Thank you,” you said automatically. You picked up your tea cup, and began turning in your hands, trying to calm your racing heart by breathing the steam slowly.

You should have known this would happen. You had carefully spent the morning afterwards dictating letters to confirm every other position. But you hadn't discussed Sayo Mdang with either your secretary or your household. So the only space granted to him, in a palace with over four hundred beds (you had seen the inventory once), was a desk in a room that was functionally a hallway.

Was there no end to the traps in your life? No end to the ways you could be casually, accidentally, cruel to those around you?

As always, wallowing in your guilt did nothing to fix the problem. You would need to carefully craft your request (your instructions, your orders, the Emperor could not be refused and so could not *ask*). If you did not, there was every chance you would find him in your own bed tonight, in a ludicrous interpretation of the secretarial robes.

“Call Lord Igel,” you said, and put down the teacup. You folded your hands in your lap, and counted to fifteen, before you heard hurried footsteps beyond the door. Lord Igel must have been eating his own breakfast.

The head of your private household was admitted, and performed the abbreviated genuflection that his elevated status permitted.

If there had been no chance for Lord Igel to take out his displeasure on anyone else, you might have let him squirm in silence for a while. Since you needed him on side, you left off the only punishment you ever allowed yourself (outside of the throne of judgement).

“Sayo Mdang needs to be outfitted as befits Our undersecretary, and provided with suitable accommodation.” 

All of your immediate staff were nobles, and had apartments appropriate to their rank. The servants (who you never saw, but you assumed that no lords were doing dishes) had rooms at the bottom of the Tower. How would Sayo Mdang rank? It would be best to be explicit.

“As a Fourth Degree Secretary, he is entitled to his own room, furnished, with ensuite. As a member of Our household he is permitted to make use of the Tower refectory or eat here. Please see to it.”

“Of course, my lord.” Lord Igel bowed and left. You appreciated that he didn't bother with obsequious apologies, even if as a result he seemed to have no personality. He was acceptably competent, at least when given explicit instructions.

Later that morning you watched as your secretary left, to see if you could catch a glimpse of Sayo Mdang. As the doors opened briefly, you caught a glimpse of red and black robes behind the desk. And your secretary dumping all of his notes unceremoniously onto Sayo Mdang's piles of paper.

Well, at least Sayo Mdang was not idle or being given make-work.
