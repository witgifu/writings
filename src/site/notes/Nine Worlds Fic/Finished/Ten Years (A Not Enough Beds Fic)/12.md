You reflected over the next few days that perhaps Lord Igel had gotten the message of your displeasure with his behaviour. He had seemingly paused his attempts to force you and Sayo Mdang to spend extra time together. It was not a relief as such, simply a return to familiar aches, pains, and boredom.

Sayo Mdang, on the other hand, was an incongruous delight. At least one report each morning came with annotations adding or clarifying information. When he took notes in meetings he would often take the chance to meet your eyes whenever someone said something particular foolish or obsequious. When you left the Apartments for afternoon or evening court, you would find him with the lordling undersecretary, elbows deep in boxes from the Imperial Archives.

You did remember to ask Lord Igel to organise the oboe for Sayo Mdang. It was always hard to tell with Lord Igel, but he did seem pleased with this request. Perhaps he thought it was some kind of 'reward for good service'. Well, perhaps it was, if not in the way Lord Igel was expecting.

Then, seven days after you had requested it, the report on your household finances appeared in the middle (how modest!) of your stack of morning reports. You read through it carefully, although it was clear that it had been thorough. 

You had expected Sayo Mdang to review the accounts and maybe talk to a few of the senior staff. He had seemingly talked to everyone who worked in the Tower. He had also looked up some historical comparisons and adjusted them for inflation. Remarkable.

There were four conclusions. The first, that your household were diligent and took their responsibilities seriously. That was a warming thought, that at least within the Tower were some who felt responsibility for you. Possibly as much as the more protective sort of museum curator.

The second was a minor point about ritual candles. Once you would have ascribed it to wild magic serendipity, this exact match between the example you gave and what Sayo Mdang found. This was probably just evidence that your example had been based on a memory or a good guess.

Finding that Lord Igel was lining his own (or his son's) pockets was not entirely surprising. You were honestly more surprised that he was the only one, and that he was limiting himself to the more gentler sort of nepotism. Previous staff had been found simply billing themselves for expenses, or outright theft. This knowledge would give you a bit of leverage with Lord Igel, which could be useful.

You stopped yourself, put down the report, and took a sip of tea. How this rotten edifice had infected you! When once we would have put scathing pen to paper, or set up a public humiliation to show him his error. Now you considered it *useful*. Political leverage. Not as bad.

You could imagine the looks Pali and Jullinar would give you. Horror, disgust. The Empire had trapped you physically and magically, and they were destroying what was left of me.

Abandoning your breakfast, you took the report into your private study. So I could have feelings with no-one to see. I didn't cry, or yell, but I did shred a piece of paper into fragments smaller than my golden pinky nail.

I would make sure Lord Igel faced consequences for his actions. I would not use it to try to blackmail him into giving me what I wanted. (As if he was capable; he was not, he could not even loosen the chains)

Once I had finished destroying the paper, I was able to resume reading the conclusions. Then I had to find a second piece of paper to turn into confetti.

Sayo Mdang reported on his own predicament with the same detachment he had reported on the candles. As if he was also an object to be pulled out from a cupboard, used, and disposed of when finished. Fed leftovers like a pet or a pauper at the gate. 

Everything in the report indicated that your household was maintaining his purifications exactly. Anything he ate, or drank, wore, or washed in, was purified. In case it came in contact with you. But *he* could touch others, as your clothes could be touched. As if Sayo Mdang was a blanket, an instrument, a pair of eating sticks.

How could he do this? How could *you* do this? As if the primary concern should be the *expense* of maintaining him, rather than his imprisonment and abuse at the whim of an idol to whom he could never say no.

After I had shredded another page, I left the report in the private study. You did not need it outside, and it had become a little crumpled. Additionally, it was the kind of document that was best not left lying around.

You dutifully returned to the breakfast table, and finished the rest of the reports. The breakfast you left untouched.

Perhaps Sayo Mdang truly did not understand the predicament he was in. What the court, what the whole world, would be expecting of him. Perhaps his friends in the Palace already rejected him. Had you isolated him as thoroughly as the throne and taboos had isolated you?

You went into the official study, in control of yourself. Sayo Mdang was waiting, as cheerful as ever. When you said 'good morning', you thought you managed not to dampen his spirits.

"We have read your report, Sayo Mdang, it was very thorough. Write a note to the appropriate person about the candles. We will handle the other conclusions with the appropriate parties."

You swept through the turn in your path. Your hands, twisting behind your back.

"Of course, Glorious One," replied Sayo Mdang. Then you moved on to the other reports. It was seemingly like any other day.

Before court that evening, you decided to confront Lord Igel. As you approached from the bath, you asked neutrally, "What does your son do in our household, Lord Igel?"

His court mask slipped momentarily, and you saw not fear but anger. That was interesting. "He works for me, Glorious One, maintaining your illustrious jewels," he said equally calmly.

"We don't recall seeing him," you said, handing off your towel onto the waiting stand.

"He works in the lower levels of the Tower, Glorious One," replied Lord Igel, following you into the dressing room.

"And yet, nobody seems to recall seeing him," you said, and walked over to the stands where he had set out your outfit. They were marginally adequate. On another day, when you had been tired rather than furious, you would have accepted them with minimal comments.

"None of these are acceptable," you said, waving at the robes. "This mantle has symbols from Colhélhé for the new moon, which is six days away. This over-robe is too tightly cut, and will wrinkle over that under-robe. The sandals were a part of what we wore in the anniversary celebrations and should not be used for at least another month."

You paused, and let the silence ring through the room. Nobody moved. Finally, you turned to face (but not to look at, never to look at) Lord Igel. "Find an excuse to send your son home, Lord Igel," you said.

Lord Igel made a first-degree bow of apology. "Thank you, Glorious One," he said quietly. You nodded, and gestured to him to rise. Then you rattled off a list of garments and jewels to the attendants, who scrambled to find them.

It was probably the best outfit you had worn since the anniversary. It included two of your favourite pieces of jewellery, and a diaphanous over-robe that reminded you of what the Hours wore in the House of the Sun. You had not been so specific in your instructions in years.

The rumours were going to run *rampant*.
