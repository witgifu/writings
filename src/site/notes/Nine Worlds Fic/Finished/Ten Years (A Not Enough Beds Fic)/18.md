The Emperor gave the gesture to rise, and Kip and Conju both stood again. “Good afternoon, Sayo Mdang, I did not realise you had a guest,” he said.

Kip blanched, but attempted to do the correct thing. “Glorious One, this is the Cavalier Conju an Vilius,” he said, bowing again, “he is the companion you so graciously appointed for me.”

The Emperor turned towards the other young man, who was carefully looking only at the Emperor's hands. Kip was reasonably sure he did _not_ need to introduce the Lord of Rising Stars to Lord Conju. So he waited patiently for the Emperor to make a request.

“And what is your impression of Sayo Mdang, Cavalier?” asked the Emperor.

Lord Conju did not flinch, but he did take a slightly drawn-out breath. “He is a capable man, Glorious One, who I hope will bring you much comfort, and be a reflection of your illustrious and regal self to your court, Glorious One,” then he made one of the court gestures that Kip did not yet know the meaning of. It seemed to be a variation on the one for 'thanks' or 'debt' that he had been taught.

“A very elegant and courtly answer, Cavalier, we see why Lord Igel recommended you,” said the Emperor, and turned back to Kip. “The court introduction has been set for ten days from now, Sayo Mdang. My costumer has been instructed, and Lord Igel has been asked to provide anything else the Cavalier an Vilius thinks you will require.”

Kip swallowed. Well, that answered the problem Lord Conju had identified with his wardrobe. Ten days seemed like not a very long amount of time to prepare. “Thank you, Glorious One, for your great generosity,” said Kip, bowing, but not daring to attempt any of the court gestures in case he got them wrong and said something stupid.

The Emperor nodded, and swept out, the guards closing the door behind him.

“He came just to tell me that?” asked Kip, incredulously.

Conju took a deep breath, and turned back to Kip. “Don't be ridiculous, *he* came to check how I was treating you,” he said, and then after a pause added, “Also, the Glorious One wanted to be sure you knew what he was giving you, he didn't trust Lord Igel to pass the message on.”

Kip blinked. “Oh,” he said softly.

“Does *he* do that often?” asked Conju, taking a chair without invitation.

“Do what?” said Kip, confused, “Turn up to my rooms? No, he's never done that before.”

Conju rubbed his forehead. “No, I mean…” he struggled to articulate, “The *presence*, I knew he glowed, but I've never felt the weight of his…. but then I've never been so close….”

Kip was amazed at the change in Conju, who had seemed so unflappable and in control. “Um, I'm not sure?” said Kip, “I have seen him overwhelm some petitioning lords. Maybe the purifications protect me a bit from whatever it is?”

Shuddering slightly, Lord Conju pulled himself together. He sat up straight, returning to his usual polite, neutral face. 

Kip sat on the other chair in the room. “So,” said Kip, “Was that the information you needed?”

“Yes,” said Lord Conju, “I wonder how far I dare push Lord Igel. At a minimum you should have a valet, but I'm not sure how much of a household you should have, given the Tower has plenty of staff… although so far…”

“I'm sorry, Lord Conju?” said Kip, trying to keep the irritation from his face, “I don't think I need a valet. Or any other staff.”

“Do you know how to care for silk velvet? Clean mud off suede boots? Tie an Alinorial cravat? Get out of a set of court robes without tearing them?” ask Conju, increasingly quietly.

“No? But surely I could just borrow someone to get me out of the court robes after the presentation. I can get myself in and out of my uniform just fine, thank you,” replied Kip, tersely.

Conju snorted, “You'll be attending court at least once a week, plus at least one other smaller dinner or party—”

“—Excuse me, what?” interjected Kip.

But Conju continued, ignoring him, “—and I imagine you'll need to spend an afternoon receiving people for tea; for which you will require at least one manservant.”

“I do have a job you know,” said Kip, furiously, “I can't just skive off to play cards and drink because that's where you'd rather be.”

Lord Conju paused, and did not answer immediately. Kip wondered if perhaps he'd gone too far and insulted him. It was true that Kip thought most nobles were useless, and did nothing more than indulged their own predilections. Perhaps it had been a little presumptive to tarnish Lord Conju with the same brush.

“Sorry,” said Kip, “That was rude, I shouldn't have said that. It just sounds like a very tiring schedule of having to perform, and I got scared.”

Lord Conju looked searchingly at Kip, who felt uncomfortable very quickly. “This *is* a part of your job, Sayo Mdang. You are the Glorious One's confidant. Once everyone knows that, they will want to court your good opinion so you might speak favourably of them to his Radiancy.”

Kip sighed, and rubbed his hands on his knees. “I don't want…” he said, but was unable to articulate the rest of his thought. He didn't want to be the Emperor's *favourite*. He didn't want to be courted by nobles, who would no doubt bad-mouth him behind his back. Furthermore, he didn't want any of this *fuss*.

“What *do* you want, Sayo Mdang?” asked Lord Conju.

“I want to serve the Emperor, of course,” said Kip, automatically, “and through his Radiancy, the lands, and people of the Empire.”

“And if that required meetings that ran late into the night, or committee luncheons, or organising focus groups?” added Lord Conju, looking serious.

Kip sighed. It was going to be a lot of work. He wished it was work he would be able to do in his uniform and at his desk. “Okay,” he said, “I give in. But what is the *absolute minimum* I could get away with?”

Lord Conju gave this some serious thought. “Two events per week, and the valet,” he said eventually.

Kip groaned.

“Look, most weeks one event will be evening court,” said Lord Conju, trying to mollify Kip, “You only have to put in an appearance there, it's more about being seen than doing anything in particular. Some of the other events will be those you attend with his Radiancy. The valet is, I'm afraid, non-negotiable. You need someone to advocate for you in the lower levels of the tower.”

“Advocate?” said Kip, echoing Conju but confused.

“You've never had servants before, Sayo Mdang?” asked Conju.

“No,” said Kip, “I can take care of myself. Wash myself and everything.”

“In a household of this size,” Conju said, and waved to indicate the Tower, “Personal servants are about status, but they're also able to organise things. Request particular foods, make sure clothes are cleaned for when they are next needed, order new things and make sure they are correct when delivered.”

Kip opened his mouth to protest, and closed it again.

Conju continued. “They are also important sources of information. Do you know who cleans your clothes?”

Kip shook his head. He knew no names, apart from Lord Igel, who had apparently decided it was beneath him to introduce Kip to anyone else.

“Do you know what they think about you? How they talk about you to the rest of the Palace staff?”

Kip bit his lip. “I overheard two of the attendants describe me as 'his Radiancy's pet' when they thought I couldn't hear,” he said softly. He'd felt awful after hearing that. He had been afraid it was true, but also knew that even if it wasn't how the Emperor saw Kip, a rumour like that would have legs.

“And that,” said Conju firmly, “Is the kind of talk we want to stop. You are a man of position and skills, not a tropical parrot.”

“I'm going to look like a parrot in court costume,” said Kip, glumly.

“Not if I have any say in it, you won't,” said Lord Conju.

Kip's face was a study in surprise and delight.

After finally falling asleep, sometime after midnight, Kip was woken by a gentle but persistent tapping at his bedroom door. Blearily, he pulled himself out from under the blankets, and shuffled over to the door.

He opened it to find a very young woman, who bobbed a curtsy. “Sorry for waking you, Sayo Mdang, sir,” she said, anxiously, “Lord Igel said if we couldn't get his Radiancy to take his medicine we should fetch you.”

Kip grabbed his dressing gown from the chair where he had discarded it, and shoved his feet into his slippers. “I'll come,” he said gently, "…my lady? I'm afraid I'm still learning names.”

“I'm Ylette,” she said softly, as they walked together through his rooms and into the hall, “I'm a journeyman dressmaker; I often get the night shift. I was volunteered to find you.”

Kip wondered why the attendants had insisted that the dressmaker go and wake him up, rather than doing it themselves. Perhaps they thought it beneath them, or maybe they were afraid. They descended the stairs quickly, but carefully, and approached the Imperial Bedchamber.

The guards outside nodded to them, and opened one door to let them slip through.

Two attendants hovered by the bed, whispering to each other. Kip and Lady Ylette joined them. Kip peeked through the partially-open bed-curtains, and saw a glowing ball, surrounded by a mess of blankets and pillows.

“We've not been able to wake his Radiancy to take the medicine,” whispered one of the attendants. Kip wondered why they were whispering, if their goal had been to wake the Emperor up, but he didn't question them.

“Has the Glorious One been thrashing around? Or screaming?” asked Kip, softly. He seemed safe in his nest, even if it might make him a little stiff in the morning.

Then a low keen came from the bed. There were no words that Kip could discern, just a quiet note of pain, shifting higher and higher in pitch. When Kip thought it would turn into a scream, it faded away, turning into sobs.

“No, Sayo Mdang,” said the attendant, the sound having frozen the four of them, “just as you heard now. His Radiancy is clearly distressed.”

Kip looked between the three of them. None of them seemed prepared to order him to drug the Emperor as Lord Igel had. Was that just because they were junior night attendants, or because they had decided his rank had changed, and he was no longer able to be ordered around. He decided he would see how far he could push them.

“Since the Glorious One is in no immediate danger, I would like to see if I can calm his Radiancy instead,” he said, trying to put authority into his whisper. 

Then without waiting for anyone to tell him otherwise, Kip climbed up onto the giant bed, shedding his slippers as he went. He crawled over to the luminous ball. 

The Emperor was curled as tight as could be, his face bent into his chest, and his arms covering his head, his knees tucked up to his elbows. Rather than looking like a sleeping god, or even a sleeping man, he looked like a child expecting to be kicked. Kip's chest tightened in grief.

“Savelin, I'm here,” he whispered. The Emperor whimpered in response. Kip wondered what had happened to this man that his nightmares could trap him like this. He reached out, hand hovering over the Emperor's shoulder.

Kip began to sing, softly. Just the same lullaby he had sung last time. Carefully, slowly, he rested his hand onto the Emperor's arm. The Emperor twitched very slightly, but Kip kept singing, stroking gently down the Emperor's arm.

A tiny muscle at a time, the Emperor relaxed. He hadn't whimpered or whined since Kip had started singing. Kip continued through his repertoire of Islander children's songs.

After maybe a bell, the Emperor had finally made it to a more normal sleeping position. Hands relaxed on the bed, next to his head. Legs stretched out a bit more, back no longer so curved. Kip stopped stroking, singing quieter and quieter, until he was only humming gently.

The Glorious One seemed peaceful now. Kip crawled back off the bed as quietly as he could. He put his slippers back on, closed the bed curtains, and turned to the attendants.

Lady Ylette had gone, no doubt she had more urgent things to do than listen to a secretary sing lullabies to the Lord of Rising Stars. The remaining two looked at Kip, slightly stunned. Had they both just spent an hour staring at him? How strange.

“If the Glorious One becomes distressed again,” he said in his quietest whisper, “please come and get me.”

They both nodded. Kip tiptoed out of the room, and then staggered back up to his own bed.
