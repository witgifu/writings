After leaving the Emperor, alone in that ocean of a bed, Kip went back to his room to dress. There wasn't really enough of the night left to bother trying to sleep, it would shortly be dawn. Feeling much more comfortable with a clean face and a clean uniform, he visited the pages to collect the morning reports. Then he sat down at his desk, and started on the summary.

He read through the various updates that different departments and nobles thought were most important. It was such a mix of irrelevant detail and missing information, Kip sighed. As he tried to synthesise something useful from the dross, he thought over the previous night.

Kip felt awkward about how Lord Igel had treated the Emperor, when he was incapacitated. It was possible that the Emperor knew about the medication, and had agreed they should do what they could to administer it. But the Emperor's reaction to Kip's explanation suggested that perhaps that was not the case.

Maybe the Emperor did not want to take the medication. Perhaps it had other side effects, like leaving him with painful headaches or dizziness. Had Kip participated in harming his Emperor? It hadn't been his intention, and he couldn't think of what else he could have done.

Kip finished the cover summary, and left the priority-ordered stack in the breakfast room on the way though. Then he went to visit the kitchen to see what he could find for his own breakfast. He struggled to remember what exactly he was and wasn't allowed to eat, but the kitchen staff knew and were happy to find him some leftovers.

As he ate his rice and grilled fish at the kitchen table, Kip continued to ruminate over the night. There was just no way he could turn the Emperor's reaction to see a situation where he wasn't in some kind of trouble. 

At a minimum, it was very clear that the Emperor had not wanted to wake and find anyone else in his bed. Kip sympathised, if he'd woken up to find anyone in his bed, especially anyone he didn't remember inviting in, he'd also be upset. 

For extra credit, Kip suspected that there was more to the issue. He didn't fully understand the relationship between Lord Igel and the Emperor. Could the Emperor not just dismiss Lord Igel if he was unhappy? Perhaps there were consequences for that which Kip couldn't see yet. There would be no consequences for dismissing Kip, who had no powerful patron, no wealth, no rank of his own.

The Emperor's displeasure was not like anyone else's. A great lord might have had Kip whipped for offending him. The Emperor... if Kip had been sure of the nature of his misstep... he didn't have much legal training, just one basic law subject from uni. But he was reasonably sure that touching the Emperor without permission would be... treason. 

Treason. The Emperor needed no court to convict, he could do so by fiat. Kip's mind recoiled from thinking down that path, but he had been at the Palace for over a year. There had been two executions for treason while he had been here. He considered it might be better to pre-empt the slow and painful death of a commoner for treason. 

With that uncomfortable thought, he put down his cup of coffee, unfinished. Excusing himself, he dashed up the stairs to his rooms, and to the toilet. He heaved up all the breakfast he'd eaten. When he was done, he lay down on the tiles, enjoying the cool feeling on his forehead.

How long would it take the Emperor to order the guards? Would he wait until they began work? Would... would the Emperor dictate the order for Kip's execution? That was entirely the kind of showmanship that Eritanyr would have enjoyed. 

Kip had heard nothing of this Emperor doing anything similar. What he had seen of the Lord Emperor Artorin had shown him to be kind, and generous, within the constraints of his role. It was clear, for example, that the reports he was sent were heavily redacted.

In just over a year in the Palace of Stars though, Kip had seen how far the Empire had changed since the stories of the days of Aurelius Magnus and Elonoa'a. The Emperor barely left the Palace of Stars. He was surrounded by a culture of corruption.

What could Kip do? Go on the run, like The Red Company? Hardly. He would have to trust his ke'ea, that had led him so far. He would have to stay, and hope, and see what this Emperor, his Emperor, would do.

Kip washed his face, and rinsed out his mouth. He put on a clean outer robe, and went back down to his desk. Perhaps his steps down the stairs were a little slow, a little shaky. But he was determined not to give in to despair, not yet.

He was ready in good time when the Emperor arrived in the official study. The glow of his presence intimidated Kip today, in a way that it hadn't previously. Maybe it was the way it glinted off the jewels, and cloth-of-gold.

Kip performed his obeisance, and rose when signalled. He couldn't face looking the Emperor in the eyes as he said 'good morning'. Kip was afraid to see the anger in those eyes, although the Emperor had carefully modulated his voice.

To Kip's immense relief, the Emperor did not begin by having Kip take dictation of his own order for execution. Instead, the Emperor started with a formula letter. Kip fell into the pattern of work. 

He did not need to think or feel, just connect his brush to his ears. The flicks and curls of scribal shorthand falling onto the paper. There was a rhythm that soothed him, like a dance, or a song.

After Kip had lunch, and wrote up the letters in longhand for sending, he considered making a visit to Court. He'd never been before, not having cause or costume. But the man who had delivered his new uniforms had carefully explained what each one was for. Why would he have a court uniform, unless he was occasionally expected to attend?

Kip turned the Emperor’s words over in his mind. He had not been told to attend, or even invited. The Lord of Rising Stars seemed to have just hinted that Kip was able to attend.

To be honest, Kip didn’t want to go. He was tired, and sore, and he didn't want to listen to a wind quartet. He was sure they would be wonderful, to be performing in the Palace of Stars in front of the Lord Emperor and his court. 

Maybe if it had been a string ensemble, or a choir. Then Kip could have enjoyed writing a long and detailed letter to Vinyë about the music and technique. Maybe he could have even got some of the sheet music for it in the city and enclosed that with his letter.

A wind quartet would have an oboe. Kip missed his oboe. He had brought it with him, all the long journey, and kept it on the little shelf in his room as a page. Not once had he played it, but it was a comfort to know that he could. If he had forgotten some alternate fingerings, if he had lost his very highest notes, well he could still have played the pieces he knew by heart. 

He had sent his oboe home when he had been selected. The priest-wizards had gone through every one of his possessions and declared what he could keep. The oboe and his efela ko were both listed as impure. Kip had fought for his efela ko (he wore it all the time, it could be purified with him). The oboe he had wrapped carefully and posted back to his mother.

The letter he had written to his mother explaining his selection had gone with the oboe. It had taken him days to write. The junior priest-wizards had explained what had happened to the previous groups. What they hoped would happen this time. Kip had not been able to write any of that.

Instead, he had told his various family members that he had been selected for a special project working with the Ouranatha. At the end of which he would have a short audience with the Emperor. He described the other people he was working with, and all the interesting places they were from.

He didn’t explain why he had sent his oboe home.
