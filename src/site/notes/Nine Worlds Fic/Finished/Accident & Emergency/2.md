Conju was very happy that they only spent an hour at the hospital. Of course, such a long time would have been unthinkable in the Palace of Stars. But out here, where Fitzroy was trying to be ordinary, it was nice to be still seen promptly.

They arrived home in good order, and everyone admired the special seaweed dressing the healer had used. Fitzroy was still trying to insist it hadn't been necessary, but Conju wasn't sure where he would source seaweed dressings the next time Fitzroy cut himself, except at the hospital. 

Thankfully, after not too long, Fitzroy moved on to wondering how he could play the harp with his bandaged finger. He spent the remainder of the evening making various small noises of frustration while he played small fragments of various songs. Kip, at least, knew enough about music to offer suggestions, and so Conju felt it was acceptable to go to bed early.

The next morning they were all _very_ surprised to get a visit from a social worker, Waso. The 'what has Fitzroy done now' look that Kip and Ludvic shared gave Conju a small tip-off that this might not be as routine as the social worker claimed it was. Waso asked to interview each of them alone, starting with Fitzroy.

Fitzroy came out of his interview looking confused, and a little sad. There was also something of the serene Radiancy about him. Kip rushed up to Fitzroy immediately, offering hugs and murmurs of reassurance. So Conju got the second interview. 

The cheerful Waso reminded him of some of Kip's young staff in the Service. That sort of enthusiasm for improving the world, combined with the sharp mind to actually do it.

“When did you first meet Fitzroy, then?” asked Waso, casually, as Conju sat down.

Conju had to think for a moment. “That's a complex question,” he said, trying to decide what counts as 'meeting Fitzroy', “but I would say I first entered the direct service of the Emperor Artorin Damara in the third year after the Fall.”

Waso wrote something in their notebook, and Conju was mildly interested to know what part of his statement caught their attention. There was a pause, which Waso was clearly leaving in case Conju wanted to add something more. Of course, even in his retirement, Conju wasn't going to fall for the simplest of the court tricks. He smiled politely.

“And you left…?” asked Waso.

Maintaining his polite smile, Conju replied, “You could argue that I haven't, although I believe the formal date of record would be the Jubilee, when Ludvic, Fitzroy, and I formally retired.”

Waso nodded, “Kip Mdang owns this house, where you all live together now?”

“I believe he sorted out the paperwork, yes. He was legally dead for a while, and I think then technically it was owned by Fitzroy,” answered Conju, unsure where this line of questioning was going.

This did not earn a note. “And how would you describe your relationship with Fitzroy?”

Conju did not stare openly at the social worker for their rudeness, although he wanted to. What a thing to ask! “He's my friend,” replied Conju, perfectly evenly.

“And before you came to live in Gorjo City?”

“It was my greatest honour,” said Conju, suppressing his urge to make the court gesture to indicate one's fealty, “to serve his Radiancy as his Groom of the Chamber.”

That did get a note. What was this young person angling for, Conju wondered.

“What did a typical day look like, then?”

Conju blinked once. “Busy,” he said, and did not clarify further.

“I mean, what did your job involve?”

Internally, Conju cringed at referring to _Groom of the Chamber to the Sun-on-Earth_ as a “job”. But he did not correct Waso. “Dressing his Radiancy, managing his household, personal care and grooming, planning menus, organising his diary with Cliopher, planning major events with the Master of Etiquette, and of course attending his Radiancy personally.”

“And you chose what he would wear? And appointed the other staff?”

“Of course,” said Conju, “Although I did seek input from his Radiancy.”

“Would you say it was a high-stress position?”

“It could be seen as that. There were sometimes long hours, and of course maintaining the taboos required a lot of attention to detail. But I greatly respected his Radiancy, and wanted to make life as easy for him as possible.” Conju did not say that, in retrospect, he had often failed spectacularly. 

For a long while after finding Fitzroy, Conju had been burdened by guilt. It had taken time, and a lot of repeated reassurances from Fitzroy, to let that weight go. He had done the best he could, with the information that he had. They had both been bound up in the rules and taboos of Astandalas, even long after the Fall.

“You mentioned the taboos, can you tell me more about that?” Waso looked comfortable, like this was a perfectly normal question they might ask anyone in Gorjo City.

“You were born after the fall of Astandalas, yes?” asked Conju, although he was pretty sure of the answer.

Waso nodded. “Yes, just after.”

“Do you know anyone who had been to the Palace of Stars when it was in Astandalas the Golden?” probed Conju, feeling more in control of the conversation now.

Waso shook their head. “A few people who have been to Solaara, but that's it.”

Conju looked pensively out the window. It was hard to explain to people who hadn't been there. Hard for them to understand just how hundreds of people had lived (and died) in the service of the Empire, in maintaining the magic that held everything together. Until it didn't, until all that remained was service and respect for the man trying to hold together the shreds.

“The taboos were a complex set of rules for the life of the person of the Lord Emperor, that helped to maintain the magic of the Pax Astandalas. In the Fall, some of them were relaxed or reduced. Since the consequences for breaking them could be dire, we did not experiment. The final remnants were removed two years before the Jubilee.”

Snapping his eyes back to Waso, Conju saw a brief flicker of confusion.

“I'm not a wizard,” said Conju, with a small sigh, “So I can't tell you much about how they actually functioned. I memorised the various lists.”

Waso nodded, and said, “That's all for now, thank you. Could you please send in,” they consulted their notes, “Ludvic Omo?”

“Of course,” said Conju, and swept out of the room.

He found the others outside in a conference around the outdoor dining table. Fitzroy was breaking raspberries into individual drupelets and eating them, one tiny drop at a time. Kip was holding his elbow, and leaving no space between them. 

“Ludvic is wanted next,” said Conju to the group. Ludvic nodded, and got up from the table. Conju took the vacated seat, next to Rhodin.

Without preamble, Kip turned on Conju. “What did you tell them?”

Conju looked confused and offended. “I answered their questions, was I not supposed to?” He looked at Rhodin, if this was some kind of threat, one of the ex-guards should have said something.

“What did they ask you?” said Rhodin, not offering either answers or comfort.

“About my relationship with Fitzroy, about my position in the Palace, about the taboos,” said Conju, feeling defensive. “It's all a matter of public record, if they wrote to Solaara I'm sure they would have been told the same.”

Rhodin and Kip gave each other matching tense looks. Fitzroy did not look up from his raspberries. Conju suspected Fitzroy rather wanted to turn into a crow, but was resisting at Kip or Rhodin's request.

“Please,” said Conju, trying his best to put aside his court manners and instincts, “What is going on?”

Kip looked Conju directly in the eyes. “Someone thinks we're abusing Fitzroy, and has reported us.”

Fitzroy bent even further down, huddling into Kip's side, and abandoning his raspberries.

Conju was unable to suppress his instinctive recoil. The idea was absurd! They had done everything to protect and serve Fitzroy in the Palace, and here they were friends. Oh, sure, sometimes Conju snapped at Fitzroy when he left a door open or ate something Conju had been saving. But Fitzroy would do the same right back if Conju reorganised the pantry and moved his favourite tea.

“I tried to explain,” said Fitzroy, mostly into Kip's shoulder. “I said you all helped me escape, that you weren't the people who trapped me, that all those people were dead.”

“I should go back, I should explain, I'm sorry Fitzroy—” said Conju, standing up.

Rhodin shook his head, “Sit down, Conju. They still have to talk to me and Kip, and Ludvic is in there now, we'll sort it out.”

Conju sat down with a thud, not his usual grace. “They're not entirely wrong,” he said, very quietly, “I'm sorry.” 

That Rhodin, Kip, and Fitzroy didn't immediately deny his statement, confirmed Conju's assessment. And of all of them, he was the one who had spent the most time with his Radiancy, done the most maintaining the taboos, had the most control. If anyone alive should be arrested for the imprisonment and abuse of Fitzroy Angursell, it was probably Conju.

Just as Conju was preparing to fully wallow in his guilt, Rhodin interrupted him. “Don't worry,” he said, smiling, “if you do get arrested, we've got two of the finest legal minds on Zunidh to defend you.” He waved a hand at Fitzroy and Kip. They both blinked, confused for a moment.

Rhodin continued, “And if that doesn't work, we'll break you out and take you to Alinor or Ysthar. Intermundial extradition orders are nearly impossible to execute.”

Fitzroy let out a slight puff of laughter at that. Kip smiled a little, and rubbed Fitzroy's shoulder.

Before Rhodin had time to make a fully detailed plan for their run from the law, Ludvic returned. “You've been excused,” he said to Rhodin, “Kip is next, and then we're done.”

Kip stood up, and squared his shoulders. Conju recognised the pose from court, and smiled at him (maybe a little weakly). “Thank you, Cliopher,” he said softly, and Kip gave him a wink and a genuine smile. Then he strode inside.

Ludvic sat down, taking the spot Kip had vacated, next to Fitzroy. Without Kip, Fitzroy looked bereft, staring at his berries. Ludvic carefully raised an arm, and wrapped it around Fitzroy. Sighing, Fitzroy lent into it.

Conju wondered how Fitzroy had coped for so many years without touch. He was so very tactile (far more than Conju). Taking a deep breath, Conju reached across the table, and rested his hand on Fitzroy's. Immediately, the dark hand caught his lighter one, and squeezed it.

They sat there in silence for some time, Conju resting in the company of his friends.

Finally, Kip returned. “They're closing the investigation,” he said, a look of satisfaction clear on his face. “They'll make sure there's a note on Fitzroy's file at the hospital, that he has experienced trauma, and there should be a family member or patient advocate with him at all times.”

Fitzroy scrambled up from the table, nearly knocking the remaining berries to the floor. He grabbed Kip in a hug and buried his face in Kip's neck. Kip stroked Fitzroy's hair, and kissed it gently.

Conju watched them both, guilt and thankfulness swirling in his heart.

“Come here,” said Kip, waving a free hand at Conju. Obediently, Conju got up and stood beside them. 

Fitzroy threw out an arm, and pulled him into the hug. “I will be more careful grating cheese in future,” promised Fitzroy, speaking mostly into Kip's hair.

“It was _not_ your fault, my dear,” said Conju, “well, the grating your finger was, but the social worker visit wasn't.”

“And I,” announced Kip, “am going to write to Aioru and ask them to update the history curriculum. I don't know _what_ they're teaching about Late Astandalas in school these days, but it's clearly been _censored_.”
