The other side of the door is unmistakably a cellar. They all file through and look around, although only Ser Rhodin and Oginu have lights. There are a lot of barrels, and a distinct smell of yeast fermentation.

“A winery, sir?” asks Oginu, cautiously.

But Sayo Nevan answers, “Mead!” Then he rushes forward, through the racks of casks.

“Find something to hold the door open, Oginu,” says Ser Rhodin, walking forward more cautiously, “We don't want it to close behind us and vanish.”

Oginu salutes, and then examines the door. It has a similar handle on both sides, and is made of wood. Firstly, he heaves down a barrel, hoping he isn't destroying whatever liquid is inside. Rolling it against the door, to push it back against the shelves. This requires a bit of careful manoeuvring as the floor isn't flat, and the barrel is round. Putting it up on end would definitely disturb whatever is inside. That done, Oginu rummages in his pack for a length of rope. He secures it to the door handle and the closest rack. 

Two methods of holding the door open seems good, but if it really is their only easy return to Zunidh he would rather have three. Finally, he pulls out a notepad and pencil, scribbling “Please do not close without asking Ser Rhodin!” He slips the corner of the paper between the iron door handle and the wood. Hopefully that's enough.

Then he re-shoulders his pack, and jogs to catch up with the others.

They are all standing at the bottom of a wooden staircase, looking up at another door. Oginu can hear footsteps above, but not voices. Are they hostile? Are they even human? None of the others look concerned.

“I'm almost certain,” whispers Sayo Nevan, as the footsteps get closer.

Suddenly the door opens, spilling daylight down the stairs. Oginu blinks, trying to clear his vision. Before he can see more than a figure holding a lantern, the person speaks.

“Toucan! And Buru Tovo? I'd ask how you got here, but since Kip fell out of the sky I suppose you must have crawled out of the earth?”

“Not entirely wrong,” says Sayo Nevan, “There's a door in the back of your cellar that leads to a sacred cave in the Epalos.” He bounds up the stairs, and hugs the man at the top fiercely.

“Oh, and this is Ser Rhodin and Guard Oginu,” adds Sayo Nevan, “They were sent by the Lord Magus to find Kip.” Then he turns to look back down the stairs at them, “This is Basil White, yet another of Kip's cousins.”

“Well,” says Sayo White, “Let's get you out of the cellar and into the light!”

They climb up and out into a well-appointed inn. It feels cosy and warm, it's hard to believe they're on an entirely different world. Sayo White and Sayo Nevan are grinning, and even Sayo Tovo looks happy.

“You said Kip fell out of the sky, Mr. White?” says Ser Rhodin, clearly not wanting to disturb the reunion but needing to get on with the job.

“Yes,” says Mr. White, “He was quite unharmed, apparently. Off at once, as usual, trying to get a letter back to you. Kip and Mr. Greenwing, the Marquess' grandson, but he doesn't like to mention it, left for Ragnor Bella just after breakfast. To see if either the bookshop or Mr. Dart's unicorn friend could help Kip.”

Oginu manages to restrain himself from giving Ser Rhodin a very bemused look. It sounds like Sayo Mdang is nearby, which is the important thing. Not that someone called Mr. Dart has a friend who is a _unicorn_. 

“Is it far to Ragnor Bella, Mr. White?” asks Oginu.

“Oh no, not far at all. Just out of the Woods,” says Mr. White with a smile.

Ser Rhodin nods, and strides through the taproom and out the front door. Oginu agrees, they should get going and see if they can catch up. He hurries out as well. It isn't until he nearly walks into Ser Rhodin, who has stopped in the middle of the path, that he notices the cloud of insects around them. Buzzing.

“Ah,” says Ser Rhodin, “If everyone could just stay where they are for now?”

Oginu looks back over his shoulder. Sayo Tovo is at the top of the stairs, Mr. White and Sayo Nevan are still inside. The bees don't seem interested in Sayo Tovo, or the inside of the inn. Instead, they seem to be forming a darkening cloud around him and Ser Rhodin.

“We mean no harm to Cliopher sayo Mdang,” says Ser Rhodin, presumably to the bees, “nor to the… Viscount? Nor to Mr. Basil White. We have come to help Cliopher return home.”

Oginu looks from Ser Rhodin, who seems to be calmly negotiating with the swarm of bees as if this is a normal thing, to Sayo Tovo, who looks curious, but not surprised. He feels like he has been cast as the straight character in a comedy play. 

Oginu is a guard, he is trained in defence and combat. Yet, he cannot think of a weapon or strategy that would help against what must be a hundred thousand bees. It's not as if any _individual_ bee could do him that much harm, he doesn't come out in hives or swell up like some people do when stung. It's just that even a thousand bee stings could probably cause him enough pain to disable him. Well, maybe smoking them to make them sleepy might work. But that would have required preparation. 

Perhaps, upon reflection, Ser Rhodin's strategy of negotiation is the best one. It's just not clear to Oginu how the bees are talking back. Or perhaps how Ser Rhodin can understand them.

“The bees,” say Ser Rhodin, noticing the confusion on Oginu's face, “Have apparently had some troubles.” He sighs, and looks up the stairs. “If Mr. White will vouch for Sayo Tovo, they will escort Cliopher's… great uncle?… yes, I think I got that right, to Ragnor Bella. Sayo Nevan will need to stay with Mr. White. Oginu and I will need to… wait for the judgement of the Viscount, at the hives.”

“The Viscount?” asks Oginu, looking around. The bees seem startlingly well-informed.

“Mr. Greenwing,” supplies Mr. White, from inside, “Hopefully he won't be too put out by having to come back here so soon.”

“Well,” says Sayo Tovo, “I'd better get going then. Don't want to get in trouble like you boys, eh?” He ambles down the stairs.

“Don't stray off the road, Buru Tovo,” calls Mr. White, “The Woods are no place for a stranger.”

Sayo Tovo nods, and keeps walking. Some of the swarm drift off after him, like a cloud of dust behind a cart. A few bees even land on him. “I suppose you can have a lift,” he says, “You're not very heavy.”

“Well,” says Ser Rhodin to Oginu, “You and I better go to the hives, and let these bees be about their usual business.”

“Yes sir,” says Oginu, looking around for any obvious bee hives.
