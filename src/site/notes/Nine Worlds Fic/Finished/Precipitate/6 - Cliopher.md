It was a lovely breakfast, if perhaps a little heavy on the sausage for Cliopher's taste. The Dart family were most accommodating, treating the arrival of a man who had fallen out of the sky as if such a thing might happen early on any Tuesday morning. They had also helpful told him it was Tuesday, on Alinor.

Mr. Dart keeps giving Cliopher the strangest looks when he thinks Cliopher isn't looking. Finally, Cliopher decides he can't take it, “I'm sorry,” he whispers to Mr. Dart, “Do I have something on my face? Perhaps a smudge of dirt from when I fell?” He pulls a handkerchief out of his pocket.

“Do you know any mages, Sayo Mdang?” says Mr. Dart, seemingly apropos of nothing, and ignoring Cliopher's questions.

“Yes?” says Cliopher, knowing he is a guest, and so it's on him to be polite, “My lord is a great mage, Mr. Dart.”

“Would I know him, Sayo Mdang?” asks Mr. Dart, liberally buttering another slice of toast.

“Ah, probably not personally, Mr. Dart. I—,” Cliopher has always struggled to explain what he does, but perhaps slightly less so with strangers. He shrugs, “I am the private secretary to the Emperor. Among other things.”

“Ah!” says Mr. Dart, “Yes, that would entirely explain it. He must hold you in very high regard, Sayo Mdang.”

“I suppose,” says Cliopher. He's used to expressions like that being followed with offers of bribery. “What exactly would it explain, Mr. Dart?”

“Why you are so thick with protective magics. They swirl around you so densely that unless I'm looking at you directly, Sayo Mdang, you are actually hard to see,” says Mr. Dart, “My apologies if that came across as staring at you.”

The squire and his husband (or partner? Cliopher isn't quite clear, and doesn't want to ask), both look slightly askance at this declaration. 

“I thought you didn't want to talk about magic?” asks the squire of his younger brother.

“Sayo Mdang asked,” says Mr. Dart, “It's only polite to answer.” He then bites off a mouthful of toast, which prevents him from having to say anything more.

“And of course,” adds Mr. Greenwing, now giving Sayo Mdang an assessing look, “If Sayo Mdang works for Lord Artorin, he can hardly be one of the people against the use of magic.”

“_Against magic?_” says Cliopher, very surprised, “Is that attitude common on Alinor, Mr. Greenwing?”

“Not in Fiellan, thankfully, Sayo Mdang,” says Mr. Greenwing, carefully not looking directly at Mr. Dart, “But elsewhere, yes.”

Cliopher is not sure what to say to this. It seems absurd. But then, perhaps Alinor was strongly dependent on Schooled Magic, like Kavinor. If they had not kept local magic traditions, as the shamans of the Vangaveye-ve did, then he could see how magic might have been blamed. Could still be being blamed. It would be making some people's lives very difficult, since magical ability could spring up anywhere.

Like, perhaps, in Mr. Dart.

Well, it would be rude to pry. “Is the Bee at the Border still open, do you know, Mr. Greenwing?” he asks instead, hoping to change to a lighter topic.

Mr. Dart gives Mr. Greenwing a significant look. “It *is* open,” answers the latter, not making eye contact, “Although it was closed for a while due to a curse.”

Ah. It seems that Cliopher has done a poor job of avoiding the topic of magic. “Oh dear,” says Cliopher, “But everyone is well? The village is fine?”

Mr. Greenwing nods, “Yes, I have done my best to make up for the troubles that befell them, Sayo Mdang.”

Once again, Cliopher feels like he is walking into things that are carefully unspoken. What is Mr. Greenwing's relation to St-Noire? Was he somehow the cause of the curse? He seems unlikely to have _set_ the curse, although Cliopher has never actually encountered a curse himself.

There is of course another reason Mr. Greenwing might feel the need to help out St-Noire. Cliopher searches his memory for the name of the Imperial title his cousin lives under. “Would you be a relation of the Marquis of the Woods Noirell, then, Mr. Greenwing?”

Mr. Dart gives a small smile, and Mr. Greenwing sighs. “My grandmother is the Marquis, yes,” he says, carefully only looking at Cliopher.

The family trees and titles of various minor Imperial nobles have never been one of Cliopher's particular interests. It's possible there's a lesser title that Mr. Greenwing is entitled to, if he is the heir. However, Cliopher is always careful to address people as they have introduced themselves. “But you prefer to be known as Mr. Greenwing,” he says, gently, “I quite understand.”

Mr. Greenwing relaxes slightly. Clearly someone, possibly the grandmother, thinks he _shouldn't_ be called by his chosen name. Ridiculous, thinks Cliopher.

Mr. Dart declares he will accompany Cliopher and Mr. Greenwing into Ragnor Bella. They take a gentle walk along a lane way that reminds Cliopher very much of his previous visits to Alinor.

“Have you worked long for Lord Artorin, Sayo Mdang?” asks Mr. Dart.

“That depends on whom you ask Mr. Dart, time has been somewhat uneven on Zunidh since the Fall. Either over nine hundred years, or perhaps twenty,” says Cliopher, with a smile, “Thankfully my lord's work has done much to stabilise the flow of time, and it now usually varies by less than five percent.”

Mr. Greenwing gives Mr. Dart a very surprised look. “That is… extraordinary,” he says, “And, have you often fallen through portals to other worlds, Sayo Mdang?”

“Ah,” says Cliopher, “No, not fallen. I have visited Ysthar as part of an embassy, but we walked through the portal. This was, rather an accident, Mr. Greenwing. I fell off the roof of the Palace of Stars.”

“Are secretaries often found on the roof of the Palace of Stars, Sayo Mdang? Or caught by surprise portals?” asked Mr. Dart.

“I was admiring the view after a long day,” says Cliopher, remembering the lights of Solaara blending into the stars above. “It's not generally accessible, for security reasons, and also because there isn't a railing to stop people falling off. And, no, Mr. Dart, I've never heard of a portal to another world in the gardens of the Palace.” He pauses for a beat, and then adds feeling he should be perfectly honest, “Although a number of people went missing in the early times after the Fall, that is, the Interim. It may be that some came across portals in the gardens.”

They all walk along in the slightly pained silence that Cliopher is familiar with when anyone mentions the Fall.

“How are the universities?” Cliopher asks, after deciding that both monetary policy and healthcare systems are unlikely to prove to be positive topics. “We've had a couple of academics visit from Tara, but the Ambassadors have, understandably, been more interested in trade.”

Mr. Dart looks pointedly at Mr. Greenwing. Did they not go to university? Cliopher had understood it was almost mandatory for gentlemen of Alinor, but perhaps the Fall has changed that too.

“Morrowlea, where I recently graduated, is still as radically egalitarian as ever,” says Mr. Greenwing. Cliopher can sense fondness in his tone, and can see how a man who wants to avoid his noble title might have enjoyed his time there.

“My professor at Stonybridge was excellent,” says Mr. Dart, “An expert in Late Astandalan history, but of course we've had limited access to the primary sources for Imperial History.”

“His Radiancy is most happy to support scholarship,” says Cliopher, warmly, “If they have the time or inclination they should come and use the Archives in Solaara. We still have almost all of it, and the Censors are gone now.”

“I will write to her and let her know,” says Mr. Dart, with a smile, “Although if you are Lord Artorin's secretary, Sayo Mdang, I am sure she would be most annoyed I am not grilling you. Her focus is the Last Emperor's reign.”

“I only came to his Radiancy's service after the Fall,” says Cliopher carefully, “I'm afraid there was no circumstance beforehand where a commoner in the Service could have done so.” Then Cliopher remembers he is walking with two nobles, even if they don't have or use titles. He winces inside. Although, if Mr. Greenwing attended Morrowlea, perhaps at least he doesn't mind.

“I'm sure she'd still want to know if you knew why he never married,” ask Mr. Dart, with a smile.

Cliopher's heart skips a beat. Do they not know? “The only candidates were his full sister, who retired from court after the unexpected death of her betrothed, and a small child,” he says, trying to sound factual rather than pained, “I have never had cause to discuss it with his Radiancy, however.”

“Oh,” says Mr. Dart, blushing slightly, “Of course, Sayo Mdang, my apologies.”

“We tend to mostly deal with matters current to Zunidh,” Cliopher offers, “We have spent so long recovering, as I am sure you have here.”

Both men nod. They turn a corner, and suddenly a village appears at the end of the road. “Ragnor Bella, Sayo Mdang,” offers Mr. Dart, as bells ring the hour.

Mr. Greenwing winces, “I am late for work, my apologies gentlemen. Mr. Dart would you mind showing Sayo Mdang to the post office? And then giving him directions to St-Noire?”

“Not at all, Mr. Greenwing,” says Mr. Dart, “We wouldn't want to upset Mrs. Etaris.”

With a hurried 'thank you' to Mr. Dart, Mr. Greenwing runs off up ahead of them.

It's not until Cliopher opens the post office door that he realises he might have a problem. Mr. Dart has given Cliopher careful instructions on how to get from the post office to St-Noire and left for his own tasks. But it might be a bank that Cliopher needs first, possibly a large one. He doesn't think he has any Alinorial money.

There's a bench along the side of the post office for wrapping parcels, and Cliopher uses it to open his writing kit and start digging. He knows he has Zuni banknotes, but those will be worthless here. In a compartment where he keeps some sentimental letters, he finds a small quantity of Imperial coins. And then, after cleaning out all his old pen nibs, he finds a single Alinorial wheatear.

He sighs. An intermundial letter is going to be more expensive than a wheatear, he is sure. Unless Alinor has suffered from terrible deflation, which seems unlikely.

“I don't suppose you do currency exchange?” he asks the woman knitting at the counter.

“No, sorry, we're a bit small for that here,” she says, sympathetically. Then she looks down at her needles, swears softly and starts counting her stitches.

“What would be the cost of a letter to Zunidh? Just the smallest one,” he asks, loath to interrupt her counting.

“Oh. Hm, let me check the book…” She puts the knitting down, and heaves a ledger up from below the counter. “Would that be first class, second class, or third?” she asks, as she flips to the back.

“Oh, I suppose third? Is there a lot of difference?” Says Cliopher, resigned.

The postmistress slides her finger down a column of numbers. “About two weeks I would think,” she says, without looking up.

Two *weeks*? Perhaps he should visit Basil first and try to borrow some money.

“Ah!” she says, and stabs the page with her finger, “Actually, it won't make any difference. All the post to Zunidh goes through in bulk when the gate opens once every six months, and the next opening is in three months. So, third class. Six wheatears.”

Cliopher smiles politely, and puts the coins in his hand into his pocket. Three months is far too long, he may as well just walk to the gate. And if they have followed the protocols he left, he'll likely have been declared dead and be out of a job.

“Thank you very much, Postmistress,” he says.

She nods, and picks up her knitting again. With a sigh, she starts counting from the start.

“It's fifty-seven,” he says, mildly, “And a hundred and thirteen on the other needle. If that's helpful?”

She smiles up at him. “I really should keep a tally on a piece of paper or something,” she says, “Thank you, Mr…?”

“Cliopher sayo Mdang,” he says, with a return smile. Then he heads out of the post office, following Mr. Dart's directions.
