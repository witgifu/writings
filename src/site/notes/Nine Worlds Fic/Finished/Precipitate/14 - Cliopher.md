Having had his evening convert into an early morning rather unexpectedly, Cliopher is willing to be herded into an early bed by Basil. He only makes one protest that he usually doesn't go to bed until after midnight, and even Clio gives him a judging look. It probably has something to do with the near-continuous yawning. And indeed, when he buries himself in the soft bedsheets, in a borrowed nightshirt, he falls asleep almost immediately.

It is Basil who wakes him the next morning, just as first light is rising. Cliopher isn't sure when he last had a sleep so long and so deep, perhaps on his last holiday home. It's good it's Basil, because that way he doesn't have to worry that it was a dream Basil is alive — instead, once he has rubbed the sleep from his eyes, he is worrying about how he will possibly get back.

He *needs* to get back, there's so much he still wants to do. The stipend, improvements to education, more grants for the arts. He has tried to build a government his Radiancy can rely on, but he worries anyway. Time, now, is not on his side.

The calculations come almost automatically, even though he doesn't want them to. He wrote the plans for what to do if he disappears unexpectedly, and although he was anticipating perhaps being kidnapped in Nanjin, they apply just as well to him being suddenly transported to Alinor. The most recent Ambassador reported a time ratio of approximately 1:6, meaning Cliopher has four and two-thirds of a day on Alinor to get a message to the Palace on Zunidh. Adding a safety margin for the variability of the time ratio, and the need for the message to possibly travel across Zunidh as well, he gives himself three days. He's spent one.

Admittedly, he spent it discovering Basil, Sarah, and Clio are still alive and well. He had also made _some_ forays into getting a message to Zunidh, it's just that the postal system here is more-or-less where Zunidh's was before his lord built the Lights. Perhaps his Radiancy could have some kind of knowledge sharing workshop with the other Lords Magi? The Lord of Ysthar could perhaps explain the system there as well, their electric mail is fascinating, and seems to be even faster than the Lights.

He pulls on his under-things and robes. Thankfully, running an inn, Basil has experience with travellers in need of rapid laundry. Cliopher wonders if it's magical, or if someone on Alinor has invented some kind of mechanical system since the Fall.

“What are you thinking about, Kip?” asks Basil, smiling at him from the doorway.

“If the mail system of Ysthar or Zunidh would work better on Alinor,” says Cliopher, embarrassed, but smiling. Of all the people on any world, Basil is the one who understands Cliopher's wandering mind the best.

“Ha! Of course you are, and here I was hoping you'd be thinking about what you wanted for breakfast,” says Basil with a wink. 

“Honey!” says Cliopher, with a grin, “Only if you have any. Even the Palace of Stars has run out of the famous honey of the Woods.”

“We do,” says Basil, as they walk to the kitchen together, “Would you like to eat it with a spoon, or perhaps on some porridge or toast?”

“Yes,” says Cliopher, “That sounds lovely.” He could really do with a change of clothes. Perhaps there would be space to fit one in his writing kit? It never seems to lack for space for documents, so maybe at least a clean set of underclothes could be folded in there somewhere.

Basil claps him on the shoulder, “You've already gone off somewhere else again, haven't you.”

“Well, yes,” says Cliopher, laughing, “But only as far as wishing unexpected portals came with unexpected pre-packed luggage.”

“Not dashing out to make a grass skirt?” asks Basil, handing Cliopher a bowl.

“I'm afraid your neighbours might be rather scandalised,” says Cliopher, ladling himself some porridge.

“Well, we don't have the right grasses anyway,” says Basil, “I tried to teach Clio with them, but the fibres are all wrong.”

Cliopher wraps an arm around Basil, “When Alinor has a reliable postal system, I'll send you some from home.”

Over breakfast Mr. Greenwing and Basil discuss the goings-on in St-Noire. There is much debate as to if the old granary should be rebuilt. Neither Basil nor Mr. Greenwing seem decided on the matter.

“Is there much corn grown here?” asks Cliopher, curious. He had seen grainfields between Ragnor Bella and the Woods, but it didn't seem like there was space around the village.

“We used to grow some oats, mostly for horses and as crop rotation,” says Basil, “Not so many horses visiting now.”

“Mostly horticulture then? The only real cash crop is honey?” asks Cliopher.

“Yes,” says Mr. Greenwing, “There is a small watermill, enough that the baker buys in wheat rather than flour.”

Cliopher nods. “Would the granary be able to store anything else? I'm not familiar with the designs around here. I was thinking dried peas, or cress seeds. Perhaps medical supplies, emergency lighting?”

Basil gives Cliopher a curious look. “I didn't know you knew so much about Alinor,” he says, laughing, “I don't know why I didn't expect it, you've always loved learning. And I suppose you did work in the Department of Agriculture.”

“Yes… I did,” says Cliopher, hesitating. He didn't have the best time in that department, mostly what he learnt about was how brutal food rent could be before famine impacted people's ability to work. “I'm mostly extrapolating from what I've learnt about similar climates on Zunidh. We've just wrapped up a project creating Provincial emergency stockpiles.”

“Like yam houses?” asks Basil, “But for whole cities?”

Cliopher nods. “Of course, what to include has to change in different locations. But yes, the ones in the Vangaveye-ve do have yams.”

Basil grins. “All right tanà Kip, you've convinced me. We'll rebuild, but something that can store more than grain.”

Mr. Greenwing and Cliopher leave St-Noire shortly after. The first question Mr. Greenwing asks is not one Cliopher expected. “What did you study at university, Sayo Mdang? You seem to have a very broad knowledge.”

“I studied statistics,” says Cliopher, with a fond smile, “I thought I might become an accountant.” He looks at Mr. Greenwing, who seems a little confused. “I learnt a lot after university,” adds Kip, “For example, I knew nothing about law when I started in the Service, but when I realised I would be drafting legislation, I found a judge who taught part-time in the university in Solaara who was willing to tutor me.”

“Has law changed much since the Interim?” asks Mr. Greenwing.

“Yes and no,” says Cliopher with a smile.

“A true lawyer's answer, Sayo Mdang,” says Mr. Greenwing. “You mean it has been substantially reformed, but not reinvented wholly?”

“Precisely,” says Cliopher, “Many principles of the Astandalan Law Code are solid, like respecting local marriage traditions, but the implicit sanctioning of the use of torture by the state had to be removed.”

“Did imperial law say nothing about marriage?” asks Mr. Greenwing, with slightly false cheer.

Cliopher wonders what might have happened in Ragnor Bella to raise such a question. In all his news, Mr. Greenwing had said nothing about any relationship of his own. But perhaps he is curious for someone else.

“Oh, it most certainly did, but it considered it a form of contract law,” says Cliopher, “That meant the parties had to be of age, and not coerced in any way. Of course, there was a large amount of case law as well, since marriage often means children, and inheritance, and so disputes.”

“Oh yes,” says Mr. Greenwing.

When he doesn't say any more, Cliopher tries to expand. “For example, a common question was if children were made illegitimate if a marriage was found to be void. There are, of course, a lot of strange edge cases, but broadly if the marriage was believed to be valid when the children were conceived or born, they remain legitimate. Or if a parent is able to write a child completely out of their will, which they cannot.”

Cliopher pauses, to try to guess if this is the direction Mr. Greenwing wants to go in. He isn't certain, but it seems like something in the younger man relaxes slightly.

“Apart from outlawing torture, what other changes have you made to Zunidh?” asks Mr. Greenwing.

There are so many places he could go with that question, but Cliopher decides to start with his favourite. “Have you heard about the post on Zunidh?”

They arrive back in Ragnor Bella late that morning. The town centre is full of people stopping to chat while running errands, and small children playing. Mr. Greenwing and Cliopher, however, make a straight line for Elderflower Books.

Mrs. Etaris smiles at them from the counter. “Back so soon?” she asks.

“Mr. Greenwing has very kindly been showing me around. I'm afraid I've rather gone and done things in the wrong order,” says Cliopher, smiling back, “I'm hoping to find any information you might have on either intermundial portals, or ways of sending messages between worlds.”

“Well,” says Mrs. Etaris, coming out from behind the counter, and gazing over the spines of the books, “We haven't much call for books on magic, it's a bit out of fashion.”

“It wouldn't need to be magical, Mrs. Etaris,” says Cliopher, “In fact it would be easier if it wasn't, I'm afraid I have no magic whatsoever.”

“Neither do I,” says Mrs. Etaris, “Most people have a tiny bit, even if they pretend they don't. Well, if it's going to be in *non*-magical books, we had best start with my speciality, Geography.”

“You studied Geography?” asks Cliopher, politely, as they walk down the non-fiction books.

“I have a doctorate from Galderon,” she says, running her fingers down the spines of books about Colhélhé.

“My apologies then, Domina Etaris,” says Cliopher, with a bow.

Mrs. Etaris laughs, “Oh, I don't think anyone has ever called me that. I wrote my thesis on Eahh and Kaphyrn, but I haven't left Alinor since.”

“Would you prefer Mrs. Etaris, then?” asks Cliopher, gently. Perhaps she has reason not to want to remember her studies. Galderon was at war for a while.

“Oh, I suppose it doesn't matter,” she says, with a sigh, “I hated Thistlethwaite, and I'm not particularly fond of Etaris. I only ever really liked Jullanar.”

“Because of Jullanar of the Sea? I always admired her,” says Cliopher, unable to resist.

“Mmm,” says Mrs. Etaris, “Everyone calls me Mrs. Etaris, it would probably be confusing if you called me Domina.”

“Well,” says Cliopher, now entirely unsure how to address her, “If you have been to Eahh and Kaphyrn, you would be the greatest expert on portals for some miles, would you not?”

“I suppose so,” she says, with a small smile, “But I'm as far as I know _finding_ them requires magic. Or at least, finding them deliberately. I always used ones that were found by others first.”

They spend a happy hour looking through possible books in the bookshop. Cliopher learns a lot more about the politics of Alinor since the Interim, but only the vaguest hints about portals. It's frustrating, he is feeling his lack of any understanding of magic is a significant hindrance.

“Well,” says Mrs. Etaris, “That's about it, I think.” She re-shelves 'Cave Formations on the Oriolan Bay', and looks around. “Would you mind walking over to the bakery and buying us some lunch, Sayo Mdang? There's money by the till.”

Cliopher crosses over to the bakery, hoping that Mr. Dart will at least have an idea of who else they might be able to ask.

When Cliopher walks back into the bookshop from the bakery, he can immediately tell that something is wrong. A woman in Imperial-era dress is staring down her nose at Mrs. Etaris, and Mr. Greenwing hovers on the side as if he wants to intervene but feels he can't.

“Good morning, Saya…,” says Cliopher, placing his package on the counter, and addressing the imposing woman.

Mr. Greenwing says quietly, “Sayo Mdang, may I introduce Saya Etaris, mother of Mr. Etaris. Saya Etaris, this is Cliopher sayo Mdang, the Hands of the Emperor.”

Cliopher is mildly surprised that Mr. Greenwing remembers that title. He wonders if any of the three others in the room actually know what it means.

“Which Emperor would that be?” Says Saya Etaris, coldly.

“His Radiancy, Artorin Damara, Hundredth and Last Emperor of Astandalas, and Lord Magus of Zunidh,” says Cliopher, just as coldly back. It was one thing to have people assume *he* was a nobody. Anyone who called themselves 'saya' should hardly be asking *which Emperor*, as if emperors popped up like the minor gentry of Alinor.

Saya Etaris looks at him disbelievingly. “And what would the Hands of the Emperor be doing in Ragnor Bella?”

“Visiting my cousin,” says Cliopher, rather shortly. 

Turning back to Mrs. Etaris, Saya Etaris says, “You cannot possibly believe this man. He is clearly lost from some… special hospital. Really, Jullanar, this is the height of impropriety.”

She then stalks out of the shop, the door jangling behind her.

Cliopher turns to Mr. Greenwing, shaking his head. The other man looks almost green with embarrassment.

“Oh don't worry, Mr. Greenwing, I'm used to people assuming I'm a hinterland nobody. It usually just means there is no substance to their arguments so they are forced to resort to insults,” says Cliopher with a smile. “If I thought it would convince her, I would have been happy to be interrogated about my work.”

“She will try to poison the town against you,” says Mrs. Etaris, “She doesn't react well to being told she is wrong.”

There is a tone there that Cliopher has heard before. It isn't uncommon, of course, to hear people complain about their in-laws. But it digs at a memory of being in Lazo's shop, and being sent to find some of the aunties. Of hearing Uncle Lazo sigh when someone leaves, and him muttering, “I wish they never went back.”

Cliopher waves his hand in dismissal. “I have never been very good at doing what authority expects. I was very lucky to not get executed for treason in Astandalas, to be honest. Or after.”

Mr. Greenwing opens the bag of rolls, and hands one to Cliopher. “What kind of crimes did you commit, Sayo Mdang?”

“Mostly refusing to lie in my reports, or to accept bribes,” says Cliopher, taking a bite of the roll.

“Those were _crimes_?” says Mr. Greenwing, incredulously. 

Mrs. Etaris and Cliopher share an understanding look. “If you were a commoner, and your superior in the Service was a noble, they could have you sent to Internal Security for any disobedience,” says Cliopher, shaking his head. “The Zunidh government is *not* like that. His Radiancy is trying to build a better world. I wish—” he cuts himself off, before he says too much in front of two near-strangers, “—I wish I knew how to get back.”

“After lunch, Sayo Mdang, we will find Mr. Dart. Hopefully he can point us in the right direction,” says Mr. Greenwing.

“Or the wrong one,” adds Mrs. Etaris, “Often you have to start in completely the opposite direction.”

Cliopher considers this advice carefully. It tickles at a memory, but he is not sure why. The introspection is probably why it takes him a long a moment to notice the bees swarming outside the door.
