In the centre of the swarm of bees outside the bookshop, is possibly the most incongruous person to have yet appeared in Ragnor Bella. Even if the stories that Mr. Greenwing has told him are all true, the residents of the sleepy market town can hardly expect the old man, dressed only in a grass skirt, who walks towards the bookshop.

“Buru Tovo!” says Cliopher, and runs out the door, “How did you get here?”

“Same road as you did, eh?” says Buru Tovo, looking Cliopher up and down.

“Thank you,” says Mr. Greenwing, behind Cliopher, but presumably to the bees.

“You can't possibly mean you fell from the sky, Buru Tovo?” asks Cliopher, grasping his great-uncle's arms.

“No, 'course not. Came through a door in Basil's cellar. Much more sensible.”

Cliopher decides not to interrogate this statement. He bends his neck to touch foreheads with Buru Tovo, “Do you know how to get back to Zunidh?” he asks, tentatively.

“Eh, what? I wasn't born yesterday, boy,” says Buru Tovo. Then he turns to Mr. Greenwing, “You'll be called Greenwing then? The bees in the Woods want to speak with you. They're not happy with Kip's friends.”

“My friends?” asks Cliopher, incredulously, “Who, Buru Tovo?”

“Toucan, Rhodin, Oginu,” says Buru Tovo, “Don't know why, seem perfectly nice.” He gives Cliopher a big wink, and Cliopher wonders just how much blatant admiring of the two guards his great-uncle has done. Hopefully that's _all_ he's done. 

The bees are slowly dispersing, until all that are left are perhaps a dozen in Buru Tovo's hair. He sees where Cliopher is looking, “My guides,” he says. Cliopher decides, as he has often done, not to ask for an explanation as to why Buru Tovo is guided by bees, but to wait for Tovo to show him in time. The bees seem content, crawling slowly through the curls.

Buru Tovo turns to Mrs. Etaris next, “Why don't you come along too, young lady?”

Mrs. Etaris pauses for a moment. She looks like she's about to refuse, so Cliopher adds, “Between Ser Rhodin and I, we can probably offer you a pardon for whatever crimes your mother-in-law thinks you have incurred by association.”

She laughs, and flips the sign on the shop to say 'closed'. Then she locks the door, and comes to stand next to Mr. Greenwing. “I can hardly be called young, sir,” she says to Buru Tovo, with a smile.

Cliopher suddenly realises he hasn't actually introduced anyone. “Sorry, Buru,” he says, “This is Mr. Greenwing, and Mrs. Etaris. Mrs. Etaris, and Mr. Greenwing, this is my great-uncle, Buru Tovo.”

They walk together back down the way to St-Noire.

“You got any other names?” Buru Tovo asks Mrs. Etaris. Cliopher flushes with second-hand embarrassment at the rudeness of the question.

Mrs. Etaris doesn't seem offended. “You can call me Jullanar, if you would prefer, sir,” she says, with a smile.

Buru Tovo snorts, “Kip used to say he was going to marry a Jullanar when he grew up.” He turns to Cliopher, “I guess this one's taken though. Too late, eh? Ain't I always saying you need a woman to keep you on your toes, boy?”

“Yes, Buru Tovo,” says Cliopher, carefully only looking at the stones of the road in front of him. He can't stand to see Mrs. Etaris looking offended, or Mr. Greenwing.

“Mr. Dart's niece is also called Jullanar,” says Mr. Greenwing, cheerfully, “I believe she isn't in a relationship with anyone at the moment. It's a pity she was out when we called by at Dart Hall, Sayo Mdang.” He pauses, and then adds, “Although, she is probably a little young.”

“Did he ever say why he liked the name, sir?” asks Mrs. Etaris, of Buru Tovo, “It is common here, but surely not on Zunidh?”

Buru Tovo shrugs. “Kip also said he was going to become fanoa with Aurelius Magnus.”

Finally, Cliopher can't help himself. “I said I was going to go to Astandalas and find out if the Emperor was worthy,” he says quietly, “I did, and he was. And I wasn't going to marry _any_ Jullanar, I just—” He manages to stop himself from saying any more. Slightly desperate, he tries to turn the conversation to anything else. “Mr. Greenwing, you didn't get to finish explaining about what happened at Winterturn.”

Mr. Greenwing, at least, is willing to continue his story and abandon grilling Cliopher about his relationships. Or lack thereof. Cliopher is listening, for the tale is fascinating, and is helping him fill in some of his protocols relating to possible appearances of the fey.

When it is over, and they are less than half way there, Cliopher turns to Mrs. Etaris. “Do you have any children? Or nieces and nephews?”

“I have a son and a daughter,” she says, with clear affection, “Both at school.”

“Do you think they'll follow you, after school?” asks Cliopher.

“Into the bookshop?” she asks, laughing, “Samantha, maybe. I doubt Benjy would be interested.”

Cliopher smiles, “I meant to Galderon.”

“Well,” says Mrs. Etaris, carefully, “I only had a choice of two universities, you know. I very nearly failed the exam entirely. I would hope they have more choices. Although Galderon ranks slightly higher than it did when I arrived.”

Buru Tovo snorts again, with a wink at Cliopher.

“As my great-uncle would no doubt love to tell you, I failed the Imperial Service exams four times,” says Cliopher.

Mr. Greenwing looks rather abashed. Cliopher suspects the young man has done rather better in his own exams, and finds it awkward that his elders did not.

“I did succeed the fifth time,” says Cliopher, with a smile, “I'm sure that should be some lesson about persisting to achieve your goals, but honestly I have always just been very stubborn. I am sure you had very many interesting experiences, even if Galderon wasn't your first choice, Mrs. Etaris.”

Mrs. Etaris smiles fondly, but doesn't say anything.

Cliopher turns to Mr. Greenwing, “You have Mr. Greenwing at the bookstore. Perhaps your children could take the place he doesn't want at the court of the King of Rondé.”

Mrs. Etaris laughs heartily at this. “I can just imagine Benjy at court,” she says, “All dressed up, trying to pretend he's important like his father would.”

“Court?” says Mr. Greenwing, weakly.

“Does the King of Rondé not hold court anymore?” asks Cliopher, genuinely, “Perhaps the ducal court in Fiellan then? Although, it's an Imperial title, maybe your grandmother attends only on the Lady Magus? It was the etiquette portion of the exam I failed four times, so I could well be mistaken. And I am sure things have changed since the Interim. After all, if they had not, I would have met you when you were introduced to his Radiancy.”

Mr. Greenwing only makes a sort of muffled squeak. 

“And are you at the centre of things in the court in the Palace of Stars?” asks Mrs. Etaris.

“Only when I act as the Hands of the Emperor, Mrs. Etaris,” says Cliopher, “Even then only literally. The focus is, of course, always his Radiancy; and anyway I am much more of a spectator myself.”

Mrs. Etaris manages to drag out some stories of the court, Cliopher's friendship with Lord Conju, and the culinary escapades he has had with Ser Rhodin. It is, he admits, very pleasant company. Mr. Greenwing and Mrs. Etaris laugh at all the right points, and even Buru Tovo only offers a couple of suggestive comments.

---

They arrive in St-Noire in the golden of the afternoon. Most of the village seems to be at or heading towards the inn, with the slow amble and gentle chatter of people who have finished what they need to do for the day. Clio is sitting outside the inn, on the edge of the steps, polishing a pair of boots. He waves to them, and points across the green.

Even at this distance, Cliopher can see two figures sitting down by the bee hives. Silently, the four of them agree to visit the bees first. As they walk closer, he can see that Oginu (who he vaguely recognises) is reading a book, and Rhodin is doing something with a bit of string. A few bees come from the hives to inspect them, but none of Buru Tovo's bees seem inclined to rejoin their fellows at a faster rate that his walk.

Oginu and Rhodin notice them, and Oginu is clearly about to put his book away and get up. Rhodin says something Cliopher can't quite hear, and the younger guard slumps back down. The hum of the bees gets very slightly louder, and then gentles down again. It seems Buru Tovo was right about the bees capturing the two guards.

“Hello, Rhodin,” says Cliopher.

“Cliopher, good to see you well. Thank you, Sayo Tovo for finding him for us,” says Rhodin, still continuing with his tatting.

Cliopher does a rapid mental calculation. He always hates etiquette. The second-in-command of the Imperial Guard, or the heir to an imperial title, who is introduced to whom. It sounds exactly like a question from his failed exams. “Ser Rhodin, may I introduce Mr. Greenwing, and Mrs. Etaris? Mr. Greenwing and Mrs. Etaris, this is Ser Rhodin, deputy commander of his Radiancy's guard, and Guard Oginu.”

Nobody seems to take offence, which is honestly about the best Cliopher can ever hope for.

Rhodin bows from his seated position to Mr. Greenwing. “My lord,” he says, and Mr. Greenwing visibly flinches, “Would you be so kind as to explain to the bees?”

“I'm not sure what they think the problem is, Ser Rhodin?” says Mr. Greenwing, “I've never known them to take a dislike to anyone before.”

“They are concerned,” says Ser Rhodin, “That as representatives of the Emperor of Astandalas, Oginu and I may be here to arrest a number of people they consider to be under their protection. Cliopher, apparently for the many things he has admitted to,” (Cliopher tries desperately to remember what he told Mr. Greenwing as they walked through the Woods) “And… Mrs. Etaris? For some historical crimes they believe she has been accused of.”

Mrs. Etaris looks completely dumbstruck. “You know?” she whispers to the bees, and three of them land on her sensible apron.

“I assure you, Mr. Greenwing, our only intention is to help Cliopher return to Zunidh. If he doesn't wish to go,” says Rhodin, but Cliopher interrupts.

“Of course I want to get back! I've been desperately trying to work out how to get you a message, so I wouldn't be declared dead by the time I managed to get through,” says Cliopher, frustrated. 

Mr. Greenwing shrugs, “Sorry bees, I think Ser Rhodin is probably safe. Thank you for your concern though, I do appreciate it.” He offers the hives a florid bow that ends with a heel click.

Cliopher looks from Rhodin, to the bees crawling over the hives, to Mrs. Etaris. She still looks a little fragile, watching the bees walk around her apron.

“Bees of the Woods Noirell,” says Cliopher, assuming his negotiating position, “As the Empire of Astandalas has fallen, any authority the Imperial Guard holds extends only to Zunidh. An extradition would require a warrant, sealed by the Emperor, and the King of Rondé. Even on Zunidh, any such accusations would require substantial proof. Village gossip is hardly sufficient, nor a shared name. The days of Emperors executing anyone they disliked are behind us.”

Mrs. Etaris looks up from the bees to Cliopher, worried.

“I thought you, Mrs. Etaris, and Mr. Greenwing, might like to know more of some of the legal reforms that have taken place on Zunidh in recent years,” says Cliopher, aware the bees are listening too, “For example, while a pardon is required to remove a name from the list of Terrors of Astandalas, every law that formed a consequence of that naming has been repealed. Where a marriage is found to be invalid due to coercion of one of the parties, any children are assumed to be given into the custody of the coerced partner, unless that conflicts with the welfare of the individual child. Lastly, Zunidh has open citizenship; all you need to apply is the support of an existing citizen of good standing.”

Ser Rhodin smiles, and nods. Then he does a double take, and looks more closely at Mrs. Etaris. “Ah,” he says quietly, “I see. Is his Radiancy aware…?”

“He sealed every repeal and new law himself,” says Cliopher, “He is the finest legal mind on Zunidh, possibly on the nine worlds, I am sure he was aware of what he was approving.”

Ser Rhodin bursts into laughter, and falls over on the grass. Oginu looks very confused, as does Mr. Greenwing. Mrs. Etaris looks pensive.

“It is purely informative,” says Cliopher, gently, “But perhaps if the way through Basil's cellar remains open, you could visit if you liked.”

The bees on Buru Tovo and Mrs. Etaris take flight, and weave their way back to the hives.

“The bees accept,” says Rhodin, levering himself back up to sitting. “Mr. Greenwing, Mrs. Etaris, should we head to the inn? It is getting dark.”
