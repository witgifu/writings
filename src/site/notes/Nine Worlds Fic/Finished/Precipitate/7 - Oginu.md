The view from the sky ship is spectacular. Oginu watches Solaara disappear until they were well into the Gray Mountains. Then, slightly embarrassed, he turns to Ser Rhodin. The older man is smiling at him gently.

“Have you not been on a sky ship before?” he asks.

“No sir,” replies Oginu, “I haven't before had the honour.”

Ser Rhodin nods. “A first experience to be treasured. Come find me when you're satiated.” He wanders off below decks.

Uncertain exactly what his superior means, Oginu continues watching the view. Perhaps half a bell later he decides to go and look for Ser Rhodin and see if there's anything he should be doing.

Ser Rhodin is in the stateroom, pouring over maps of Zunidh and Alinor. He looks up when Oginu comes in.

“Sir,” says Oginu, and salutes.

“Good,” says Rhodin, pushing aside the maps, “We need to work on our cover story.”

Oginu walks over to the table and takes the chair opposite Ser Rhodin. “Cover story, sir?” he asks. 

“Imagine if we land in Nanjin and tell someone we are two Imperial Guards looking for Sayo Mdang, who has gone missing,” says Rhodin, with a smile. Oginu wonders if maybe he actually does want to try that.

“There would be a riot, sir. But it's Nanjin, they have a riot most weeks,” says Oginu.

“True,” says Ser Rhodin, “But not everywhere on Zunidh is friendly to Imperial Guards, and it could cause political problems if we snuck into Alinor. If, however, I am simply a nobleman with a hobby of collecting folk stories, I can wander around the countryside talking to anyone.”

Oginu nods. If it's a political issue, well, that's far above his pay grade. But he can see where this is going, “Am I going to be your valet, sir? I don't think I really know how.”

“Well,” says Ser Rhodin, with a mischievous twist to his mouth, “I also don't know what to do with a valet. So if we did that, everyone would assume we were lovers. Not a problem in most places, but in Chare I've heard they've made it illegal.”

Blinking several times, Oginu asks, “Made taking a lover illegal, sir? You have to get married?”

“No, or, well, not yet anyway. Two men is the problem,” says Ser Rhodin, shaking his head. “Do you have any hobbies or skills? I know you are a great guardsman.”

Oginu preens inside at the praise. “I like reading, sir,” he says, trying to think of any particular skills he has that might be unusual for a guard, “I know a lot of knots? You already know I enjoy camping, sir.”

Ser Rhodin thinks for a moment. “I was wondering if you could pass as a naturalist, maybe with a speciality in frogs. But we should probably keep it simple. We're friends from the same lending library with an interest in folk tales. You know how to camp, and I know how to get invited to a country party.”

“Yes, sir,” says Oginu, “Do I need an occupation? Or am I a guard on extended holiday?” This is rather like getting to write his own adventure story, and he's invested now.

“I would probably be easier if people didn't know you knew how to fight,” says Ser Rhodin, “Maintain the element of surprise.”

Oginu looks down at his heavily muscled arm and chest. Unless he covers himself from neck to knees in loose robes, there's really no hiding that he spends a lot of time working out. “Um, possibly I could be a strongman? Or a tour guide?”

Ser Rhodin snaps his fingers, “Yes, if you're a tour guide for nobles in Zunidh wanting a bit of adventure, then I can hire you to help me with camping and so on. And I'm refusing to bring servants or a horse because I'm an eccentric trying to be authentic.”

“Thank you, sir,” says Oginu. He's looking forward to getting to make up silly stories of mountain escapades with hapless nobles. Which will only be lightly based on what new recruits get up to. 

“Can I still call you sir, sir? Or would 'my lord' be more correct?” asks Oginu, uncertain.

Ser Rhodin visibility flinches at the second question. “Sir or Ser Rhodin is fine,” he says, not making eye contact.

“Thank you sir,” says Oginu, relieved. He didn't want to slip up. Ser Rhodin's reaction makes Oginu wonder if it's something to do with the Fall that makes him not want to be called 'my lord'. Or maybe it's just too close to how he addresses his Radiancy.

“Is the ribbon still pointing west, sir?” asks Oginu, curious. Perhaps they will be going to Cisven, or the City of Emeralds.

Pulling the crimson knot out of the chest pocket of his Alinorial-style jacket, Ser Rhodin closes his eyes. “Yes,” he says firmly, “Still heading along the usual path of the Lights.”

Oginu is impressed that the magic knows about the Lights. Going by sky ship is much faster than hiking over the Grey Mountains would have been. But then, the same great mage made the Lights and the finding charm, so perhaps it's expected.

---

The next two days are unremarkable. He and Ser Rhodin exercise. The crew fly the ship. Skies are clear, and the red ribbon continues to point along the sky ship route.

When they pass Isiguro and the ribbon points directly at Gorjo City, Ser Rhodin frowns very slightly. The captain jokingly asks if perhaps Sayo Mdang hasn't just gone on holiday, and they got the dates wrong. 

The sky ship docks at the spire in Gorjo City. Not only is there no further Light to pull the ship onward, but the captain rightly points out that a sea ship will know the area better and move at a similar speed.

Ser Rhodin disembarks with a letter in his hand. Oginu wonders what it contains, is it the first report back to Commander Omo? It doesn’t seem particularly thick, but then nothing much has happened. 

The Light Minders, both colourfully and casually dressed, turn when Oginu and Ser Rhodin enter the top room. They are dressed in civilian clothes, although Ser Rhodin has said to follow his cues on if they were being honest or using their cover story. Oginu decides this means he should shut up and wait.

“Is one of you Ser Rhodin?” asks the woman standing by a stack of letters on the table.

“Yes, that’s me,” says Ser Rhodin. His tone is jovial, but he surely must not expect to be recognised.

“Excellent,” says the woman, and hands him a stack of letters. She shamelessly ogles both of them.

“I wonder what I forgot to bring?” says Ser Rhodin, with a laugh.

“I’m surprised you didn’t bring Cousin Kip!” she replies, “He’s the only one who usually comes to the Spire, everyone else goes to the princess’s palace.”

Ser Rhodin doesn’t reply, but leans up against the wall and slips the seal on the first letter. After a quick scan, he chuckles and opens the next one. “Hm, well, someone back home has been reading my mind,” he says with a wink at Oginu, who tries to smile back. “When do I need to get my letter back here to get it to Solaara today, so my friends stop worrying?”

“In two hours, for an hour,” the letter manager says, with a smile, “and then again six hours after that.”

“I'll see you in two hours then,” says Ser Rhodin, and gives her what can only be called a flirtatious smile and wink. She titters back at him, and Oginu wishes he were anywhere except watching his _boss_ flirt.

Thankfully, they descend to the ground without further incident. Oginu looks around, and the city spread out before him is like nothing he's ever seen. He'd thought Solaara's golden sandstone beautiful, but Gorjo City is painted — red, yellow, green, blue — seemingly every building a different colour. Above, the rainforest and the volcano peaks. Below, the perfect cerulean of the sea. It looks like something out of a painting that he would dismiss as an artist's fancy.

“Well,” says Ser Rhodin, “I could get used to that view. I see why Cliopher comes back here every chance he gets.”

He unfolds one of the letters again, “Conju has kindly given me directions to three different hotels, with the caveat that he has only been inside the bar of one of them.”

They begin to walk together into the city. “It seems Conju thinks the best course of action is for us to introduce ourselves to Cliopher's mother,” says Ser Rhodin, settling the straps of his pack, “Although he kindly included a letter from his Radiancy to the Princess as well. Hopefully we won't need that.”

“Why, sir?” asks Oginu. Usually going right to the top is the best way to get things done quickly.

Ser Rhodin gives him and assessing look that he remembers from his training. He squirms a little.

“You haven't seen a Council of Princes session,” says Ser Rhodin, “She's an idiot.”

It's true, Oginu typically gets morning or late night shifts. So he hasn't yet witnessed the Council of Princes. But he's surprised to hear Ser Rhodin speak so casually and scathingly of a ruling Princess in that way. 

“She must have a credible seneschal though, or the province would have fallen apart,” continues Ser Rhodin, not bothering to keep his voice down. “But still, Conju recommends the mother, and he writes to her occasionally, so we will start there.”

They pass a series of cafés and hole-in-the-wall shops all producing delicious smells. “After lunch,” says Ser Rhodin, and joins the queue under a sign that reads 'Sandwich Clinic'. The first word having been painted over 'Veterinary'.

Oginu joins the queue as well. The sandwiches in the hands of those leaving do look fantastic, piled high with fried fish.
