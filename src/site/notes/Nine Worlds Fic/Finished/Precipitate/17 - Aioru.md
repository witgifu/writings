It feels like Aioru has only just closed his eyes, when he is woken by one of Lord Conju's servants. They let him know there is another set of fresh clothes in the dressing room (where are these all coming from?) and leaves a covered tray on the little table.

Aioru hurries through a wash, uncertain how much time he actually has. The tray, it transpires, has two sweet buns and a mug of strong coffee. He doesn't remember telling anyone his breakfast preferences, and he feels slightly strange that someone has worked it out. Or guessed correctly. Well, it isn't worth wasting a good breakfast just because he wasn't expecting it.

While he is eating, the bells ring, it's half a bell after the fifth of the night. When he is finished, he ventures out into the hall. He hasn't heard anything, but perhaps the others have just been very quiet. There he finds Lord Conju's majordomo.

“If you would like to head to the sky ship now, Sayo Aioru, I'm sure that would be fine,” she says, “The others may take a little longer.”

Aioru collects his writing kit, and walks to the sky ship dock. When he gets all the way to the top, he finds he is just in time to watch Princess Oriana board. She looks tousled, as if she had been dragged from bed. Her maid behind her is trying to convince her to wear a wrap, and a number of disgruntled guards seem to have been pressed into acting as porters for her many trunks. There are less of the Princess's staff in evidence than Aioru expected; perhaps they are already on board. It would not surprise him if she has been allowed to board last, and get the most sleep.

The Princess's sky ship, now she has been stowed below decks, casts off and floats away from the tower. The other two ships are clearly still waiting for people to board. It's immediately obvious which one is the Emperor's flagship, with its Imperial yellow sails. Aioru dithers, uncertain if he should try to get himself out of the way, or attempt to make himself useful. He will be no use with the ship itself, he knows nothing of sailing.

“You going to be on the flagship?” asks a woman in the Palace staff uniform, delivering crates to the deck.

“Yes” he says, “Can I help?”

“It's all food for the kitchen,” she says, “Or I suppose galley, since it's a ship. Anyway, if you could take the ones stamped with the sun-in-glory across, that will at least give me some more space.”

“Of course,” says Aioru, picking up two of the crates, which contain smaller paper packages.

He has no trouble with the rope bridge, heights have never particularly bothered him, although he knows that being quite _so_ high would disturb a good many people. Aioru winds his way around the sailors, doing important things with ropes and carrying other burdens onto the ship. 

Finding his way based on the diagrams he only half-remembers, he eventually spies a door marked 'galley'. The kind head chef from the previous night is there — have they not slept? — and directs Aioru where to put the crates.

“Go find your berth and hide there,” they say, with a wink, “Or you'll be conscripted as a kitchen hand.”

Aioru smiles, and wanders back down the hallway. The doors all have little slots for cards, he realises, so they can be labelled with their current use. Some have names on them, or what is stored there. At the end of the hallway, he can see through the open door of the Sun Cabin.

It wasn't obvious on the maps, but when Aioru finds the door with his name on it, there is nobody else listed. That's mildly embarrassing, he saw many senior staff were sharing. But then he remembers that they are reusing plans, so this must have been Sayo Mdang's room. No doubt Aioru will be assigned elsewhere on the way back.

He opens it, to find a small but efficient room. A porthole window to the outside, a bed, a table that folds down from the wall. There must be a shared washroom elsewhere. A cupboard fills the remaining space next to the door.  

Opening the cupboard, Aioru is relieved to find his bags have also arrived. He closes the cupboard again, and sits on the bed. What should he do now?

Did those colour-coded documents also include a schedule for his Radiancy? In the Palace Sayo Mdang attends him from the third bell of the morning until noon. Will he want the same in the air? Possibly. For lack of much else to do, Aioru pulls down the table, sets out his writing kit, and cleans his pens.

Not long after, there is a piercing whistle, and the sound of feet from all over the ship. Someone he doesn't recognise runs past the open door and says to him, “Come on!”

Leaving his writing kit behind, Aioru slips out the door and up to the deck. Everyone is lined up in an order that they all know. It must be for the Emperor's boarding. The whistle is blown again, just as Aioru slips into a gap at the back. It's probably not where he should be, but it's better than blocking the stairs.

Spears stamp, and everyone on deck falls into the obeisance. There's barely space, and so Aioru's face is somewhat uncomfortably close to the rear of the person in front of him. Presumably at his Radiancy's signal, everyone rises again. Then they all file back down into the ship. Aioru cannot think what purpose that ceremony could possibly serve, with more than half the staff unable to even see anything.

He returns to his room, and packs up his writing kit, leaving the door open. If Lord Conju boards soon, he would like to inquire if there is a schedule for the day. Or if there are any dispatch boxes sent by the Offices of State for him.

The next person down the hall isn't Lord Conju, or any of the other household staff. It's Pikabe, one of the inner guards, in full panoply. Aioru shimmies himself around so he can stand. He only makes it just in time, so he can bow low as his lord walks by.

His Radiancy walks briskly. Lord Conju follows behind, but Aioru doesn't dare interrupt him. The Groom of the Chamber's face is perfectly composed, but Aioru wonders if everything really has gone smoothly. It seems impossible to organise so many people with such little notice.

One of the trailing guards gives Aioru a look he can only interpret as 'come along'. So he snatches his writing kit from the table, and follows them to the doorway of the Sun Cabin. His Radiancy swirls around just before the door to the balcony, and looks at him.

Aioru, of course, drops into the obeisance. He then has to try to suppress a groan and wince of pain as the edge of the door frame hits his shins. When he is rapidly waved up he suspects he was unsuccessful.

His Radiancy directs Aioru to a desk on the side of the ostentatiously large room. As quickly as he can, Aioru takes his place and pulls out his tools. His Radiancy begins pacing and dictating. Drafts of memos, responses to reports, letters of inquiry. Someone has kindly placed the morning reports on the desk as well, so Aioru can reference them. 

His lord is fast, so Aioru is focusing too closely on the work to notice when the ship departs. And of course, once they have, there are no bells. Instead, he just feels he has been running at full pace, like an intense brainstorming session in the Offices.

When Lord Conju arrives with tea for his Radiancy, Aioru is relieved to have a moment to organise the papers that have accrued on his desk. This absorbs him enough that Lord Conju has to make a small cough to get his attention.

The Groom of the Chamber is offering Aioru a cup of tea (tea!) and a biscuit. “Thank you, Lord Conju,” Aioru says very quietly, and manages to arrange a small square of space for it. He looks back up to see Lord Conju giving him a disapproving look, which means he must have just broken one of the many special rules of etiquette that surround his Radiancy. He will have to ask for a list of his mistakes later.

With a smile, Aioru takes a tiny sip of the hot tea, picks up his pen, and looks to his Radiancy. His lord has accepted the comfortable chair that Lord Conju coaxed him into, and is sipping tea while looking out the window.

“Sayo Aioru,” he says, without taking his eyes from the view, “It seems we have been… Overly trusting in our Princess to disseminate important news. It would seem prudent to look for additional channels.”

Aioru takes another sip of tea to give himself a moment to gather his thoughts. “My lord currently has three institutions whose influence is broadly distributed. The Imperial Cult, the Post Office, and the Ministry of the Common Weal. Any or all of those could be venues for sharing news, in addition to the memos that are already sent to the major newspapers.”

“Not the cult,” says his Radiancy, firmly. Privately Aioru agrees, but he also knows that for those who do follow it, receiving information via those channels *would* be taken more seriously. He didn't expect his Radiancy to be so decidedly opposed.

“Very well, my lord,” says Aioru, “Then the Post Office has by far the larger network. In many towns too small to have their own Office for the Ministry of the Common Weal, the Post Office is able to provide some of those functions. However, for complex processes, or if translation is required, the Ministry of the Common Weal is better equipped.”

They continue the discussion until his Radiancy has finished his cup of tea. It reminds Aioru very much of the discussions that go on in the Offices of State, and makes him feel much more at ease. As if his Radiancy is not a god, not the Emperor, but only Sayo Mdang's boss. 

And then Aioru's half-finished cup of tea and neglected biscuit are whisked away with his Radiancy's empty ones. He remembers where he is, and who he is with, and why he is here. Shuffling the paperwork to take advantage of the extra space, he looks up only far enough to see his lord is standing again. Quickly, he slides a fresh sheet forward, pen poised to begin.

Before long, Lord Conju is back, silently laying out a luncheon for his Radiancy at the dinning table. Of course, his Radiancy cannot miss it, since the room is only partitioned with screens. While it is large for a room on a ship, it is tiny by the standards of the rooms in the Palace of Stars. Aioru glances at the table, where there is one place setting, one chair. It commands the best view out the windows.

Perhaps it strikes him more than usual, because most of his own lunches are with colleagues. They laugh and talk about work with each other as they head to the nearest refectory, trading stories and gossip and news over curries and stews. Today, he supposes, he will be eating alone in his room. Like his Radiancy, looking out the window.

As far as Aioru knows, his Radiancy's colleagues are just Lord Conju and Sayo Mdang. Perhaps also Commander Omo. He had thought there might have been someone in the Ouranatha as well, except for his Radiancy's earlier dismissal of using the Imperial Cult as a news distribution channel. Lord Conju clearly cares a great deal for his Radiancy, but is exceedingly formal. Perhaps that is what his Radiancy likes? If that were so, however, Aioru does not think Sayo Mdang could have lasted for a thousand years as the Private Secretary.

Quietly, Aioru puts away his tools, and organises his documents. As his Radiancy sits down to his solitary lunch, Aioru slips out the door, and back to his room.
