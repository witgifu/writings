Cliopher is walking through the central part of Ragnor Bella, when he spots Mr. Greenwing. The young man is chatting to a motherly looking woman, in the doorway of a bookshop. The whole scene looks perfectly charming.

He must stop and look, because shortly Mr. Greenwing waves him over.

“Good morning again, Mr. Greenwing,” Cliopher says as he approaches, “I hope I did not make you too late?”

“Not at all, not at all. Sayo Mdang, this is the proprietor of Elderflower books, Mrs. Etaris. Mrs. Etaris, this is Sayo Mdang, who as I said, fell out of the sky,” says Mr. Greenwing.

Cliopher is not entirely sure if having fallen out of the sky is a qualification. Nevertheless, he smiles politely, and offers a respectful bow to a prosperous merchant. “Good morning, Mrs. Etaris.”

“She was just telling me how important it is to be hospitable to people who fall out of portals in the sky,” says Mr. Greenwing, “And she very kindly suggested that she would handle the store today so I could escort you.”

Cliopher again bows his thanks to Mrs. Etaris. He's still not entirely sure what correct manners are on Alinor, but since it seems more formal than home, he defaults to his court ones. “My thanks, for sparing your assistant, Mrs. Etaris. Do you often find people who have fallen through portals in the sky?”

“Only once!” she says with a warm look at both of them, “A very long time ago now. You can make it up to me by doing the accounts, Mr. Greenwing.” She shoos both of them across to the bakery to get something to eat for lunch while they walk.

“I might be able to help with the accounts, if you need, Mr. Greenwing,” says Cliopher when they are inside the bakery and out of earshot of Mrs. Etaris.

“I don't mind, actually, Sayo Mdang,” says Mr. Greenwing, with a small smile, “Something about the tallies and numbers is soothing. It's just that you tend to get interrupted a lot.”

Cliopher gives Mr. Greenwing a broad grin, “I have often said the same, Mr. Greenwing, but my friends despair of me for it.” 

“Better be careful who you hear say that, or you two'll have requests from every business in town,” says the baker, handing Mr. Greenwing a paper packet that smells of fresh bread and sharp cheese.

On the walk out of town, Cliopher finally gets up the courage to ask the question that has been nagging at him. “So what *is* the currency system these days? Who mints the coins?”

Mr. Greenwing happily begins explaining the coinage, pulling a couple of examples from his pocket. Then he explains which kingdoms and duchies mint which coins. There is a long aside about the mint in Fiellan, which seems to be some kind of architectural folly. Unusual enough that a rather bad epic poem by an important general was written about it. Cliopher can't quite picture it, but Mr. Greenwing describes it as somewhere between a boat set on its stern, and a tall mushroom.

This happy discussion manages to bring them all the way to the edge of the forest. Or rather, the woods.

“Please, whatever you may see in the woods, Sayo Mdang, do not stray from the path,” says Mr. Greenwing. He hands Cliopher a bun filled with sliced cheese.

“Of course, Mr. Greenwing,” says Cliopher, politely, “I heard much the same when I was here before, and also from Mr. Dart's directions. But, could you tell me what the danger is?”

“A sensible question, and one which shouldn't be answered on the path. Let us say for now, Sayo Mdang, only that you might have an even more unusual adventure than you have already had this morning,” says Mr. Greenwing, with a slightly tense smile. He takes a nibble of his own bun.

“Of course, Mr. Greenwing,” says Cliopher, understanding that there might be many reasons why telling him here and now would be a bad idea. “You said you studied poetry, I think? Would that be modern works, or Classical?”

“Well,” says Mr. Greenwing, “Theoretically in Modern Shaian, but they thought themselves intellectuals, you know, so they like to use Classical illusions…”

Cliopher isn't sure if he entirely understands some of the more complex metaphor deconstructions, but he does enjoy Mr. Greenwing's enthusiasm for his subject. It is also clear he had put some of his theories to the test (although, Cliopher isn't quite sure _why_ he had been in the prison), and found himself correct.

The topic of poetry, despite Cliopher's limited knowledge, manages to last them until they arrive at St-Noire.

It looks much like Cliopher remembers, if a bit more run down and quiet. He can see evidence that the Bee has closed off some of its rooms. He holds his breath as he walks towards the front door, afraid to even think to himself what he might find within.

But then a boy comes bounding out, with not quite the same colouring as Mr. Greenwing, Mr. Dart, and his family. More like Cliopher's own. “Hello, Mr. Greenwing,” he says with enthusiasm, “Are you here to see dad? He's down in the cellar at the moment, but he should be up soon.”

“Clio?” breathes Cliopher, softly. Too softly, perhaps, for the boy to hear.

“I was showing Sayo Mdang the way,” says Mr. Greenwing, with a curious look between the two of them.

“Mdang?” says the boy, eyes wide, “Are you my uncle? One of my uncles? You're dressed so fancy, are you Uncle Kip, who works at the Palace? Is that your uniform?”

“Yes,” squeaks Cliopher, all his courtly graces abandoning him.

Just at that moment, someone else comes out into the light. He shades his eyes with his hands, as if he had just been somewhere quite dark, and was now struggling to see.

“Mr. Greenwing! Thank you for dropping by I—,” he says, and then stops, dropping his arm and staring open-mouthed. “Kip! Whatever are you doing here? How did you _get_ here?”

“It's a long tale,” says Cliopher, grinning from ear to ear.

“Of the sea?” says Basil, with a chortle, “We're a long way from the sea here.”

“I fell out of the sky,” says Kip, still staring at Basil, struggling to believe this is real. But, he does look older, as surely Cliopher does. And Clio isn't a baby, or even a toddler, but a child of an age to help his parents around the inn.

Basil looks rather confused by this pronouncement.

“I can confirm,” says Mr. Greenwing, “I saw Sayo Mdang fall myself. He seemed totally unharmed by the experience.”

Basil and Cliopher close the few steps left between them, both staring at the other like they expect the other to disappear. Finally, they grasp arms, and touch their heads together. Basil is real, Cliopher can feel the muscle of his arms, the warmth of his breath, the smell of his soap.

“I wrote to you,” says Cliopher, slightly awkwardly, “But given what I heard at the post office this morning, I wouldn't be surprised if you didn't get them.”

“I didn't, and I haven't had any letters from anyone back home,” says Basil, “I couldn't bare to write, not knowing if anyone would be there to receive them.”

“And I couldn't stop writing, just in case you were there,” says Cliopher, “But everyone is there, Basil. There was almost no damage in the Vangaveye-ve. Mama is there, and Vinyë, even Buru Tovo.”

Basil takes a big, shuddering breath in. “I am *so* glad to hear that.”

“Is Sara…” asks Cliopher, anxiously.

“In the kitchen! Come in, come in, both of you,” says Basil, waving to Mr. Greenwing. “Surely you have heard me talk enough about my Cousin Kip enough, Mr. Greenwing, that you practically already know him.”

Mr. Greenwing looks from Basil to Cliopher confused. “Are you telling me you were chased through Gorjo City with the _Personal Secretary to the Emperor_, Mr. White?”

They turn together and walk into the taproom. “You're the Emperor's secretary, Kip? Of course you are, you always said you'd be Elonoa'a,” says Basil chuckling. “And is your Aurelius as magnificent as you hoped?”

“You know, I can't decide if it's worse blasphemy to say 'yes' or 'no'?” says Cliopher, “But Basil, I wasn't joking when he said I fell out of the sky. I really need to get back to Zunidh as quickly as possible.”

“You probably want Mr. Greenwing, or his friend Mr. Dart for that kind of problem, Kip,” says Basil, with a shake of his head, “I haven't left St-Noire since the Interim.”

“Sorry,” says Cliopher, awkwardly, “I didn't mean… I just meant I can't stay long.”

Basil claps him on the back. “Just knowing you're alive is worth _everything_, Kip.”

Sara spots Cliopher in the hallway, through the kitchen door. She drops the mug she's holding, with a soft “Oh!” Thankfully it's already empty, and nothing breaks.

They spend a happy while catching up in the kitchen, before Cliopher broaches the subject of his return to Zunidh again. 

“I'm afraid I don't know of any portals, other than the one the Ambassador goes through,” says Mr. Greenwing. “You hear stories, of course, of someone's neighbour's friend's cousin who has come back from a trip to the barn seventy years older and claiming to have been a street performer on Ysthar. But never anything one could act on.”

“You could talk to the bees, Mr. Greenwing,” says Sara, enigmatically, “Unless Mr. Dart…?”

Mr. Greenwing shakes his head. “We had breakfast with Mr. Dart this morning. He seemed curious, Mrs. White, but he did not make any offer of assistance. I would not want to pressure him.”

“He might ask Ballory,” said Sara, “But you should tell the bees anyway, Mr. Greenwing, they'll want to know.”

“Would Mrs. Etaris perhaps have a helpful book?” asks Cliopher.

“I can't remember any on intermundial travel,” says Mr. Greenwing, sadly, “But it is probably worth asking, just in case.”

“There you go Kip, that's three people to ask,” says Basil, “Mr. Greenwing better head out now if he wants to talk to the bees before they're asleep for the night. Then come back here, both of you. I don't want to hear of either of you walking through the woods in the dark.”

Cliopher smiles, so grateful at the way Basil has welcomed him back into being family. How different this reunion is to the one in the Vangaveye-ve with the rest of his family, all those years ago. Perhaps one tear escapes, but nobody mentions it.

Mr. Greenwing tries to protest that he is fine to walk back, has done so before in worse weather. But it's Clio's insistence that they tell Cliopher's tale in full after dinner that convinces him to stay.

Basil stays to tend the bar, while Cliopher and Mr. Greenwing wander across the village commons. There's a row of hives up against the trees. Some looking new and modern, just like the ones the Palace beekeeper had proudly shown Cliopher one day. Some looking more traditional, like the ones he'd spotted about Ragnor Bella. In the middle is a pyramid of pottery cylinders, unlike anything he has seen before.

“The oldest hives,” says Mr. Greenwing, with a wave of his hand. Cliopher can see how they almost resemble a honeycomb structure. He wonders when they were built.

Mr. Greenwing adds, quietly, “That we know of.”

Unbidden comes the image to Cliopher's mind of a giant stone version of the old hives, deep in the woods, thrumming with bees.

There are only a few bees now, heading back to their homes. Darkness comes quickly at the edge of the trees.

“Good evening,” says Mr. Greenwing, addressing the hives, “I wanted to let you know that Mr. Basil White's cousin, Cliopher sayo Mdang is here. He's safe, and Mr. White is very happy. Um.”

Cliopher can't see Mr. Greenwing well enough, with the light and the angle, to tell if he's blushing. He sounds awkward, anyway.

“But he needs to get back to Zunidh, so if you know any way he could do that, we would very much appreciate it,” adds Mr. Greenwing, in somewhat of a rush. Then he clicks his heels, and offers the hive a very florid bow.

Cliopher bows as well, deciding somewhat arbitrarily to offer them what he would give an Imperial Duke. It's been a while since he's used that one, but then, it seems to have been a day for things from the past.
