Lord Conju holds court — Aioru can't think of a better term for it — in the staff hall behind the kitchen. Various people come, and go. Staff ask questions, drop off notes, report goods transferred or people notified. In the centre, Lord Conju directs.

The letter to Vinyë has been sent, and all Airou feels he can do now is to sit and wait for the reply. Well, perhaps it doesn't hurt to be one room away from the kitchen, when the head cook has taken a liking to him. There is a selection of food that could be eaten on the go set out on the tables, but it seems that the plates nearest Aioru are refilled the fastest.

Lord Conju hadn't even let him go and fetch his own bags from the Offices. He'd sent someone else to collect them, and stow them in the berth he had assigned to Aioru. There are colour-coded maps and charts laying on the tables, apparently adapted from the last tour. 

Three sky ships are going. His Radiancy's flagship, of course, which will have Lord Conju, a small guard contingent, and some key staff. Including, to Aioru's embarrassment, him. The Princess's ship, which is being commandeered, will include her and most of the rest of the guards they are taking. The final sky ship will have a truly impressive amount of luggage, and the rest of the staff that are going with them.

The bells ring the first hour of the evening, when a very sweaty and out-of-breath page appears in the doorway. “Urgent letter,” she gasps, “For Lord Conju.”

Lord Conju takes the letter himself, and a kitchen hand leads the poor page to a seat and a glass of water. Lord Conju slides a thin finger under the seal, and flicks it open. His eyes dart across the page.

Aioru freezes, wondering if the letter is the one they have been hoping for.

Looking up, Lord Conju offers Aioru a small smile. “You were correct,” he says.

Nodding, Aioru stands up. “Thank you, Lord Conju. How can I help?” he asks, nervously.

“I will be too busy to attend court this evening,” he says, waving elegantly to indicate the charts and lists, “But someone should go and report on the result.”

“I would be delighted, Lord Conju,” says Aioru, with a smile. 

“Good,” says Lord Conju, “I need to attend his Radiancy.” He sweeps out of the room.

There's three-quarters of a bell until court starts. That seems like a lot of time to dress, to Aioru. But perhaps his Radiancy also has a bath.

Looking down at his robes, Aioru rather wishes he had a clean change. The stress has left him a little sweaty, and despite the spells, the periodicals in the Archives do always manage to impart a musty smell. But all his clothes are in the city or the sky ship.

Someone, however, notices him. “Sayo Aioru?” comes a small voice from behind.

He turns around to find a young woman, who looks surprised by her own question. Something in her demeanour warns him to tread softly. Perhaps it is just the way the two other women in the room immediately give him cool assessing looks.

“Yes ma'am?” he asks, on his best manners.

“Would you like a wash and a fresh uniform? We can request one in your size from the palace stores. It won't be tailored, I'm afraid…” she says, looking embarrassed.

“None of my uniforms are tailored, ma'am,” he says with a smile, “Please don't worry about me. I know you're all very busy, and I wouldn't want to put you to any trouble.”

She gives him a look up and down, “No, it really would better if you weren't seen coming out of the Imperial Apartments and heading to court like that,” she says more confidently.

“Yes ma'am,” says Aioru, slightly bewildered, “What would you like me to do?”

“Come with me, please, Sayo Aioru,” she says, and walks out in the direction of the back rooms.

Aioru rushes to catch up, “Excuse me, ma'am, but who are you?”

“Oh, I'm Féonie Durgalen, I work for Lady Ylette,” she says.

That last part probably explains her critical eye for his uniform. “Thank you, ma'am,” he says, dodging around two people carrying what must be a heavy wicker basket.

Saya Durgalen points to a door labelled 'washrooms', with a smile. “It's nothing fancy, but you should find soap and towels and everything you need. A clean outfit will be left for you… you're, what, a 25-thumb chest and 24-thumb hip?”

“Um, yes?” says Aioru, “Sorry, I always have to check I never remember.”

Tutting, Saya Durgalen pulls a tape measure from her pocket. “Under your arms, and around your hip bones,” she says, and drops it into his hand.

Aioru does as instructed, and reads out the numbers. Saya Durgalen had guessed exactly. Then he rolls up the tape and returns it to her with a bow. He is careful not to make contact with her, he noticed she was carefully leaving space around him. Aioru isn't sure if it's cultural or personal, but he is happy to respect her choice either way.

“Thank you very much, Saya Durgalen,” he says, “I will endeavour to be a appropriate ambassador of the Tower to the Court this evening.”

She smiles, and turns away. “Don't recount the gossip before I get there!” she says with a laugh, as she disappears around the corner.

Aioru opens the door to the washrooms, wondering vaguely when and where he is going to sleep tonight.

Upon arriving, Aioru's initial impression is that the court is subdued. Perhaps it's the expected absence of the Emperor, or maybe it's just that there's not any truly juicy gossip today. Well, both those things are about to change.

He takes up a position on the side, with the watchful parents, and starts to mentally tick off who is here. There are representatives from all the provinces, but perhaps only half the Princes. Only a couple of mid-level members of the Ouranatha have decided it's worth coming. Or perhaps some people are planning on turning up late? It's usually considered rude to turn up more than a quarter bell after the Emperor enters.

Two more of the Jilkano royals trickle in, but then the musicians pause, and everyone turns to the Grand Duchess, standing by the steps of the lower dais. She doesn't move up the steps, and the silence sharpens.

The Emperor's door opens, and his Radiancy sweeps in. There is a rustle of silks, as everyone, including Aioru, drops into the obeisance. His Radiancy will often wave everyone to stand before he reaches the throne. Always before he sits down. Tonight he does it *as he sits*, and nobody could possibly miss the message. He is displeased. But is it with the court in general or someone in particular?

The musicians begin a new piece. Although the court has risen, everyone has at least one eye on the Emperor. If his reluctance to release them hasn't been enough of a clue, surely his sartorial choices are. His Radiancy and Lord Conju have conspired an outfit that is both magnificently dramatic, and manages to echo in every line the Emperor's robes of judgement. Black is the predominant colour, with hints of gold edging, and flashes of imperial yellow from layers below.

Over the next quarter bell, the court becomes packed. Nobles have thrown on court outer robes over whatever they were wearing. Princesses and Princes have arrived with their hair slightly less than perfect. Even many of Aioru's colleagues have turned up, dashing over when they spot him and asking what they've missed.

After half a bell, Princess Oriana arrives. It is clear she decided that impeccable dress was more important than prompt arrival. Her Jilkanese-style outfit is immaculate, edged in foamwork. Her hair is dripping with pearls. She settles in amongst the various factions of her family.

Moments later, his Radiancy leans forward ever so slightly. The flash of his jewels caused by the movement causes the throne room to stop. Everyone silently turns to face him directly. “We are disappointed to announce,” he says, quietly, “The arrest on charges of treason of Princess Oriana.”

Seemingly out of nowhere, a group of Imperial Guards surround the princess. She is pale, staring around at her family for a moment. Aioru wonders if she is going to fake a swoon. Apparently she is smart enough not to, and instead falls prettily to her knees, eyes on the throne.

“The proper procedures will need to be followed, of course. But we have been presented with evidence of her repeatedly failing to publish our decrees. This would be a forswearing of her oaths, and a violation of the Littleridge Treaty.”

“Glorious One,” (even Aioru could have told her not to use that form of address) “Please do not listen to this slander, I have always been a loyal servant of your Imperial Majesty,” Princess Oriana entreats, her hands raised, tears sparkling on face.

Nobody else moves. She looks from the throne to her family members and toadies. None of them will look at her, all of them are carefully facing forward, watching his Radiancy.

He ignores her supplication. “We are therefore forced to cut the current session short, as we will need to travel to her province for the investigation and trial,” says his Radiancy. He gestures, and the musicians begin a gentle waltz, correctly reading the tension in the room needs soothing.

Aioru watches as the guards close in on Princess Oriana. “Get your hands—” he hears her snap, but then she looks back up at the Emperor, who is most definitely watching her. Perhaps, in another place, at another time, some of her supporters might have felt empowered to stand beside her. Not now.

She is escorted out of the throne room by eight guards. Not one of her ladies or toadies dares to follow immediately, although no doubt they will not stay long. 

Aioru sighs, thinking that she will presumably be held in her luxury suite, and be allowed all her servants and staff. If he, or any other commoner, was arrested for treason, they would be held in the dungeon cells. Sayo Mdang and his Radiancy have worked hard to get more rights for commoners, and less special privileges for nobles, but there's still further to go.

After her departure, there's a lot less dancing and a lot more talking. A few people ask Aioru if he knows anything more, and he denies it. Which is true, kind of. He hasn't read the reply to Lord Conju's letter.

After another half-bell, his Radiancy makes another new move. At a signal that Aioru must have missed, the Grand Duchess approaches the lower dais. His Radiancy gestures for her to come further, all the way to the bottom of the stairs to the _upper_ dais. 

“We hope our court has proved peaceable over the past few days, Duchess?” says his Radiancy.

The Grand Duchess curtsies before replying, “My lord, you have been dearly missed, but everyone has been delightful.”

Has this conversation been pre-arranged, Aioru wonders? But if it had, when could it have been? Surely not while his Radiancy was dressing, but perhaps the Grand Duchess had dinner with his Radiancy. They have not been known to have dinner before, but then, these past few days have seen many changes.

“That is welcome news. It is unfortunate that we must depart unexpectedly, and be without your company,” says his Radiancy. Aioru wonders if that 'your' is singular or plural. Does his Radiancy enjoy court? It has never occurred to him before to even pose the question.

“The world knows of your great dedication to your duty, my lord,” she replies, “I am sure the whole court wishes you well.”

This is apparently the end of their discussion, as the Grand Duchess withdraws. The music takes a more lively tone, and the younger set seem willing to ignore the drama for now and dance. Aioru watches them from his position along the side.

Thankfully, that is the extent of the major drama for the evening. There is a minor moment when someone accidentally slights Prince Rufus's favourite composer, but the moment is quickly smoothed over by his very capable wife.

Just before midnight, his Radiancy departs, ending court for the evening. Aioru has a brief moment of wondering where he should go, before remembering he was asked to attend court specifically to pass on to the rest of the Tower what happened. So, he duly returns to the staff hall.

The Tower is abuzz with the news that they will be leaving at the second hour of the morning. Which will mean that everything must be in readiness for the first hour, since it will take some time for everyone to board. Including the Princess under arrest.

When the other staff realise who has come, they turn and all begin asking questions at once. “I'm terribly sorry,” he says, “But I promised I wouldn't say anything without Saya Durgalen.”

There is then a small commotion as three different people head off to find her. Eventually, she arrives with Lady Ylette, and Aioru is able to begin. He recounts the evening to the best of his ability, trying to capture the mood of the nobles and the elegance of the Emperor. Aioru offers many complements to the costumers, who are justly proud of the effect.

After half a bell, he has finally exhausted everyone's questions. If he needs to be at the sky ship dock at the first bell of the morning, it would be nice if he could find somewhere to sleep. There's a daybed, he remembers, in the back of one of the meeting rooms in the Offices. It gets used during emergencies, although whenever possible they prefer to operate in shifts so everyone can be properly rested.

Before Aioru can shuffle back downstairs, Lord Conju catches his eye. He hadn't even noticed the Groom of the Chamber arriving, but it seems he can be unobtrusive when he wants to. Aioru weaves around the few people having a snack before they go off-shift for the night, to Lord Conju's side.

“The image his Radiancy made at court this evening will be remembered for years, I am sure, Lord Conju,” says Aioru, with a bow.

“I should hope so,” says Lord Conju, with a sniff, but he is also smiling slightly. “Now, you aren't the only one who would be going home tonight only to have to turn right around at their front door in order to make it to the sky ship in time for departure. We have a couple of bunk rooms in the Tower, but given the short notice, even those are full. So I shall put you in one of my guest rooms.”

Aioru actually gapes at this. He remembers his manners just in time. “Thank you very much, Lord Conju,” he says, and this time offers a deeper bow.

Lord Conju waves away his thanks, and instead calls, “Féonie, my dear, are you and your friend ready?”

“Yes, my lord,” Saya Durgalen calls back. She and another young woman rush over, offering Lord Conju a curtsy each.

“Come then,” says Lord Conju, and leaves the apartments by the back entrance. The three young people trailing him, in a manner that puts Aioru in mind of some waterbirds he had seen.

Lord Conju's own apartment is very conveniently located, which Aioru supposes is some consolation for a man who can be, and often is, summoned to work at any time of the day or night. Once there, the Groom of the Chamber's own servants whisk them away. 

Aioru is shown to a very elegantly appointed warm red room, with a large Alinoral bed. Well, he won't have much time to appreciate the luxury. He sighs, and gets ready for bed as quickly as possible.
