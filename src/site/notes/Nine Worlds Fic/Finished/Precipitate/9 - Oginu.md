Oginu rings the brass doorbell of the brightly painted house. He and Ser Rhodin look a little out of place here, even though they aren't in uniform. Solaara is incredibly diverse, and it feels strange to be somewhere most people look similar.

An elegant woman answers the door. “Hello,” she says, and leans against the door post. She quirks one eyebrow in a way Oginu is sure he has seen Sayo Mdang do.

Ser Rhodin bows, and proffers a letter. She laughs and says, “You two don't look like any of the usual postal workers.”

“I am Ser Rhodin, a colleague of Cliopher sayo Mdang and Lord Conju,” says Ser Rhodin, formally. For a moment, Oginu wonders if that was the wrong move, as the woman initially looks stunned. Then she breaks into a full throated laugh.

“Excuse me,” she says, trying to catch her breath, “Kip had mentioned you of course, but his descriptions are not very useful for identifying anyone. Come in, come in.” She waves them in, while calling up the stairs. “It's not Kip, Mama, but two of his friends.”

Oginu feels like he should correct that. He has worked in the same room as Sayo Mdang, but he hardly thinks they could be counted *friends*. They probably haven't said more to each other than a passing greeting.

Before he can work out how to frame a protest, they are presented to Sayo Mdang's mother.

Ser Rhodin strides forward, and bows low and flamboyantly. "Saya Mdang,” he says, in a slightly lower voice than Oginu is used to. He offers her the letter directly. “I asked Conju to write a few lines to make me know to you,” (which was, Oginu thought, not strictly true) “In case Cliopher had neglected to mention my name. I am Ser Rhodin.”

“The one who shows him all the interesting restaurants, yes?” she says, “Welcome! And this is…?" She looks expectantly from Ser Rhodin to Oginu.

“Guard Oginu,” says Ser Rhodin, and he bows at his name, “He kindly agreed to help me on my journey, as he is a much more confident camper than I.” Slightly stretching the truth. Implying he isn't good at camping, when Oginu knows that of course he is.

Saya Mdang invites Oginu and Ser Rhodin both to sit, and introduces Vinyë, Sayo Mdang's sister. She asks after “sweet Conju” (not something Oginu had ever thought of the fearsome Groom of the Chamber), and Ser Rhodin himself, before finally working around to the question Oginu had expected her to start with.

“Now, Rhodin,” she says, warmly, “What excuse do you have for coming all the way here and not bringing Kip with you?”

“He's on Alinor,” Ser Rhodin replies, with rather more candour than Oginu was expecting.

“Oh Kip!” interrupted Saya Mdang, and covered her mouth with her hand. “He's gone after Basil!”

“He's been gone rather longer than we expected,” says Ser Rhodin, and Oginu says nothing about them not expecting him to be gone much more than the few seconds it took to fall and become a pancake on the pavement below. “And so I'm trying to find him. We don't, I want to reassure you, believe it likely any harm has come to him. We have good relations with Alinor, and it is a peaceable place.”

There is clear relief evident on Saya Mdang's face at Ser Rhodin's reassurances.

“Most likely, he has simply been caught up mediating a local dispute over water rights, or consulting on currency reform,” adds Ser Rhodin.

“I'm afraid we didn't know anything of his plans,” says the younger Saya Mdang, looking troubled, “Although perhaps he gave some hint to Bertie or Toucan? I know Bertie keeps all of Kip's letters.”

“We have evidence to suggest he may have used an unusual portal to Alinor,” says Ser Rhodin, “And that it might be possible to reach Alinor through somewhere in the Vangaveye-ve.”

Saya Mdang looks thoughtful. “I wouldn't think he'd be able to walk through Gorjo City without me hearing about it,” she says, a little confused, “But perhaps if he was *very* determined. And he has always been interested in the most old-fashioned and esoteric lore. In which case, your best chance of following him is to ask my brother Lazo and his friends.”

“Of course, Saya Mdang,” says Ser Rhodin, bowing in his seat, “Could we trouble you for an introduction?”

“Gaudy should be back any minute now, he can take you down to the barber shop to see Uncle Lazo,” says Sayo Mdang's sister. “I need to head to work soon, or I would show you myself,” she adds.

“And how is the orchestra going?” asks Ser Rhodin, warmly.

They spend a happy while catching up on gossip. Realising there's nothing more of importance being said, Oginu assumes his well-practice guard poise. Hearing without listening, and watching carefully. As promised, it is not much later when he hears the front door open below, and the steady pound of footsteps up the stairs.

“Gaudy!” calls the younger Saya Mdang. The footsteps stop, and much slower, start approaching.

A young man, perhaps twenty, steps into the room. On seeing they have company, his face swaps dramatically from irritated to curious. “Yes, mama?” he says, politely.

“Could you please take Ser Rhodin and Guard Oginu, here,” (Oginu is impressed she remembered his name) “To see Uncle Lazo?” says his mother, with a smile.

“Okay,” he says, and then he looks more closely at Ser Rhodin, “Wait, Ser Rhodin? Are you the Rhodin who convinced Uncle Kip to try the curry so spicy he ate an entire bowl of plain yoghurt and then was sick?”

“Um, yes?” says Ser Rhodin, clearly taken by surprise that this young relative of Sayo Mdang's knows that particular story. 

“Just let me drop off my books, and I'll be right back!” says the young man, holding up a well-loved satchel briefly. Then he bounds on up the hallway.

“I would appreciate it,” says Ser Rhodin, “If you not tell anyone where Cliopher is. Or that we are looking for him. We just don't want anyone getting the wrong idea and running off into the wilderness looking for portals and getting lost.”

“Of course,” says Saya Mdang, “The young will think more of adventure than how they make their elders anxious.”

“Precisely,” says Ser Rhodin, as Gaudy comes bounding back.

“Let's go!” says Gaudy, and Ser Rhodin and Oginu both stand up, and follow him downstairs.

As they leave the house, (Oginu notices they don't lock the door), Gaudy asks Ser Rhodin, “Can you tell me more about what Uncle Kip does, Uncle Rhodin?”

Oginu nearly trips, which would have been extremely embarrassing. Thankfully Ser Rhodin is as distracted by the address, and doesn't notice. 

“I know most of what he does is _secret_, or at least he's not allowed to talk about it,” says Gaudy, when Ser Rhodin doesn't answer immediately. “Just, you know, what does his day look like? Who is his lord?”

“Thinking of joining the Service, are you?” asks Ser Rhodin, with a smile. Oginu is impressed with how well he is keeping it together. 

Oginu has had to stand with a straight face while the Last Emperor used the toilet in front of him. And while Princess Anistiasia went on one of her rants about how his Radiancy has destroyed the Empire and ruined the dynasty. Yet he finds it hard to not startle at the idea Sayo Mdang's family don't know who he works for. It's in the papers! He's usually featured in the political cartoon of the Cisven Flyer about once a month!

“Maybe,” says Gaudy, uncertainly, “I like the idea of getting to help solve hard problems that really matter, you know? Do you work with him?”

“Not exactly,” says Ser Rhodin, “But we attend a few of the same meetings, and we're friends.”

“Does he have a lot of meetings?” asks Gaudy, “He tells us something of his travels around the world, but I don't even know where in the Palace he lives!”

“He has rooms at the end of the Alinoral wing,” answers Ser Rhodin, “It's a forty-minute walk to his office in the morning. He says he likes the exercise. What does your Uncle Lazo do?”

“He's my great-uncle, actually. He's a barber, and the tanà,” says Gaudy, and then he sees the slightly blank look on their faces. “Oh, I guess Uncle Kip hasn't told you about that. He's a lore-keeper of the Mdang family, he's good at helping people solve their problems.”

“I think I see why we have been sent to see him then,” says Ser Rhodin, cryptically.

“Oh?” says Gaudy. Oginu can tell he's uncertain if he's allowed to ask. Obviously the tanà deals with _personal_ problems, as well.

“Indeed!” says Ser Rhodin, not answering the implied question at all. “What do you do with yourself, Gaudy, when you aren't showing your uncle's friend around?”

“I've just finished university,” says Gaudy, “So you can see why joining the Service might be top of my mind.”

“Oh! A scholar! What did you study, then?” asks Ser Rhodin.

Gaudy's journey through choosing what to study at university seems long and complicated. It seems that _Anthropology_ and _Topology_ are wildly different fields, and Gaudy tried both of them. 

They have just about exhausted the topic of Gaudy's studies, when they turn a corner to see a small set of shops. Each brightly painted, with an apartment above, and out the front a promenade facing the water. By the railing are a number of chairs and tables. A small group is watching a pair play backgammon, but it seems to be a background activity to the conversation.

“Uncle Lazo!” calls Gaudy, and one of the men turns to look at them.

“Hello Gaudy,” he says warmly, “And who have you brought with you?”

“Ser Rhodin and Guard Oginu,” says Gaudy, and Oginu wonders if the memory for names runs in the family, “They work with Uncle Kip in Solaara.”

Oginu ignores that one of the old men is very definitely ogling him. It happens less often when he's out of uniform, but it's hard to complain when people appreciate all the work he puts into his physique. He knows he'll never be as alluring as Ser Rhodin who is conventionally attractive, well-muscled, and charismatic. 

“We were recommended to seek your advice by Cliopher's, by Kip's, mother,” says Ser Rhodin.

Lazo, Oginu is not quite sure if he is another Sayo Mdang, or has another name or title, nods. “Thank you for bringing them, Gaudy. I heard the bookshop got your order in today.”

Gaudy looks torn between wanting to stay, and understanding the dismissal. After a few seconds, he gives in. “Thank you, Uncle Lazo, I'll go and get it then,” he turns to Ser Rhodin, looking a little wistful, “It was nice to meet you both.” Then he walks back the way they had come.

“Would you like to go inside?” asks Sayo Mdang's uncle.

“Saya Mdang suggested we might also need the advice of your friends,” says Ser Rhodin, “We were asking about the possibility of there being natural passages to other worlds in the Vangaveye-ve.”

This gets them some very suspicious looks. One of the women says, “It's not impossible, everyone knows passages between worlds open all over. There are plenty of stories about of sailors finding themselves on Ysthar or Colhélhé.”

Ser Rhodin nods, in a way that is almost a bow. “And I have no wish to pry into secrets or sacred places,” he says. The old man who was appreciating Oginu before grins. Ser Rhodin continues, “I would not ask at all, but that Cliopher, that is, Kip, has gone to Alinor, and seems to be stuck there.”

A look passes around the group. Slightly sceptical, but with a tinge of worry. The woman who spoke before crosses her arms. Oginu notices that Lazo is focused entirely on his compatriots, and is almost ignoring Ser Rhodin. Is he their leader?

“You're a wizard,” says another woman, “surely you can make a simple finding spell.”

Ser Rhodin offers her a shallow bow. “If you can tell I have magic, Saya, then you can also tell I only just have enough to light a candle.” He pauses for a moment, and Oginu wonders if he's going to turn up the charm. Quietly, he continues, “I couldn't make a finding charm to fetch a lost button in my own room, much less a friend on another world.”

The modesty is not impressing the woman who can sense Rhodin's magic. She continues to look at him with challenge showing in every inch of her small frame.

Eventually, reluctantly, Ser Rhodin says, “A wizard did give me a finding spell.”

The woman holds out her hand, “Hand it over, young man, I knew I saw more magic on you.”

To Oginu's surprise, Ser Rhodin pulls the knotted ribbon from his pocket. He doesn't get time to have to think about actually giving it away though, because the woman immediately takes back her hand and clamps it over her eyes.

“You could have just *said* you'd been sent by the Lord Magus,” she says, her voice strained, “Ow! Ugh.” One of the other women wraps an arm around her and glares at Ser Rhodin. The first woman shakes her off, “I'll be fine, it just surprised me, is all.”

“My sincere apologies,” says Ser Rhodin, and offers one of those special court bows that Oginu has never bothered learning the meaning of. “I had no idea it would have that effect.”

“We will discuss your request,” she says, uncovering her eyes, “And give you an answer tomorrow. But first you must answer our questions *honestly*.”

Ser Rhodin nods, and says, “There are some questions which I may be bound not to answer, but I will not lie.”

“Who is Kip to the Lord Magus?” she starts with, and Oginu is impressed with her forthrightness, if not her interrogation technique.

“Cliopher sayo Mdang is the Private Secretary and Hands of the Emperor,” says Ser Rhodin, which really should not be a surprise to anyone, but is apparently a surprise to a number of people here. “Of anyone on Zunidh, Cliopher is the person who works closest with his Radiancy.”

“Are you sure Kip wants to be found?” she challenges them. It is, Oginu concedes, a very fair question. If Sayo Mdang had been accused unjustly, or treated poorly (never!), then his best course of action might well be to seek refuge on another world. And his family and friends might have good reason not to want to aid the guards tasked with bringing him back.

“I cannot be certain, of course, as I haven't spoken to him since his disappearance,” says Ser Rhodin, with a small smile, “But I know he did not intend to go through the portal, and did not know it was there.”

“What will you do, if you do find him?” she continues.

“Aid him in his return to Zunidh,” says Ser Rhodin, “I grew up on Alinor.”

She nods. “Come back tomorrow then, and Lazo can tell you what we've decided.”

Ser Rhodin bows to them all this time, and belatedly Oginu offers a bow of his own. “Thank you for your consideration,” says Ser Rhodin. He then turns to Oginu with a raised eyebrow. Together, Oginu and Ser Rhodin leave the group to their deliberations.

“Couldn't we just follow the charm without them, sir?” asks Oginu, wondering why Ser Rhodin is wasting time with these people.

“Can you sail, Oginu?” asks Ser Rhodin, mildly.

“A little, sir, well enough for island hopping,” says Oginu, slightly defensively.

“And do you have a boat?” asks Ser Rhodin, seeming to examine the windows of the shops they pass.

“This is a port, sir, we could hire one,” says Oginu, gesturing down to the harbour where, indeed, there are many vessels from little canoes to a few large trading ships.

“I think you might struggle to do so, if the people in that meeting said not to rent to us,” says Ser Rhodin, looking wistfully at the menu of a noodle restaurant. “Perhaps a Jilkanese person might rent you one, if you paid enough. But I would be worried about it staying seaworthy.”

“What?” says Oginu, startled, “I mean, sorry sir, I don't understand. Could you please explain?” He's blushing, and feeling like a raw recruit.

“Who did you see at that meeting, Oginu?” asks Ser Rhodin.

Ah, this is going to be a lesson. “Sayo Mdang's uncle and his friends?” offers Oginu.

“Nothing else?” queries Ser Rhodin.

Oginu tries to think of any detail he saw that might give a clue to who they were. “They were mostly older people,” he says, reluctantly, “I think I saw a guild badge on two of them? One of them might have been a guild master?”

“True,” says Ser Rhodin, “Can you see or perceive magic at all, Oginu?”

“When it's worked, sir, I get an itchy nose like I need to sneeze,” says Oginu, slightly embarrassed. “I don't think anyone was working magic, or at least not schooled magic.”

“There were two mages there, more powerful than any wizard of the Ouranatha,” says Ser Rhodin, “Of course, neither as powerful as his Radiancy.” If that isn't startling enough, Ser Rhodin adds, “And a god.”

Oginu doesn't know what to say to that. Gods make him nervous. Most of the gods of his hometown are associated with natural disasters, and are definitely not anyone you would want or expect to come across watching a game of backgammon in a suburban street. Technically, his Radiancy is a god, and even he makes Oginu nervous at times.

“Oh,” says Oginu, suddenly realising how big and serious this adventure is. “What do we do now, sir?”

“First, we find a hotel, thankfully Lord Conju gave me some recommendations. Then we find dinner,” says Ser Rhodin, as they turn into what seems to be the main entertainment area of the city.

These seem to be both problems for Ser Rhodin to solve. “Yes, sir,” says Oginu, and wonders what kind of hotel _Lord Conju_ would recommend.
