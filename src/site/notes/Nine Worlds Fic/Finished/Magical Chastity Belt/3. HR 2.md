I dropped into the fire at the heart of me. Hope, and other strong emotions, had fed it. It was much brighter and more lively than I had seen it since I had been crowned. Perhaps another time I might investigate further, but for now I turned outward. Not, as usual, to Zunidh. Or even as I had once done to the twisted magic of the Palace.

Instead, I looked around to see Cliopher. Or rather, what my soul thought of Cliopher. In his red-and-black uniform, with a cheerful smile. Swirling over his skin, stranger than any lotion that Conju had ever produced, were the layers of my protections.

“Would you mind undressing?” I asked, knowing that I was not _actually_ asking Cliopher, but instead doing a mental trick to examine the magic on him more closely.

Cliopher smiled, and stripped exactly as he had to go swimming. It was a little strange to be looking closely at him naked, even in your imagination. You tried to stay focused on those areas that you had noticed often went uncovered, at least on this island. If perhaps I also took advantage to admire his shoulders, well, I hoped he didn't mind too much.

As with any citizen of the fallen Empire, small scraps of Schooled magic had clung to him. I took the opportunity to clean them out from under my protective magics. A tracking spell that looked so old and worn it might well have been from his childhood. The tatters of various healing magics that had done their work centuries ago. The compulsion to not look in the eyes of the Emperor that used to automatically be given to every member of the Service, and clearly had done nothing to Cliopher since the Fall.

There was something under all of that. It only became clear as I tidied the magic (even some of my own, I had to admit, that had gotten a little disorganised). No, two things. One was unmistakably the claim of a god. Not any other divine influence, just the kind of stamp that told other gods to stay away. I wondered if the Moon Lady had noticed it, and which god it might be. Perhaps the one who had bartered with Cliopher, and given him a necklace of cowries.

I don't know why it took so long for me to recognise the signature of the final magic. Perhaps because I was looking for gods and great works, or the oil slick of minor necromancy. Perhaps because I was looking _through_ it all the time, I had become as accustomed to seeing it as I was my own nose.

Imperial bindings and purifications. Fine golden chains, anchored in his body, twisting around him. Once I could see them I could follow them, grasp what they meant. Desperately trying to hold onto a clinical detachment, I circled Cliopher, the pattern clear. I wanted to tear it off him, burn it to ash. 

I couldn't. It was anchored in me too.

In a deep trance, if I screamed, I might cause an earthquake. I very nearly didn't care. I collapsed on the ground and shook, trying to hold on to my control. The sick, twisted people who had done this weren't here to feel my wrath. They were half a world away. I would not destroy the Zunidh Cliopher loved, had rebuilt so painstakingly, simply for revenge.

I needed to tell him. Not needing to worry about your imperial dignity, I crawled back to the fire. It was throwing up sparks, reflecting my fury. I breathed slowly, deeply, closing my eyes.

Conju knew the signs your body made as you returned. By the time you were aware of sound and light, he had placed a fragrant cup of tea by you, so that the rising steam wafted into your face. You dug your fingers into the blanket, and tried to summon your voice as quickly as possible.

“Cliopher, have you been involved in any rituals with the Ouranatha where I wasn't present?” you asked, the body not quite used to having to make sounds yet, it crackled a little.

“Many, my lord,” said Cliopher, blinking as he sat up again, “Although none recently.”

You picked up the cup of tea, so that I didn't begin shredding the blanket. It had done nothing wrong, supporting me through many trances. You asked “Could you identify any pattern?”

Cliopher didn't seem perturbed by this question, but you could see that Conju was stiffening very slightly. He knew you had found something, and who had done it. Could he guess what? Did he blame you for not noticing? He should.

“They started after the high priests were informed I was to be appointed as your Hands, my lord,” said Cliopher, as if this was a question about one of the regular reports, “They were more frequent in that first year, my lord, and since then perhaps one or two per year.”

You were sure that at least Rhodin, and probably also Ludvic, were now blaming themselves for not noticing. But what would there have been for them to see? But you should have. I should have.

Cliopher's face showed that he had seen their reactions, and he quickly added, “I never felt any ill effect from any of them, my lord. They were usually quite short.”

You had to put down the teacup, because I very desperately wanted to smash it, just to relieve some of them tension inside. Carefully, you recited the names of all those who you had killed with careless words or actions, using it to count your breaths, and force your lungs and heart to behave. Once you were again in control, you tried to apologise. 

“I know it does not in any way excuse what has been done to you, Cliopher, but I want you to know that I had no idea, and if I had known, I would have ordered it stopped immediately.”

“I don't understand, my lord,” said Cliopher.

You had to resort to one of your least favourite methods of asserting control over the body of the Emperor. Once, a young woman had taught you some tricks she had learnt to get unwanted hands to release, by digging fingernails into the quick of the assailant's. You had found it also worked to maintain your grip. It was painful, but it kept you grounded and your magic contained. 

“Is this related to what Conju and I discussed before? Did the Ouranatha place a curse?” added Cliopher.

As if this was a question raised by someone in a magical journal, you said, “That depends on one's perspective. I would consider it a curse, but so much of the magic that bound the Empire together was similar.”

Then he asked the question I was dreading, the one I knew I would have to answer, that you could have no answer for. “They placed an Imperial binding on me, my lord? To what purpose?”

I tried to gather the right words, that wouldn't hurt him more. He looked at me, and all I could think of was when one of Damien's children had asked me if I had a father. What could I say that wouldn't shatter his world? That would be within the bounds of things I was allowed to say. Then, bound by magic. Now, bound to be you. I couldn't face it, I closed my eyes.

“My lord, if it has not impeded my work all these years, it does not seem to have been a problem, perhaps it can be forgotten. Unless it poses some future danger, my lord?” offered Cliopher.

As if the only way he mattered to me, was in his ability to complete his work. No, you were not allowed to care for your household, not allowed to want them to have complete and fulfilling lives. All that was important was that _your_ every whim was catered to, and damn everyone else. He didn't even think it worth removing unless it was a danger to _you_.

If I was going to tell him, I could not do it in front of everyone. “Please leave us, everyone,” I said, in a passable imitation of you. To my relief, the guards accepted this, and moved to stand outside the doors. As did Rhodin and Ludvic, making themselves scarce. Conju bowed, and picked up the tray, and began moving towards the dining room.

Suddenly I realised that perhaps this was something where Cliopher might benefit from a friendly hand to hold. “Unless?” I said, “Cliopher, I know Conju is your friend. If you would like his support.” Conju paused, looking back at Cliopher.

Seemingly still unconcerned, Cliopher only said, “I'm fine, my lord.”

Some look I didn't recognise passed between Cliopher and Conju as the latter slipped from the room. I pretended I had seen nothing.

When I was finally sure we were actually alone, I just said it, as bluntly as I could. “Cliopher, the Ouranatha purified you, and bound your body as… as… an Imperial concubine.”

Cliopher blinked, seemingly still not grasping what this meant. What had happened to him. I went on, almost unable to stop now that I had begun.  “I cannot imagine you will want to stay, knowing that has been done to you. I am not sure if it will be possible to remove, although I will try everything I can. Anything you want, Cliopher, anything I can do, please ask.”

Cliopher took a deep breath, and then said, “May I stay, my lord?” in a small voice. As if perhaps he was afraid you would dismiss him for being purified. As if you blamed _him_. Oh, it was so very cruel, the way the throne twisted everything so you always had to be in the right.

“But Cliopher,” I said, trying to understand, “How… How can you stand to? Surely you would hate me, you can't…” I trailed off, images of him finally understanding and screaming at me flashing through my mind.

Shrugging, Cliopher said, “It has been mildly inconvenient, but my lord, it was before I came into your service that I last had dreams of having children. I am more troubled that they did so without your consent.”

“Without—my—Cliopher…” I looked up, my eyes threatening to spill tears through Conju's work. “They should have asked _you_.”

“Well, yes,” conceded Cliopher, in the exact tone of voice he used when you told him the council of Princes would block him, “But if you had agreed, I don't think I would have seen a problem with it.”

I dropped my head into my hands. “That is _why_ I never allowed them to purify anyone, which they knew, so they didn't ask.” How much easier Conju's work would have been, or Ludvic's, if they could have been purified.

Cliopher still didn't grasp what I was trying to say. “You wouldn't allow them to purify anyone who agreed?”

I tried again, being as blunt as I could. “If I gave permission, that person would be unable to refuse. They would not have had a choice.” I was looking at the floor, not wanting to see the betrayal on his face. 

“I wouldn't have said yes if it had blinded me, or if I had been required to never see my family, my lord,” said Cliopher, much in the same way he tried to explain budget forecasts. “But if I was already blind, or had lost my family in the Fall, then those would not have been such a great sacrifice.”

There was a time of tense silence, interrupted only by the birds. You had thought, once, that you had blinded him. Thought you would add his name to the list you recited, as the bars of your cage pulled in around you. A blind member of your household would be at increased risk from the touch taboo, which nobody dared to test.

Ludvic had not lost his family in the Fall. He had lost them when you (I, I) burned them alive. While he watched, and gave the order to shoot any who escaped the configuration. Would he have rather died with them? You had never known him to go home to visit family, you had not dared to ask such questions. Had you, however inadvertently, asked him to kill his own family for you?

“My lord,” said Cliopher, pulling me from my spiral of guilt, “If you are worried that I can touch you without consequence, of course I would not impose my presence on you. But please, believe me when I say, it has not been a sacrifice, not even something I made much note of.”

I tried to stamp down my fear and self-loathing, and listen to what Cliopher was saying. In all our years working together, you had only been afraid *for* him. Unless one counted that dream where he was Master Tutor, forcing me to redo my budgets until the numbers came out correct.

In my life between towers I had met people who like me, but didn't want to have sex. Usually I found this out when they politely turned me down, and I treated it as an anthropology lesson. Monogamy, celibacy by choice, how different cultures treated age and gender. So many lessons I was too naive to find awkward. Including some people who found no pleasure in it. But Cliopher had not actually said he had no interest.

He thought for a bit, and then added, “Although, I would be more comfortable if you reformed the Ouranatha. I can't imagine Conju would feel the same way if it happened to him.”

I took a shaky breath, acknowledging the attempt to lift the mood and trying desperately not to imagine Conju in the same circumstance. It *was* true, I knew, that Conju very much enjoyed physical pleasure, and probably struggled to understand Cliopher's lower interest. On two occasions he had inquired as delicately as possible if you would like any kind of *aids*. It was sweet, if entirely misplaced.

“He is very concerned for you,” I said.

Cliopher let out a small huff. “I did tell him it was fine,” he said, “But we are talking about the man who dates every unsuitable man he can find, so…” he paused, realising perhaps it wasn't quite appropriate to discuss Conju's love life with his boss. Not knowing, I assume, that I got every detail direct. “I mean, uh,” he added, “Conju is of a romantic disposition. My lord.”

Ludvic I might describe as a *romantic*, Conju did not really fit that word. Perhaps a small smile crept to my face as I thought of Conju the Romantic.

“He is, isn't he,” I said softly. Then trying to pull myself back to the current problem.

First, I needed to reassure Cliopher. “If you genuinely want to stay, Cliopher, I would never send you away. I trust you completely.”

“But it may not be possible to unwind the magic on you until I am also unwound from the taboos.” I had to admit this, that from the inside I did not know how much I could undo. It would be a tense negotiation with whatever remained of the Ouranatha, after Cliopher had his audit.

“Well then,” said Cliopher, with a smile, “I will stay. And if you ever want someone to hold your hand, or scratch an itch between your shoulder blades that you can't quite reach, let me know.”

It was so generous and kind. I didn't deserve it, you didn't either. Would you ever dare ask? Maybe after I had retired. Maybe then I would be enough of a man to hold his hand.
