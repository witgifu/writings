I was so thoroughly outside what you were expected to be, I hardly knew how to speak. Eventually I managed to get out something, because Cliopher looked at me with so much empathy I knew he understood.

My initial, slightly mad thought, was that the Moon Lady came down and assaulted someone at every lunar eclipse in the Vangaveye-ve. But no, if that had been true, Cliopher would have warned us, and the entire party would not have seemed surprised.

It wasn't as if I had no experience of unwanted arousal, but the experience of a teenager locked in a tower and beset by spells were not much applicable now. Cliopher suggested playing music, and while I looked around to see who was carrying the harp, Conju must have suggested he leave. When I had the instrument in hand, and had decided on a chair, the rest of them quietly left without a word. The guards on duty doing their best to look obviously and intently away from me.

My mind was fizzing with the feeling of her hands, her lips. Enchantress, I had called her once, when I thought it only flattery. Deep breathing kept the nausea at bay. To be captured, even by the pure, wild, lustful magic of the Moon, hurt.

I ran my fingers up and down the scales. The exercises I hadn't bothered with for years when I had been a regular performer. Trying to remember the name of the gentle giant who had explained that music drills were just as important as sword drills.

Focusing on the strings. Getting the perfect tone, the resonances between strings. I tried to play every song that you had given an award as Emperor. Every court dance, shifting between them as my mind drifted back to her hands, her lips, the scent in her hair.

Perhaps it was the music, or simply time. Eventually you returned sufficiently that sleep might be possible. Washing, changing, with sufficient control to not accidentally touch Conju. You put the harp down, and went sedately to your room.

I did not remember my dreams, which was always a relief.

The next morning, Conju clearly had something on his mind that was distressing him. I wanted to ask, but even with all we had been through in these stolen weeks in paradise, you didn't dare ask directly.

“Is everyone well, Conju? After last night?” you said, gently. It gave him the chance to offer nothing but banal pleasantries, or to explain. Hopefully you had guessed the correct track.

“Yes, my lord,” he said, not entirely automatically. “That is, we are all concerned for you, my lord, but none of us has had any ill effects.”

You only had a second to feel that you had guessed wrong, before Conju decided you had provided a sufficient entry point.

“My lord,” he said, and you could see the tension in his body. You suspected he wanted to prostrate himself but was forcing himself to stop. It was such a gift, that he had understood something at all of what I wanted. Eventually he compromised, by offering a bow with the court gesture for one asking a favour. You nodded for him to continue. With the gesture, not of a great lord accepting a petition, but of an equal offering a gift freely.

He startled slightly at this response, but went on. “My lord, I am concerned that Cliopher may be under some malicious magical influence,” he said, and you contained my immediate recoil. You were the lord magus, Conju would never _dare_ imply that you had not ascertained that all your staff were magically protected. 

“Or possibly divine,” he added, which given the day before, was perhaps something that had only recently occurred to him as similar. Then he suddenly realised what he had implied, and I could see his entire body go ridged, as he forced himself to remain in place.

“I have never noticed any, but something that has been there for a very long time, or something with a similar signature to the schooled magic he interacts with regularly, might pass my notice. And yes, divine influence may choose not to leave an obvious mark,” you said, trying to reassure him you had not taken offence.

Conju took a slow breath, and relaxed slightly. Not that he was ever completely relaxed around you, it would be irresponsible to do so when you could so easily end his life.

“He said, my lord, that he had discussed it with doctors, but that they were not able to provide an explanation,” he said. That, it seemed, was his final piece of information.

It seemed that Conju thought the problem was not something that could simply be said politely. There was presumably something since the arrival of the Moon Lady that had caused him to feel the need to tell you, or to ask Cliopher. Perhaps still addled by the Moon, it took me a surprising amount of time to assemble the pieces.

There was no need to embarrass Conju further and attempt to confirm your suspicions.

“Thank you, Conju,” you said, sincerely, “I will ask Cliopher if he would be amenable to being examined more closely.”

Considering the delicate nature of the issue, I wanted to ask Cliopher in private, or as much privacy as you thought possible. So you invited him snorkelling. This was unexceptional, you had done so for many previous mornings. It was lovely, once again, to admire the life in the water. And to share that with someone else. As if you were any other people in this lagoon.

We sat on the sandbar, basking in the sunlight. 

“Cliopher,” you said, when you realised we would return soon, and so you were running out of time to ask.

“Yes, my lord?” said Cliopher, sounding slightly sleepy. I cherished that he could be so relaxed around you.

“Conju is worried you might be cursed,” you said, carefully. Keeping my emotions well clear. As if this was only Conju being worried that Cliopher was in need of a new hat.

Cliopher looked at you, his amusement showing clear on his face. He was obviously not bothered by Conju's concerns, not that they had been shared with you. He said, “Yes, my lord, he did say. But I have no reason to believe it is anything more than the unfortunate long term effects of a bout of illness.”

I did not know enough about medicine to know if this was possible. But from Conju's statement earlier and Cliopher's well known refusal to see a doctor for anything other than imminent death, it was reasonable to conclude this was his own conclusion. Conju had previously resorted to asking you to schedule a magical working so he could lure Cliopher to see a doctor about an infection that was causing him to limp.

Not wanting to impose on him, you asked carefully. “You may say no, Cliopher, and I will not mention it again, but if I examined you magically you could be sure. It would put Conju at ease.”

Cliopher seemed totally unfazed. “If it will make Conju happy, then of course, my lord.” He smiled warmly, as he might after a successful session wrangling some problem into a solution. Perhaps he really did appreciate finding a way to reassure Conju. He certainly did sound like he expected you to find anything.

“After lunch, perhaps?” I offered. Going into a trance on the sandbar would be possible, but it would be more comfortable for both of us to do so on a full stomach. And you had got rather attached to the way Conju so carefully roused us.

“Of course, my lord,” said Cliopher, “Thank you.”

You swam back together to the shore. Conju was there, of course, with towels and dressing robes. Cliopher wandered off wrapped only in a small towel, much to the irritation of Conju. I smiled inside at the little moments that passed between them.

Once Cliopher was out of earshot, you said, “Cliopher has agreed. I will need to do a deep trance to make sure it is a thorough examination.”

Conju, in his new mode of being _slightly_ less formal in private, gave only a deep bow of thanks. Then you all returned to the house so you could wash off the salt water, and dress for the afternoon.
Without any comment, Conju set up the common room for you to trance. Adjusting the louvres to reduce the light to a gentle warmth, preparing tea. He had even brought along the soft throw that was placed on your trancing couch. Of course, he had noticed how you twisted your fingers in it, and being prepared for seemingly any course, had considered it necessary to bring. I wondered if he thought it part of the magic.

Cliopher joined us, and you directed him to sit down as he liked. You must have been slipping, because I said, “Hopefully this won't take long, but…” and trailed off. Wondering exactly how many layers of my own magical protections I would have to dig through. Nine hundred years? I was reminded of a picture of geological strata from a book I had as child.

“Of course, my lord,” said Cliopher, and followed your instructions. “Should I close my eyes? Or do anything?”

I smiled, and suggested only, “Relax, perhaps imagine you are floating on the lagoon.”

As if this was an instruction you gave him regularly, he closed his eyes and lent back.
